Data logging is the automatic recording of successive instrument readings against time, either into memory inside the instrument or into a file on an attached computer. It converts a measuring instrument from something that shows a present value into something that captures behaviour over an interval — drift, thermal response, discharge curves, intermittent events — that no operator watching a display could usefully record by hand.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-835" title="EEVblog #835 - Wekomm Resistance Standard Part 2">835</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-576" title="EEVblog #576 - Advantest R6142 Current Voltage Generator">576</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-774" title="EEVblog #774 - Low Battery Discharge Testing Part 1">774</a></sup> On handheld multimeters it is the single feature that most sharply separates top-of-range models from mid-range ones: the difference between a Fluke 289 and a Fluke 87.5 comes down to data logging, PC interface capability, and tighter accuracy specifications.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-15" title="EEVblog #15 Part 1 of 2 - Fluke 189/289 multimeter review">15</a></sup>

## Memory depth and sample interval

An instrument-resident logger is defined by two numbers: how many samples it can hold and how fast it can take them. The [[agilent-u1272a|Agilent U1272A]] carries 10,000 data points of [[sample-memory|sample memory]],<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-249" title="EEVblog #249 - Agilent U1272A Multimeter Review">249</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-216" title="EEVblog #216 - Gaussian Resistor Redux">216</a></sup> the U1253A stores 1,000 measurements,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-56" title="EEVblog #56 - Agilent U1253A OLED Multimeter Review &amp; Teardown">56</a></sup> and the Gossen Metrawatt Xtra holds roughly 15,000 samples in about 540 KB of memory.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-46" title="EEVblog #46 - Gossen Metrawatt Xtra Multimeter Review">46</a></sup> The Fluke 189/289 family provides tens of thousands of samples built in.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-15" title="EEVblog #15 Part 1 of 2 - Fluke 189/289 multimeter review">15</a></sup> Bench instruments extend this considerably; the Keithley DMM7510 carries a large internal memory, and an unpopulated memory site on its [[processor]] board suggests a factory expansion option.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-731" title="EEVblog #731 - Keithley DMM7510 7.5 Digit Multimeter Teardown">731</a></sup>

Sample interval is usually adjustable over a wide range. The U1253A logs from one reading per second out to intervals of thousands of seconds.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-56" title="EEVblog #56 - Agilent U1253A OLED Multimeter Review &amp; Teardown">56</a></sup> The Fluke 289 allows either a total duration in days, hours and minutes or an explicit sample interval, and reports the remaining sample time once running.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-15" title="EEVblog #15 Part 1 of 2 - Fluke 189/289 multimeter review">15</a></sup> One reading per second is a common working default for both handheld and bench logging.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-774" title="EEVblog #774 - Low Battery Discharge Testing Part 1">774</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1592" title="EEVblog 1592 - Fluke 287 Multimeter Teardown">1592</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1379" title="EEVblog 1379 - What&#x27;s all this NPLC Stuff Anyhow?">1379</a></sup>

The lower bound matters as much as the upper. The Keysight EDU34450A will not go below a one-second sample interval in data log mode.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1382" title="EEVblog 1382 - Keysight EDU34450A 5.5 digit Bench Multimeter TEARDOWN">1382</a></sup> A web-access adapter retrofitted to older instruments imposes a three-second minimum log time, though it does at least store readings internally rather than requiring a permanently attached PC.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1232" title="EEVblog #1232 - Add Web Access To Old Instruments!">1232</a></sup> Where the interval floor is too coarse for the phenomenon under test, the log is simply the wrong tool.

## Trigger, hold and event modes

Logging need not be time-driven. The U1272A offers three distinct data logging modes — triggered, hand and event — selected from the DLOG menu.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-216" title="EEVblog #216 - Gaussian Resistor Redux">216</a></sup> Combined with trigger hold, this turns the meter into an unattended sampler: the reading settles, the meter locks and stores it automatically, and the sample counter increments without a button press.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-249" title="EEVblog #249 - Agilent U1272A Multimeter Review">249</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-216" title="EEVblog #216 - Gaussian Resistor Redux">216</a></sup> Applied to a batch of 1,000 resistors with a purpose-built probe jig, the arrangement gave roughly a fivefold increase in testing speed over manual recording, with no script or software written at all.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-216" title="EEVblog #216 - Gaussian Resistor Redux">216</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-249" title="EEVblog #249 - Agilent U1272A Multimeter Review">249</a></sup> The practical caveat is timing — probing too quickly does not give the meter time to register a new event, so the operator must confirm each event is actually captured; an audible event beep, configurable in the setup menu, resolves this.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-216" title="EEVblog #216 - Gaussian Resistor Redux">216</a></sup>

Autonomous logging can be pushed below the level of the instrument [[firmware]]. On the Energy Micro Tiny Gecko, a timer repeatedly triggers an ADC conversion and DMA moves each result to [[ram|RAM]], waking the CPU by interrupt only once a given number of conversions has accumulated — logging from the ADC without powering up the processor at all.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-269" title="EEVblog #269 - Energy Micro Tiny Gecko">269</a></sup>

## Retrieving and analysing the data

Reviewing a log should not require a PC. The U1253A and U1272A both provide a view mode reached by holding a dedicated button, identifying the sample index and cycling through stored readings.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-56" title="EEVblog #56 - Agilent U1253A OLED Multimeter Review &amp; Teardown">56</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-249" title="EEVblog #249 - Agilent U1272A Multimeter Review">249</a></sup> The Fluke 289 goes further, offering either save-for-later-upload or an immediate trend plot of the captured data, auto-scaled vertically — a scale of ±0.001 V on a quiet DC measurement, which resolves [[noise]] invisible on the numeric display.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-15" title="EEVblog #15 Part 1 of 2 - Fluke 189/289 multimeter review">15</a></sup> That trend plotting is not universal: the EDU34450A can data log but has no live trend plot, restricting on-instrument review to stepping through readings one at a time.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1382" title="EEVblog 1382 - Keysight EDU34450A 5.5 digit Bench Multimeter TEARDOWN">1382</a></sup>

Graphical bench meters treat logging as one element of a wider analysis suite. The Agilent 34461A combines a trend chart, histogram and statistics with the log, and its data can be downloaded over LAN or GPIB or dumped to a CSV file on disk — a path that yields better resolution than the front-panel display shows.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-489" title="EEVblog #489 - Agilent 34461A Multimeter Review">489</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-515" title="EEVblog #515 - Battery Ionic Resistance Investigation">515</a></sup> The same reasoning applies at the low end: on a 6.5-digit meter measuring a 9 V [[battery]], 10 µV of [[display-resolution|display resolution]] can be bettered still further through the logging and trend plot function.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-515" title="EEVblog #515 - Battery Ionic Resistance Investigation">515</a></sup> The addition of a graphics display, data logging and histograms was a deliberate differentiator when the 34401A was succeeded.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1032" title="EEVblog #1032  Part 4 - John Kenny Keysight Interview">1032</a></sup>

Export formats are consistently CSV or plain text. The BK Precision 879B [[lcr-meter|LCR meter]] records data at full resolution and saves to Excel or text format.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-137" title="EEVblog #137 - BK Precision 879B Handheld LCR Meter Review">137</a></sup> The Rohde & Schwarz NGP800 logs all four channels with a log indicator on screen and exports to CSV, and logging can be bound to the user-defined front-panel button.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1293" title="EEVblog #1293 - New Rohde and Schwarz NGP800 800W PSU">1293</a></sup> The [[brymen-bm787bt|Brymen BM787BT]] app saves a real-time logger session as CSV, though the route to it is buried under a dashboard rather than placed with the record function where it would be expected.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-ruxzp0OMicg" title="Brymen BM787BT Bluetooth App First Impressions">•</a></sup>

Nomenclature can mislead. On the LeCroy WaveJet Touch 354, the logging function under the measure menu does not log or download [[waveform]] data at all — it logs measurement results, specifically pass/fail measurement data, saved to [[usb|USB]] memory as a CSV file.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-792" title="EEVBlog #792 - Lecroy Wavejet Touch 354 Oscilloscope Review">792</a></sup>

## Software as the weak point

The recurring failure in commercial data logging is not the hardware but the host software. A Rigol instrument with both Ethernet and USB interfaces shipped with drivers but no software capable of extracting and plotting the data, leaving LabVIEW or a third-party package as the only route.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-647" title="EEVblog #647 - Agilent 53131A Frequency Counter Oven Upgrade">647</a></sup> An [[electronic-load|electronic load]]'s bundled software would not log in constant-power mode at all, and its nominal Excel and CSV export simply did not work.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-774" title="EEVblog #774 - Low Battery Discharge Testing Part 1">774</a></sup> The Gossen Metrawatt's Windows software was rudimentary despite the interface cable and software being a several-hundred-dollar extra on top of a $499 meter.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-46" title="EEVblog #46 - Gossen Metrawatt Xtra Multimeter Review">46</a></sup>

The consistent engineering judgment is that instrument vendors should not attempt to build analysis into their applications. The correct scope is to let the user log to text at the fastest rate the hardware supports and dump it out; spreadsheet analysis belongs to the user afterwards.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1558" title="EEVblog 1558 - Mailbag">1558</a></sup> A supplied calculation workbook that turns out to be a spreadsheet, or in-program arithmetic on the measurement input, adds nothing that export would not provide better.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1558" title="EEVblog 1558 - Mailbag">1558</a></sup> Third-party software fills the gap where vendors do not: general logging packages exist that drive a range of multimeters and instruments, support very large data files over long logging periods, and allow arbitrary logging intervals and user-added instrument support.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1684" title="EEVblog 1684 - Awesome Logging Software:  Test Controller">1684</a></sup>

## Long-duration runs

Logging over days changes the failure modes. A [[resistance]] standard placed in a thermal chamber set to 20 °C was recorded continuously overnight, with a brief interruption while the leads were rerouted through the chamber's side port; the earlier data was retained rather than restarted, since prior history is itself evidence of stability.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-835" title="EEVblog #835 - Wekomm Resistance Standard Part 2">835</a></sup> A companion run captured a large sample set at intervals of a couple of seconds with 10 power line cycles of integration, resolving diurnal structure — a rise, a dip, and a reversal corresponding to the laboratory being occupied or empty.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-834" title="EEVblog #834 - Wekomm Resistance Standard">834</a></sup> A precision [[current]]-[[voltage]] generator was characterised over a couple of days of logging to establish how accurate and stable its 1 mA output was over time, and measured better than its [[datasheet|data sheet]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-576" title="EEVblog #576 - Advantest R6142 Current Voltage Generator">576</a></sup> The 34461A left running overnight showed visible drift when the full record was recalled.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-489" title="EEVblog #489 - Agilent 34461A Multimeter Review">489</a></sup>

The dominant risk on such runs is loss of the entire record. A month-long thermal chamber cycling test set up without [[battery-backup|battery backup]] would have reset and lost all accumulated data on any mains glitch; the run completed only by luck, and full battery backup on all logging equipment is the correct practice for unattended tests.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-s2KkgI-kyK0" title="The Signal Path Lab">•</a></sup> Battery-powered loggers face the same exposure at the other end: a meter whose contacts are corroded from an alkaline leak may work well enough for casual use but is not something to entrust a logging run to.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1687" title="EEVblog 1687 - A Pointless Alkaline Battery LEAKAGE Repair">1687</a></sup> Better-behaved designs handle it gracefully — the BM787BT detects low battery, shuts the meter down cleanly rather than dying mid-record, and on fresh cells and reconnection both the waveform and the data log resume from where they stopped.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-XOuYJ1nxVJQ" title="BM787BT Bluetooth CYBLE-212006-01 Fimware OTA Update">•</a></sup>

Where absolute timing matters, the log's own clock becomes a measurand. [[oscillator|Oscillator]] drift over a logging interval is not known in direction in advance, and if it is close to linear it can be removed after the fact: take an accurate timestamp at the start against a GPS-locked rubidium standard orders of magnitude better than the oscillator in use, repeat the [[calibration]] at the end, and offset the difference between true and recorded time across the record.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-61" title="EEVblog #61 - Crystal Oscillator Drift">61</a></sup> Logging a counter's output against a rubidium reference is likewise the way to characterise drift that is invisible on the display.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-647" title="EEVblog #647 - Agilent 53131A Frequency Counter Oven Upgrade">647</a></sup>

## Battery life

Logging is not free in power terms. Around 300 hours is taken as a decent [[battery-life|battery life]] for a handheld meter, and that figure applies to standard measurement functions — data logging and other extras draw more.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-184" title="EEVblog #184 - Open Hardware Multimeter Concept">184</a></sup> The Fluke 287 is an instructive exception: nominally 100 hours of battery life, but 200 hours in logging mode, because it samples more slowly there and consumes roughly half the power.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1592" title="EEVblog 1592 - Fluke 287 Multimeter Teardown">1592</a></sup> Auto power-off, which normally protects battery life, is correctly suppressed in logging modes.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-56" title="EEVblog #56 - Agilent U1253A OLED Multimeter Review &amp; Teardown">56</a></sup>

## Implementation inside instruments

Logging capability shows up plainly in a teardown. In the Fluke 287, an MSP430 handles the [[multimeter]] function exactly as in the rest of the range, while a Freescale processor with two memory devices including flash was bolted on to drive the [[lcd|LCD]] graphics and the data logging.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1592" title="EEVblog 1592 - Fluke 287 Multimeter Teardown">1592</a></sup> The Dräger Multiwarn II gas monitor logs for around 50 hours using an 8-channel, 8-bit ADC built into a 68HC11, with external memory holding the logged data — crude conversion, adequate for the purpose.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-603" title="EEVblog #603 - Gas Sensor Teardown - Dräger Multiwarn II">603</a></sup> A smart meter separates a power board from a dedicated data logging and processing board.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-409" title="EEVblog #409 - EDMI - Smart Meter Teardown">409</a></sup> Conversely, the absence of logging is visible too: the [[bm235|BM235]] does no data logging at all, which makes the presence of two external [[eeprom|EEPROM]] chips on its board unexplained.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-912" title="EEVblog #912 - BM235 Multimeter Reverse Engineering">912</a></sup>

Storage medium is often decided incidentally. The [[121gw|121GW]] gained a micro [[sd-card|SD card]] only because a supplier changed part, and with it both [[firmware-update|firmware updating]] and a logging capability that had not been designed in — the original plan assumed limited internal EEPROM.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-e9cpKN69Avk" title="121GW Development History">•</a></sup> Instruments that combine an SD card with a LAN interface are typically doing so precisely to support logging.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1745" title="EEVblog 1745 - Continuity Tester Spectral Probing">1745</a></sup> A meter with internal memory and USB that nonetheless offers no recording of its trend chart is a genuine design omission rather than a cost decision.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1723" title="EEVblog 1723 - AlienTek DM40 Multimeter/Oscilloscope Review">1723</a></sup>

Where logging is distributed, its location must be stated. In the Fluke CNX3000 wireless system, logging is done internally in each of the remote units; the CNX3000 meter itself has no logging capability and acts only as the star controller for the wireless network.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-417" title="EEVblog #417 - Fluke CNX3000 Wireless Multimeter Teardown">417</a></sup>

## Multi-channel and PC-based logging

Single-channel logging quickly proves limiting. PC-based oscilloscopes are best suited to exactly the applications a bench scope cannot serve — multi-channel data logging and higher-resolution front ends of 12 or 16 bits — so a PC scope should be chosen for vertical resolution and channel count rather than [[bandwidth]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-13" title="EEVblog #13 Part 2 of 2 - Comparison of PC Based Oscilloscopes">13</a></sup> The same reasoning drove a proposed multimeter with two or three blade-type [[thermocouple]] inputs at the top of the case alongside the two volts and two amps channels, giving a six-channel logging instrument without consuming the main inputs with thermocouple adapters.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-184" title="EEVblog #184 - Open Hardware Multimeter Concept">184</a></sup> A 14-bit scope with a claimed data logger function is attractive for the same reason, though claiming data logging and providing a usable implementation are different matters.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-ByUiOk00K0U" title="OWON 14bit XDS3202A Oscilloscope Unboxing &amp; First Impressions">•</a></sup>

Standalone multi-channel logging can also be built around a small computer running vendor Python software, driving the acquisition hardware and an HDMI monitor without a full PC.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1127" title="EEVblog #1127 - Mailbag Monday">1127</a></sup> Where instruments are Ethernet/LXI controlled, the whole measurement can be scripted — sweeping a supply through output voltages while LXI-connected bench meters log the results — although for a one-off [[efficiency]] measurement, four meters read manually into a spreadsheet is faster than a day spent building the automation.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-957" title="EEVblog #957 - How To Measure DC-DC Converter Efficiency">957</a></sup>

## Applications

Logging is the enabling step in most quantitative bench work. Battery discharge testing runs a constant-power load down to cutoff with logging once per second and a 60,000-reading budget, stoppable early.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-774" title="EEVblog #774 - Low Battery Discharge Testing Part 1">774</a></sup> A cell tested under four-terminal sensing to eliminate lead [[voltage-drop|voltage drop]] is logged to a USB stick so the curves can be brought into a spreadsheet and overlaid against others.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1732" title="EEVblog 1732 - 20yo 1000+ Cycle Panasonic Li-Ion Battery TESTED">1732</a></sup> Reflow oven characterisation pairs a reference thermocouple of matched [[thermal-mass|thermal mass]] with a [[bluetooth|Bluetooth]] module on the meter and a logging app, producing profiles rather than spot readings.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-558" title="EEVblog #558 - Beta Layout DIY SMD Thermal Reflow Oven">558</a></sup> Logging a meter's microamp range through a reflow cycle reveals current generated by capacitors during heating, with the caveat that different meters present different shunt resistances on the current ranges.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1455" title="EEVblog 1455 - Capacitors Produce Current During Reflow Soldering! WTF!">1455</a></sup>

At longer timescales, a domestic solar installation logged continuously supports averages over time, which is what shows that cleaning panels that are not visibly filthy produces no measurable increase in output.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-0KX5F7Si-TY" title="Does Cleaning Solar Panels Make a Difference?">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-484" title="EEVblog #484 - Home Solar Power System Installation">484</a></sup> Radiation monitors store readings internally and continuously, including in nominally switched-off states, for later download to a PC, and maintain an event log of power-ups and activity.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-775" title="EEVblog #775 - Mailbag">775</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1621" title="EEVblog 1621 - Mailbag: RadiaCode Radiation Monitor + LattePanda Mu Compute Module">1621</a></sup> A slow log at one sample per second on a high-input-[[impedance]] meter, viewed afterwards as a trend chart, reveals the wandering couple of volts of pickup that a lower-impedance meter reads as zero.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1379" title="EEVblog 1379 - What&#x27;s all this NPLC Stuff Anyhow?">1379</a></sup>
