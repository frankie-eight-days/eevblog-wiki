Capacitance is the property by which any two conductors separated by a dielectric store charge, and it exists everywhere, not only where a component has been deliberately fitted.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1406" title="EEVblog 1406 - DC Fundamentals Part 7: DC Circuit Transients Fundamentals">1406</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-486" title="EEVblog #486 - Does Current Flow Through A Capacitor?">486</a></sup> Two plates, two wires side by side, or the open contacts of a switch all form a [[capacitor]], with air serving as the dielectric just as well as any polymer film.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1406" title="EEVblog 1406 - DC Fundamentals Part 7: DC Circuit Transients Fundamentals">1406</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-486" title="EEVblog #486 - Does Current Flow Through A Capacitor?">486</a></sup> All conductive objects have some intrinsic capacitance; for simple shapes such as a sphere it can be calculated directly, while irregular bodies are harder but no less capacitive.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1567" title="EEVblog 1567 - Wireless ESD Monitor? - The IONA StatIQ Band TESTED">1567</a></sup> Because of this, capacitance is simultaneously the intended function of a component, an unavoidable parasitic in every circuit, and a sensing mechanism in its own right.

## Fundamentals

The defining relation is Q = CV, so the [[voltage]] across a capacitor is the charge divided by the capacitance.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1406" title="EEVblog 1406 - DC Fundamentals Part 7: DC Circuit Transients Fundamentals">1406</a></sup> A completely discharged capacitor therefore has zero volts across it regardless of its value — one microfarad, one farad, or a million farads makes no difference — which is why a discharged capacitor looks like a [[short-circuit]] at the instant voltage is applied, and why large capacitances cause inrush problems in power supplies.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1406" title="EEVblog 1406 - DC Fundamentals Part 7: DC Circuit Transients Fundamentals">1406</a></sup> Integrating the charging process, with the average voltage being half the final value, yields the stored energy as one half C V squared.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-3MtK035qiT4" title="Calculating the Energy in a Capacitor">•</a></sup>

Capacitance also sets a time constant when paired with [[resistance]], tau = RC, which is rearranged in practice to size a part: a one-second delay against a 10 megohm [[resistor]] calls for 100 nanofarads, conveniently the same value already stocked as a [[bypass-capacitor]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-471" title="EEVblog #471 - Overload Detector Circuit Design">471</a></sup> Under DC steady state the capacitance ceases to matter at all, since capacitors and inductors influence only transient and AC behaviour; a DC operating point simulation accordingly treats capacitances as open circuits and inductances as shorts.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1439" title="EEVblog 1439 - Analysing Veritasium&#x27;s Electricity Misconceptions Video">1439</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-516" title="EEVblog #516 - LTSPICE Tutorial - DC Operating Point Analysis">516</a></sup>

At sufficiently small scale the discrete nature of charge becomes visible: placing a single electron onto a capacitor of capacitance C costs an energy of e squared over 2C, the classical Coulomb charging energy that dominates in a small enough island.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1594" title="EEVblog 1594 - Inside a Quantum Computer! with Andrea Morello">1594</a></sup>

## Parasitic and distributed capacitance

No real component is purely one thing. An [[inductor]] has [[inductance]] in its leads and capacitance between its windings, and a resistor has inductance of its own; every element carries all three properties in some amount, so a purely inductive circuit does not exist.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1728" title="EEVblog 1728 - AC Basics Tutorial Part 6: Impedance, Conductance, Susceptance, Admittance">1728</a></sup> Cables are modelled the same way, as a chain of lumped RLC elements, each unit length contributing series resistance, series inductance, and shunt capacitance.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-756" title="EEVblog #756 - Mailbag">756</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1439" title="EEVblog 1439 - Analysing Veritasium&#x27;s Electricity Misconceptions Video">1439</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1445" title="EEVblog 1445 - How to Simulate an Oscilloscope Probe in LTSPICE">1445</a></sup>

Transmission line behaviour follows directly from that shunt capacitance. Two wires a metre apart still have capacitance between them, and it is this capacitance — not any exotic mechanism — that lets a lamp begin conducting within a couple of nanoseconds of switch closure, the delay being one metre divided by the speed of light.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1439" title="EEVblog 1439 - Analysing Veritasium&#x27;s Electricity Misconceptions Video">1439</a></sup> In practice the capacitance and inductance involved are tiny and the power delivered is negligible, but the effect is real, and after sufficient time the circuit settles into DC steady state where only line resistance remains.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1439" title="EEVblog 1439 - Analysing Veritasium&#x27;s Electricity Misconceptions Video">1439</a></sup>

Measured parasitics recur at characteristic magnitudes:

- A solderless [[breadboard]] exhibits about 2 pF between adjacent contacts, consistently across four different brands, since the capacitance is set by physical dimensions that scarcely vary between manufacturers.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-568" title="EEVblog #568 - Solderless Breadboard Capacitance">568</a></sup> Across the central dividing strip the figure falls to roughly 0.2 pF, from a power strip to a column it is just over 1 pF, and the power strip itself measures around 20 pF.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-568" title="EEVblog #568 - Solderless Breadboard Capacitance">568</a></sup> The value rises with test [[frequency]]: about half a picofarad at 100 Hz, just over 2 pF at 1 kHz, and 2.25 pF at 10 kHz.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-568" title="EEVblog #568 - Solderless Breadboard Capacitance">568</a></sup> Forcing a large leaded component into the holes brings the contacts marginally closer and raises the reading slightly, but not enough to matter.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-568" title="EEVblog #568 - Solderless Breadboard Capacitance">568</a></sup>
- A [[pcb]] power plane pair behaves as a capacitor of about 2 nF, flat from 100 Hz to 100 kHz with a dissipation factor of 0.005.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1117" title="EEVblog #1117 - PCB Power Plane Capacitance">1117</a></sup> That capacitance is nonetheless of little use for bypassing, because taking the whole plane capacitance means spreading out across the plane, and the resulting inductance spreading dominates the small capacitance available at any one point.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1117" title="EEVblog #1117 - PCB Power Plane Capacitance">1117</a></sup>
- [[oscilloscope-probe]] tip capacitance sets high-frequency loading. A very good passive probe presents 3.9 pF, which is 40 ohms at 1 GHz, whereas an active probe of the same 1 GHz rating presents 1.2 pF, or 132 ohms at 1 GHz — a substantial difference in the load placed on the line under test.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1368" title="EEVblog #1368 - Active Oscilloscope Probes COMPARED (Part 2)">1368</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-Y7t6BIhBZhc" title="High Frequency Active FET Probing DEMONSTRATED">•</a></sup>
- Bus capacitance slows edges. Additional devices on an open-collector I²C bus add capacitance and lengthen the low-to-high rise time, which is why the customary 2.2 k pull-up is sometimes reduced to 1 k for faster operation or heavier loading.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1208" title="EEVblog #1208 - Circuit Analysis &amp; Debugging">1208</a></sup>
- A chain of 283 double adapters fed from 241.3 V draws 229 microamps of leakage, some 54 milliwatts, purely from the capacitance of the assembly.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1526" title="EEVblog 1526 - I put 283 Double Adapters in Series with a 2kW Load!">1526</a></sup>

Parasitic capacitance also produces measurement artefacts. A fast latching continuity tester can pick up microseconds of [[current]] flow through in-circuit capacitance and pulse-stretch it into an audible beep where the actual resistance is 1.75 k, whereas a slower meter is not fooled; the latching time can be tuned to reject something like 0.1 microfarads, but a large-value capacitor will trigger it every time and there is no design fix.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1671" title="EEVblog 1671 - Beware of Multimeter Continuity Latching">1671</a></sup> Similarly, capacitance distributed through a mains-referenced test setup contributes to conducted common-mode problems that appear as spurious meter readings.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-933" title="EEVblog #933 - Keysight U1272A EMC Issue">933</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-987" title="EEVblog #987 - Keysight U1272A Multimeter EMC Followup">987</a></sup>

## Voltage dependence of ceramic capacitors

Class 2 ceramic capacitors change capacitance with applied DC bias, a behaviour poorly documented in many data sheets.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-626" title="EEVblog #626 - Ceramic Capacitor Voltage Dependency">626</a></sup> Measured by RC time constant on an [[oscilloscope]], a nominal 10 microfarad part read 10.6 microfarads unbiased — within about 6% of the [[lcr-meter]] value — and fell to 4.82 microfarads with a 5 V offset applied.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-626" title="EEVblog #626 - Ceramic Capacitor Voltage Dependency">626</a></sup> Manufacturer curves show drops as large as 60% by 6.3 V on a 6.3 V rated part, and a 16 V rated Y5V part measured 1.5 microfarads under test, close to an order of magnitude below nominal.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-626" title="EEVblog #626 - Ceramic Capacitor Voltage Dependency">626</a></sup>

The applied AC level matters as well, and its effect can run in the opposite direction: capacitance change with AC RMS voltage spans roughly plus 15% to minus 30%, and manufacturer data shows increases of 50-odd percent for some multilayer ceramics depending on drive level.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-626" title="EEVblog #626 - Ceramic Capacitor Voltage Dependency">626</a></sup> The magnitude varies within a single family between different case sizes of the same dielectric rating.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-626" title="EEVblog #626 - Ceramic Capacitor Voltage Dependency">626</a></sup> The practical consequence is that a capacitor's marked value cannot be assumed to be its operating value; dielectric type and bias conditions must be considered alongside the temperature coefficients that designers more commonly attend to.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-626" title="EEVblog #626 - Ceramic Capacitor Voltage Dependency">626</a></sup>

Dielectric choice trades capacitance density against stability. A very thin 10 microfarad part is likely a Y5V, since only a high-permittivity dielectric gives that capacitance for the size, at the cost of much poorer performance.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-855" title="EEVblog #855 - Ceramic Capacitor Piezoelectric Effect">855</a></sup> Mechanical countermeasures carry their own penalty: soft termination technology, developed to stop ceramic capacitors cracking under board flex, costs roughly a 12.5% decrease in volumetric capacitance plus extra manufacturing cost, which is accepted in automotive and other high-vibration markets for the reliability gained.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1037" title="EEVblog #1037 - Solving Ceramic Capacitor Cracking">1037</a></sup>

## Capacitance in design

Bulk capacitance is sized from the ripple requirement — a rule-of-thumb formula gives, for instance, 1,000 microfarads to meet a required ripple current ahead of a [[linear-regulator]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-742" title="EEVblog #742 - Why Electrolytic Capacitors Are Connected In Parallel">742</a></sup> Meeting the value with a single part is not the whole decision, because ESR sits in series with the capacitance in the capacitor model, along with lead inductance.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-742" title="EEVblog #742 - Why Electrolytic Capacitors Are Connected In Parallel">742</a></sup> A 100 microfarad capacitor with 0.1 ohms ESR can be built instead from two 50 microfarad parts in parallel each with 0.2 ohms ESR, since ESR is largely determined by physical size; with identical capacitance and identical load current the ripple voltage is the same, but the single-capacitor arrangement runs visibly hotter within five minutes of power-up.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-742" title="EEVblog #742 - Why Electrolytic Capacitors Are Connected In Parallel">742</a></sup> Specifications for such a part therefore extend beyond capacitance to a maximum ESR — 0.1 ohms is common for DC-DC converter stability, sometimes as a minimum, maximum, or a window.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-742" title="EEVblog #742 - Why Electrolytic Capacitors Are Connected In Parallel">742</a></sup>

There is a ceiling to what added capacitance buys. On a charge pump output, capacitance cannot be increased indefinitely because the ESR of the capacitor eventually dominates the achievable ripple, and higher capacitance in a given package size means higher ESR.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1115" title="EEVblog #1115 - Traps In Chips - And the 7660">1115</a></sup> Filtering follows the expected scaling in the meantime: increasing a filter capacitance by a factor of ten moves the corner from 159 Hz to 15.9 Hz and drops the ripple by an order of magnitude, at the cost of a longer settling ramp.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-225" title="EEVblog #225 - Lab Power Supply Design Part 4 - PWM Control">225</a></sup> A [[transistor]] capacitance multiplier achieves the same end differently, with a beta of 100 making 100 nF behave like 10 microfarads and moving a 1.59 kHz corner down to 15.9 Hz.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1116" title="EEVBlog #1116 - How to Remove Power Supply Ripple">1116</a></sup>

Too much capacitance is itself a fault condition. An [[inverter]] with excessive input capacitance drew enough surge current at turn-on that the controlling processor repeatedly aborted its start-up and hiccupped; with far less capacitance to charge, the controller started cleanly.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1707" title="EEVblog 1707 - REPAIR: Goal Zero Yeti 400 Lithium Battery">1707</a></sup> Conversely, some capacitance is mandatory — a linear supply output needs enough to lower output noise and to support [[current-limiting]] behaviour.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-224" title="EEVblog #224 - Lab Power Supply Design - Part 3">224</a></sup>

Where capacitance must be traded against voltage rating, [[series-connection]] serves both. Stringing capacitors in series adds their voltage ratings, giving a 3 kV total from parts rated individually lower, at the cost of dividing the capacitance.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-932" title="EEVblog #932 - How Does A HV Differential Probe Work?">932</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-85" title="EEVblog #85 - High Voltage Oscilloscope Probe Design">85</a></sup> Putting two capacitors in series halves the capacitance but improves reliability where a shorted capacitor cannot be tolerated, an approach built into parts intended for that duty.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-9V99J22aiLE" title="Full Discussion - Film Capacitor Failures with Ron Demcko from AVX">•</a></sup> In a peak detector, a smaller hold capacitance gives better tracking — 100 nF produced large errors and overshoot into a 100 k load where 1 nF followed the input closely — but demands a genuinely high-[[impedance]] load.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-490" title="EEVblog #490 - Peak Detector Circuit">490</a></sup>

When replacing electrolytics in a repair, the guidance is not to go down in capacitance value; going up is acceptable, as is substituting 470 microfarads for 330 microfarads, provided the 105 °C rating is retained.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-347" title="EEVblog #347 - Bad Cap LCD Monitor Repair">347</a></sup> Raising the value is not a universal cure, however: replacing a 4.7 microfarad part with 100 microfarads failed to clean up a persistent fault in an arcade machine board.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1301" title="EEVblog #1301 - Arcade Machine Repair">1301</a></sup>

## Capacitance loss as a failure mode

Film and ceramic capacitors fail by losing capacitance outright, whereas electrolytics more often retain their capacitance while developing high ESR — which is why an electrolytic failure cannot reliably be detected from a [[capacitance-measurement]] alone.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-379" title="EEVBlog #379 - Yamaha RX-V557 Receiver Fix">379</a></sup> A failed film capacitor measured 585 picofarads against a 22 nanofarad nominal, consistently wrong from 1 kHz through 100 kHz.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-379" title="EEVBlog #379 - Yamaha RX-V557 Receiver Fix">379</a></sup> The mechanism is not obvious on dissection, since a capacitor that has lost half its capacitance does not necessarily show corresponding damage in the wound foil.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1486" title="EEVblog 1486 - What you DIDN&#x27;T KNOW About Film Capacitor FAILURES!">1486</a></sup>

Capacitance loss propagates into circuit failure in predictable ways. In a mains capacitive dropper feeding a Zener regulator, a 220 nanofarad capacitor falling in value raised its reactance, starved the load of current, and left insufficient voltage to pull in a [[relay]] coil.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1482" title="EEVblog 1482 - Mains Capacitor Zener Regulator Circuit">1482</a></sup>

## Sensing and measurement

Capacitance change is the transduction mechanism in condenser and electret microphones, where the sensed quantity is the capacitance between a conductive — often gold-spotted — film on a flexing diaphragm and a fixed metal plate.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-602" title="EEVblog #602 - Introduction to Microphones">602</a></sup> A pressure wave decreases the plate spacing and increases the capacitance; because the charge is preserved, the voltage changes accordingly.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-609" title="EEVblog #609 - Condenser Microphone Design Tutorial">609</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1199" title="EEVblog #1199 - Samson C01U USB Microphone Teardown">1199</a></sup> The excursions are extremely small, and the source capacitance is low — of the order of 50 pF, against perhaps 10 pF of amplifier input capacitance — so a high-impedance amplifier is required, and internal standoffs are used to keep stray capacitance as low as possible.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1199" title="EEVblog #1199 - Samson C01U USB Microphone Teardown">1199</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-616" title="EEVblog #616 - How Microphone Phantom Powering Works">616</a></sup> A 50 pF source against 10^9 ohms gives a 50 ms time constant, corresponding to about 3 Hz at the -3 dB point.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-609" title="EEVblog #609 - Condenser Microphone Design Tutorial">609</a></sup>

Touch sensing works the same way. Dedicated touch-switch chips are designed for up to 50 picofarads of sensor capacitance with automatic recalibration, and membrane touch sensors on a robot bumper measured only tens of picofarads, essentially trace capacitance.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1272" title="EEVblog #1272 - Mailbag">1272</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1523" title="EEVblog 1523 - Dumpster Samsung Cleaning Robot TEARDOWN">1523</a></sup> Capacitive digital isolators pass signals across a few picofarads of internal capacitance at much lower power than older isolator types.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1264" title="EEVblog #1264 - uSupply Software Development Setup">1264</a></sup>

For instrumentation, most multimeters measure capacitance, but few measure inductance — the distinction that justifies an LCR meter, which additionally reports quality factor, dissipation factor, and ESR.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-115" title="EEVblog #115 - Extech 380193 LCR Meter Review">115</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1636" title="EEVblog 1636 - Ultimate Multimeter Beginners Guide!">1636</a></sup> The underlying technique is to drive a known signal, sense voltage and current through a shunt, and derive capacitance, inductance, series and parallel resistance, quality factor and dissipation factor from the magnitudes and phase.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-81" title="EEVblog #81 - Smart Tweezers LCR Meter Review And Teardown">81</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-858" title="EEVblog #858 - Red Pitaya">858</a></sup> Accurate phase measurement is the prerequisite: measuring inductance or capacitance well requires low phase error between channels.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1119" title="EEVBlog #1119 - Designing a 1kV Isolated Oscilloscope">1119</a></sup>

Meter specifications deserve scrutiny at the range extremes rather than the headline figure. A basic accuracy of 0.7% plus three digits applies only in the middle ranges; the 10 millifarad range at 120 Hz degrades to 5% plus counts, and the lowest range to 1% plus five counts.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-115" title="EEVblog #115 - Extech 380193 LCR Meter Review">115</a></sup> Readings also change with test frequency and with whether series or parallel mode is selected.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-115" title="EEVblog #115 - Extech 380193 LCR Meter Review">115</a></sup> Resolution at the low end can be remarkable — 10 femtofarads on a bench LCR meter at 1 kHz, and 0.1 femtofarads read over a [[serial-port|serial interface]], finer than the front panel displays.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-568" title="EEVblog #568 - Solderless Breadboard Capacitance">568</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-137" title="EEVblog #137 - BK Precision 879B Handheld LCR Meter Review">137</a></sup> Low-cost component testers are adequate for identification and roughly correct on capacitance, but their dissipation factor readings are not meaningful.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1020" title="EEVblog #1020 - Is A $7 LCR / Component Tester Any Good?">1020</a></sup>

In-circuit measurement is possible but bounded. Large electrolytics — the parts one usually wants to check in circuit — can be measured even with diodes across them, whereas at low values the reactance becomes so high that 1.5 nanofarads and 10 picofarads simply cannot be resolved at the test frequency, whatever range resistor is used.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1474" title="EEVblog 1474 - Can You Measure Capacitors IN Circuit?">1474</a></sup> Test fixturing matters too: over 100 picofarads of capacitance was found between the sense terminals and the front banana terminals of one meter, which is why the supplied leads must be used.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-D2PANd9Hu3U" title="Zotek/Zoyi Test Equipment MAILBAG BONANZA UNBOXING!">•</a></sup> Human proximity is enough to perturb a reading — probing one's own fingers registers as a capacitance, and pressing harder on a device under test raises its measured value.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-81" title="EEVblog #81 - Smart Tweezers LCR Meter Review And Teardown">81</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-477" title="EEVblog #477 - Mailbag">477</a></sup>
