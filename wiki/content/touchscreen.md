A touchscreen is a transparent input sensor laid over a display so that the display surface itself becomes the control surface. In electronics it appears in two quite different roles: as the entire user interface of a consumer device that has no other controls, and as an additional input layer on test instruments that retain their traditional knobs and buttons.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-383" title="EEVBlog #383 - Agilent 4000X Oscilloscope Review">383</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-701" title="EEVblog #701 - Keysight 3000T Oscilloscope Review">701</a></sup> The distinction matters, because a touch layer that supplements physical controls is a usability gain, while one that replaces them becomes a single point of failure for the whole product.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1370" title="EEVblog #1370 - Kindle Paperwhite REPAIR">1370</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-2" title="EEVblog2 #650 - Canon HF G30 Camcorder Review - FULL">2</a></sup>

## Sensing technologies

Capacitive sensing dominates modern equipment. A capacitive panel responds only to a conductive object, so a plastic stylus or non-conductive probe will not operate it at all.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-983" title="EEVblog #983 - A Shocking Oscilloscope Problem!">983</a></sup> It is also what makes the surface feel light to use: a capacitive panel needs no pressure, only contact.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-650" title="EEVblog #650 - Canon HF G30 / XA20 / XA25 Camcorder Review">650</a></sup>

Resistive panels work by pressure and wear out with use, with individual button areas degrading over time; a recalibration routine is normally provided in the device's own menus and is the first thing to try when a resistive panel starts missing presses.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-899" title="EEVblog #899 - Extreme Mailbag!">899</a></sup>

Optical and infrared systems place emitters and detectors around the bezel rather than over the glass. A 21.5-inch all-in-one machine carries four infrared transceivers, one in each corner and none along the bottom edge, indicating a triangulation-by-reflection scheme rather than a grid.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1615" title="EEVblog 1615 - Dumpster Dive HP Pro One PC">1615</a></sup> Optical touch is also used on large desktop monitors, where it avoids putting either a capacitive or a resistive layer in the optical path.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1542" title="EEVblog 1542 - A Decade of Dumpster Diving!">1542</a></sup> Bezel-mounted optical sensing is why such panels can be built at monitor sizes where an overlay would be prohibitive.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1542" title="EEVblog 1542 - A Decade of Dumpster Diving!">1542</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1615" title="EEVblog 1615 - Dumpster Dive HP Pro One PC">1615</a></sup>

Touch sensing predates the modern panel by decades. A 1980s calculator watch used exposed contact surfaces with the traces routed down to them, an approach that was state-of-the-art at the time; the same function is now a peripheral integrated into general-purpose microcontrollers, or bought as a dedicated touch controller chip.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1166" title="EEVblog #1166 - Amazing 1980&#x27;s Touch Screen Calculator Watch!">1166</a></sup>

Most panels in general equipment register a single contact and will not accept two simultaneous presses.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1725" title="EEVblog 1725 - Mailbag: with special guest Sagan!">1725</a></sup>

## Integration

The touch layer is electrically separate from the display and is normally routed as its own flex connection, even when it shares a cable assembly with the [[lcd|LCD]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-418" title="EEVblog #418 - Mailbag Apple Newton Teardown">418</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-334" title="EEVblog #334 - History of PDA&#x27;s in Teardowns">334</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-672" title="EEVblog #672 - GoPro Hero 4 Silver Teardown">672</a></sup> In a portable device the display flat flex and the touch flat flex run together from the panel to the main board.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-334" title="EEVblog #334 - History of PDA&#x27;s in Teardowns">334</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-672" title="EEVblog #672 - GoPro Hero 4 Silver Teardown">672</a></sup> Front-panel touch is often driven directly by the same application [[processor]] that generates the video, which already carries the LCD drivers on chip.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-823" title="EEVBlog #823 - Rigol DSG815 RF Signal Generator Teardown">823</a></sup> In one instrument the display engine inside a single device drives a 1280 by 800 display, the touchscreen, and the [[waveform]] generator output together.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1477" title="EEVblog 1477 - TEARDOWN! - NEW Tektronix 2 Series Oscilloscope">1477</a></sup> High-end instruments may instead dedicate human-machine-interface processors to the display and user interface, driving a WXGA panel independently of the acquisition hardware.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-731" title="EEVblog #731 - Keithley DMM7510 7.5 Digit Multimeter Teardown">731</a></sup>

At the module level, small graphical display modules are commonly offered in touch and non-touch variants of the same part, so touch becomes a purchasing option rather than a redesign — a 4.3-inch 480 by 272 RGB TFT module, for example, exists in both.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-633" title="EEVblog #633 - Mailbag">633</a></sup> A further step up is the self-contained smart touchscreen board that carries the graphical user interface itself, which lets a low-volume product acquire a full touch UI without the developer writing display code.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1410" title="EEVblog 1410 - Mailbag">1410</a></sup>

Board evidence does not always match the shipped behaviour: one instrument that is genuinely touch-operated has an unpopulated touchscreen [[connector]] on its main board, meaning the touch layer is wired somewhere else entirely.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1723" title="EEVblog 1723 - AlienTek DM40 Multimeter/Oscilloscope Review">1723</a></sup>

## Cost

The panel is a significant line item. A 1280 by 800 touchscreen LCD prices at roughly $35 in a bill-of-materials analysis of a mid-range instrument, against about $5 for the keypad [[pcb|PCB]] and membrane overlay together.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1679" title="EEVblog 1679 - Why Does a Uni-T AWG Cost $5800? - BOM Cost Analysis">1679</a></sup> Cost also scales badly with size: extending an instrument's touch interface to an external display would require a 25-inch touchscreen and add on the order of $4,000 to the instrument price.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-207" title="Rohde &amp; Schwarz / Hameg Oscilloscopes - EEVblog #207 (3 of 3)">207</a></sup>

Panel grade is one of the reasons premium instruments cost what they do. Higher-tier manufacturers specify industrial-rated LCDs and touchscreens, while manufacturers designing to a lower price bracket use consumer-grade parts.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-3t9G80wk0pk" title="eevBLAB 101 - Why Are Tektronix Oscilloscopes So Expensive?">•</a></sup>

Touch is largely absent from the bottom of the instrument market. Low-end oscilloscopes generally do not have touchscreens, and a numeric keypad on the front panel is the substitute for entering values.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1231" title="EEVblog #1231- New Uni-T UPO3000 Oscilloscope Teardown">1231</a></sup>

## Oscilloscopes and instruments

Touch arrived on mid-range oscilloscopes as a headline feature rather than a quiet addition — one series was designated with a T purely because it was touch-capable,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-701" title="EEVblog #701 - Keysight 3000T Oscilloscope Review">701</a></sup> another vendor's model used T for touch in the same way,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-792" title="EEVBlog #792 - Lecroy Wavejet Touch 354 Oscilloscope Review">792</a></sup> and a teardown of one touch model found it to be architecturally the earlier non-touch model with a touchscreen added.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-384" title="EEVblog #384 - Agilent 4000X Oscilloscope Teardown">384</a></sup> By the time of a 1 GHz class comparison, every model in the field was touchscreen.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1218" title="EEVblog #1218 - 1GHz Oscilloscope Spec Shootout">1218</a></sup>

The capability is genuinely useful where a task is inherently spatial. Waveforms can be dragged vertically and horizontally,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-701" title="EEVblog #701 - Keysight 3000T Oscilloscope Review">701</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-114" title="EEVblog #114 - Electronex 2010 Electronics Exhibition">114</a></sup> cursors dragged directly and recovered when scrolled off-screen,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-383" title="EEVBlog #383 - Agilent 4000X Oscilloscope Review">383</a></sup> and information panels moved anywhere on the display and set transparent so they do not obscure the trace.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-383" title="EEVBlog #383 - Agilent 4000X Oscilloscope Review">383</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-CMoBGGqojqs" title="Rigol MSO7000 Unboxing &amp; First Impressions">•</a></sup> Gesture control extends this to scaling.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1220" title="EEVblog #1220 - Siglent SDS5000X 1GHz Oscilloscope Review">1220</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1146" title="EEVblog #1146 - New Rigol MSO5000 Oscilloscope">1146</a></sup> The strongest case is zone triggering, where the trigger region is drawn directly around the feature of interest — a capability that is powerful in itself but becomes markedly more so with a touchscreen to draw with.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-701" title="EEVblog #701 - Keysight 3000T Oscilloscope Review">701</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-383" title="EEVBlog #383 - Agilent 4000X Oscilloscope Review">383</a></sup> On-screen QWERTY entry for channel labelling is another case where touch is simply faster than an encoder.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-812" title="EEVblog #812 - Varta 15min NiMH Charger Part 2">812</a></sup> Where an instrument offers an on-screen keyboard without touch, it remains usable but fiddly.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-11-AQ_E1fz8" title="Siglent SDS1000X-E Serial Decoding">•</a></sup> Some functions on a knob-sparse instrument are difficult to perform without the touchscreen at all.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1566" title="EEVblog 1566 - Rigol DHO800 12 Bit Oscilloscope FULL REVIEW">1566</a></sup>

Against this, several recurring failures of touch interfaces show up in instruments. Responsiveness varies widely: one instrument's touch response is laggy to the point of being unusable,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-ByUiOk00K0U" title="OWON 14bit XDS3202A Oscilloscope Unboxing &amp; First Impressions">•</a></sup> another shows a consistent delay between contact and response,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1501" title="EEVblog 1501 - Rigol HDO4000 Low Noise 12bit Oscilloscope Unboxing &amp; First Impression">1501</a></sup> and dragging can be slow though still workable.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1146" title="EEVblog #1146 - New Rigol MSO5000 Oscilloscope">1146</a></sup> Selection feedback is a common weak point — on one instrument gesture scaling acts on a channel other than the one apparently selected, with no clear indication of which channel is active.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1220" title="EEVblog #1220 - Siglent SDS5000X 1GHz Oscilloscope Review">1220</a></sup>

Touch also interacts badly with a bench environment. A large instrument touchscreen is designed to be poked and prodded, but on one scope a light tap on the screen — and a fingernail more than a fingertip, being higher in [[frequency]] — couples mechanically into the acquisition and produces a visible artifact at 20 mV per division.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-983" title="EEVblog #983 - A Shocking Oscilloscope Problem!">983</a></sup>

## Disabling touch

Because accidental contact is a real hazard on a bench, a hardware touch-disable control is treated as a necessary feature rather than a convenience, and it appears across oscilloscopes and power supplies alike.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-383" title="EEVBlog #383 - Agilent 4000X Oscilloscope Review">383</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-5YjS4DHKlQU" title="Tektronix 5 Series Oscilloscope">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1501" title="EEVblog 1501 - Rigol HDO4000 Low Noise 12bit Oscilloscope Unboxing &amp; First Impression">1501</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1293" title="EEVblog #1293 - New Rohde and Schwarz NGP800 800W PSU">1293</a></sup> With touch off, an instrument must revert to full operation from its traditional menu buttons with nothing lost.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-383" title="EEVBlog #383 - Agilent 4000X Oscilloscope Review">383</a></sup> Dave Jones works by resting hands on the display and describes himself as a "screen poker", which makes touch-off the default working state for him on any instrument that offers it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1501" title="EEVblog 1501 - Rigol HDO4000 Low Noise 12bit Oscilloscope Unboxing &amp; First Impression">1501</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-5YjS4DHKlQU" title="Tektronix 5 Series Oscilloscope">•</a></sup> A touch lock also has a second use: locking out the panel before handing a prototype to someone else prevents them from disturbing a running setup.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1293" title="EEVblog #1293 - New Rohde and Schwarz NGP800 800W PSU">1293</a></sup>

Implementation details of the lock matter. A touch-off indicator rendered in white is hard to see under bench lighting, where red would read immediately as a disabled state.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-nO09bc5ozng" title="NEW Tektronix 2 Series MSO Oscilloscope FIRST REACTION">•</a></sup> And disabling touch should also suppress the touch-oriented popup windows, which on at least one instrument continue to appear after touch is turned off.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-383" title="EEVBlog #383 - Agilent 4000X Oscilloscope Review">383</a></sup>

## Touch on external displays

Instruments that output video over HDMI generally render to the external monitor's native resolution rather than mirroring the internal panel — one 7-inch, 1024 by 600 instrument renders its HDMI output at a higher resolution than its own screen, though without any increase in bit depth.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1563" title="EEVblog 1563 - New $389 12bit Rigol DHO800 Scope TEARDOWN">1563</a></sup> Touch can follow the video: connecting the [[usb|USB]]-C port of a touchscreen monitor to the instrument's USB host port makes the external panel a working touchscreen for the instrument.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-r_BYYgCqScE" title="Rigol DHO800 HDMI output &amp; Touch Screen">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1566" title="EEVblog 1566 - Rigol DHO800 12 Bit Oscilloscope FULL REVIEW">1566</a></sup> A USB hub can be inserted on that port as well.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1566" title="EEVblog 1566 - Rigol DHO800 12 Bit Oscilloscope FULL REVIEW">1566</a></sup>

The harder case is splitting a touch-driven interface between the internal and external displays. An instrument whose entire man-machine interface is touch-driven cannot simply mirror part of itself to a passive external monitor, because the split portion becomes undrivable and a large part of the interface functionality is lost.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-207" title="Rohde &amp; Schwarz / Hameg Oscilloscopes - EEVblog #207 (3 of 3)">207</a></sup>

## Failure modes and repair

A cracked front glass on a phone or tablet usually kills the digitizer while leaving the LCD working, which is why the touch panel and the LCD are ordered and replaced as separate parts — on one phone the touch layer responded only on the lower half of the screen with the display entirely intact, and only the touchscreen was replaced because the LCD was expensive.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-j5XmxupjTdE" title="Sony Experia Z3 Compact LCD Repair">•</a></sup> A failed digitizer typically does not go dead so much as go random, reporting touches at points nobody is touching and rendering the device unusable.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1255" title="EEVblog #1255 - Apple iPad 5th Gen Screen REPAIR">1255</a></sup> Replacement front assemblies are widely available and cheap, and are bonded with [[adhesive]] strips; separating the original requires heat, around 150 °C on a hot air gun, and more on a waterproof phone whose glue is deliberately tougher.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-j5XmxupjTdE" title="Sony Experia Z3 Compact LCD Repair">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1255" title="EEVblog #1255 - Apple iPad 5th Gen Screen REPAIR">1255</a></sup>

In larger equipment the fault is more often the connection than the panel. A photocopier with a completely dead touchscreen was traced to the panel's tail connection, where flexing the joint restored function; the fix was reheating the connections with an iron at around 260 °C with no added [[solder]], since the attachment is conductive adhesive rather than a reflowed [[solder-joint|solder joint]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1100" title="EEVblog #1100 - Dumpster Photocopier Repair">1100</a></sup> Continuity across the panel's four wires can be checked pair by pair to confirm the panel itself is intact before disassembly goes further.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1100" title="EEVblog #1100 - Dumpster Photocopier Repair">1100</a></sup>

An intermittent touchscreen is not reliable evidence of a touchscreen fault. A reader with months of intermittent touch response, unfixed by repeated resetting, turned out to have a [[battery]] and software lock-up problem rather than any physical panel defect — the touch layer was simply the most visible symptom, and treating it as the fault would have meant chasing the wrong subsystem entirely.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1370" title="EEVblog #1370 - Kindle Paperwhite REPAIR">1370</a></sup> The general shape of that failure is worth recognising: on a device where touch is the only input, every fault presents as a touch fault.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1370" title="EEVblog #1370 - Kindle Paperwhite REPAIR">1370</a></sup> Similar intermittency on a tablet resolved itself once the device was handled further.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-940" title="EEVblog #940 - Mailbag">940</a></sup>

Touch failures can also be systemic rather than local. An all-in-one PC with a non-working touchscreen had no BIOS option for touch at all and no discoverable jumper or switch to disable it in hardware.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-NLwgFYt1pjA" title="Dumpster HP ProOne 400 PC Touch Screen">•</a></sup> An [[inverter]] whose panel appeared dead came back fully working, touchscreen included, after a complete power cycle.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-GFlckdPzYQQ" title="Deye Solar Inverter Screen FAIL + Hoymiles Microinverter Input">•</a></sup>

Mechanically, glass over the touch layer is a questionable choice in ruggedised equipment: a field-service tablet built to be dropped used glass for the front touch panel, which shattered completely on impact.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-925" title="EEVblog #925 - Panasonic CF-U1 ToughBook Teardown">925</a></sup>

## Touch as the only interface

Where a touchscreen is the sole control mechanism, its shortcomings become the product's shortcomings. A camcorder that was touchscreen-only with no external cursor control required the operator to brace a hand behind the panel and press hard to register anything, making ordinary operation a chore; its successor added a dedicated joystick alongside a capacitive panel, so the touch interface became a choice rather than a requirement.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-2" title="EEVblog2 #650 - Canon HF G30 Camcorder Review - FULL">2</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-650" title="EEVblog #650 - Canon HF G30 / XA20 / XA25 Camcorder Review">650</a></sup> An e-reader with no controls other than its touch layer is entirely unusable when that layer misbehaves.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1370" title="EEVblog #1370 - Kindle Paperwhite REPAIR">1370</a></sup>

The same tension appears in small-screen devices, where the touch target is the limiting factor. On a hardware wallet with a small display, hitting the on-screen buttons is difficult even for someone without large fingers,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1374" title="EEVblog #1374 - DIY Trezor Crypto Hardware Wallet - Part 1">1374</a></sup> and the same device intermittently fails to register presses on a clearly indicated button.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1062" title="EEVblog #1062 - Trezor Model T Hardware Wallet Review">1062</a></sup> A later generation enlarged the on-screen keyboard specifically to address this.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-uOOGXORqsqQ" title="Trezor Safe 5 Bitcoin Crypo Hardware Wallet">•</a></sup> The cheaper variant of that product line drops the touchscreen for two physical buttons and a display, which changes the interaction model but costs considerably less.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-uOOGXORqsqQ" title="Trezor Safe 5 Bitcoin Crypo Hardware Wallet">•</a></sup>

Physical stability is a related constraint that is easy to overlook: a device meant to be poked needs to resist being pushed over, and a stand that is adequate for a passive display can be inadequate for a touch panel on a slippery surface.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1209" title="EEVblog #1209 - Mailbag">1209</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-RmpYQBg864M" title="HDMI Microscope Brainstorming">•</a></sup>
