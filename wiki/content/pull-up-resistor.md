A pull-up [[resistor]] is a resistor connected between a signal node and the positive supply rail, holding that node high whenever nothing else is actively driving it low. It exists because a large class of outputs — open collector, open drain, wired-or [[comparator]] outputs — can only sink [[current]] and have no [[transistor]] capable of driving the line up to the rail, so the resistor supplies the rising edge instead.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1208" title="EEVblog #1208 - Circuit Analysis &amp; Debugging">1208</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1464" title="EEVblog 1464 - TOP 5 Jellybean Comparators">1464</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-fm13tIe5wSc" title="EEVacademy #4 -  I²C (I2C) Bit Banging">•</a></sup> The same component also defines the resting state of inputs that would otherwise float, sets the off state of switches and gates, and appears in the earliest logic families as a load element.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1611" title="EEVblog 1611 - Top 5 Jellybean 7400 &amp; 4000 Logic">1611</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-961" title="EEVblog #961 - Monkey Debouncing">961</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-638" title="EEVblog #638 - Apollo Saturn V LVDC Testing">638</a></sup>

## Open collector and open drain outputs

An [[open-collector-output|open collector output]] has no totem-pole stage: there is no active device in the output that can drive the pin quickly up to the positive rail, so a pull-up to VCC is mandatory for the high level to exist at all.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1208" title="EEVblog #1208 - Circuit Analysis &amp; Debugging">1208</a></sup> The mechanism is visible in a single [[bipolar-transistor|bipolar transistor]] stage — base current turns the transistor on and the output is pulled down to 0 V; remove the base current and the output goes positive purely by virtue of the pull-up resistor.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-748" title="EEVblog #748 - How Do Transistors Work?">748</a></sup> Open drain behaves identically in [[mosfet|MOSFET]] terms, and it is not magic: an open drain output switching a 12 V load still needs a pull-up resistor returned to a real [[voltage]], in that case the 12 V input rail, before it can control a MOSFET or solid state [[relay]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-971" title="EEVblog #971 - Zero Standby Power TV - BUSTED!">971</a></sup>

Whether the pull-up is needed at all depends entirely on the output structure. A [[555-timer|555 timer]]'s discharge pin is open collector and needs a 1K pull-up; pin 3, the Q output, is not open collector, so rearranging a [[pwm|PWM]] circuit to drive from pin 3 instead of pin 7 removes the resistor from the [[bill-of-materials|bill of materials]] outright.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-392" title="EEVblog #392 - 555 LED PWM Hack">392</a></sup> The same distinction separates comparator families: an open collector comparator's positive-going transition is set by the external pull-up the designer adds, while nano-power push-pull parts actively drive the output high and need no external pull-up.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1464" title="EEVblog 1464 - TOP 5 Jellybean Comparators">1464</a></sup>

Detection circuits exploit the arrangement directly. In an EPIRB test path, an RF amplitude rising past a threshold switches on a transistor whose open collector output, held up by a pull-up resistor, then switches low so the [[microcontroller]] can confirm the transmitter is producing RF.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-368" title="EEVblog #368 - EPIRB Teardown">368</a></sup> Removing the output transistor, its [[ac-coupling|AC coupling]] [[capacitor]] and the 10K resistor leaves the pull-up with nothing to pull the node down, which is how it was established that the output is ignored during transmission.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-368" title="EEVblog #368 - EPIRB Teardown">368</a></sup>

## The I2C bus

[[i2c|I2C]] works over two wires shared by many devices precisely because those wires are open drain: the driving side only ever drives low, and each wire must have a pull-up so that the line returns high when it is not being pulled down.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-fm13tIe5wSc" title="EEVacademy #4 -  I²C (I2C) Bit Banging">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-9hMsNOwY5AQ" title="EEVacademy #3 - Bit Banging &amp; SPI Tutorial">•</a></sup> Driving the pins actively would violate the protocol specification; the only way to meet it is to leave the output high [[impedance]] and let the resistor do the work.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-9hMsNOwY5AQ" title="EEVacademy #3 - Bit Banging &amp; SPI Tutorial">•</a></sup> Two resistors returning to the [[power-rail|power rail]] alongside an SCL/SDA pair on a [[schematic]] are always this.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-fm13tIe5wSc" title="EEVacademy #4 -  I²C (I2C) Bit Banging">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-912" title="EEVblog #912 - BM235 Multimeter Reverse Engineering">912</a></sup>

The value is a speed-versus-power trade-off. The typical recommendation is 2.2k, dropped to around 1k where the bus must run faster or carries more devices, since more devices mean more [[capacitance]], which means longer [[rise-time|rise time]] on the low-to-high transition.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1208" title="EEVblog #1208 - Circuit Analysis &amp; Debugging">1208</a></sup> Lower [[resistance]] gives a faster response, and the appropriate value tracks the speed grade of the interface.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-fm13tIe5wSc" title="EEVacademy #4 -  I²C (I2C) Bit Banging">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1307" title="EEVblog #1307 - TUTORIAL: PCB BOM Consolidation">1307</a></sup> At the extremes the nominal 2K2 of the standard stops being adequate — bus capacitance dominates by the time an interface is advertised at 10 MHz, and pull-up selection becomes the limiting question for a 100 MHz I2C stimulus.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-991" title="EEVblog #991 - Mailbag">991</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-fM4bbKVw2og" title="Uni-T 500MHz 4CH ARB Signal Generator Unboxing">•</a></sup> A rise time failure captured on I2C lines during instrument testing was traced to the pull-up value rather than to the transmitting device.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-876" title="EEVblog #876 - NI VirtualBench Review">876</a></sup> Bus analysers accordingly expose the pull-ups as a user-selectable setting alongside the 100 or 400 kHz speed choice.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1291" title="EEVblog #1291 - Mailbag">1291</a></sup>

## Rise time, capacitance and slew rate

The pull-up and the load capacitance form an RC that governs every rising edge, and the resulting asymmetry can be severe. In one [[optocoupler]]-driven clock path the fall time was 500 ns with the transistor actively pulling down, against a [[datasheet]] rise time of 45 microseconds specified for a nominal 4.7K pull-up into 30 pF — a figure representative of a gate's [[input-capacitance|input capacitance]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1208" title="EEVblog #1208 - Circuit Analysis &amp; Debugging">1208</a></sup> With a 2K7 pull-up the rise remained in the order of tens of microseconds, far too slow for the clock input it fed, driving the input into a metastable state where the counter skipped or doubled pulses.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1208" title="EEVblog #1208 - Circuit Analysis &amp; Debugging">1208</a></sup> Two remedies apply: reduce the pull-up resistor, or interpose a Schmitt trigger that converts an arbitrarily slow edge into a sharp one before the clock input.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1208" title="EEVblog #1208 - Circuit Analysis &amp; Debugging">1208</a></sup> Optocouplers are poor at driving logic directly for exactly this reason.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1208" title="EEVblog #1208 - Circuit Analysis &amp; Debugging">1208</a></sup>

## Choosing and consolidating values

Selecting a pull-up is genuinely a design calculation rather than a default, and the arithmetic runs into manufacturing economics. If a calculation yields 1.8K and no other 1.8K appears in the design, using an existing 1.5K or 1K value instead costs a little extra power but saves a reel on the pick-and-place machine.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1307" title="EEVblog #1307 - TUTORIAL: PCB BOM Consolidation">1307</a></sup> Boards that need a second pass through the machine to load extra reels cost money, so component-by-component consolidation is worthwhile: a 15k pull-up can usually become a 10k already used elsewhere on the board.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-127" title="EEVblog #127 - PCB Design For Manufacture Tutorial - Part 1">127</a></sup>

CMOS relaxes the constraint at the other end. The 4000 series has effectively infinite [[input-impedance|input impedance]], so 10 meg pull-ups work fine and dissipate essentially nothing, enabling ultra low power designs — with the caveat that 10 meg is not a hard pull-up and copes badly with long lines.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-801" title="EEVblog #801 - How To Design A Digital Clock">801</a></sup> Unused inputs on spare logic gates should still be tied rather than left floating; routing the spare input to a solderable pad or [[via]] and adding a very weak pull-up of around a meg holds the input defined while leaving the gate available for later bodging.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1611" title="EEVblog 1611 - Top 5 Jellybean 7400 &amp; 4000 Logic">1611</a></sup> Historic practice sat between these extremes: [[diode]]-transistor logic built its gates from diodes, transistors and pull-up resistors, with the Apollo LVDC modules using a 2.5k pull-up and values generally in the range of several K to tens of K, consistent with a low-current digital machine.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-638" title="EEVblog #638 - Apollo Saturn V LVDC Testing">638</a></sup>

## Internal pull-ups

Microcontrollers commonly provide software-configurable pull-ups, which removes the external part entirely. Optical [[rotary-encoder|rotary encoder]] outputs that would normally need external pull-ups can go straight into the micro with the pull-ups enabled internally, saving components and board space, and an I2C chip with internal programmable pull-ups does not strictly need external ones — external resistors added anyway are candidates for removal on a later revision because they consume layout area.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-232" title="EEVblog #232 - Lab Power Supply Design Part 5">232</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-245" title="EEVblog #245 - PSU Design Part 10 - PCB Layout Editing">245</a></sup> GPIO configuration in either register-level or abstracted embedded code sets pull-up state along with mode and speed.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-A_saS93Clgk" title="C++ for the Embedded Programmer">•</a></sup> Some peripheral controllers advertise pull-ups on every one of dozens of software-controlled digital outputs.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-377" title="EEVblog #377 - Mailbag Monday">377</a></sup> Internal pull-ups on logic analyser inputs, where present, tend to be weak and unreliable.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-436" title="EEVblog #436 - Saleae USB Logic Analyser Review &amp; Teardown">436</a></sup>

## Reset, power and switch circuits

Active low reset pins are normally held high by a pull-up and pulled low only at power-up, so an RC network to VCC on the reset pin provides both behaviours without additional parts.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-710" title="EEVblog #710 - Intercom System Repair">710</a></sup> Reset pull-ups are also where marginal designs surface: a 10K pull-up that failed to reliably reset an intercom chip was replaced by progressively lower values until a 1K worked, with 100 ohms known good, pointing at something wrong with the reset pin itself rather than the resistor.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-710" title="EEVblog #710 - Intercom System Repair">710</a></sup> Reset nodes are sensitive to loading in general — hooking instruments and parallel resistors to a reset line disturbs the pull-up enough to stop the product switching on.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-u2-ot2vWLxI" title="121GW Range Switch Cycle Testing">•</a></sup> The sensitivity cuts the other way too: with a 2.2 meg pull-up on the reset pin, a microcontroller can be kept alive through its [[input-protection|input protection]] diodes with the VCC pin disconnected entirely, whereas a 100K pull-up drops too little voltage to matter and cannot supply the chip or its LEDs.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-831" title="EEVblog #831 - Power A Micro With No Power Pin!">831</a></sup>

In power switching, a pull-up from gate to source holds a P-channel MOSFET off until a switch grounds the gate.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-995" title="EEVblog #995 - Power Supply Sequencing">995</a></sup> Soft latching switch circuits use a pull-up from the input rail to a transistor base to arm the latch.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-262" title="EEVblog #262 - World&#x27;s Simplest Soft Latching Power Switch Circuit">262</a></sup> Soft power buttons routed across a system are traced by following the pull-up: a single pull-up resistor can be at the far end of a [[flat-flex-cable|flat flex cable]] from the [[processor]] that reads it, with 4.8 volts measurable across the whole path.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-378" title="EEVblog #378 - Dumpster Diving Teardown Repair">378</a></sup> Power good outputs likewise arrive with a pull-up already fitted.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1710" title="EEVblog 1710 - Mailbag: Tennismatic, Breadboards, Books &amp; Boost Converters">1710</a></sup>

Mechanical contacts are read the same way: a switch shorting a pulled-up node to [[ground]] generates one counted edge per press in principle, and contact bounce in practice — a single manual press registering hundreds of counts on a totalize-mode counter.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-961" title="EEVblog #961 - Monkey Debouncing">961</a></sup> Instrument trigger inputs therefore offer selectable pull-up strength, such as 20 mA and 2 mA options, alongside debouncing.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-947" title="EEVblog #947 - Chronos High Speed Camera Review">947</a></sup> Two pull-up resistors to a 9 V rail around an [[inverter]] stage allow either junction to be dragged low or driven high without shorting out the rail.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-242" title="EEVblog #242 - Hakko FX-888 Soldering Iron Hack">242</a></sup> Multiplexed front panels use pull-ups and pull-downs feeding a priority encoder to report which [[range-switch|range switch]] position is selected, or sense the position through a node pulled high inside the micro.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-853" title="EEVblog #853 - How A Multimeter Works">853</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1520" title="EEVblog 1520 - Troubleshooting a Faulty BM786 Multimeter">1520</a></sup> A refinement puts several switches on one microcontroller pin by switching different resistances into the pull-up divider and reading the result with the ADC — elegant until a tactile switch with [[contact-resistance|contact resistance]] varying from a couple of ohms to many hundreds corrupts the divider.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1361" title="EEVblog #1361 - Dodgy Tactile Switch TEARDOWN">1361</a></sup> A Hall sensor feeding an interrupt pin is wired the same way, with a 1K pull-up between 5 V and the signal pin.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-EHLRQbCW5oM" title="Guest Video: Electronoobs - Building a POV Display">•</a></sup>

Pull-up and pull-down strapping can also carry information rather than just a logic level: [[usb|USB]] speed grade is signalled by which of the data lines carries a pull-up.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-ql7J_tFBVfY" title="The Amp Hour #51 - Vafrous Video Vaniloquence">•</a></sup>

## Recognising pull-ups on hardware

In [[reverse-engineering|reverse engineering]], a resistor running from a signal pin to a large copper pad that is almost certainly a power plane identifies itself as a pull-up or pull-down before any measurement is taken.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1475" title="EEVblog 1475 - What&#x27;s This SMD Part?">1475</a></sup> Resistance measurements then confirm the topology — 100k measured to a common node identifies a network rather than discrete resistors, and 200k between two pins with 100k to each confirms which end is common.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1245" title="EEVblog #1245 - A Most Excellent REPAIR: IBM PCjr Keyboard">1245</a></sup> Resistor networks used as pull-ups cluster around microcontroller ports and interface connectors, such as the 100k network on a portable computer's RS-232 port or the array serving a [[vacuum-fluorescent-display|vacuum fluorescent display]]'s control lines.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1662" title="EEVblog 1662 - Vintage Tandy 200 Portable Computer Claytons Repair/Teardown">1662</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-717" title="EEVblog #717 - How To Hack Vacuum Fluorescent Displays">717</a></sup> Value alone is diagnostic: on an unfamiliar board some resistors sit at pull-up-like values and others clearly do not.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-8qmU7SZu_-I" title="Altera Software Dongle Teardown">•</a></sup> Not every resistor to a rail is a pull-up, and a 10k to a node with a capacitor on the other side is more likely a bypass arrangement than a pull-up.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1360" title="EEVblog #1360 - REPAIR - Aircon Control Panel">1360</a></sup> Even printed carbon resistors, whose value depends on track length, width and thickness and which are typically 30% tolerance parts, are used for pull-up and pull-down duty.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-335" title="EEVblog #335 - Carbon Printed Resistors">335</a></sup>

## Pull-ups as after-the-fact fixes

Omitted or wrongly valued pull-ups are among the commonest reasons for a factory bodge. Sleeved leaded resistors bussed together on a digital IO board, a single leaded resistor tacked onto a CPLD where a pin was probably left floating, and hand-added pull-ups on a computer bus are all recognisable as pull-up mods rather than respins.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-738" title="EEVblog #738 - Yamaha DME32 Digital Mixer Teardown">738</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-598" title="EEVblog #598 - Sony DME7000 Video Multi Effects Processor Teardown">598</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-Gbn_51IoJiM" title="Amiga 2000 Teardown &amp; Power Up">•</a></sup> Repairs use the same lever: fitting a low-impedance 100-ohm pull-up to a clock rail restored a NAS whose clock had failed, an inelegant fix but preferable to cutting tracks and running mod wires, with the alternative being a mod board carrying its own 25 MHz [[oscillator]] at the risk of losing clock synchronisation.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1288" title="EEVblog #1288 - Synology NAS Dumpster Find! (REPAIR)">1288</a></sup>
