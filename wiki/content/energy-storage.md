Energy storage is the holding of energy in a form from which it can later be released to do work — chemically in a [[battery]], electrostatically in a [[capacitor]], magnetically in an [[inductor]], or thermally in a heated mass. It matters because generation and consumption rarely coincide in time: a [[solar-array|solar array]] produces at midday, a household consumes at night, and an airbag squib needs [[current]] at the instant the car's wiring has already failed.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1086" title="EEVblog #1086 - 5 Year Solar Power Results - Payback?">1086</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-517" title="EEVblog #517 - Car Airbag Controller Teardown">517</a></sup> The distinction that governs the whole subject is that energy can be stored while power cannot.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1009" title="EEVblog #1009 - DC Fundamentals Part 1: Voltage vs Power vs Energy">1009</a></sup>

## Energy versus power

Power is instantaneous; energy is power integrated over time, and confusing the two is the most common error in the field.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1009" title="EEVblog #1009 - DC Fundamentals Part 1: Voltage vs Power vs Energy">1009</a></sup> "You can generate power, but you can't generate energy."<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1009" title="EEVblog #1009 - DC Fundamentals Part 1: Voltage vs Power vs Energy">1009</a></sup> The converse also holds: a battery does not store power, it stores energy, and a circuit is what converts that stored energy back into power when current flows.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1009" title="EEVblog #1009 - DC Fundamentals Part 1: Voltage vs Power vs Energy">1009</a></sup> A dam illustrates the split — the volume of water impounded is the energy, the rate of flow through the gate is the power, and closing the gate leaves zero power and zero current while the energy remains.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1009" title="EEVblog #1009 - DC Fundamentals Part 1: Voltage vs Power vs Energy">1009</a></sup>

This is why the correct capacity figure for a battery is in watt-hours rather than in amp-hours or volts: a watt-hour rating accounts for the fact that terminal [[voltage]] sags through the discharge rather than holding constant and then abruptly collapsing.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1009" title="EEVblog #1009 - DC Fundamentals Part 1: Voltage vs Power vs Energy">1009</a></sup> A 25 Wh battery can in principle deliver 25 W for an hour or 1 W for 25 hours.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1009" title="EEVblog #1009 - DC Fundamentals Part 1: Voltage vs Power vs Energy">1009</a></sup> Claims expressed as "volts of energy" or "volts of power" are category errors.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1009" title="EEVblog #1009 - DC Fundamentals Part 1: Voltage vs Power vs Energy">1009</a></sup>

## Storage in passive components

A capacitor stores energy in the [[electric-field|electric field]] of its dielectric, and an inductor stores it in a [[magnetic-field|magnetic field]] — in the inductor's case the field strengthens as turns are added, because the fields of successive turns sum.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1406" title="EEVblog 1406 - DC Fundamentals Part 7: DC Circuit Transients Fundamentals">1406</a></sup> Releasing an inductor's stored energy by removing the load produces a voltage spike, a hazard the component's own physics guarantees.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1406" title="EEVblog 1406 - DC Fundamentals Part 7: DC Circuit Transients Fundamentals">1406</a></sup> The field picture is also what resolves the question of how current appears to pass through a capacitor: within the conductor one can reason in terms of [[electrical-current|electric current]], but inside the dielectric the description becomes changing electric fields producing changing magnetic fields, with energy stored and transferred in those fields.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-486" title="EEVblog #486 - Does Current Flow Through A Capacitor?">486</a></sup>

Storage is not incidental to switching power conversion — it is the mechanism. A buck converter differs from a [[linear-regulator|linear regulator]] essentially by the addition of an inductor as an energy storage component, plus a catch [[diode]] and an output filter capacitor, with the pass [[transistor]] switched in bursts instead of being held in a continuously variable loop.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-90" title="EEVblog #90 - Linear and LDO regulators and Switch Mode Power Supply Tutorial">90</a></sup> In a large solar hybrid [[inverter]], most of the [[power-dissipation|power dissipation]] concentrates in exactly these switching, storage and transformation elements, which is why the storage inductors carry substantial heatsinking.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1620" title="EEVblog 1620 - Deye Solar Hybrid Inverter EXTREME TEARDOWN">1620</a></sup>

Supercapacitors occupy the niche between capacitor and battery. Parts in the single-farad range at 5 V exist in compact packages resembling an HC49U crystal outline, and suit memory backup, data retention and energy-harvesting storage — a charged [[supercapacitor]] can hold a product's standby state through a battery change more or less indefinitely.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1043" title="EEVblog #1043 - Mailbag">1043</a></sup>

## Buffering intermittent and pulsed sources

Where a source produces energy slowly or in bursts, storage decouples its delivery profile from the load's demand profile. An energy-harvesting front end typically supplies only the average power drawn by a circuit; the peaks — a [[microcontroller]] waking each second and drawing hundreds of microamps — are supplied from local storage such as decoupling [[capacitance]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1383" title="EEVblog 1383 - Human Battery Energy Harvesting BUSTED">1383</a></sup> A thermoelectric harvesting board makes this explicit, using roughly 1500 µF of storage assembled from 100 µF capacitors, charged from a Peltier device run as a Seebeck generator to about 5.2 V, after which the accumulated charge runs the load for an extended interval; the runtime follows directly from the total capacitance.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-664" title="EEVblog #664 - Peltier TEG Energy Harvesting Experiments">664</a></sup>

The same principle explains parasitically powered equipment. A payphone drawing its supply from the telephone line at roughly 48 V with very little available current — one standard phone load — must accumulate that trickle in a storage capacitor or battery before it can operate.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-363" title="EEVblog #363 - Gold Phone Teardown">363</a></sup> Automotive airbag controllers carry capacitors far too large for ordinary rail smoothing, sized instead to hold the rail up long enough to fire the squib after a crash has already compromised the car's wiring.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-517" title="EEVblog #517 - Car Airbag Controller Teardown">517</a></sup>

Storage also sets the ceiling on what harvesting can achieve. Accumulating energy over a day or a week and then spending it in one useful burst is a legitimate strategy, but only if the source voltage is high enough for a harvesting front end to work at all; a source delivering tens of microvolts sits three orders of magnitude below what the best harvesting chips require, and no amount of storage rescues it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1680" title="EEVblog 1680 - Free Energy from the Earth&#x27;s Magnetic Field?">1680</a></sup>

## Grid and residential scale

Storage is the central obstacle to renewable generation, because solar produces nothing at night and wind produces nothing when it is still; useful deployment requires baseload storage.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-sJS2paVW-kc" title="Renewable Energy vs Fossil Fuels Presentation">•</a></sup> Approaches extend well beyond batteries — including gravitational schemes that raise large masses during the day and recover the energy as they descend overnight.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-sJS2paVW-kc" title="Renewable Energy vs Fossil Fuels Presentation">•</a></sup> The economics are conditional: storage is only worth installing if generation exceeds consumption during the production window. A 10 GW array whose entire output is consumed during daylight has nothing to store and no reason to build storage; the case for a 30 GW storage bank rests on the surplus, which then feeds the transmission link overnight in place of the panels.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-AWYuyf3ILLk" title="World&#x27;s Largest Solar Array - HVDC Transmission Calculations...">•</a></sup>

The grid itself is not a storage medium. Exported energy is not held on the operator's behalf for later retrieval — grid supply is essentially use-it-or-lose-it, and schemes premised on drawing back at night the surplus they injected by day rest on a misconception.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-632" title="EEVblog #632 - Solar Roadways Are RUBBISH!">632</a></sup> Grid-scale batteries are a distinct asset class, physically installed and commercially rented: utilities hire storage capacity from the private owners of installations such as the Victoria Big Battery and the Hornsdale Power Reserve.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1411" title="EEVblog 1411 - Tesla Victoria Big Battery FIRE!">1411</a></sup> The idea is not new — the first commercial [[solar-power|solar power]] station used mining batteries charged by daytime surplus to supply the town at night.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1553" title="EEVblog 1553 - World&#x27;s First Commercial Solar Power Station">1553</a></sup>

At household scale the driver is the gap between feed-in tariff and import rate. Panels generate through the day while the occupants are out, and that surplus is exported for very little; a home battery captures it and returns it to whatever load happens to be present, whether household circuits or an EV, with the object being a lower quarterly bill rather than any particular load being served.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1634" title="EEVblog 1634 - 15kWh AERL LFP Home Storage Battery Install!">1634</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-gAu8CvMjDrU" title="15kWh AERL LFP Home Storage Battery - First Analysis">•</a></sup> The arithmetic is unforgiving. Storing 7 kWh a day that was previously exported saves about $306 a year at $0.12/kWh, which against installed cost worked out to a 41-year payback — and even then the household still imported 5.1 kWh per day, implying a larger array would be needed.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1086" title="EEVblog #1086 - 5 Year Solar Power Results - Payback?">1086</a></sup> Round-trip [[efficiency]] of a lithium-ion pack is around 90% end to end, a factor sometimes set aside in first-pass calculations.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1086" title="EEVblog #1086 - 5 Year Solar Power Results - Payback?">1086</a></sup>

Heat is a cheaper storage medium where the end use is heat. A hot water tank charged from surplus solar functions as a thermal battery, absorbing energy that would otherwise be exported at a poor rate; a heat-pump system using around 3 kWh per day of excess solar consumes far less energy per litre heated than even a high-efficiency gas unit, and oversizing the tank buys storage capacity rather than draw-off capacity.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-iavM2IqueM8" title="Heat Pump Hot Water System vs Gas Hot Water">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1517" title="EEVblog 1517 - Heat Pump Hot Water Install &amp; Analysis">1517</a></sup>

## Storage as a tell in dubious claims

Because storage cannot be conjured, its absence or vagueness is diagnostic. Road-based harvesting concepts that promise energy usable instantly or stored for later are describing a pulsed source, which necessarily requires a battery storage stage before the output is usable at all — a solvable engineering problem, but not one the claims acknowledge.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1600" title="EEVblog 1600 - Rouute: Road Based Energy BUSTED">1600</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1647" title="EEVblog 1647 - Road Based Energy REBRANDED. Rouute vs R-2ENERGY">1647</a></sup> A [[solar-roadways|solar roadway]] installation generating around 1300 kWh a year amounts to roughly 34 charges of a 38 kWh EV pack, or one charge every ten days, regardless of what storage is bolted on.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1356" title="EEVblog #1356 - The USA finally gets Solar Roadways! LOL!">1356</a></sup> Devices claiming 24-hour output, or output amplified twentyfold over conventional panels, are implicitly claiming a storage element, and when the product has negligible capture area the storage claim becomes the entire product.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1633" title="EEVblog 1633 - WAVJA Solar Balls, 60x Better Than A Solar Panel? - Part 1">1633</a></sup> Wireless power demonstrations similarly reveal themselves through their storage: off-the-shelf modules backed by very large reservoir capacitance, charged slowly and then dumped, rather than a continuous power transfer.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-MCyLO-1grEk" title="WiGL Wireless Power BUSTED AGAIN!">•</a></sup> The general test is to ask what the storage element actually is, how much energy it holds, and how long it took to accumulate.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1633" title="EEVblog 1633 - WAVJA Solar Balls, 60x Better Than A Solar Panel? - Part 1">1633</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1680" title="EEVblog 1680 - Free Energy from the Earth&#x27;s Magnetic Field?">1680</a></sup>
