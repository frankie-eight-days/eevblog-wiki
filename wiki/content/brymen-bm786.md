The Brymen BM786 is a handheld digital [[multimeter]], an EEVblog-exclusive variant within Brymen's BM780 series, sold from 2020 onward.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-PfzgOuBHJFM" title="Brymen BM786 vs BM869 DC Current">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-TkmMitYnHUA" title="New Brymen BM787BT Bluetooth Multimeter Teardown (Live Recorded)">•</a></sup> Its significance is positional: it is the handheld that a mid-range Fluke — specifically the 77 IV — is most directly measured against, matching that meter in shape, form factor and overmolded case while costing between a half and a third as much.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-ay9wFQAW19Y" title="eevBLAB 91 - Why Are Fluke Meters So EXPENSIVE?">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1447" title="EEVblog 1447 - EXPENSIVE Fluke vs CHEAPER Brymen - Teardown">1447</a></sup> The models across the 780 series are functionally the same instrument with different feature sets; the 786 is the branded configuration.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-TkmMitYnHUA" title="New Brymen BM787BT Bluetooth Multimeter Teardown (Live Recorded)">•</a></sup>

## DC current performance

The 780 series specification for DC [[current]] is tighter than that of Brymen's own flagship BM869, an inversion of the usual assumption that the top-of-the-line model wins on every parameter.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-PfzgOuBHJFM" title="Brymen BM786 vs BM869 DC Current">•</a></sup> The BM786 specifies roughly 0.075% plus 20 digits on DC current.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-PfzgOuBHJFM" title="Brymen BM786 vs BM869 DC Current">•</a></sup> The 869's corresponding figure is 0.15%, with a best case of 0.1% plus 20 digits on its 6 mA and 6,000 µA ranges, and it degrades to plus 30 digits on the 600 mA range where the 786 holds plus 20 digits at the same percentage spec.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-PfzgOuBHJFM" title="Brymen BM786 vs BM869 DC Current">•</a></sup> The 869's 500,000-count resolution applies to [[voltage]], not to current, so it confers no advantage here.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-PfzgOuBHJFM" title="Brymen BM786 vs BM869 DC Current">•</a></sup>

[[burden-voltage]] follows the same pattern. The BM786 is specified at 0.15 mV per microamp against 0.2 for the 869, and it is lower on the two principal milliamp ranges as well.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-PfzgOuBHJFM" title="Brymen BM786 vs BM869 DC Current">•</a></sup> Better accuracy and lower burden voltage together make the cheaper meter the correct choice where absolute DC [[current-measurement]] matters.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-PfzgOuBHJFM" title="Brymen BM786 vs BM869 DC Current">•</a></sup> Verified against a calibrator at test points up to 100 mA, both meters read accurately, which is the expected outcome and does not discriminate between them — the difference lives in the specification, not in a spot check.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-PfzgOuBHJFM" title="Brymen BM786 vs BM869 DC Current">•</a></sup>

## Non-contact voltage detection

The BM786 carries non-contact voltage detection with two sensitivity ranges, defaulting to the high range, actuated by a tab on the side of the case.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1378" title="EEVblog 1378 - NEW Uni-T UT61E+ Multimeter - Still bang-per-buck king?">1378</a></sup> Its detection performance is at the top of what handheld meters achieve: it will pick up a light switch, a target that weaker implementations miss entirely.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1378" title="EEVblog 1378 - NEW Uni-T UT61E+ Multimeter - Still bang-per-buck king?">1378</a></sup> Sensitivity of this order is the practical dividing line between a usable NCV function and a decorative one.

## Resistance behaviour

Certain BM869 units exhibit an anomaly in the region of 500 kΩ, where moving the leads produces reading instability at particular values.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-EJ6KCpHc--4" title="Strange Brymen BM869 Resistance Quirk">•</a></sup> The BM786 does not reproduce this behaviour under the same conditions.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-EJ6KCpHc--4" title="Strange Brymen BM869 Resistance Quirk">•</a></sup>

## Auto power-off

The BM786 uses a plain auto power-off timer that is not reset by taking a measurement, so the meter shuts down after its timeout — around 30 minutes — regardless of activity.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-BuFoA-qt1PY" title="Brymen BM2257 Intelligent Auto Power Off (iAPO) tested">•</a></sup> It also powers off while in min-max recording mode, which defeats the purpose of leaving a meter logging.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-BuFoA-qt1PY" title="Brymen BM2257 Intelligent Auto Power Off (iAPO) tested">•</a></sup> The later BM2257 implements Intelligent Auto Power Off, which resets the timer on measurement and suppresses shutdown in capture and recording modes.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-BuFoA-qt1PY" title="Brymen BM2257 Intelligent Auto Power Off (iAPO) tested">•</a></sup> The much older BM235 already behaved this way, so the omission on the comparatively recent 786 is not a generational limitation.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-BuFoA-qt1PY" title="Brymen BM2257 Intelligent Auto Power Off (iAPO) tested">•</a></sup> [[firmware]] headroom is a plausible constraint on Brymen designs of this class — the BM235's [[microcontroller]] has extremely limited memory space, and feature requests have been declined on those grounds.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-BuFoA-qt1PY" title="Brymen BM2257 Intelligent Auto Power Off (iAPO) tested">•</a></sup>

## Construction

The fuses sit inside the main case rather than behind a separate access hatch, the same arrangement used by the Fluke 70 and 80 series.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1351" title="EEVblog #1351 - Mailbag">1351</a></sup> Case assembly uses proper plastic self-tappers of the type Fluke and Brymen both employ.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1351" title="EEVblog #1351 - Mailbag">1351</a></sup> Power-on is not a mechanical switch but an electronically switched arrangement using a [[mosfet]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-f-LTv1GqCMw" title="Brymen BM786 Power On Fault Investigation">•</a></sup> The 780-series board carries provisions for Bluetooth in the back of the unit — the series was originally promised with a Bluetooth option — but that option did not ship until the BM787BT appeared roughly five years later.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-TkmMitYnHUA" title="New Brymen BM787BT Bluetooth Multimeter Teardown (Live Recorded)">•</a></sup>

## Field failures

The BM786 does not have a zero failure rate.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-f-LTv1GqCMw" title="Brymen BM786 Power On Fault Investigation">•</a></sup> Three distinct fault patterns are documented in returned units.

**Intermittent power-on.** A unit would come on only at certain switch positions, or not at all, with the behaviour changing after the meter had been left off for a period.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-f-LTv1GqCMw" title="Brymen BM786 Power On Fault Investigation">•</a></sup> Running the meter from a bench supply set to 4.5 V with a 100 mA limit, the fault reproduced: the unit drew about 1 mA when it started correctly, and in the failed state settled at a consistent 0.3 mA, later shifting to a consistent 1.2 mA — a supply current that is repeatable but wrong, and that changed value over the course of the investigation.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-f-LTv1GqCMw" title="Brymen BM786 Power On Fault Investigation">•</a></sup> Pressing on the [[battery]] compartment restored reliable starting for ten consecutive attempts, pointing at mechanical rather than purely electrical causation.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-f-LTv1GqCMw" title="Brymen BM786 Power On Fault Investigation">•</a></sup> The original owner had already tried a bench supply and cleaned the spring contacts and the pads beneath them without effect.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-f-LTv1GqCMw" title="Brymen BM786 Power On Fault Investigation">•</a></sup>

**Rotary switch misbehaviour.** Two returned units, out of three faulty over the first three or four months of sales, showed a switch-related fault in which the meter appeared to jump between adjacent positions such as [[diode]] and [[resistance]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-IoRks5bJw8Y" title="Brymen BM786 Switch Fault Investigation">•</a></sup> The fault is reproducible on some units far more readily than others, and forcing it requires deliberate mechanical loading of the switch.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-IoRks5bJw8Y" title="Brymen BM786 Switch Fault Investigation">•</a></sup> Root-causing it properly would require dimensional measurement of the plastic parts against the manufacturer's tolerance data — many days of work, and realistically the manufacturer's job rather than the seller's.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-IoRks5bJw8Y" title="Brymen BM786 Switch Fault Investigation">•</a></sup>

**Insertion error.** Separate returns exhibited a persistent input jack alert, with the meter reporting an insertion error on every range including milliamps and microamps, refusing to take a measurement until switched off.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1663" title="EEVblog 1663 - BM786 Multimeter REPAIR + InEr Error Investigation">1663</a></sup>

## Relationship to other meters

The teardown comparison of the Fluke 77 IV against the BM786 produced an unrelated finding about the Fluke: an average-responding 77 IV can be converted to true-RMS operation, matching a Fluke 177, for roughly ten cents in parts.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1448" title="EEVblog 1448 - Convert a Fluke 77 IV to True RMS for 10 CENTS!*">1448</a></sup> Within Brymen's own line, the 786 was under development at a point when it already outclassed contemporaneous models by a wide margin.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-SKuZ4_cZjDU" title="MYSTERY Box of Multimeters">•</a></sup>
