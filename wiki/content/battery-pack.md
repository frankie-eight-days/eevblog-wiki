A battery pack is an assembly of two or more cells wired in series, in parallel, or in both, so that a product sees one terminal pair with a [[voltage]] and capacity that no single cell could provide.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-176" title="EEVblog #176 - Lithium Ion/Polymer Battery Charging Tutorial">176</a></sup> The pack, not the cell, is what an engineer actually designs around: it sets the supply rail, it usually carries the protection and management electronics, and in large systems it is the single most expensive item in the product.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-8P8Af5SR57U" title="Goal Zero Yeti 400 Lithium Battery Follow--up">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-3EgRMEc8R_I" title="eevBLAB #63: How Affordable Are Electric Cars?">•</a></sup> Because the pack multiplies cell voltage, every calculation done from a single-cell characteristic curve has to be scaled by the number of cells in series before it means anything at the pack terminals.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-204" title="Designing a Li-Ion Battery Gauge with the LM3914 - EEVblog #204">204</a></sup>

## Topology

The simplest packs are pure series strings: a UPS pack of six 6 V blocks gives 36 V at 9 Ah,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1168" title="EEVblog #1168 - Dumpster Diving UPS">1168</a></sup> a network tester pack is six NiCad cells,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-XpZVIWdXliY" title="Fluke OneTouch Network Tester Battery Restoration Attempt">•</a></sup> and a [[multimeter]] can use three AAA cells stacked vertically in a custom carrier.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1520" title="EEVblog 1520 - Troubleshooting a Faulty BM786 Multimeter">1520</a></sup> Alkaline strings are often built as sub-packs of two cells each, so a thirteen-pack string sits near 26 V once the cells are down to about a volt apiece.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-XDjyY48u0PU" title="Alkaline Battery Discharge Testing Part 2">•</a></sup>

Larger packs are built as parallel strings that are then put in series. A 12.8 V lithium pack uses four parallel strings in series, with the [[battery]] management system tapping each string to keep the series groups balanced.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-8P8Af5SR57U" title="Goal Zero Yeti 400 Lithium Battery Follow--up">•</a></sup> The same pattern scales up: a battery-electric train uses a battery system made of two parallel packs, each pack carrying its own battery management system and each containing twelve modules, with a common thermal conditioning unit serving both.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1570" title="EEVblog 1570 -  Tesla DIESEL Electric Train at Berlin Gigafactory ?">1570</a></sup> Where a string is tapped at its midpoint rather than only at its ends, the result is a bipolar pack with sense wires brought out at the taps.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1258" title="EEVblog #1258 - MORE Dumpster Wandel And Goltermann!">1258</a></sup>

Series count is a design constraint in its own right. Two packs in series at an absolute maximum of roughly 8 V each means the downstream switching device only ever sees 17 or 18 V, so a 30 V [[mosfet|MOSFET]] in that position is heavily over-specified.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1461" title="EEVblog 1461 - The MOSFET Search CHALLENGE">1461</a></sup> A bar-graph driver that works from 3 V to about 15 V can be run directly from the pack it is measuring, with no intermediate rail.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-204" title="Designing a Li-Ion Battery Gauge with the LM3914 - EEVblog #204">204</a></sup>

## Capacity and how it is rated

Pack capacity marked in milliamp-hours is close to meaningless because the figure is referred to the internal cell voltage rather than the output. A pack sold as 20,000 mAh is rated against a nominal internal [[battery-voltage|battery voltage]] of 3.85 V held constant, which is "this is not how you rate [[battery-capacity|battery capacity]]" territory: the honest unit is the watt-hour, and packs are only comparable to each other in watt-hours.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1649" title="EEVblog 1649 - Mailbag: M5stack, Beelink Mini PC, ZOYI LCR, ToughOn Battery">1649</a></sup> Products that do label in watt-hours make the comparison immediate — 6,000 mAh at 22 Wh in a portable speaker,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1605" title="EEVblog 1605 - JBL Charge Speaker REPAIR">1605</a></sup> 72 Wh in a larger one,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1672" title="EEVblog 1672 - JBL REPAIR: You&#x27;ll never guess the fault!">1672</a></sup> 427 Wh nominal from a 10.8 V, 40 Ah portable power station pack,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1707" title="EEVblog 1707 - REPAIR: Goal Zero Yeti 400 Lithium Battery">1707</a></sup> 400 Wh enough to run a camping fridge for a couple of days.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-t9o1xhAHREE" title="Camping">•</a></sup>

Capacity is also the main product tier in battery-powered appliances: a pool-cleaning robot is sold in a 5,000 mAh version and a 10,000 mAh version, the larger pack being what buys the extra runtime.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-S5JPr_XQcCc" title="Platy: The Madimack i60 AI Pool Cleaning Robot">•</a></sup>

## Protection and management

Beyond balancing, the pack's protection circuit is what keeps an over-discharged system recoverable — an undervoltage lockout mounted directly on the battery is credible as the reason a deeply discharged portable power station survived at all.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-8P8Af5SR57U" title="Goal Zero Yeti 400 Lithium Battery Follow--up">•</a></sup> Small consumer packs carry their own protection board even at 1,500 mAh.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-f_SdM6sXHD4" title="FNIRSI SG-003A Process Meter + DSO-TC2 Oscilloscope &amp; Transistor Tester">•</a></sup> Instrument packs may be fused at the pack itself.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-808" title="EEVblog #808 - Fluke 196 Scopemeter Repair">808</a></sup>

Thermal instrumentation is a serious sub-problem in high-energy packs. One purpose-built 3.6 V, 20 Ah, 427 g module contains a four-point analog [[temperature-sensor|temperature sensor]] acting as a hot-spot detector, reporting only the highest temperature of its eight cells — a deliberate reduction of eight measurements to the one that matters.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-917" title="EEVblog #917 - Mailbag">917</a></sup> The commercial motivation for such modules is that student and small-team electric vehicle projects are limited chiefly by pack size, weight and construction safety.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-917" title="EEVblog #917 - Mailbag">917</a></sup>

Self-discharge sets a floor on standby designs. At a maximum of about 5% per month, a lithium-ion pack left armed for years has nothing useful left in it — "There's not going to be enough juice left in that pack." — and a design that must sit dormant for that long belongs on lithium primary cells instead.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1136" title="EEVblog #1136 - Banksy Artwork Shredder - Part 2 (Still Trolling!)">1136</a></sup>

## Construction quality

Packs are frequently custom, welded or bonded shut, and therefore not serviceable: portable power banks are typically welded closed and cannot be opened,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1649" title="EEVblog 1649 - Mailbag: M5stack, Beelink Mini PC, ZOYI LCR, ToughOn Battery">1649</a></sup> and small accessory packs are ultrasonically welded.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1034" title="EEVblog #1034 - Mailbag">1034</a></sup> Custom cell arrangements are common even where the cells themselves are ordinary — standard Panasonic AA cells welded together into a custom pack, potted in styrofoam for thermal protection in a high-altitude payload.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1207" title="EEVblog #1207 - ARM Dev Boards Falling From The Sky!">1207</a></sup> Where a pack is a custom size with no [[connector]], replacement depends entirely on whether the original part remains available.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1605" title="EEVblog 1605 - JBL Charge Speaker REPAIR">1605</a></sup> A replaceable pack that is not externally accessible is a missed opportunity rather than a fault.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1672" title="EEVblog 1672 - JBL REPAIR: You&#x27;ll never guess the fault!">1672</a></sup>

Bad pack construction is conspicuous. A tablet found to contain a hand-assembled pack drew the assessment "Unbelievable, they've bodge soldered a pack together. That is the worst I have ever seen." — the extension section of the pack being the same cells wrapped in paper and soldered on the end rather than an off-the-shelf assembly.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-822" title="EEVblog #822 - World&#x27;s Worst Tablet Computer Teardown">822</a></sup> Poorly designed packs are the mechanism behind the self-igniting hoverboards of the mid-2010s, a bell-curve statistics problem in which some fraction of any large production run will fail.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-833" title="EEVblog #833 - Mailbag">833</a></sup> Modular construction is the counterexample: a 3.6 kWh portable power station is fully modular, so a failed pack module can be removed and replaced rather than condemning the unit.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1499" title="EEVblog 1499 - EcoFlow Delta Pro 3.6kWh Portable Battery TEARDOWN!">1499</a></sup>

## Diagnosis

The pack is the first suspect and often the wrong one, so it is worth measuring before opening anything further. A portable [[soldering]] station that would not run measured 10.77 V against a 10.8 V nominal pack, proving the battery was charged and moving the fault onto the board.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1646" title="EEVblog 1646 - iFixIt FixHub Portable USB Soldering Station REVIEW">1646</a></sup> Conversely a UPS pack measuring 0.6 V is unambiguously dead.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1168" title="EEVblog #1168 - Dumpster Diving UPS">1168</a></sup>

Loaded and unloaded measurements can disagree spectacularly. An alkaline string that read 25 V open-circuit collapsed to essentially zero volts while 44 to 50 mA was still flowing through it, a result that points at contact integrity — weak spring contacts in the holders — rather than at true cell depletion.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-XDjyY48u0PU" title="Alkaline Battery Discharge Testing Part 2">•</a></sup> The same class of complaint appears in charging: an intermittent DC jack that only passes [[current]] when physical pressure is applied will draw about 73 mA at 12 V and still charge nothing useful.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-XpZVIWdXliY" title="Fluke OneTouch Network Tester Battery Restoration Attempt">•</a></sup> A pack-plus-temperature-controller assembly that draws only [[quiescent-current|quiescent current]] and refuses to charge at all is simply failed, even though it powers up correctly from an external pack.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-h9V0qJ4p3Aw" title="2010 Nissan Dualis Power Window &quot;REPAIR&quot;">•</a></sup>

Under fault conditions the pack itself reports the problem: a shorting compute module drives the supplying pack into overload.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-E1IqcGcZKHE" title="RPi CM4 Compute Module FAILURE Part 2: Thermal Boogaloo">•</a></sup> Thermal imaging of a working [[led|LED]] strap light shows the pack running around 30 °C, comfortably below the surrounding [[heat-sink|heatsink]] temperatures.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1010" title="EEVblog #1010 - Zyntony Ra LED Strap Light Teardown">1010</a></sup>

## Packs in test equipment

Battery operation in bench instruments is almost always an option rather than a fitting. Scopes and analysers ship with a slot or slider on the bottom or rear for a pack that is bought separately,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-480" title="EEVblog #480 - 300MHz Owon SDS Oscilloscope">480</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1503" title="EEVblog 1503 - Rigol HDO4000 12bit Oscilloscope TEARDOWN">1503</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-207" title="Electronex Expo 2011 - EEVblog #207 (1 of 3)">207</a></sup> and the moldings for that slot survive into models where the connection is deleted, because the case is reused.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1510" title="EEVblog 1510 - $699 Rigol 12bit HDO1000 Teardown - WOAH!">1510</a></sup> A [[frequency-counter|frequency counter]] of the same lineage had its optional pack fitted internally, making the instrument fully portable.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-265" title="EEVblog #265 - Philips PM6672 Timer Counter Teardown">265</a></sup>

The Tektronix 2 Series treats the pack as a structural part: it is an option that ships with a single battery, the kickstand mounts on the pack rather than on the instrument, the pack has its own alignment protrusion, and the same pack works with the main stand.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-nO09bc5ozng" title="NEW Tektronix 2 Series MSO Oscilloscope FIRST REACTION">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-PcxEO3fA_Ls" title="Tektronix 2 Series Discussion with Andy Tedd from Tektronix">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1477" title="EEVblog 1477 - TEARDOWN! - NEW Tektronix 2 Series Oscilloscope">1477</a></sup> Batteries are hot-swappable and a separate external charger is offered, so a user can keep charged spares in rotation.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-nO09bc5ozng" title="NEW Tektronix 2 Series MSO Oscilloscope FIRST REACTION">•</a></sup> Shipping constraints bound the design — at about 97 Wh the battery is close enough to the air-freight limit that the product ships with only one.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-nO09bc5ozng" title="NEW Tektronix 2 Series MSO Oscilloscope FIRST REACTION">•</a></sup>

Where no factory option exists, external packs fill the gap. A 35 W scope can be run from a pack mounted on its VESA points with a cable run to the input,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1563" title="EEVblog 1563 - New $389 12bit Rigol DHO800 Scope TEARDOWN">1563</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1566" title="EEVblog 1566 - Rigol DHO800 12 Bit Oscilloscope FULL REVIEW">1566</a></sup> and the fuel-gauge and mounting problem is one the user community solves on its own.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1566" title="EEVblog 1566 - Rigol DHO800 12 Bit Oscilloscope FULL REVIEW">1566</a></sup>

## Bench use as a clean supply

A pack is a supply with no mains-referred path, which makes it a diagnostic instrument in itself. Running a circuit from a battery pack while disconnecting all external supply inputs isolates whether an observed modulation originates in the supply or in the circuit — in one case a 55.5 Hz modulation persisted unchanged on battery, exonerating the supply entirely.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-160" title="EEVblog#160 - 555 Timer Easter Egg?">160</a></sup> A [[power-supply|power supply]] fed from an external 12 V battery instead of a lab supply shows significantly cleaner output [[noise]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1606" title="EEVblog 1606 - $45 AlienTek DP100 100W USB-C PSU REVIEW">1606</a></sup>

Products with both an internal pack and a DC input handle the changeover mechanically rather than electronically. A three-pin DC barrel jack with a normally-closed switch contact carries the battery positive through pins 2 and 3; inserting the plug breaks that contact so the battery positive is completely disconnected from everything, and the external supply takes over.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1015" title="EEVblog #1015 - Beware Evil (But Clever) DC Jacks!">1015</a></sup> The battery [[ground]] stays permanently tied to common, so only the positive needs switching.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1015" title="EEVblog #1015 - Beware Evil (But Clever) DC Jacks!">1015</a></sup>

## Electric vehicles and grid storage

In an electric car the pack dominates cost. The premium for the fully electric version of a car also sold as a hybrid runs to about $20,000, and that premium is the pack.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1337" title="EEVblog #1337 - I Bought a Fully Electric Elite Car!">1337</a></sup> Stated more bluntly, the pack for a Tesla costs more than a whole new internal-combustion car, which is why electric cars would have a serious hard time competing without subsidy — and battery technology improves at single-digit percentages per year, not in steps large enough to change that quickly.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-3EgRMEc8R_I" title="eevBLAB #63: How Affordable Are Electric Cars?">•</a></sup>

Pack size is not the same as range. A 37.5 kWh pack delivers more range than a 40 kWh one in a competing car because the higher-performance car consumes 20 to 25% more energy per unit distance, on the order of 15 to 20% worse [[efficiency]] per range.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-XmBW_MV-TBU" title="Hyundai IONIQ Electric EV First Drive!">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1337" title="EEVblog #1337 - I Bought a Fully Electric Elite Car!">1337</a></sup> Trading punchy performance for range in this way is a defensible choice, and the smaller pack makes the car three or four thousand cheaper as well.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1337" title="EEVblog #1337 - I Bought a Fully Electric Elite Car!">1337</a></sup> Efficiency figures anchor the comparison: 10.5 kWh per 100 km for a solar-assisted production car with a 60 kWh pack and 625 km nominal range,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1480" title="EEVblog 1480 EXTENDED VERSION - LightYear Zero + Aptera Solar EV">1480</a></sup> against 6.25 kWh per 100 km implied by a 100 kWh pack claiming 1,000 miles.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1480" title="EEVblog 1480 EXTENDED VERSION - LightYear Zero + Aptera Solar EV">1480</a></sup> Working backwards from claimed solar range gains — 20 km added in two hours on a car needing 10.5 kWh per 100 km — gives 2.1 kWh into the pack, 1.05 kWh per hour, and over 5 m² of panel, 210 W/m².<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1480" title="EEVblog 1480 EXTENDED VERSION - LightYear Zero + Aptera Solar EV">1480</a></sup>

Pack architecture in a production EV is many modest cells in a long series string: 50 Ah cells, about 82 of them, totalling roughly a 320 V pack.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-179" title="Mitsubishi iMiEV Electric Car Test Drive - EEVblog #179">179</a></sup> Mounting the whole pack under the floor puts the centre of gravity very low, so a narrow car handles like a much wider one.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-179" title="Mitsubishi iMiEV Electric Car Test Drive - EEVblog #179">179</a></sup> Larger-format cells attack the mass problem from the other direction — for the same kilowatt-hour pack size, fewer and larger cells mean far less steel casing and therefore less weight, along with fewer cells to handle on the production line.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1340" title="EEVblog 1340 - New Tesla 4680 Battery Cell EXPLAINED">1340</a></sup> Market pressure runs toward larger packs regardless, because range anxiety is real for new owners and manufacturers build what buyers want.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1584" title="EEVblog 1584 - Wireless Freakin&#x27; EV Charging Roads! A $250M Boondoggle">1584</a></sup>

At grid scale the modular pack is also the fire compartment: in a large battery installation a fire that starts in one cell engulfs the pack containing it, and spreads from there unit to unit, with toxic smoke as a primary hazard.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1411" title="EEVblog 1411 - Tesla Victoria Big Battery FIRE!">1411</a></sup> Residential storage is sold in the same modular way — nominal 5.1 kWh packs at around A$2,900 street price each, with a 15 kWh installation expandable to six packs, so capacity is purely a spending decision.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-vNKcRs3zDBI" title="We&#x27;re Gonna Need a Bigger Solar Battery...">•</a></sup>

## Miscellaneous applications

Packs turn up wherever mains cannot go. Autonomous ocean-bottom seismic recorders carry a huge pack to run a data logger, hydrophone and tilt sensor for the duration of a deployment on the sea floor.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-61" title="EEVblog #61 - Crystal Oscillator Drift">61</a></sup> A CubeSat-class satellite kit ships the pack alongside the solar cells, power management controller and microcomputer as part of a complete kit.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-shSoVLSPbaQ" title="eevBLAB #23 - Interorbital Systems TubeSat vs Australia Post">•</a></sup> A shredding mechanism concealed in a picture frame needed a pack with enough energy in reserve for a couple of test shreds before the real one, because a mechanism you cannot test after final assembly is a reliability gamble.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1131" title="EEVBlog #1131 - £1M Prank - Banksy Artwork Shredded! HOW?">1131</a></sup> In benchtop projects the pack is often two 18650 cells on a connector, with a switching pre-regulator taking the place of a heatsink.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-258" title="EEVblog #258 - PSU Housing Design - Part 11">258</a></sup> Custom high-capacity pack assembly is a trade in its own right, extending to home-storage-sized packs of 15 kWh and above.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-UJ6JG4eV0nY" title="BM036 Clamp Meter Magnetisation Test">•</a></sup>
