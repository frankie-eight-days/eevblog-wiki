Parametric search is the practice of finding a component by filtering a database on its specifications rather than by knowing its part number in advance — "searching for a component based on parameters, hence the name, parametric search".<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1180" title="EEVblog #1180 - Component Parametric Search Tutorial">1180</a></sup> It is the standard tool for turning a list of design requirements into a shortlist of orderable parts, and it is the term the industry uses for the activity.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1180" title="EEVblog #1180 - Component Parametric Search Tutorial">1180</a></sup> It is not a minor clerical step in design work: the search, the resulting [[bill-of-materials]], and the cost optimisation that follows can consume 80 to 90 per cent of a design engineer's time.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-87" title="EEVblog #87 - Let&#x27;s hop on the Electronics Design Merry-Go-Round">87</a></sup> Every component class has parameters attached to it, from a [[microcontroller]] down to a humble [[resistor]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1180" title="EEVblog #1180 - Component Parametric Search Tutorial">1180</a></sup>

## The basic workflow

A search begins from a category and narrows by filters, with the part count acting as the running feedback signal. A distributor's microcontroller category may hold 32,651 devices, an analog-to-digital converter category nearly 13,000, a discrete BJT category around 22,500, and a toggle switch category 37,208 — none of which can be evaluated by hand.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-248" title="EEVblog #248 - LCD Enabled Microcontroller Selection">248</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-232" title="EEVblog #232 - Lab Power Supply Design Part 5">232</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1157" title="EEVblog #1157 - Transistor Zener Clamp Circuit">1157</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-k1C0qz8fR_U" title="A Totally Pointless Video About Toggle Switches (Haefely ESD Tester)">•</a></sup> Applying requirements collapses these quickly: restricting an ADC search to 12-bit resolution leaves 4,434 parts, and adding a through-hole-only constraint for a kit product brings it to 439.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-232" title="EEVblog #232 - Lab Power Supply Design Part 5">232</a></sup> A microcontroller search constrained to between 24 and 40 programmable I/O pins falls from 19,000 parts to 5,600.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-87" title="EEVblog #87 - Let&#x27;s hop on the Electronics Design Merry-Go-Round">87</a></sup> Once the count is manageable, the list is sorted — most often by price, which is a very common design requirement, and by stock availability.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1180" title="EEVblog #1180 - Component Parametric Search Tutorial">1180</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-232" title="EEVblog #232 - Lab Power Supply Design Part 5">232</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-248" title="EEVblog #248 - LCD Enabled Microcontroller Selection">248</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-87" title="EEVblog #87 - Let&#x27;s hop on the Electronics Design Merry-Go-Round">87</a></sup>

Availability filters are applied early, since a part that cannot be bought is not a candidate.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1180" title="EEVblog #1180 - Component Parametric Search Tutorial">1180</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1387" title="EEVblog 1387 - MOSFET Repair Replacement Search">1387</a></sup> Searching out-of-stock parts is still occasionally worthwhile when the goal is to identify equivalents rather than to place an order, or when a part is needed badly enough to chase an obscure supplier.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1387" title="EEVblog 1387 - MOSFET Repair Replacement Search">1387</a></sup> Some filters are not worth the effort: restricting results to parts that have a [[datasheet]] excludes almost nothing, and RoHS status generally resolves itself unless the requirement is genuinely strict.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1180" title="EEVblog #1180 - Component Parametric Search Tutorial">1180</a></sup>

## Distributors versus manufacturers

Broad-line distributors — Digi-Key, Mouser, Element 14, RS Components, Arrow — and aggregators such as Octopart and FindChips all offer parametric search, and their defining advantage is that they search across manufacturers at once.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1180" title="EEVblog #1180 - Component Parametric Search Tutorial">1180</a></sup> That matters when the manufacturer has not yet been chosen.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-139" title="EEVblog #139 - Let&#x27;s Select a DC-DC Boost Converter">139</a></sup> It also makes them a discovery mechanism: a search for an unfamiliar function returns the list of firms that make it, including ones the designer has never heard of.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1180" title="EEVblog #1180 - Component Parametric Search Tutorial">1180</a></sup>

Manufacturer sites usually offer deeper parameter sets on their own catalogue, and typically better than what a distributor exposes for the same part family.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-87" title="EEVblog #87 - Let&#x27;s hop on the Electronics Design Merry-Go-Round">87</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1180" title="EEVblog #1180 - Component Parametric Search Tutorial">1180</a></sup> When a requirement is a specific on-chip peripheral — a particular timer configuration, for instance — the manufacturer's own search is generally the only place it can be expressed.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1538" title="EEVblog 1538 - NEW PROJECT Part 2 - Microcontroller Selection">1538</a></sup> Manufacturer sites also sometimes carry commercial data a distributor lacks, such as 5,000-quantity pricing.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1180" title="EEVblog #1180 - Component Parametric Search Tutorial">1180</a></sup> The trade-off is that they cover one vendor only, and the sites themselves vary in quality and are not always easy to navigate.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-87" title="EEVblog #87 - Let&#x27;s hop on the Electronics Design Merry-Go-Round">87</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1180" title="EEVblog #1180 - Component Parametric Search Tutorial">1180</a></sup>

The quality gap runs both ways and depends on the part type: one supplier's search may lack exactly the parameter needed while another's has it, so working across several sites is normal.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1180" title="EEVblog #1180 - Component Parametric Search Tutorial">1180</a></sup> Some vendor interfaces are genuinely well built — a checkbox for single-cell alkaline or nickel-metal-hydride input that immediately refreshes the results table, or a slider on an input-[[voltage]] minimum that reports the surviving part count as it moves, narrowing 87 candidates to 31 at 1.2 V and under.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-139" title="EEVblog #139 - Let&#x27;s Select a DC-DC Boost Converter">139</a></sup> Others are close to useless for the job, offering nothing beyond through-hole versus SMD and package type.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-139" title="EEVblog #139 - Let&#x27;s Select a DC-DC Boost Converter">139</a></sup>

Regional catalogues extend the reach. LCSC is valuable for Asian manufacturer names and for functional search, though its parametric search operates within a single manufacturer's range rather than across manufacturers.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-kU7zSSuy9WQ" title="How to ID a Mystery Microcontroller">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1541" title="EEVblog1541 - What is this Blown SMD Component? Redux">1541</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1180" title="EEVblog #1180 - Component Parametric Search Tutorial">1180</a></sup> Where price is the overriding constraint, a Chinese-sourced site and an unfamiliar brand may be an acceptable outcome.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1180" title="EEVblog #1180 - Component Parametric Search Tutorial">1180</a></sup>

## The data is not trustworthy

Parametric databases are populated by people and are frequently wrong or incomplete, which is the single largest practical hazard of the method. A distributor may fail to capture a peripheral correctly in its feature column, so devices that meet the requirement are silently absent from the result set.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-248" title="EEVblog #248 - LCD Enabled Microcontroller Selection">248</a></sup> A known-good part found by direct part-number lookup can be missing from the parametric results for the same category — a wide-body package device that appeared on one site's product page did not appear in its parametric search at all.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-V0RWwSw96Sw" title="Jackery Battery Bank FAIL - Part 2">•</a></sup> A microcontroller with 256 K of flash and the required I/O failed to surface in one search and turned up only on the manufacturer's own site, apparently because of an error in the parameters.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1180" title="EEVblog #1180 - Component Parametric Search Tutorial">1180</a></sup> Entries can also be flatly incorrect: a jelly-bean surface-mount [[transistor]] whose emitter-base voltage is 5 V was listed with the wrong figure, which raises the possibility that a large fraction of that column is unreliable.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1157" title="EEVblog #1157 - Transistor Zener Clamp Circuit">1157</a></sup> Sorting on a display-controller field returns parts on the first page that do not actually satisfy it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1055" title="EEVblog #1055 - How to Design a Custom LCD- µSupply Part 16">1055</a></sup>

The working rule that follows is not to trust a single website, and to return to the manufacturer's own parametric search and [[current]] datasheet whenever a parameter is critical — including for negative results such as a part being marked discontinued.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1180" title="EEVblog #1180 - Component Parametric Search Tutorial">1180</a></sup>

## Parameters that cannot be searched

Some requirements have no column anywhere. Emitter-base breakdown voltage is a headline parameter almost nowhere; Octopart exposes it under additional filters, where parts rated well above the usual 5 V or 6 V can be located.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1157" title="EEVblog #1157 - Transistor Zener Clamp Circuit">1157</a></sup> [[mosfet]] gate-threshold voltage is listed only as a maximum, never as a typical, which makes it hard to select a device on threshold using parametric search alone.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-260" title="EEVblog #260 - Tracking Pre-Regulator Simulation in LTspice - PSU Part 13">260</a></sup> Integrated ESD protection on a MOSFET has no filter on the major distributors — one lists brand, channel type, transistor configuration and elements per chip and nothing resembling a special-features field, and another offers only trade names — leaving keyword search or reading through candidates as the fallback.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1461" title="EEVblog 1461 - The MOSFET Search CHALLENGE">1461</a></sup> Behavioural detail is entirely absent: nothing in a switching-regulator table records the internal reference voltage or the feedback formula, so identifying an unmarked part from its resistor network requires manual datasheet work after the parametric stage narrows the field.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1475" title="EEVblog 1475 - What&#x27;s This SMD Part?">1475</a></sup> No known search spans manufacturers at the level of an individual on-chip peripheral such as a timer.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1538" title="EEVblog 1538 - NEW PROJECT Part 2 - Microcontroller Selection">1538</a></sup>

Search-engine behaviour differs too. Partial part-number matching works on some sites without any wildcard and fails on others unless an asterisk is supplied, which can make a part look unavailable when it is in stock.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1387" title="EEVblog 1387 - MOSFET Repair Replacement Search">1387</a></sup>

## Choosing the right category

A common failure is searching correctly in the wrong place. Categories carry fine subdivisions — a [[voltage-regulator]] and a regulator controller are separate things — and a search run in the neighbouring category returns nothing useful while appearing to work.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1180" title="EEVblog #1180 - Component Parametric Search Tutorial">1180</a></sup> The requirement is terminology: knowing what the part is actually called in the catalogue's taxonomy.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1180" title="EEVblog #1180 - Component Parametric Search Tutorial">1180</a></sup> A related trap is a chip that spans two categories, such as a dual-mode converter, which may only be findable through the manufacturer's own product categories rather than a distributor's parametric table.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1115" title="EEVblog #1115 - Traps In Chips - And the 7660">1115</a></sup>

## Applications beyond ICs

The method applies to anything with a catalogue. It has been used to select a double-pole three-throw [[pcb]]-mount slide switch, of which few were actually available;<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-72" title="EEVblog #72 - Let&#x27;s Design a Product">72</a></sup> to shortlist momentary toggle switches as a replacement for an unobtainable rotary encoder;<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-k1C0qz8fR_U" title="A Totally Pointless Video About Toggle Switches (Haefely ESD Tester)">•</a></sup> to pick a seven-segment [[lcd]] by filtering the digit-count column to between three and four-and-a-half digits, then sorting by price;<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-248" title="EEVblog #248 - LCD Enabled Microcontroller Selection">248</a></sup> to find a custom LCD with at least 215 segments;<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1055" title="EEVblog #1055 - How to Design a Custom LCD- µSupply Part 16">1055</a></sup> and to narrow unknown [[battery]]-protection ICs by their unusual six-pin package.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-RasOXxxEhCk" title="Jackery Battery Bank FAIL Investigation - Part 1">•</a></sup> It is also the entry point for repair work, where a blown MOSFET is replaced by parametric-matching voltage, current, channel type and package against in-stock local inventory.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1387" title="EEVblog 1387 - MOSFET Repair Replacement Search">1387</a></sup>

Enclosures are the notable weak case. Enclosure manufacturers largely lack usable parametric search on their own sites, so the practice there is to work through a handful of familiar suppliers by hand, supplemented by whatever filtering the broad-line distributors provide.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src---oKMQITODc" title="eevBLAB #29 - How To Search For An Enclosure">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1537" title="EEVblog 1537 - NEW PROJECT Enclosure Search FAIL">1537</a></sup>

## Why it repays the time

Beyond fulfilling a specification, the process is a way to learn the component landscape: it surfaces obscure parts and capabilities a designer did not know existed, occasionally suggesting new product features rather than merely satisfying existing ones.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1180" title="EEVblog #1180 - Component Parametric Search Tutorial">1180</a></sup> Familiarity with the available parts is the accumulated residue of years of doing it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1180" title="EEVblog #1180 - Component Parametric Search Tutorial">1180</a></sup> The corresponding judgment is that spending extra hours, or an extra day, on the right part is worthwhile, since abandoning a search too early can mean designing in a part several times more expensive than one that exists.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1180" title="EEVblog #1180 - Component Parametric Search Tutorial">1180</a></sup>

The cost is that the search never truly terminates. A single part choice feeds back into package, price, availability and performance trade-offs across the rest of the design, and each of those reopens the others — "It's just one big merry-go-round, and once you hop on, it can be difficult to get off."<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-87" title="EEVblog #87 - Let&#x27;s hop on the Electronics Design Merry-Go-Round">87</a></sup>
