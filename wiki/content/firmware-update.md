A firmware update replaces the embedded software stored in a product's non-volatile memory, and for modern test and measurement equipment it is the primary mechanism by which defects are corrected, features are added, and hardware capabilities are unlocked after sale.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-178" title="EEVblog #178 - Agilent&#x27;s U1272A Response">178</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-699" title="EEVblog #699 - Rigol DS1054Z Oscilloscope Jitter Fix Testing">699</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-829" title="EEVblog #829 - Siglent SDM3055 Bench Multimeter Teardown">829</a></sup> Because contemporary instruments implement triggering, decoding, and even sample processing in processors and [[fpga|FPGAs]], a single update package may rewrite the instrument's entire logic configuration, not just its application code.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-PcxEO3fA_Ls" title="Tektronix 2 Series Discussion with Andy Tedd from Tektronix">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-369" title="EEVblog #369 - Rigol DS2000 Oscilloscope Playing Around">369</a></sup> The quality and longevity of a manufacturer's [[firmware]] support is therefore part of what a buyer pays for in a premium instrument.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1220" title="EEVblog #1220 - Siglent SDS5000X 1GHz Oscilloscope Review">1220</a></sup>

## Update mechanisms

Removable SD and microSD media are a common delivery path. The [[121gw|121GW]] [[multimeter]] updates from a `.bin` file copied to the root directory of its [[sd-card|SD card]]; holding the HOLD and MEM keys while powering on, then pressing SETUP, starts the download.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-96XE5Ye1q6I" title="121GW EEVblog Multmeter Firmware Update">•</a></sup> A Siglent [[signal-generator|signal generator]] was re-initialised from a FAT32-formatted microSD card with vendor-supplied files after early pre-release firmware caused a failure.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-tM2j9zuBiRQ" title="Siglent Signal Generator Fix">•</a></sup> The Chronos high-speed camera carries a microSD slot expressly for firmware updates, and storing the entire operating system on an SD card — as in one Uni-T [[arbitrary-waveform-generator|arbitrary waveform generator]] — makes both development and field updating straightforward.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-947" title="EEVblog #947 - Chronos High Speed Camera Review">947</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1679" title="EEVblog 1679 - Why Does a Uni-T AWG Cost $5800? - BOM Cost Analysis">1679</a></sup>

[[usb|USB]] flash drives dominate on oscilloscopes. The Rohde & Schwarz procedure is regarded as exemplary: the firmware file is downloadable from the website without registration, is placed on a USB stick, and the instrument displays the before and after versions.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-6XpyOGw6RFM" title="Rohde &amp; Schwarz RTB2000 Scope Pulse Response">•</a></sup> Rigol scopes take a `.GEL` file from a stick and prompt before installing.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-699" title="EEVblog #699 - Rigol DS1054Z Oscilloscope Jitter Fix Testing">699</a></sup> Simpler still, some instruments enumerate as a mass-storage drive: the Miniware DT71 LCR tweezers accept a dropped hex file, rename it automatically, and update on the next power cycle without any prompt.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1335" title="EEVblog #1335 - Miniware $70 DT71 LCR Tweezer REVIEW">1335</a></sup>

PC-side utilities range from small dedicated updaters that display old and new versions before proceeding, as with the RØDE AI-1 interface, to full device managers such as RØDE Central and update wizards that detect outdated firmware automatically, as with the Digilent OpenScope MZ.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1405" title="EEVblog 1405 - Focusrite Scarlett Solo vs Rode AI-1 TEARDOWN">1405</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-f7jPJek3XuQ" title="New Rode Podmic USB Review">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1056" title="EEVblog #1056 - Digilent Open Scope MZ Review">1056</a></sup> Network-based updating appears in [[oscilloscope]] web interfaces, in AV receivers — one Yamaha unit jumped from version 1.09 to 2.59 over USB or network — and in the web dashboards of open-source miners.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-YkgCcYG29nk" title="Siglent SDS1000X HD Display Update Rate + Web Interface">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1151" title="EEVblog #1151 - Dumpster Dive Yamaha Receiver">1151</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-4ANGcEJbrTE" title="$260 NerdQaxe++ Open Source Solo Bitcoin Miner REVIEW">•</a></sup> Over-the-air updating is used where a radio already exists: the CYBLE-212006-01 [[bluetooth|Bluetooth]] module in the BM787BT multimeter was updated from version 1.03 to 1.04 through a phone app.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-XOuYJ1nxVJQ" title="BM787BT Bluetooth CYBLE-212006-01 Fimware OTA Update">•</a></sup> At the lowest level, in-circuit programmers remain the fallback, with devices such as a networked radiation monitor requiring an AVR ISP and an individually programmed hex file per unit.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-MWHamD1Hj58" title="Adventures in AVR ISP Programming">•</a></sup>

## Robustness, bricking, and side effects

A well-designed bootloader survives a failed update. The 121GW's bootloader could not be broken even by pulling the SD card mid-download, a design decision described as the "best decision we ever made" because it freed users from needing an in-circuit programming header and a purchased programmer.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-121" title="EEVblog 121GW Multimeter Kickstarter Live Chat">121</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-e9cpKN69Avk" title="121GW Development History">•</a></sup> Similarly, an interrupted update on the µSupply can simply be run again from the start.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1264" title="EEVblog #1264 - uSupply Software Development Setup">1264</a></sup>

Where the bootloader is not robust, an update can brick the device. A Zoyi ZT-703S oscilloscope-multimeter reported "Upgrade failed" during a USB drag-and-drop update and was left unable even to power off.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1597" title="EEVblog 1597 - Zoyi ZT-703S $80 2CH 50MHz Oscilloscope/Multimeter Review">1597</a></sup> A MakerBot printer was bricked during a firmware update, and recovery was frustrated when the published hex file would not load into AVR Studio.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-500" title="EEVblog #500 - Live Show">500</a></sup> The Haasoscope Pro documentation explicitly warns against unplugging the unit while its flash is being erased and written.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1709" title="EEVblog 1709 - Haasoscope Pro USB Oscilloscope: An Oscilloscope Oddity">1709</a></sup> Devices built around FTDI USB interface chips inherit a particular wariness from the episode in which a vendor driver update disabled counterfeit chips.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-692" title="EEVblog #692 - Digilent Analog Discovery Review">692</a></sup>

Updates can have side effects beyond the intended fix. Reflashing a Siglent generator resets its power-on startup counter, which also makes the instrument appear factory-new.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-tM2j9zuBiRQ" title="Siglent Signal Generator Fix">•</a></sup> The BM786 multimeter requires no recalibration after a firmware update, because [[calibration]] is tied to the sigma-delta converter inside the [[microcontroller]]; replacing the chip itself, by contrast, can shift calibration.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-0Oo2J66FLZA" title="BM786 Firmware Update Test">•</a></sup> The Rohde & Schwarz MXO4 retains the user's complete setup across an update, booting back into the exact prior configuration.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1529" title="EEVblog 1529 - R&amp;S MXO4 12bit Oscilloscope Unboxing and Experiments">1529</a></sup>

## Bug fixes and feature additions

Firmware updates are the standard remedy for instrument defects found after release. Agilent investigated a reported U1272A multimeter fault, confirmed it was a firmware issue, and published a downloadable fix within three weeks, additionally supplying free USB cables so owners could apply the update.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-178" title="EEVblog #178 - Agilent&#x27;s U1272A Response">178</a></sup> Rigol corrected a jitter problem in the DS1054Z by changing PLL coefficients in firmware version 4.02.04.07, having acknowledged the cause.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-699" title="EEVblog #699 - Rigol DS1054Z Oscilloscope Jitter Fix Testing">699</a></sup> Keysight added a [[capacitance]] function to its bench multimeter purely through a firmware update.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-829" title="EEVblog #829 - Siglent SDM3055 Bench Multimeter Teardown">829</a></sup> The BM787BT's 1.04 module firmware fixed a long-term logging bug that cut off notifications.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-XOuYJ1nxVJQ" title="BM787BT Bluetooth CYBLE-212006-01 Fimware OTA Update">•</a></sup>

Updates also resolve system-level mismatches. A home [[battery]] installation with modules of mixed firmware generations had one module repeatedly shutting itself down; updating all modules to a common version restored normal operation with no hardware damage.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-aANZNwmnC_4" title="AERL Lithium Home Storage Battery - Cloud Data">•</a></sup> Some product categories depend on updates by nature: banknote validators must receive new firmware to accept newly issued note series.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-525" title="EEVblog #525 - Bank Note Acceptor Teardown">525</a></sup> Consumer devices show the same pattern, with [[power-consumption|power consumption]] on the Nokia E71 improving across successive firmware revisions.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-80" title="EEVblog #80 - Nokia E71 + Garmin Mobile XT = Embedded Hell">80</a></sup>

## Version and toolchain compatibility

Firmware versioning can create chicken-and-egg dependencies. The Aorus B450M motherboard supports Ryzen 5000-series processors only from firmware version 60 onward, but applying that update requires an older working [[processor]] already installed — a board with the old firmware cannot boot the new CPU to perform its own update.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-MiqxUaWkGYY" title="AMD Ryzen 5900X Upgrade S**T SHOW">•</a></sup> The general practice when upgrading a PC processor is therefore to update the BIOS to the latest version first, since socket compatibility alone does not guarantee processor support.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1280" title="EEVblog #1280 - Dumpster Lab PC Upgrade">1280</a></sup> A similar circularity afflicted the PICkit 3 under MPLAB X: the environment wanted to download new firmware to the programmer before it could talk to it, and the resolution was to run the older PICkit 3.1 standalone programmer software first.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-841" title="EEVblog #841 - Microchip MPLAB X PICkit 3 Woes">841</a></sup>

## Updatability as a design consideration

Many instruments are deliberately not field-updatable. Brymen multimeters historically used one-time-programmable microcontrollers, making firmware updates impossible; the BM780 series moved to a reprogrammable part with an internal programming port, but access to that port is not given to the public — only the Bluetooth module firmware is user-updatable.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-0Oo2J66FLZA" title="BM786 Firmware Update Test">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-XOuYJ1nxVJQ" title="BM787BT Bluetooth CYBLE-212006-01 Fimware OTA Update">•</a></sup> Low-cost meters frequently ship with undisclosed chipsets and no update path at all, so the buyer gets whatever firmware shipped.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1731" title="EEVblog 1731 - $50 Ziboo 17B Pro Multimeter vs $150 Fluke REVIEW">1731</a></sup> Some updatable instruments restrict the function: the Agilent 34461A requires a secure code to apply firmware.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-489" title="EEVblog #489 - Agilent 34461A Multimeter Review">489</a></sup> The 121GW is one of the few multimeters on the market offering genuine user firmware updates.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-XOuYJ1nxVJQ" title="BM787BT Bluetooth CYBLE-212006-01 Fimware OTA Update">•</a></sup> Older equipment may simply be abandoned — a 2014 Tagarno microscope has no path to the version 5.8 firmware released for later models.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-JJsKYNh2H0A" title="The Tagarno is Looking DODGY (ATEM Flicker Fault)">•</a></sup>

Firmware is also a product-tiering mechanism. The Rigol DS1052E and the 100 MHz DS1102E were the same hardware distinguished by a model string, so a firmware change could have closed the upgrade loophole — though a hardware modification tying a control line would remain as a fallback unless the board itself changed.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-70" title="EEVblog #70 - Turn your Rigol DS1052E Oscilloscope into a 100MHz DS1102E (Hack)">70</a></sup> Trial option licenses on the Rigol DS2000 behave differently from purchased licenses, with only the latter reporting an "official version".<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-369" title="EEVblog #369 - Rigol DS2000 Oscilloscope Playing Around">369</a></sup>

Long-term update support differentiates manufacturers. Rohde & Schwarz released a feature-adding version 3 firmware for the RTB2004 seven years after its release, backward compatible from the renamed RTB 2 series.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-hv8ptePTbAc" title="R&amp;S RTB2004 Firmware Update">•</a></sup> Siglent has continued supplying new firmware for the discontinued SDS2000 series as a matter of stated service policy.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-826" title="EEVblog #826 - Siglent CEO Eric Qin Visits The EEVblog Lab">826</a></sup> By contrast, no firmware at all had been published for the Rigol DHO4000 and DHO1000 nine months after release.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-r_BYYgCqScE" title="Rigol DHO800 HDMI output &amp; Touch Screen">•</a></sup> Within a large vendor, consolidating per-product update methods into a single common mechanism is itself an engineering goal, since maintaining several different update paths multiplies the testing burden and degrades quality.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1032" title="EEVblog #1032 Part 1 - John Kenny Keysight Interview">1032</a></sup> Forward-looking designs provision for updates: one isolated oscilloscope design left spare FPGA gates specifically so features could be added later [[via]] USB at no extra cost.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1119" title="EEVBlog #1119 - Designing a 1kV Isolated Oscilloscope">1119</a></sup>

## Forced updates, remote updates, and right to repair

Updates can be made a condition of use. A discarded Sonos speaker refused to function at all until its firmware was updated, and the update itself demanded creation of an account with an email address and password.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1514" title="EEVblog 1514 - Sonos: The Juicero of Wifi Speakers? (TEARDOWN)">1514</a></sup> Manufacturers have also ended products through software, either by bricking devices with an update or by discontinuing support so the device disables itself.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1383" title="EEVblog 1383 - Human Battery Energy Harvesting BUSTED">1383</a></sup> Enphase [[microinverter]] warranty coverage is reportedly conditional on keeping the system permanently connected to the internet so it can receive automatic firmware updates, and the vendor can push firmware remotely through back-end access to diagnose faults.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-isnXYy9vCag" title="Enphase Microinverter FAIL?">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1626" title="EEVblog1626 - YOU HAD ONE JOB! (Solar Power Install FAIL)">1626</a></sup> A new solar installation required a firmware update on commissioning day, followed by a 24-hour wait before the owner was granted portal access.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1385" title="EEVblog 1385 - 8kW Home Solar Power System EXPANSION!">1385</a></sup>

The policy dimension has reached formal inquiry. The Australian Productivity Commission's right-to-repair inquiry sought evidence on whether manufacturers should be mandated to provide software updates for a reasonable period after purchase, weighing the cost of maintaining updates for discontinued products against the cost of products rendered unrepairable.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-xiXucGYuQ6M" title="Australian Right to Repair Inquiry with Productivity Commissioner Paul Lindwall">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-qDWLTSfrq_E" title="2021 Australia Right to Repair Summit - Session 2 of 4">•</a></sup>
