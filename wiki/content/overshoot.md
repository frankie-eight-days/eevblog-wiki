Overshoot is the excursion of a signal beyond its intended final value before it settles, and its mirror image, undershoot, is the excursion below.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-859" title="EEVblog #859 - Bypass / Decoupling Capacitor Tutorial">859</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-304" title="EEVblog #304 - Mailbag">304</a></sup> It matters because the excursion is real energy delivered to whatever is connected: a supply set to 3.3 V that overshoots to 4 V on switch-on can damage the board it is powering, and a logic edge that overshoots into an indeterminate region can be latched as several clock pulses instead of one.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-315" title="EEVblog #315 - Korad KA3005P Review/FAIL">315</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1365" title="EEVblog #1365 - Viewer PCB &amp; Circuit Design Review">1365</a></sup> Overshoot appears wherever a system with [[energy-storage|energy storage]] is driven faster than it can settle — [[power-supply|power supply]] control loops, amplifier compensation, transmission lines, thermal controllers — and in nearly every one of those domains its presence or absence is a headline acceptance criterion.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-224" title="EEVblog #224 - Lab Power Supply Design - Part 3">224</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-629" title="EEVblog #629 - How To Design a Microphone Preamplifier">629</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-487" title="EEVblog #487 - Tekway DST1102B Oscilloscope Review">487</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-VVOi2dbtxC0" title="EEVacademy #6 - PID Controllers Explained">•</a></sup>

## Physical causes

In a digital power distribution network the culprit is [[inductance]]. An ideal output would switch from 0 V to 5 V with no [[ringing]], overshoot or undershoot; the real [[waveform]] rings because of inductance in the supply trace, which cannot be eliminated by local bypassing alone since the [[capacitor]] cannot sit exactly on the pins, and because of further inductance inside the package in the bonding wires to the die.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-859" title="EEVblog #859 - Bypass / Decoupling Capacitor Tutorial">859</a></sup>

On fast edges the mechanism is transmission-line reflection. Reflections from an unterminated or badly matched line produce overshoot and undershoot that arrive back in time to corrupt the following symbol, which is the intersymbol interference component of an eye diagram budget.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1247" title="EEVblog #1247 - DDR Memory PCB Propagation Delay &amp; Layout">1247</a></sup> The same non-perfect [[transmission-line|transmission line]] shows up directly as level problems at a [[usb|USB]] 3.0 receiver.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-340" title="EEVblog #340 - USB 3.0 Eye Diagram Measurement">340</a></sup> A pulse generator with a 350 ps edge must be plugged straight into the scope with no lead at all, because any lead has inductance and even the [[pcb|PCB]] traces, laid out poorly, degrade the [[rise-time|rise time]] and add overshoot and undershoot.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-304" title="EEVblog #304 - Mailbag">304</a></sup>

Capacitive loading is the third mechanism. Stray [[capacitance]] alone — the wire and the circuit board, with nothing deliberately added — is enough to produce visible overshoot and undershoot on an amplifier output.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-n7XX75S98e8" title="µCurrent Opamp Divider Input Bypass Testing">•</a></sup> Adding load capacitance makes the response progressively worse until the gain peaks and the output overshoots severely.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-l2LBkXxN81Y" title="µCurrent Offset Voltage Fix">•</a></sup>

Probing adds overshoot that is not in the circuit. A channel run without a [[ground]] connection shows conspicuous overshoot and undershoot on fast transitions, degrading [[signal-integrity|signal integrity]] even though the underlying event is still visible for timing purposes.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1362" title="EEVblog #1362 - A Very Skookum Mailbag">1362</a></sup> Between two active probes on the same signal, the one with the longer ground lead shows extra undershoot and a slower recovery while the other shows extra overshoot.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1368" title="EEVblog #1368 - Active Oscilloscope Probes COMPARED (Part 2)">1368</a></sup> [[ac-coupling|AC coupling]] itself generates an apparent overshoot at the start of a captured burst that has nothing to do with the signal under test.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-762" title="EEVblog #762 - How Secure Are Electronic Safe Locks?">762</a></sup>

## Bench power supply switch-on

Switch-on behaviour is among the most important criteria for a lab bench supply: the output must ramp cleanly to the set [[voltage]] and stay there, not spike above it and then settle back.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-224" title="EEVblog #224 - Lab Power Supply Design - Part 3">224</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-8" title="EEVblog #8 Part 2 of 2 - Review of the BK Precision 1697 Programmable Lab Power Supply">8</a></sup> The test is a single-shot scope capture of the output as the supply is switched on, repeated at several set voltages and with the load in constant-[[current]] mode at various currents, since the behaviour differs between them.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-439" title="EEVblog #439 - Atten PPS3205T-3S Triple Output Power Supply Review">439</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-315" title="EEVblog #315 - Korad KA3005P Review/FAIL">315</a></sup> A supply that does not overshoot is safe to put on a board that cannot tolerate more than its rail; one that does is not.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1691" title="EEVblog 1691 - Uni-T UDP6731 360W Bench PSU REVIEW">1691</a></sup>

Well-behaved units ramp almost linearly and then clamp into voltage regulation with no overshoot at all — observed with a linear ramp under full load and again with no load, with a switch-on of roughly 300 ms, with about 50 ms from 0 to 5 V and identically from 0 to 45 V, and with about 2 ms of ramp into a 2 Ω load at 3.3 V with a 2 A limit.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-439" title="EEVblog #439 - Atten PPS3205T-3S Triple Output Power Supply Review">439</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1691" title="EEVblog 1691 - Uni-T UDP6731 360W Bench PSU REVIEW">1691</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1265" title="EEVblog #1265 - $53 360W Lab Bench PSU!">1265</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1402" title="EEVblog 1402 - Rohde &amp; Schwarz NGA100 PSU Teardown + GIVEAWAY">1402</a></sup> Small artefacts short of true overshoot are routinely tolerated: a knee or step partway up the ramp, a brief shelf, or a barely visible excursion on exit from constant-current mode described as "nothing to write home to your mom about".<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-224" title="EEVblog #224 - Lab Power Supply Design - Part 3">224</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-315" title="EEVblog #315 - Korad KA3005P Review/FAIL">315</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1265" title="EEVblog #1265 - $53 360W Lab Bench PSU!">1265</a></sup>

Failures are judged harshly and by repetition. A supply set to 3.3 V that ramps up to 4 V is unfit for powering a large 3.3 V board, and the finding is confirmed by re-triggering to establish that the behaviour is repeatable rather than a one-off; changing the constant-current load to 1 A produced a different but still unclean switch-on from the same unit.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-315" title="EEVblog #315 - Korad KA3005P Review/FAIL">315</a></sup> Switch-on testing is also applied to DC-DC converters at full load, where a 5 V nominal output was seen reaching just over 6 V and taking around 50 ms to come back down.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-324" title="EEVblog #324 - DC-DC Converter Testing - USB Supply Part 4">324</a></sup>

## Loop compensation and stability

In a compensated amplifier, overshoot is the deliberate design target rather than something to eliminate. The intended step response is a slight taper — driven to the point of a small amount of overshoot, which indicates the circuit is stable while remaining fast; a fully critically damped response is not aimed for, and a response tuned any further risks turning into an [[oscillator]] once production variations are accounted for.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-629" title="EEVblog #629 - How To Design a Microphone Preamplifier">629</a></sup>

The complementary use of overshoot is as a stability stress test. Deliberately loading an op-amp output with worst-case capacitance to force severe overshoot, and confirming that the circuit still does not oscillate under that condition, is a sound way to qualify a compensation fix short of a full network-analyser sweep.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-l2LBkXxN81Y" title="µCurrent Offset Voltage Fix">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-n7XX75S98e8" title="µCurrent Opamp Divider Input Bypass Testing">•</a></sup> [[output-capacitance|Output capacitance]] also damps transient overshoot in a [[current-source|current source]]: with the 470 µF [[output-capacitor|output capacitor]] removed the circuit remained stable but produced a large amount of overshoot on transients.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-577" title="EEVblog #577 - Precision 1A Current Source Part 2">577</a></sup>

Overshoot on the output of a peak detector appears when the input [[frequency]] rises and the discharge load is too large for the circuit to track.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-490" title="EEVblog #490 - Peak Detector Circuit">490</a></sup>

## PID control

In a [[temperature-control|temperature control]] loop, overshoot is the excursion past the set point that follows an over-large accumulated control effort, and its principal cause is integral windup — the build-up of accumulated error area behind the current reading.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-VVOi2dbtxC0" title="EEVacademy #6 - PID Controllers Explained">•</a></sup> Where the integral component dominates, integral windup is the likely source of the observed overshoot, and the standard countermeasure is to clamp the integral term to a fixed maximum value.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-VVOi2dbtxC0" title="EEVacademy #6 - PID Controllers Explained">•</a></sup>

Tuning trades the three terms against each other. Increasing the integral component removes steady-state error but substantially increases overshoot, as does increasing the proportional component; the derivative component, which resists change, then reduces the resulting oscillations and overshoot because both are forms of change.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-VVOi2dbtxC0" title="EEVacademy #6 - PID Controllers Explained">•</a></sup> Starting from Ziegler-Nichols constants, reducing the integral component kills the [[oscillation]], and taking the average of the pre- and post-reduction constants recovers response time at the cost of a little overshoot, which the derivative term is then tuned to handle.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-VVOi2dbtxC0" title="EEVacademy #6 - PID Controllers Explained">•</a></sup> The same criterion applies to [[soldering]] station [[firmware]]: a station whose tip temperature does not overshoot has an adequate PID loop regardless of other complaints about it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1106" title="EEVblog #1106 - Pace ADS200 Soldering Station Review (JBC Killer?)">1106</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1110" title="EEVblog #1110 - Mailbag">1110</a></sup>

## Overshoot as an instrument specification

Oscilloscopes expose overshoot as an automatic measurement, generally split four ways by edge and by side of the edge: overshoot after the rising edge, preshoot before the rising edge, and the falling-edge equivalents, sometimes under opaque abbreviations such as FOV, FPRE and ROV.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-797" title="EEVblog #797 - Siglent SDS1000X Oscilloscope Review">797</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1220" title="EEVblog #1220 - Siglent SDS5000X 1GHz Oscilloscope Review">1220</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-474" title="EEVblog #474 - GW Instek GDS-2000A Series Oscilloscope Unboxing &amp; First Impression">474</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1501" title="EEVblog 1501 - Rigol HDO4000 Low Noise 12bit Oscilloscope Unboxing &amp; First Impression">1501</a></sup>

Instruments are also judged on the overshoot they add to a clean input. A 100 MHz scope was distinctly ringy on a 700 ps pulse with visible overshoot and undershoot where a competing 100 MHz model was not, and a 500 MHz instrument on the same pulse showed overshoot and undershoot attributable to its greater [[bandwidth]] rather than to a fault.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-487" title="EEVblog #487 - Tekway DST1102B Oscilloscope Review">487</a></sup> Aggressive sin(x)/x interpolation is a known source of artificial overshoot and undershoot on reconstructed edges; an instrument without it shows little of either.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1231" title="EEVblog #1231- New Uni-T UPO3000 Oscilloscope Teardown">1231</a></sup> A pocket instrument that produced gross overshoot and undershoot on a 1 MHz [[square-wave|square wave]], with no compensation adjustment available on the instrument or the probe, was useless for high-frequency detail.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-359" title="EEVblog #359 - QDSO Pocket Oscilloscope Review">359</a></sup> Pulse response also depends on the source and on the vertical scale in use, so the same generator into different instruments yields differing degrees of overshoot and ringing.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-6XpyOGw6RFM" title="Rohde &amp; Schwarz RTB2000 Scope Pulse Response">•</a></sup>

Digital multimeters are subjected to a simple overshoot test: with all meters reading a steady voltage of about 3 V, the source is switched off and back on and each display is watched for an excursion past the final reading.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-99" title="EEVblog #99 - $100 Multimeter Shootout - Extech Amprobe BK Precision Ideal UEi Uni-T PART 2of2">99</a></sup> Meters that merely ramp up slowly without overshooting pass.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-99" title="EEVblog #99 - $100 Multimeter Shootout - Extech Amprobe BK Precision Ideal UEi Uni-T PART 2of2">99</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-60" title="EEVblog #60 - Fluke 117 Multimeter Review and Teardown">60</a></sup> Overshoot on the display is treated as a minor defect rather than a disqualifier when the rest of the meter is good.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-91" title="EEVblog #91 - $50 Multimeter Shootout - Extech EX330, Amprobe AM220, Elenco, Vichy VC99, GS Pro-50">91</a></sup>

Probes carry an overshoot budget of their own. Passive probe design is largely a matter of getting the physical properties of the cable and its lossy transmission-line parameters matched to the compensation network so that no overshoot or undershoot appears at the top of the band.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-453" title="EEVblog #453 - Mysteries of x1 Oscilloscope Probes Revealed">453</a></sup> In a probe design tool optimising step response for a 1.4 m cable of 140 pF, 22 Ω was the optimum series value, with any departure from it changing the overshoot in one direction or the other.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-341" title="EEVblog #341 - Mailbag">341</a></sup> Plain unterminated [[coaxial-cable|coax]] used as a ×1 probe shows overshoot and ringing.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-453" title="EEVblog #453 - Mysteries of x1 Oscilloscope Probes Revealed">453</a></sup> Overshoot is also a [[datasheet]] line item for amplifiers, and its removal from the specification table of a revised NE5532 datasheet is a loss of characterisation data.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1752" title="EEVblog 1752 - Texas Instruments SCREWED UP the NE5532!">1752</a></sup>

## Measurement consequences

Overshoot corrupts derived measurements. Rise-time-to-bandwidth calculations using a 0.4 factor depend on the waveform having as little overshoot as possible, on a sufficiently extended pulse, and on knowing which formula and factor the particular instrument implements.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-311" title="EEVblog #311 - Jim Williams Pulser Followup">311</a></sup> A pulse source producing overshoot followed by ringing compromises the rise-time figure it is meant to establish.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-316" title="EEVblog #316 - More PFANG, More 13GHz Scope, &amp; More Pulser">316</a></sup> In true-RMS measurement, overshoots and spikes raise the crest factor of the waveform, which is why converters intended for such signals provide both a main averaging capacitor and an additional higher-frequency one.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-972" title="EEVblog #972 - Operating Chips Outside Their Spec">972</a></sup>

Not all overshoot warrants attention. Overshoot on a digital bus line being probed for protocol content can be ignored, since the interest is in the data rather than the edge quality.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1522" title="EEVblog 1522 - BM786 Multimeter Repair PART 2">1522</a></sup> Overshoot introduced by the horizontal position control of a scope's user interface is an instrument annoyance rather than a signal property.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-771" title="EEVblog #771 - Electronic Safe Lock Powerline Attack Part 2">771</a></sup> Overshoot and undershoot on top of DC characteristics are simply part of the dynamic behaviour a [[pcb-layout|PCB layout]] has to accommodate, alongside transient response to large load steps.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1216" title="EEVblog #1216 - PCB Layout + FPGA Deep Dive">1216</a></sup>
