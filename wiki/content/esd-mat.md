An ESD mat is a bench-surface covering that provides a static-dissipative working surface for electronics assembly and repair, and doubles as the primary work surface of the bench itself.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-228" title="EEVblog #228 - New Lab Progress...">228</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-679" title="EEVblog #679 - How To Build A Small Electronics Work Bench">679</a></sup> It is the blue rubber benchtop material visible across most EEVblog bench work.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-228" title="EEVblog #228 - New Lab Progress...">228</a></sup> The critical property is that a dissipative surface does not build up a charge, and that dissipative is not the same as conductive.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-228" title="EEVblog #228 - New Lab Progress...">228</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-ddQF9U5CRNA" title="New Grey ESD Mat &amp; Camera Exposure">•</a></sup>

## Construction and layers

A typical rubber mat is a laminate: a static-dissipative top surface with a conductive layer beneath it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-679" title="EEVblog #679 - How To Build A Small Electronics Work Bench">679</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-ddQF9U5CRNA" title="New Grey ESD Mat &amp; Camera Exposure">•</a></sup> The distinction matters — the top is dissipative, the bottom conductive, and the mat as a whole is not a conductor.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-228" title="EEVblog #228 - New Lab Progress...">228</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-679" title="EEVblog #679 - How To Build A Small Electronics Work Bench">679</a></sup> Variants exist: an RS Components mat is static dissipative on both outer faces with a conductive inner layer, whereas an older mat is conductive on the bottom face directly.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-ddQF9U5CRNA" title="New Grey ESD Mat &amp; Camera Exposure">•</a></sup> The two faces of a laminated mat are not interchangeable; the underside is visibly shinier and a different surface, so a faded mat cannot simply be flipped to recover a fresh top.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-ddQF9U5CRNA" title="New Grey ESD Mat &amp; Camera Exposure">•</a></sup>

Mats ship with press studs — commonly four, one per corner — for snapping on earth leads and wrist straps.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-ddQF9U5CRNA" title="New Grey ESD Mat &amp; Camera Exposure">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-679" title="EEVblog #679 - How To Build A Small Electronics Work Bench">679</a></sup> A complete bench setup runs an ESD binding point down to [[mains-earth|mains earth]], then out to the mat and to a wrist strap.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-51" title="EEVblog #51 - A tour of the EEVblog Electronics Lab">51</a></sup> The path to earth is deliberately resistive rather than a short: a wrist strap reaches mains earth through a couple of megohms, and the mat spreads [[resistance]] across its area, so touching an earthed mat pulls a charged body low but not to zero.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1567" title="EEVblog 1567 - Wireless ESD Monitor? - The IONA StatIQ Band TESTED">1567</a></sup>

Rubber is the material to buy; PVC mats are not worth having.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-679" title="EEVblog #679 - How To Build A Small Electronics Work Bench">679</a></sup> The rubber type is tear-resistant, heat-resistant, [[solder]]-proof, and dissipative all at once.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-679" title="EEVblog #679 - How To Build A Small Electronics Work Bench">679</a></sup>

## Sizes and cost

Mats are sold by the roll and cut to bench depth. Standard depths are 600 mm and 900 mm, with anything else effectively a custom order.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1391" title="EEVblog 1391 - Mailbag">1391</a></sup> A 10 m roll cost around 300 Australian dollars at the time of one bench build, and 300 to 400 dollars at a later one.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-679" title="EEVblog #679 - How To Build A Small Electronics Work Bench">679</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-ti8mbPIAvbI" title="New EEVblog Lab Tour (Incomplete Edition)">•</a></sup> Off-cuts are worth keeping: a strip can be fitted with studs and straps to make a portable fieldwork surface, or laid over the main mat as a sacrificial second layer for heavy-duty work.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-679" title="EEVblog #679 - How To Build A Small Electronics Work Bench">679</a></sup>

## Ageing and UV degradation

The dominant failure mode in practice is not wear but colour change. Blue rubber matting installed in a lit lab shifts over a couple of years to a greenish cast, attributed to UV from the bench lighting.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-oILC5TsG2_k" title="ESD Work Bench Matting Ageing">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-ti8mbPIAvbI" title="New EEVblog Lab Tour (Incomplete Edition)">•</a></sup> The change is in the material, not on it: the discolouration cannot be removed with Windex or other cleaning chemicals, and the affected area is the exposed area — anything left standing on the mat for years, such as a microscope base, leaves behind a patch of the original colour when moved.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-oILC5TsG2_k" title="ESD Work Bench Matting Ageing">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-ti8mbPIAvbI" title="New EEVblog Lab Tour (Incomplete Edition)">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-ddQF9U5CRNA" title="New Grey ESD Mat &amp; Camera Exposure">•</a></sup> High-quality matting is not immune; the effect appears across mats generally, with some examples worse than others.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-oILC5TsG2_k" title="ESD Work Bench Matting Ageing">•</a></sup> Given that the degradation is driven by ambient light, repurchasing the same product is poor value for a lit lab.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-ti8mbPIAvbI" title="New EEVblog Lab Tour (Incomplete Edition)">•</a></sup>

Mat colour also interacts with photography. Camera exposure metering is calibrated to an 18% grey card, so a grey mat rather than a blue one under an inspection microscope removes the exposure problem outright.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-ddQF9U5CRNA" title="New Grey ESD Mat &amp; Camera Exposure">•</a></sup> Colour varies between suppliers even within nominally the same product — a traditional deep blue against a much lighter blue from a later order.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-585" title="EEVblog #585 - Lab Bench ESD Matting Upgrade + Tagarno HD Microscope">585</a></sup> The mat's large flat expanse has also been considered as a seamless photographic backdrop, curved up the wall behind a bench, though bench geometry made the camera placement impractical.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-640" title="EEVblog #640 - How To Build An Electronics Work Bench">640</a></sup>

## Grounding, floating mats and 50 Hz pickup

An ungrounded mat is not neutral: left floating, a large conductive-backed sheet under the bench becomes an efficient capacitive coupler and an effective 50 Hz [[antenna]]. Sensitive unshielded circuitry sitting on it picks up mains hum that grows as the circuit is brought closer to the mat surface, and connecting the mat to earth removes the pickup almost entirely.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-e4wvxWWMla0" title="Rohde &amp; Schwarz HMO1202 - Like a Bought One">•</a></sup> Floating mats have been the root cause of spurious measurement behaviour on the bench before, which makes an unplugged mat earth lead a standard suspect when unexplained mains-[[frequency]] signals appear.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-933" title="EEVblog #933 - Keysight U1272A EMC Issue">933</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-e4wvxWWMla0" title="Rohde &amp; Schwarz HMO1202 - Like a Bought One">•</a></sup> The same coupling shows up incidentally in other work — a [[mains-voltage|mains voltage]] detector stick registers stray 50 Hz from the mat.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-VZuebPVrzI8" title="Fluke 1AC Voltage Detection Stick Repair">•</a></sup>

Because the mat couples to whatever rests on it, isolating a device under test from the mat is a legitimate diagnostic step: mounting sensitive input circuitry on a wooden frame so nothing touches the mat isolates its contribution.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-660" title="EEVblog #660 - Electrocardiogram (ECG) Experiments">660</a></sup> Conversely, the mat serves as a defined reference surface for electrostatic measurement, with a surface DC voltmeter's [[ground]] taken to the mat and high-[[voltage]] supply references returned to it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-768" title="EEVblog #768 - Cordless Anti-Static Wristbands BUSTED!">768</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-247" title="EEVblog #247 - Anti Static Bag Myth Revisted">247</a></sup>

[[grounding|Grounding]] discipline is easy to let slip. A microscope-supplied mat may carry a proper ESD stud and still sit unconnected in normal use.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1361" title="EEVblog #1361 - Dodgy Tactile Switch TEARDOWN">1361</a></sup> Equipment such as hot air rework stations ships with a grounding point and strap intended to be run over to the mat and thence to mains earth.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1058" title="EEVblog #1058 - Quick 861DW Hot Air Rework Station Review">1058</a></sup>

## In use

The mat is the default surface for whatever happens on the bench, which means it also becomes an uncontrolled variable in ad hoc experiments — dropping cells onto rubber matting to compare their rebound is surface-dependent and not a controlled test.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-508" title="EEVblog #508 - Can You Test Battery Charge By Dropping It?">508</a></sup> It is soft enough to run wires underneath and bring them up through a drilled hole to a [[breadboard]] beneath.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-161" title="EEVblog #161 - 555 April Fools Revealed">161</a></sup> Rubber matting sits at the boundary of what counts as a hard surface for small mechanical toys.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-541" title="EEVblog #541 - Mailbag">541</a></sup>

Nice matting belongs on the list of lab equipment worth funding ahead of over-specified test gear.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-ln_XJDPKJlc" title="eevBLAB 103 - Is an $800 Oscilloscope OVERKILL?">•</a></sup> Australian supply has come through Oratec, an importer rather than the manufacturer, which also carries ESD trays and related consumables.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-oILC5TsG2_k" title="ESD Work Bench Matting Ageing">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1468" title="EEVblog 1468 - Electronex Show Tour 2022">1468</a></sup>
