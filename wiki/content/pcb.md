A printed circuit board is the substrate that mounts and interconnects the components of an electronic system, and it remains unavoidable because chips have to be mounted and connected somehow: even a fully integrated system still needs a [[bypass-capacitor|bypass capacitor]], a regulator and a way to bring signals out.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-500" title="EEVblog #500 - Live Show">500</a></sup> It is not a passive backdrop to the circuit but a component in its own right, with a dielectric constant, distributed [[inductance]] and [[capacitance]] that matter once frequencies rise.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-343" title="EEVblog #343 - Spectrum Analyser Tracking Generator Tutorial">343</a></sup> It is also a mechanical part — a stiffener, a [[heat-sink|heat sink]], a front panel, a [[battery]] holder — and much of the design effort on a real product goes into those roles as much as into routing.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-11" title="EEVblog #11 Part 2 of 2 - More on DIY product design">11</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-191" title="EEVblog #191 - Mouse Trap Triggering">191</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-644" title="EEVblog #644 - How To Design Front Panels On Extruded Enclosures - µSupply Part 14">644</a></sup>

## Material, thickness and rigidity

The standard laminate is FR4 fiberglass, which is more than adequate as a dielectric at frequencies in the tens of megahertz.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-343" title="EEVblog #343 - Spectrum Analyser Tracking Generator Tutorial">343</a></sup> Higher-temperature grades such as FR5 exist and tolerate rework better.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-167" title="EEVblog #167 - Atten 858D Hot Air Rework Review">167</a></sup> The default thickness is 1.6 mm; 0.8 mm is available where height is critical but costs more and is noticeably less rigid.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-291" title="EEVblog #291 - USB Power Supply Part 2 - The Housing">291</a></sup> The difference is obvious in the hand — a large 0.8 mm board can be flexed easily, where a 1.6 mm board of the same size cannot.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1245" title="EEVblog #1245 - A Most Excellent REPAIR: IBM PCjr Keyboard">1245</a></sup> Thinner boards go down to 0.5 mm and below, thin enough that a key can still be pressed through a 0.5 mm keypad overlay.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-11" title="EEVblog #11 Part 2 of 2 - More on DIY product design">11</a></sup> A miniature calculator design used two 0.5 mm boards sandwiched with a third forming the outer shell, all glued together.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-872" title="EEVblog #872 - Mailbag">872</a></sup>

Thickness is sometimes chosen for structure rather than electrical reasons. A 4.2 mm board in high-power equipment carries [[current]] in multiple internal layers of 2 oz copper or heavier, but the thickness itself is there for rigidity.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1289" title="EEVblog #1289 - Mystery Huawei Teardown">1289</a></sup> At the extreme, an 8 mm, 24-layer board is achievable.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-532" title="EEVblog #532 - Silicon Chip Wafer Fab Mailbag">532</a></sup> A 1.6 mm core happens to match the 1.6 mm thickness of a CR2016 cell, which allows coin cells to be wedged and held purely by friction between two boards, dispensing with a battery holder entirely.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-131" title="EEVblog #131 - Live Show #7">131</a></sup>

## Copper, traces and vias

Trace current capacity is set by copper weight and geometry, and where a single trace has to carry far more than the copper allows, the practical answer is often to bypass the board: run a thick wire soldered on at assembly, which can carry tens of amps and be snaked around components on a dense layout.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1559" title="EEVblog 1559 - PCB Design: Trace Current Rating">1559</a></sup> A production variant of the same idea uses large jumper links across the board instead of specifying something like 12 oz copper.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1610" title="EEVblog 1610 - Deye 5kW Hybrid Solar Inverter">1610</a></sup>

Vias and pads are constructed identically on a bare board and differ only in how they are used.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-543" title="EEVBlog #543 - PCB VIA Current Investigation">543</a></sup> Their thermal performance is poor enough to require deliberate design: a 1 mm hole in a 1.6 mm double-sided board with 1 oz copper and 1 oz (35 micron) plating comes out at roughly 50 °C per watt, so a single [[via]] is nowhere near sufficient to move heat to the other side.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-744" title="EEVblog #744 - SMD Thermal Heatsink Design - µSupply Part 15">744</a></sup> Castellated edge connections, used to butt modules together, are designed simply as ordinary pads placed on the board outline — there is no special castellated-hole component.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1681" title="EEVblog 1681 - Mailbag: Nice DIY CC Load,  Amazing Proto Boards, Ian Johnston">1681</a></sup>

Copper features also serve non-electrical ends. Capacitive touch keys can be etched directly as pad pairs on the board, with the finger acting as the dielectric, although on a two-layer board the routing around them needs care and the tracks may be visible through the [[solder-mask|solder mask]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-97" title="EEVblog #97 - Live Show #2">97</a></sup> Reverse-mount, bottom-emitter LEDs shine through a slot or hole cut in the board, which can be cheaper than buying assembled seven-segment displays when individual LEDs cost around a cent in reel quantities.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-298" title="EEVblog #298 - Dave&#x27;s Decade Digit Display - USB Supply Part 3">298</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1292" title="EEVblog #1292 - $2 Meter vs JVA Electric Fence Controller!">1292</a></sup> Board area itself provides creepage distance, and a generously sized board may need no slots or cutouts at all to meet clearance requirements.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1292" title="EEVblog #1292 - $2 Meter vs JVA Electric Fence Controller!">1292</a></sup>

## Board versus breadboard

Prototyping on a [[breadboard]] is limited to roughly a few megahertz because of the high stray capacitance and inductance and the long leads.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-500" title="EEVblog #500 - Live Show">500</a></sup> Circuits with fast switching edges and large switching loops, such as a switching supply, are unsuitable for breadboarding and belong on a board; a 555 or a [[microcontroller]] doing slow I/O gains nothing from being moved.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-500" title="EEVblog #500 - Live Show">500</a></sup> Component packaging pushes the same way — surface-mount-only parts make a breadboard useless, and going straight to a board is often the faster path.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-97" title="EEVblog #97 - Live Show #2">97</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-259" title="EEVblog #259 - PSU Rev C Schematic - Part 12">259</a></sup> A circuit that oscillates on a breadboard will behave better on a board because the loops are tighter, but the reverse does not hold: a design that is wildly unstable when lashed up is a warning, not a breadboard artifact.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-567" title="EEVblog #567 - Precision 1A Current Source">567</a></sup>

## From schematic to board

Errors in the [[schematic]] propagate: an uncaught shorted output or wrong net flows through to the netlist, into the routed board, and is discovered weeks or months later in a built prototype, which is why [[electrical-rules-checking|electrical rules checking]] before the board is generated matters at least as much as [[design-rule-checking|design rule checking]] on the board itself.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-953" title="EEVblog #953 - What Is Schematic ERC?">953</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-253" title="EEVblog #253 - KiCAD Install &amp; Schematic - First Impressions">253</a></sup> Some designers deliberately keep the schematic arrangement matching the physical layout — components in the same orientation, chip pins in physical order — so that [[troubleshooting]] maps directly onto the board.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1129" title="EEVblog #1129 - Creating a Nice Readable Schematic">1129</a></sup>

Layout is a large, slow job. A board with several thousand-pin BGAs is an eight- to ten-layer design that can take a month or two to lay out and is expensive enough to manufacture and assemble that a formal design review before release is warranted.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1193" title="EEVblog #1193 - KiCAD PCB 4 Layer Swapping &amp; Stackup">1193</a></sup> Enclosure and system decisions constrain the result heavily: fixed [[connector]] positions, heat sink placement and case mounting posts drive board outline and mounting hole positions, where a board floating in the middle of a case with flying leads would have been a simple square.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-244" title="EEVblog #244 - How To Lay Out A PCB - PSU Design Part 9">244</a></sup> Odd outlines also panelise badly, reducing the number of boards per panel.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-3_CQKSC1JNE" title="Davinci Resolve Speed Editor TEARDOWN">•</a></sup>

Quality of layout is visible on the finished article. Right-angle traces mark a board that was not laid out by an experienced designer.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-956" title="EEVblog #956 - Countersurveillance Monitor Teardown">956</a></sup> Correct [[solder-mask-expansion|solder mask expansion]], with mask between fine-pitch pins, is what makes surface-mount parts hand-solderable with a basic chisel tip.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-186" title="EEVblog #186 - Soldering Tutorial Part 3 - Surface Mount">186</a></sup> Mask can also be abused: covering pads with [[silkscreen]] so that pins are physically unable to connect is a decision taken at the layout stage, and has been used to make a device appear connected when it is not.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1181" title="EEVblog #1181 - Car ECO OBD2 OptiFuel Fuel Saver SCAM!">1181</a></sup>

## The board as a thermal and mechanical element

Copper area is a usable heat sink. A [[resistor]] mounted with short leads dumps heat into the pads and copper, flattening its derating curve, though lead [[thermal-resistance|thermal resistance]] sets a limit to how much this helps.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-191" title="EEVblog #191 - Mouse Trap Triggering">191</a></sup> A TO-220 tab soldered to a [[ground-plane|ground plane]] uses the board the same way, and [[soldering]] it requires a much larger tip to overcome that [[thermal-mass|thermal mass]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-183" title="EEVblog #183 - Soldering Tutorial Part 2">183</a></sup> The corresponding hazard is expansion mismatch: ceramic hybrid modules have a far lower [[temperature-coefficient|temperature coefficient]] than FR4, so a rigid ceramic part mounted to a board can crack its [[solder]] joints unless compliant leads absorb the movement.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-564" title="EEVblog #564 - Tektronix TDS3054 Oscilloscope Teardown Repair">564</a></sup>

Mechanically, the board is frequently the chassis. Front panels cut from board material can be ordered to exact size with routed, rounded edges and a chosen mask colour, which is why front panel PCBs are a standing preference in Dave Jones's own designs over machined or engraved alternatives.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-644" title="EEVblog #644 - How To Design Front Panels On Extruded Enclosures - µSupply Part 14">644</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-400" title="EEVblog #400 - 400th Live Show">400</a></sup> The same logic produced a watch built with no case at all, using PCB turrets soldered to the board to anchor the watch band and a 0.5 mm board as the keypad overlay.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-11" title="EEVblog #11 Part 2 of 2 - More on DIY product design">11</a></sup> Enclosure interaction runs the other way as well: standoffs moulded into the case remove mounting hardware from the board,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1155" title="EEVblog #1155 - Mailbag">1155</a></sup> support posts sandwich a board to suppress vibration modes during a drop,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-171" title="EEVblog #171 - Agilent U1272A Multimeter Teardown">171</a></sup> boards may slide into notches rather than lifting out,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-478" title="EEVblog #478 - Keithley 2015 THD Multimeter Teardown">478</a></sup> and compressing rubber against the board edge provides the water seal in a sealed strip light.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1010" title="EEVblog #1010 - Zyntony Ra LED Strap Light Teardown">1010</a></sup> At the low-cost end, wedging the board between two plastic halves eliminates the screw-driving operation altogether.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-595" title="EEVblog #595 - World&#x27;s Worst Shittiest Camcorder: Teardown">595</a></sup>

Partitioning follows assembly practicality. A single board running the full width of a large console — 800 to 900 mm — is awkward to assemble, so such systems break into modular strips.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-840" title="EEVblog #840 - Yamaha M3000 Mixing Console Teardown">840</a></sup> A [[power-supply|power supply]] may use one large analog board while keeping the front panel separate because it is a different technology entirely, with higher layer count for the digital parts and [[galvanic-isolation|galvanic isolation]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1402" title="EEVblog 1402 - Rohde &amp; Schwarz NGA100 PSU Teardown + GIVEAWAY">1402</a></sup> Splitting a connector onto its own small board can be justified by mechanical assembly order, but adds cable, connectors and bill-of-materials cost.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-ag-MjKAfATw" title="Rode AI-1 USB Audio Interface Teardown">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-3_CQKSC1JNE" title="Davinci Resolve Speed Editor TEARDOWN">•</a></sup>

## Vibration

Because a populated board is a stiff plate carrying mass, it has vibrational modes of its own, and a large board can be seen to flex under excitation.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1442" title="EEVblog 1442 - DON&#x27;T DO THIS! (PCB Vibration Testing)">1442</a></sup> The standard instrumentation is a small [[accelerometer]] superglued to the board, often several at different locations and orientations to capture different axes, with a second channel on the shaker plate for reference.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1504" title="EEVblog 1504 - The COOL thing you MISSED at Tesla AI Day 2022">1504</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1442" title="EEVblog 1442 - DON&#x27;T DO THIS! (PCB Vibration Testing)">1442</a></sup> Uncalibrated accelerometers still reveal where a product has problems, even though quantitative work against a military standard requires [[calibration]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1442" title="EEVblog 1442 - DON&#x27;T DO THIS! (PCB Vibration Testing)">1442</a></sup> The board's own components can act as sensors: multilayer ceramic capacitors are piezoelectric, so a vibrating board generates a [[voltage]] across them, and in reverse an audio-[[frequency]] drive makes them emit a tone that the board amplifies.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1743" title="EEVblog 1743 - Mechanical Vibration Detection with your Oscilloscope Probe">1743</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-855" title="EEVblog #855 - Ceramic Capacitor Piezoelectric Effect">855</a></sup> The effect is strong enough that a shorted times-ten [[oscilloscope-probe|oscilloscope probe]] pressed against a board picks up its vibration, correctly reporting an 11.1 kHz excitation.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1743" title="EEVblog 1743 - Mechanical Vibration Detection with your Oscilloscope Probe">1743</a></sup> Mechanical force applied to a board in normal use — pressing a rotary selector switch, for instance — is transmitted into the laminate and can expose a cracked component or [[solder-joint|solder joint]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-9u8TEJdAVdQ" title="Returned Faulty BM235 Switch Investigation">•</a></sup>

## Failure and damage

Boards fail in characteristic ways. Conductive fluid on the surface causes arcing that etches into the laminate and self-perpetuates, eventually chewing entire sections away; damage is far worse at the 120 V end of a board than at the 12 V end.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-354" title="EEVblog #354 - CityRail PA Amp Teardown">354</a></sup> Thermal design errors that pass bench testing show up as field failures after tens of hours, with the board otherwise looking sound.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-880" title="EEVblog #880 - Mailbag">880</a></sup> Gross overload chars the laminate to the point that surface-mount chips rotate off their pads.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1457" title="EEVblog1457 - Old School Mailbag - ESC Burnout">1457</a></sup> A board inserted the wrong way into its plastic housing, applying mains to the output, produces heavy charring.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-362" title="EEVblog #362 - LED Tube Lighting Install &amp; Theory">362</a></sup> Battery [[electrolyte]] travels along the board and can reach the opposite side through vias, and its residue raises [[impedance]] enough to kill [[lcd|LCD]] contrast while the board itself still functions.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-VZuebPVrzI8" title="Fluke 1AC Voltage Detection Stick Repair">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1670" title="EEVblog 1670 - BM786 Multimeter BATTERY LEAKAGE REPAIR + GIVEAWAY">1670</a></sup> Potting compound that turns out to be hygroscopic becomes conductive on the board and shorts the components it was meant to protect.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1073" title="EEVblog #1073 - MORE KRK Rokit Black Gunk Speaker Repair">1073</a></sup> Ageing laminate loses pad adhesion — a 35-year-old board can lift a pad at ordinary soldering temperatures.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-804" title="EEVblog #804 - HP1740A Oscilloscope Repair - Part 2">804</a></sup> Preventive measures include [[conformal-coating|conformal coating]] to stop moisture spreading and creepage across the surface.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1126" title="EEVblog #1126 - Mystery Teardown!">1126</a></sup>

## Working on an existing board

There is no inherent top or bottom side to a board; the designation is a reference convention.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1110" title="EEVblog #1110 - Mailbag">1110</a></sup> Tracing a two-sided board is normally done by flipping between aligned photographs of both sides, rotated, flipped and cropped so features line up, which makes the mirrored bottom view read as if looking through the board.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1449" title="EEVblog 1449 - What Causes Excess Battery Drain? (BM235)">1449</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1550" title="EEVblog 1550 - Keysight E36731A Battery Emulator Teardown">1550</a></sup> Signals disappearing into vias must be followed to the other side, where they may pass through zero-ohm jumpers before reappearing.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-912" title="EEVblog #912 - BM235 Multimeter Reverse Engineering">912</a></sup> Published schematics are not always trustworthy: a documented pinout can disagree with the silkscreen and the actual board, with [[ground]] on different pins than the schematic claims.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-XUyjRm1Upjs" title="That moment you realise the Schematic &amp; PCB don&#x27;t match">•</a></sup> Continuity beeping while dragging a probe is the fastest way to establish where connections actually go.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1636" title="EEVblog 1636 - Ultimate Multimeter Beginners Guide!">1636</a></sup> Older service documentation solved the same problem with coded test points printed both on the schematic and on the board.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1364" title="EEVblog #1364 - Compaq Portable PSU REPAIR">1364</a></sup>

Handling and viewing tools matter. A spring vice with v-grooves holds boards down to 0.8 mm for placing surface-mount parts.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-775" title="EEVblog #775 - Mailbag">775</a></sup> Reading part markings requires the right light angle, and a couple of degrees of board tilt can make text appear or vanish.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1372" title="EEVblog #1372 - DIY PCB Photography Light Box">1372</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1458" title="EEVblog 1458 - Microscope Polarising MAGIC!">1458</a></sup> Photographing boards is shot at a small aperture so that a board viewed at an angle is in focus front to back.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1373" title="EEVblog #1373 - DIY PCB Photograhy LED Light Box - Part 2">1373</a></sup> Two spare boards of the same thickness placed either side of the work hold it steady under a stencil during paste application.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-415" title="EEVblog #415 - SMD Stencil Reflow Soldering Tutorial">415</a></sup>

## Manufacture and cost

Fabrication starts from artwork sent to a board house.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-351" title="EEVblog #351 - Silicon Chip Magazine - Electronex 2012">351</a></sup> Offshore prototype quantities are cheap enough that shipping can exceed the cost of the boards themselves for a board of roughly 185 by 69 mm.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-990" title="EEVblog #990 - Getting The PCB Manufactured (Nixie PART 5)">990</a></sup> Quality differences between suppliers show up in the silkscreen and in edge finish, with sharper printing and cleaner routed edges distinguishing better processes.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-241" title="EEVblog #241 - Circuit Labs PCBs from New Zealand">241</a></sup> Local bare-board manufacturing has contracted sharply, with only a handful of suppliers remaining in Australia.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-358" title="EEVblog #358 - Electronex 2012">358</a></sup> Boards are frequently reused across product lines to consolidate manufacturing: two [[multimeter]] series share one identical board revision,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1447" title="EEVblog 1447 - EXPENSIVE Fluke vs CHEAPER Brymen - Teardown">1447</a></sup> two [[oscilloscope]] models differ only in the board,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-149" title="EEVblog #149 - Agilent Infiniivision 3000 X Series Oscilloscope Review">149</a></sup> and a twelve-bit scope shares a board with its larger sibling, populated near-identically apart from an extra ADC.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1510" title="EEVblog 1510 - $699 Rigol 12bit HDO1000 Teardown - WOAH!">1510</a></sup> A different board colour within one product is a reliable indication that a module came from a different manufacturer.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-936" title="EEVblog #936 - Mailbag">936</a></sup>

Home fabrication was once routine, using positive pre-sensitized photoresist board and a photocopied transparency, capable of reliable 8/8 double-sided work, and was worth doing when boards were expensive or needed within hours.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1197" title="EEVblog #1197 - DIY PCB Etching With Water">1197</a></sup> Earlier still, hobbyist boards arrived undrilled, and holes were drilled by hand in the hundreds.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-KKEYAdXEW-M" title="Dick Smith Kits">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-747" title="EEVblog #747 - PC Based Logic Analyser Project">747</a></sup>

## Surface finish and colour

A conventional board offers five distinguishable colours without exotic processing: the silkscreen overlay, the solder mask, a plated finish such as gold flash over exposed copper, the bare fiberglass where copper and mask are absent, and a fifth appearance obtained by removing the copper from the back so that light passes differently through the laminate.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-zHpsA1RtQhw" title="How To Get 5 Different &quot;Colours&quot; On A PCB">•</a></sup> Solder mask is available in matte and gloss black, green, red, yellow, purple and pink, and additional silkscreen colours require an extra pass and extra cost per colour.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-zHpsA1RtQhw" title="How To Get 5 Different &quot;Colours&quot; On A PCB">•</a></sup> Gold flash is inexpensive but is thin enough that it reads as a coppery gold rather than true gold.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-zHpsA1RtQhw" title="How To Get 5 Different &quot;Colours&quot; On A PCB">•</a></sup> Boards can be backlit through the fiberglass for decorative effect.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-zHpsA1RtQhw" title="How To Get 5 Different &quot;Colours&quot; On A PCB">•</a></sup> Gloss black is difficult to work with; matte black is the more usable dark finish.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-833" title="EEVblog #833 - Mailbag">833</a></sup> Older boards often omit solder mask entirely, using tin plate with black silkscreen printed directly over the tracks.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1169" title="EEVblog #1169 - TI 1972 Computer Interfacing">1169</a></sup> Single-sided boards sometimes carry a silkscreen on the component side depicting the copper layout underneath, which makes tracing and repair straightforward.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1714" title="EEVblog 1714 - Presonus Eris Speaker REPAIR (Capacitor Pop Noise)">1714</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-502" title="EEVblog #502 - $19 Hameg Analog Oscilloscope">502</a></sup>

## Alternatives and boundaries

Not every assembly uses a printed board. Membrane construction with a chip bonded directly to the film removes the board entirely and is a classic cost reduction, though some low-cost calculators have returned to fiberglass boards.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-633" title="EEVblog #633 - Mailbag">633</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1093" title="EEVblog #1093 - Casio FX260 Solar II Calculator Review">1093</a></sup> Precision instruments sometimes avoid the board deliberately, running point-to-point wiring on insulated standoffs so that only air serves as the dielectric between critical nodes.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-544" title="EEVblog #544 - Fluke 5450A Resistance Calibrator Teardown">544</a></sup> Turret board and point-to-point construction predate printed boards in the same role.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-693" title="EEVblog #693 - AVO Transistor Analyser Teardown">693</a></sup> High-end [[processor]] packaging departs further still: a multi-layer ceramic substrate with molybdenum inner layers, thin-film and thick-film printed layers and tens of thousands of vias is a different technology from an etched copper board, despite superficially resembling a very high layer count one.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1341" title="EEVblog #1341 - AMAZING $250,000 IBM Processor TEARDOWN!">1341</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1380" title="EEVblog 1380 - BBQ IBM Chips">1380</a></sup>

Single-sided construction persists wherever cost dominates, and its presence is a useful signal — a consumer amplifier board that is single-sided is easier to trace and repair precisely because it was built down to a price.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1072" title="EEVblog #1072 - KRK Rokit 6 Studio Monitor Speaker Repair">1072</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1727" title="EEVblog 1727 - REPAIR: Casio CT-X700 Keyboard">1727</a></sup>
