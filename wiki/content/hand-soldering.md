Hand soldering is the manual formation of [[solder]] joints with an iron, as opposed to the automated wave and reflow processes that assemble most production boards.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-256" title="EEVblog #256 - Yamaha PSR-80 Keyboard Teardown">256</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-239" title="EEVblog #239 - PCB Design For Manufacture Part 2">239</a></sup> It remains a core skill even for those who stencil and reflow their own work, because parts eventually need reworking and rework technique differs from first-pass assembly, particularly when removing components.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-186" title="EEVblog #186 - Soldering Tutorial Part 3 - Surface Mount">186</a></sup> In production it is a distinct labour step with its own cost, and in teardowns it is a visible signature that separates machine-assembled boards from human intervention.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-239" title="EEVblog #239 - PCB Design For Manufacture Part 2">239</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-511" title="EEVblog #511 - Rigol DP832 Power Supply Teardown">511</a></sup>

## Technique

[[surface-mount-technology|Surface mount]] is not the obstacle it is often assumed to be: the basic tooling is a chisel-tip iron, fine solder, and a well-designed board with well-designed pads, and useful results follow with little experience.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-186" title="EEVblog #186 - Soldering Tutorial Part 3 - Surface Mount">186</a></sup> SO packages at 1.27 mm [[pin-pitch|pin pitch]] are large enough to solder pin by pin with the iron and fine solder, one pin at a time.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-186" title="EEVblog #186 - Soldering Tutorial Part 3 - Surface Mount">186</a></sup> Larger passives are trivial — 1206 parts are enormous by surface-mount standards and require nothing more than adequate eyesight to solder by hand.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-558" title="EEVblog #558 - Beta Layout DIY SMD Thermal Reflow Oven">558</a></sup> Below that, 0603 begins to get annoying for both hand soldering and hand placement.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-558" title="EEVblog #558 - Beta Layout DIY SMD Thermal Reflow Oven">558</a></sup>

The [[flux]] carried inside cored solder is generally sufficient for placing ordinary components; separate flux is not required for that stage.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1306" title="EEVblog #1306 (2 of 5): PCB SMD Hand Soldering &amp; Assembly">1306</a></sup> Gel flux is also used for hand soldering work.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-433" title="EEVblog #433 - Mailbag">433</a></sup>

Access geometry matters as much as pitch. A part recessed among taller neighbours can force the iron to near-vertical to reach it, which is a layout consequence that should be anticipated at design time rather than discovered at the bench.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1306" title="EEVblog #1306 (2 of 5): PCB SMD Hand Soldering &amp; Assembly">1306</a></sup> A systematic order of attack prevents missed joints: having soldered one end of every part, the other end is known to be on the opposite side, so the board can be worked through in a single direction.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1306" title="EEVblog #1306 (2 of 5): PCB SMD Hand Soldering &amp; Assembly">1306</a></sup> On through-hole boards, components are fitted by height — flat parts such as resistors first, then ICs — so that the board sits level against the bench at each stage instead of rocking while it is being soldered.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-555" title="EEVblog #555 - 555 Timer Kit">555</a></sup>

Too much solder is the characteristic hand-[[soldering]] error, and on fine-pitch parts it bridges pins directly.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1306" title="EEVblog #1306 (2 of 5): PCB SMD Hand Soldering &amp; Assembly">1306</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1353" title="EEVblog #1353 - WHY Are These Pins Shorted?">1353</a></sup>

## Package limits

Some packages are simply outside the reach of an iron. Leadless packages such as LGA have no pads extending down the side of the case, so once the part is laid on the board the pads are not directly accessible to the iron at all; such chips are designed only for reflow.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-181" title="EEVblog #181 - Dead Bug Prototype Soldering">181</a></sup> The workaround is to invert the part and run individual bond wires out to a coarser prototyping footprint that can then be hand soldered.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-181" title="EEVblog #181 - Dead Bug Prototype Soldering">181</a></sup> A 0.5 mm leadless chip proved troublesome enough that the joints had to be reflowed before the circuit worked, raising the question of what would happen to the same part in volume production.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-366" title="EEVblog #366 - USB PSU Troubleshooting">366</a></sup> An 88-lead QFN at 0.4 mm pitch — about as fine as pin pitch gets in the industry — is challenging for anyone to solder by hand, and assumes [[solder-mask|solder mask]] can still be placed between the pads.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-408" title="EEVBlog #408 - Schmart Board 0.4mm QFN SMD Soldering">408</a></sup>

Where miniaturisation is the whole point of a design and the smallest available packages are used to fit the smallest possible board, hand soldering ceases to be a viable option and reflow is mandatory.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-866" title="EEVblog #866 - Mailbag">866</a></sup> Conversely, hand-solderability is a legitimate part-selection criterion alongside [[voltage]] range and accuracy: a three-pin SOT-23 is chosen partly because it is not too small to solder by hand.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-87" title="EEVblog #87 - Let&#x27;s hop on the Electronics Design Merry-Go-Round">87</a></sup> Footprint size and difficulty of soldering are among the parameters weighed when selecting a converter IC.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-139" title="EEVblog #139 - Let&#x27;s Select a DC-DC Boost Converter">139</a></sup>

## Designing for hand soldering

Solder mask between pins is the single most important layout provision. Its absence — for example where mask has been removed as one large square over a fine-pitch footprint — permits solder to bridge between pins during hand, drag, or [[reflow-soldering|reflow soldering]] if too much solder is applied, and the problem intensifies as pitch drops below 0.5 mm.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1353" title="EEVblog #1353 - WHY Are These Pins Shorted?">1353</a></sup> Getting mask between pins both prevents shorting during reflow or hand soldering and allows tighter conductor spacing.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1222" title="EEVblog #1222 - Apple&#x27;s MacBook Design FAIL - Who&#x27;s To Blame?">1222</a></sup> A practical target is a four- to five-thou gap of solder mask between pads; a couple of thou cannot be manufactured reliably.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-244" title="EEVblog #244 - How To Lay Out A PCB - PSU Design Part 9">244</a></sup>

Vias made needlessly small — 0.3 to 0.4 mm — cost extra by forcing a finer drilling process and make it harder to solder wires to the board for hacking and modification.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1034" title="EEVblog #1034 - Mailbag">1034</a></sup> Choosing through-hole switches over surface mount allows a board to be hand-assembled to a professional finish with well-spaced keys, at the cost of losing usable surface area on both sides of the board.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-11" title="EEVblog #11 Part 2 of 2 - More on DIY product design">11</a></sup> For a one-off board that will be hand soldered rather than manufactured, single-sided component loading is unfussy and parts can simply be placed on the top side.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-974" title="EEVblog #974 - PCB Layout Walkthrough - PART 4">974</a></sup>

Hand-soldered through-hole joints also differ thermally from [[wave-soldering|wave soldering]]: hand soldering heats only the individual pins from the underside.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-782" title="EEVblog #782 - The Dangers Of Reflow Soldering">782</a></sup> A joint formed by hand tends to get solder right over the top of the termination rather than only a fillet around the side, which is more robust than the reflowed result.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1137" title="EEVblog #1137 - Mailbag Monday">1137</a></sup> Placing a [[multilayer-ceramic-capacitor|multilayer ceramic capacitor]] directly across two pins that must be hand soldered is poor practice, since thermal stress adds to mechanical torsion as a cause of short-circuit failure.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1036" title="EEVblog #1036 - PSU Fire PCB Repair">1036</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1035" title="EEVblog #1035 - Flaming DIY Power Supply!">1035</a></sup>

## Production economics

Hand soldering is the residual process for everything a machine cannot handle, and every hand-solder step carries cost. Parts supplied loose in bags rather than on reels, in tubes, or in trays must be hand placed and soldered, which drops assembly [[efficiency]], raises the price charged, and lengthens the build.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-127" title="EEVblog #127 - PCB Design For Manufacture Tutorial - Part 1">127</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-239" title="EEVblog #239 - PCB Design For Manufacture Part 2">239</a></sup> Components cut from a reel and delivered in individual bags are good only for hand soldering, requiring an assembler to remove each one and place it with tweezers.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-239" title="EEVblog #239 - PCB Design For Manufacture Part 2">239</a></sup> Through-hole switches, binding posts, and 4 mm banana plugs on an otherwise surface-mount board have to be hand soldered; selective wave soldering is available in principle but unlikely to be worth the effort, so once other parts already require hand work, the switches are done by hand too.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-239" title="EEVblog #239 - PCB Design For Manufacture Part 2">239</a></sup> Even so, the surface-mount conversion can pay for the extra hand-solder labour, with the benefit beginning to appear at perhaps 100 to 200 boards.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-239" title="EEVblog #239 - PCB Design For Manufacture Part 2">239</a></sup>

The corresponding design goal is to eliminate hand-solder steps entirely. Shield cans held down by an accurately dimensioned [[heat-sink|heat sink]] pressing on their tops need no hand soldering at all, and at volumes in the hundreds of thousands every step removed from assembly is money saved.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1717" title="EEVblog 1717 - Rigol&#x27;s INSANE New $999 350MHz Oscilloscope: TEARDOWN">1717</a></sup> A display technology costing 32 cents or less that involves no hand soldering, unlike [[lcd|LCD]] and seven-segment [[led|LED]] alternatives, is placed entirely by the pick-and-place machine and avoids paying for hand assembly labour.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-298" title="EEVblog #298 - Dave&#x27;s Decade Digit Display - USB Supply Part 3">298</a></sup> For a board with roughly 30 parts, hand assembly is quicker than setting up a pick-and-place machine; the machine wins at 500 parts per board or panel, where hand placement risks the [[solder-paste|solder paste]] drying out.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-740" title="EEVblog #740 - LitePlacer Pick &amp; Place Machine Kit Unboxing">740</a></sup> Hand placement of paste-printed parts is acknowledged as inefficient for anything beyond one-off builds.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-562" title="EEVblog #562 - More SMD Oven Reflow">562</a></sup>

Hand soldering also persists as a deliberate industrial process, not merely a fallback: final hand soldering will always exist for components that cannot go through pick-and-place or wave solder, whether because they are physically difficult to access or because they are temperature-sensitive parts such as supercapacitors.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-207" title="Electronex Expo 2011 - EEVblog #207 (1 of 3)">207</a></sup> It is the assembly route for substrates that cannot survive a reflow profile, alongside specialised leaded solders run at lower temperature for shorter duration.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-aLcdm6gB6eE" title="DragonFly $300,000 3D PCB Printer Prototype">•</a></sup>

Hand soldering is also chosen on cost grounds where a [[connector]] would otherwise be needed: soldering ribbon cables directly to a board, possibly with a jig, is cheaper than fitting a connector, and appears in consumer keyboards where every cent counts.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1108" title="EEVblog #1108 - Casio CT-X700 Keyboard Teardown">1108</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-256" title="EEVblog #256 - Yamaha PSR-80 Keyboard Teardown">256</a></sup>

## Joint quality and inspection

High-reliability hand soldering aims for a shiny joint, cleaned with flux remover and inspected for a perfectly mirror finish.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1041" title="EEVblog #1041 - Keysight Calibration Lab Tour">1041</a></sup> That criterion does not transfer to lead-free work, where every joint inspected under the same conditions looks frosted.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1041" title="EEVblog #1041 - Keysight Calibration Lab Tour">1041</a></sup> Poor joints are identifiable by eye: dull, cracked joints on an interface [[pcb|PCB]] warrant a minute of resoldering before any other diagnosis is attempted, and cheap instruments have been found with blow holes in the solder and joints that are dry throughout.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1394" title="EEVblog 1394 - Onkyo Receiver Repair - Part 1">1394</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-6" title="EEVblog #6 - Part 2 of 2 - Why cheap multimeters suck">6</a></sup>

Uncleaned flux residue is the most reliable visual marker of hand work. On otherwise well-executed instruments, residue localises the hand-soldered items exactly — front-panel BNC connectors, board connectors, and power jacks — even where reflow quality elsewhere is very good.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-360" title="EEVblog #360 - Rigol DS2000 Oscilloscope Teardown">360</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-511" title="EEVblog #511 - Rigol DP832 Power Supply Teardown">511</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-521" title="EEVblog #521 - Picoscope 5000 USB Oscilloscope Teardown">521</a></sup> The same signature distinguishes factory hand soldering from later repair: a row of capacitors carrying original wave soldering next to one that has been hand soldered and replaced indicates prior servicing, as do a few pins with flux residue scattered around an otherwise perfect connector.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1203" title="EEVblog #1203 - REPAIR: Tektronix 2465B Oscilloscope">1203</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1237" title="EEVblog #1237 - Old School Teardown: HP3785A Jitter Analyser">1237</a></sup> Where every other joint on a board is wave soldered, isolated hand soldering suggests either a factory touch-up or an attempted repair.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1189" title="EEVblog #1189 - Ebay $140 6.5 digit DMM Bargain!?">1189</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1571" title="EEVblog 1571 - Nobel Prize Winning Mailbag. 121GW, Retro calcs, Assembly, and Discrete Logic">1571</a></sup> Rework caused by parts falling off the pick-and-place heads and being detected at optical inspection is the ordinary explanation for isolated hand-soldered passives on a production board.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1522" title="EEVblog 1522 - BM786 Multimeter Repair PART 2">1522</a></sup>

Hand soldering quality is treated as a diagnostic of overall manufacturing care. Manually folded and hand-soldered contacts in budget multimeters are readily distinguished from automated soldering.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-99" title="EEVblog #99 - $100 Multimeter Shootout - Extech Amprobe BK Precision Ideal UEi Uni-T PART 1of2">99</a></sup> [[transistor|Transistor]]-test sockets in cheap meters are consistently hand soldered and consistently poor.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-91" title="EEVblog #91 - $50 Multimeter Shootout - Extech EX330, Amprobe AM220, Elenco, Vichy VC99, GS Pro-50">91</a></sup> Parts sitting at odd angles with heavy residue point to hand assembly on boards where SO-8 packages were done adequately.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-440" title="EEVblog #440 - Atten PPS3205T-3S Triple Output Power Supply Teardown">440</a></sup> The opposite outcome is equally possible: hand-soldered wiring can be executed beautifully, with each joint formed deliberately.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-330" title="EEVblog #330 - Medela Swing Teardown &amp; Repair">330</a></sup>

## Repair, prototyping, and modification

Hand soldering is the enabling technique for board-level modification. Mod boards are attached as components [[via]] half-moon castellations and hand soldered as a retrofit, though the same board can be machine placed and reflowed if it is designed in from the start.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1158" title="EEVblog #1158 - How To Create PCB Mod Boards">1158</a></sup> Ad-hoc modification wires are hand soldered and look ugly, which is tolerable on prototypes but undesirable in production or as field rework by repair technicians.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1158" title="EEVblog #1158 - How To Create PCB Mod Boards">1158</a></sup>

Instrumentation access frequently depends on it: two wires soldered to transmit and receive pins expose a serial debug interface;<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-977" title="EEVblog #977 - Keysight 1000X Hacking - Part 1">977</a></sup> wires tacked to a point of interest and routed outside the enclosure allow probing after reassembly;<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-855" title="EEVblog #855 - Ceramic Capacitor Piezoelectric Effect">855</a></sup> and wires soldered directly to front-panel traces substitute for spade lugs.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-667" title="EEVblog #667 - Agilent 6643A Power Supply Binding Post Hack">667</a></sup> The counterpart is that hand soldering to a live product is intrusive — reaching a tiny quad flat pack pin or an 0402 component may require removing the board, and risks shorting something out or ruining the board, which is why non-invasive measurement methods are preferred where they suffice.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1324" title="EEVblog #1324 - Oscilloscope Reference Waveforms are USEFUL">1324</a></sup>

Destructive-repair work also relies on it. A moulded connector whose body was damaged can be salvaged by chipping away the plastic, extracting and reinserting each pin individually, and resoldering the assembly, though a dual-row 1 mm pitch part makes this awkward and the pins do not seat until all are aligned.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-794" title="EEVblog #794 - Samsung Dumpster LCD Repair Connector Followup">794</a></sup> When a header is soldered back in, the mating connector's height must be checked first and a spacer used, or the assembly will sit too flush to mate.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-794" title="EEVblog #794 - Samsung Dumpster LCD Repair Connector Followup">794</a></sup>

## Learning and access

Hand soldering has long been taught outside formal engineering education. Retail electronics chains ran extensive in-store soldering setups and advertised teaching customers to solder, an initiative that did not survive past the early 1990s.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-8Oragx4JJrE" title="Memories of Sydney Electronics Stores &amp; Work Experience at Tandy">•</a></sup> Some engineering programmes historically forbade students from soldering at all on burn-risk grounds.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-818" title="EEVblog #818 - Embedded Electronics With Jack Ganssle">818</a></sup> Community workshops fill the gap, teaching soldering to newcomers including young hobbyists building projects of their own.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1759" title="EEVblog 1759 - ACES Hardware Meetup (Startup Hardware is Hard)">1759</a></sup> University electronics labs now include dedicated hand-soldering stations for build projects.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1565" title="EEVblog 1565 - UNSW Electronics Lab &amp; Makerspace Tour">1565</a></sup> Kit documentation that includes assembly instructions covering how to solder for those who have not done it before is regarded as the standard to aim for.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-872" title="EEVblog #872 - Mailbag">872</a></sup> Hand soldering also appears as a timed competition event at industry trade shows.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1686" title="EEVblog 1686 - Electronex 2025 Melbourne - Walkaround">1686</a></sup>

Building a circuit physically and soldering it together is a legitimate way to settle a theoretical question — the [[resistor]] cube problem was solved by measuring matched resistors, soldering them into the network, and verifying the answer directly.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1472" title="EEVblog 1472 - Resistor Cube Problem SOLVED">1472</a></sup>
