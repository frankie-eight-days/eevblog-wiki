A bleeder resistor is a [[resistor]] placed permanently across a [[capacitor]] so that the stored charge drains away once power is removed.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-268" title="EEVblog #268 - Xantrex 300V 4A Power Supply Teardown">268</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1482" title="EEVblog 1482 - Mains Capacitor Zener Regulator Circuit">1482</a></sup> Its purpose is safety and serviceability: a large filter capacitor left with no discharge path can hold hundreds of volts for a long time after the equipment is unplugged, and the energy is sufficient to injure whoever opens the case.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-631" title="EEVblog #631 - How To: Soniq LCD TV Repair Part 2">631</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1388" title="EEVblog 1388 -  Dumpster Diving 4K TV Murphy&#x27;s &quot;Repair&quot;">1388</a></sup> Because the resistor is always connected, it also appears in circuit during [[resistance]] measurements and can make surrounding components look shorted when they are not.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1364" title="EEVblog #1364 - Compaq Portable PSU REPAIR">1364</a></sup>

## What it does and why it is fitted

A well-designed supply puts bleed resistors on the main filter capacitors so the reservoir energy is gone shortly after shutdown.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-725" title="EEVblog #725 - LG Plasma TV Teardown">725</a></sup> The effect can be quick: on one instrument the front-panel [[led|LED]] extinguished only a second or two after the plug was pulled, which is the visible signature of bleeders draining the rails.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-452" title="EEVblog #452 - Stanford Research SR430 Teardown">452</a></sup> On a switchmode supply with a 1 kV cap on the output, a 330 ohm 1 W bleeder drains the output filter capacitors very fast at switch-off, and details of that kind separate the better supplies from the cheap ones.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-272" title="EEVblog #272 - Manson 9400 40A 3-15V Switchmode PSU Teardown">272</a></sup>

The same reasoning applies at the other end of the scale. A 10 kW argon-ion laser supply carries a bleed resistor across its main capacitor bank, so a board that had sat in a dumpster for a decade or more could be handled without concern about residual charge.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1381" title="EEVblog 1381 - Argon Ion Laser 10kW PSU Teardown">1381</a></sup> A 600 V, 5100 W supply that had been powered off for a long period was likewise expected to have bleeders, though the working habit is still to probe the bus bars and confirm both the secondary high-[[voltage]] DC side and the primary side are dead before touching anything.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-814" title="EEVblog #814 - Keysight N8762A 600V 5100W PSU Teardown">814</a></sup>

## Values and typical implementations

Bleeder resistors are high in value relative to the load, since they dissipate power continuously while the equipment runs. Across mains input filter capacitors a 1 meg half-watt axial part is representative.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-268" title="EEVblog #268 - Xantrex 300V 4A Power Supply Teardown">268</a></sup> In an X-class dropper supply, two 750 k resistors in series give 1.5 meg total across a 220 nF cap, which is enough to remove the charge from the pins after the appliance is unplugged.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1481" title="EEVblog 1481 - Dodgy Dangerous Heater REPAIR">1481</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1482" title="EEVblog 1482 - Mains Capacitor Zener Regulator Circuit">1482</a></sup> Where a high-[[voltage-rail|voltage rail]] is built from a series stack of capacitors, resistors across each element serve two functions at once — bleeding and voltage balancing; a source measure unit using four series banks of three paralleled ceramics used 10 meg parts for the job.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-607" title="EEVblog #607 - Agilent B2912A Source Measure Unit SMU Teardown">607</a></sup> The same dual role appears on supplies where the resistors across the two main capacitors provide sharing as well as discharge.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1301" title="EEVblog #1301 - Arcade Machine Repair">1301</a></sup>

Bleeders are also found on smaller and less obvious nodes: across the DC output rail of a bench supply to discharge the [[output-capacitor|output capacitor]],<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-861" title="EEVblog #861 - Rigol DP832 PSU FAIL &amp; REPAIR">861</a></sup> across a mains input filter cap in parallel with the X-class part,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-272" title="EEVblog #272 - Manson 9400 40A 3-15V Switchmode PSU Teardown">272</a></sup> across the MOV in a console supply to remove residual charge when the cord is pulled,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1164" title="EEVblog #1164 - Xbox Engineering Baptism Of Fire">1164</a></sup> and in linear supplies where the resistors sit across the reservoir caps that protrude through the chassis.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-709" title="EEVblog #709 - EDC 4601 AC Voltage Standard Teardown">709</a></sup> A single design may carry several, for instance one large bleeder on the pass-[[transistor]] side and another across the bank of 2,200 µF 63 V filter caps.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-512" title="EEVblog #512 - Rigol DP832 Bad Design Investigation">512</a></sup>

## When they are absent

Omission is common, and it is the case that matters practically. A vintage [[oscilloscope]]'s 5,300 µF capacitor retained charge with the regulator pass transistor switched off simply because no bleeder was fitted.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-804" title="EEVblog #804 - HP1740A Oscilloscope Repair - Part 2">804</a></sup> A 4K television's main filter capacitor was still at 150 V DC with the set unplugged, decaying only through the 4 meg loading of the probe attached to it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1388" title="EEVblog 1388 -  Dumpster Diving 4K TV Murphy&#x27;s &quot;Repair&quot;">1388</a></sup> An [[lcd|LCD]] television's main capacitor held a couple of hundred volts and would take tens of minutes to decay on its own, a case complicated further by dielectric absorption returning charge to a capacitor that has been briefly shorted.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-631" title="EEVblog #631 - How To: Soniq LCD TV Repair Part 2">631</a></sup> Consumer gear built without serviceability in mind frequently has none — a solar monitoring unit's DC input caps sat charged to mains potential and delivered a shock during disassembly.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1511" title="EEVblog 1511 - Solar Analytics System FAIL! (+ Dave gets ZAPPED!)">1511</a></sup> Capacitor-based mains products sold on dubious energy-saving claims are another category where a bleeder is often simply not present.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1191" title="EEVblog #1191 - Digital Energy Savers BUSTED!">1191</a></sup>

Some equipment discharges itself through the rest of the circuit even without a dedicated bleeder; a vintage computer's 35 V rail was found already at zero for that reason, which is fortunate rather than by design.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-905" title="EEVblog #905 - REPAIR: HP85 Vintage Computer">905</a></sup>

## Bench practice

The engineering rule is that the presence of a bleeder is assumed but never trusted: always measure before touching.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-365" title="EEVblog #365 - ESR Meter Bad Cap Monitor Repair">365</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-780" title="EEVblog #780 - Samsung LCD TV Dumpster Dive Teardown">780</a></sup> The low-[[impedance]] (low-Z) voltage function on a [[multimeter]], nominally intended to kill ghost voltages, is well suited to this because it places a couple of kilohms across the capacitor while measuring it, both reading and discharging in one operation.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-631" title="EEVblog #631 - How To: Soniq LCD TV Repair Part 2">631</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-8blgmbXfDEc" title="New Dumpster Diving Monitor Score">•</a></sup> If no voltage is present the meter drops to a megohm resistance reading, confirming the node is safe.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-780" title="EEVblog #780 - Samsung LCD TV Dumpster Dive Teardown">780</a></sup> Where the meter lacks the function, an appropriately calculated high-power dummy resistor across the capacitor serves the same purpose,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-630" title="EEVblog #630 - How To: Soniq LCD TV Troubleshooting Repair - Part 1">630</a></sup> and on a set with no bleeder at all the main filter cap can be shorted out once removed.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1388" title="EEVblog 1388 -  Dumpster Diving 4K TV Murphy&#x27;s &quot;Repair&quot;">1388</a></sup> For measurements that must be made with the supply live, a high-voltage differential probe is the correct instrument on the primary side of a mains switcher.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-630" title="EEVblog #630 - How To: Soniq LCD TV Troubleshooting Repair - Part 1">630</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1388" title="EEVblog 1388 -  Dumpster Diving 4K TV Murphy&#x27;s &quot;Repair&quot;">1388</a></sup>

Bleeders also complicate diagnosis. Diodes that appear shorted or that read the same in both directions may simply have a bleed resistor of a couple of hundred ohms in parallel with them, combining with the secondary-side winding resistance to produce a misleading in-circuit reading.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1364" title="EEVblog #1364 - Compaq Portable PSU REPAIR">1364</a></sup> In a dropper-supply repair, the bleeder and [[dropper-resistor|dropper resistor]] both measured correctly while the actual fault lay in the capacitor value, a reminder that a component measuring fine in circuit does not narrow the fault as much as measuring the rail voltage would.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1481" title="EEVblog 1481 - Dodgy Dangerous Heater REPAIR">1481</a></sup>
