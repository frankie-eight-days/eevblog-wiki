Inductance is the property by which a conductor opposes a change in the [[current]] flowing through it, storing energy in a [[magnetic-field|magnetic field]] as it does so. It is measured in henries, and the defining relationship is that the instantaneous [[voltage]] across an [[inductor]] equals the inductance in henries multiplied by the rate of change of current in amps per second.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1660" title="EEVblog 1660 - AC Basics Tutorial Part 4: Resistors, Capacitors, Inductors">1660</a></sup> Inductance matters in practice for two quite different reasons: it is deliberately designed in, as inductors, chokes, transformers and magnets; and it is unavoidably present everywhere else, because every wire, every [[pcb-trace|PCB trace]], every component lead — every conductor in electronics — has some inductance.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1439" title="EEVblog 1439 - Analysing Veritasium&#x27;s Electricity Misconceptions Video">1439</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1117" title="EEVblog #1117 - PCB Power Plane Capacitance">1117</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-304" title="EEVblog #304 - Mailbag">304</a></sup>

## Behaviour in circuits

At the instant a switch closes, an inductor with no established magnetic field behaves as an open circuit: no current flows at all, because the inductance resists the flow of current until the magnetic field builds up.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1406" title="EEVblog 1406 - DC Fundamentals Part 7: DC Circuit Transients Fundamentals">1406</a></sup> The current then rises exponentially, reaching 63.2% of its final value in one time constant.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1406" title="EEVblog 1406 - DC Fundamentals Part 7: DC Circuit Transients Fundamentals">1406</a></sup> The reverse case is the dangerous one — interrupting the current collapses the field and drives the terminal voltage as far as the physical limits of the inductor allow, so hundreds or even thousands of volts can appear from a source of only a few volts, the magnitude depending on the magnetic properties of the inductor and the amount of inductance present.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1406" title="EEVblog 1406 - DC Fundamentals Part 7: DC Circuit Transients Fundamentals">1406</a></sup>

Inductors and capacitors only matter for transient and AC cases; once a DC circuit reaches steady state the inductance no longer matters and only the line [[resistance]] remains.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1439" title="EEVblog 1439 - Analysing Veritasium&#x27;s Electricity Misconceptions Video">1439</a></sup> Simulators encode this directly: a DC operating point analysis computes its answer by treating capacitances as open circuits and inductances as short circuits, because that is exactly what those components do at DC.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-516" title="EEVblog #516 - LTSPICE Tutorial - DC Operating Point Analysis">516</a></sup>

There is no such thing as a purely inductive circuit.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1728" title="EEVblog 1728 - AC Basics Tutorial Part 6: Impedance, Conductance, Susceptance, Admittance">1728</a></sup> Real components are equivalent networks — an inductor has inductance in its connecting leads and [[capacitance]] between its windings, and a [[resistor]] has some inductance of its own — so every practical circuit is a complex one in which voltage and current can be out of phase.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1728" title="EEVblog 1728 - AC Basics Tutorial Part 6: Impedance, Conductance, Susceptance, Admittance">1728</a></sup>

## Parasitic inductance

Every piece of wire, every trace on a [[pcb|PCB]], every conductor has inductance in some form, and even a [[ground-plane|ground plane]] has spread inductance.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1117" title="EEVblog #1117 - PCB Power Plane Capacitance">1117</a></sup> The consequences are pervasive and mostly [[bandwidth]]-related.

Test leads used to jump from a jig to a board are inductors, and an [[lcr-meter|LCR meter]] must be recalibrated open/short/load at the end of them to take them out of the measurement.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1117" title="EEVblog #1117 - PCB Power Plane Capacitance">1117</a></sup> Even at the level of a supposedly pure resistive measurement, inductance in the test leads shows up as a [[phase-angle|phase angle]], and swapping the leads over changes the result.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1473" title="EEVblog 1473 - How Your LCR Meter Works">1473</a></sup> A [[power-rail|power rail]] probe soldered directly to a board rather than plugged into a two-pin header carries extra inductance and correspondingly less bandwidth,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1733" title="EEVblog 1733 - Mailbag: $100 2GHz Power Rail Probe, Retro Chip Tester">1733</a></sup> and shorter probe leads are preferred precisely because they give higher bandwidth, less inductance and less interference.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1631" title="EEVblog 1631 - $230 Micsig MDP700 HV Differential Probe Review">1631</a></sup> A fast-edge pulse generator is plugged straight into the scope input with no lead at all, since any lead has inductance and the resulting [[overshoot]] and undershoot corrupt the [[rise-time|rise time]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-304" title="EEVblog #304 - Mailbag">304</a></sup>

The same reasoning applies inside packages. A [[bypass-capacitor|bypass capacitor]] built into a [[crystal-oscillator|crystal oscillator]] module still sits behind the inductance of the internal lead lengths and traces, so it is not equivalent to a [[capacitor]] connected directly to the external ground plane that the measurement is referenced from.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1089" title="EEVblog #1089 - XTAL Oscillator Teardown">1089</a></sup> When shorting out an RF bead on a board, a long mod wire can act as an inductor in its own right, so proper surface-mount zero-ohm resistors are the better substitution.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-987" title="EEVblog #987 - Keysight U1272A Multimeter EMC Followup">987</a></sup>

Electrolytic capacitors are specified for ESR at typically 100 kHz because that is roughly where they are purely resistive; above that [[frequency]] the inductance dominates and the [[impedance]] climbs again, which is why electrolytics are poor at bypassing an IC.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1103" title="EEVBlog #1103 - Omicron Labs Bode 100 Review &amp; Experiments">1103</a></sup>

## Inductance in PCB layout

The default [[ground]] strategy is a flood-filled plane — a big low-impedance, low-inductance ground over the whole board — with star [[grounding]] reserved for cases where there is a specific known reason for it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-500" title="EEVblog #500 - Live Show">500</a></sup> Multiple vias placed around a board's outer edge lower the inductance of the ground return,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1193" title="EEVblog #1193 - KiCAD PCB 4 Layer Swapping &amp; Stackup">1193</a></sup> and dense [[via-stitching|via stitching]] top and bottom serves the same purpose on RF boards.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-856" title="EEVblog #856 - Mailbag">856</a></sup> In fast ECL designs the inductance of the ground and power planes is essential to operation: such a board almost certainly would not work on two layers, because the edges are too fast and the current demand at each switching event too large.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-452" title="EEVblog #452 - Stanford Research SR430 Teardown">452</a></sup> A large ground pin on a [[connector]] is sized for inductive reasons — a low-impedance ground path — rather than for current-carrying capability.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1416" title="EEVblog 1416 - Random TEARDOWN: Sennheiser EW100 True Diversity Receiver">1416</a></sup>

Bypassing is an exercise in minimising loop inductance. Placing capacitors on the underside of a [[bga|BGA]] directly beneath the pin, with vias running straight up to the balls, bypasses individual pins and is about the only way to improve further.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1512" title="EEVblog 1512 - Why Bypass Your PCB Like THIS?">1512</a></sup> Where a design instead relies on one large 3.3 V plane and a single ground plane over the whole board, inductance is not being optimised and a bulk [[decoupling-capacitor|decoupling capacitor]] or two suffices — a capacitor per pin is not required.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1512" title="EEVblog 1512 - Why Bypass Your PCB Like THIS?">1512</a></sup> Bulk capacitance can sit further from the chip, even on another board, because it does not handle the high-frequency current spikes and the inductance of the longer traces therefore matters less — though placing it remotely is not good design practice.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-326" title="EEVblog #326 - Makerbot Replicator Teardown">326</a></sup>

## Transmission lines

Distributed inductance is one of the three elements of a [[transmission-line|transmission line]]. A line is analysed by breaking it into a lumped element model of Ls, Cs and Rs, each unit length having series inductance, series resistance and shunt capacitance, with the elements chained together and the chain extended as far as needed.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1439" title="EEVblog 1439 - Analysing Veritasium&#x27;s Electricity Misconceptions Video">1439</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-756" title="EEVblog #756 - Mailbag">756</a></sup> The unit length is arbitrary — a centimetre, an inch or a metre — and the same construction is used to simulate long cable runs.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1439" title="EEVblog 1439 - Analysing Veritasium&#x27;s Electricity Misconceptions Video">1439</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-756" title="EEVblog #756 - Mailbag">756</a></sup> The same lumped model underlies [[oscilloscope-probe|oscilloscope probe]] analysis, where inductance is present in any lossy transmission line model even when it is not drawn.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-453" title="EEVblog #453 - Mysteries of x1 Oscilloscope Probes Revealed">453</a></sup>

Modern passive probes achieve their high-frequency performance using lossy [[coaxial-cable|coax]], simulated with inductance in henries per unit length alongside capacitance per unit length and a conductance term G in siemens, the last of which is usually negligible enough to omit.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1445" title="EEVblog 1445 - How to Simulate an Oscilloscope Probe in LTSPICE">1445</a></sup> A worked probe model uses 210 ohms, 83 pF and an inductance of 208 over a length of 1.2 m.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1445" title="EEVblog 1445 - How to Simulate an Oscilloscope Probe in LTSPICE">1445</a></sup>

## Reactive power

No real power is dissipated in inductance. Real power is dissipated only in resistances, not in the capacitance of wires nor in the inductive magnetic fields of transformers,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1730" title="EEVblog 1730 - AC Basics Tutorial Part 8: Apparent, Reactive &amp; Real Power">1730</a></sup> yet the reactive current still causes real I²R copper losses elsewhere in the delivery system.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1730" title="EEVblog 1730 - AC Basics Tutorial Part 8: Apparent, Reactive &amp; Real Power">1730</a></sup> Reactive power Q is computed from the reactive component alone — the current or voltage in just the capacitance or just the inductance — multiplied by the reactance in ohms, which is why an X appears in the formula rather than an R.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1730" title="EEVblog 1730 - AC Basics Tutorial Part 8: Apparent, Reactive &amp; Real Power">1730</a></sup>

Correction consists of adding the opposite kind of reactive component. Large inductive loads are compensated with parallel capacitance, which is why power stations and factories install capacitor [[power-factor-correction|power factor correction]] banks; a capacitive load, such as the switch-mode supply in a piece of test equipment measured at a [[power-factor|power factor]] of 0.45, is instead compensated by adding inductance.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1730" title="EEVblog 1730 - AC Basics Tutorial Part 8: Apparent, Reactive &amp; Real Power">1730</a></sup>

## Practical values

A henry is a massive amount of inductance; ordinary work is done in millihenries, microhenries and nanohenries.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-827" title="EEVblog #827 - Mailbag">827</a></sup> The extremes bracket a wide range. A niobium-titanium superconducting [[solenoid]] in a quantum computer magnet has an inductance of about 10 henries, and at around 100 amps produces roughly 6 tesla.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1594" title="EEVblog 1594 - Inside a Quantum Computer! with Andrea Morello">1594</a></sup> A 1944-vintage field coil measures 3.4 millihenries.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-705" title="EEVblog #705 - Mailbag">705</a></sup> An ultrasonic delay line uses four inductors of about 33 to 37 microhenries for the inner pair and 24 microhenries for the outer pair, with 370 picofarad capacitors on a common pin.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-381" title="EEVblog #381 - How Do Ultrasonic Delay Lines Work?">381</a></sup> At the other end, a standard 0603 capacitor contributes roughly 450 picohenries of parasitic inductance.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-9V99J22aiLE" title="Full Discussion - Film Capacitor Failures with Ron Demcko from AVX">•</a></sup>

For a wound core, inductance follows from the AL value given in the core's [[datasheet|data sheet]]: L equals n² times AL, with n the number of turns.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-708" title="EEVblog #708 - Free Energy Overunity is RUBBISH!">708</a></sup> A core specified at 17,600 nanohenries gives 17.6 microhenries for a single turn passing through it, and three such cores stacked together sum to on the order of 50 microhenries — a substantial inductance for a high-frequency [[oscillator]] running at hundreds of kilohertz or into the megahertz range, and far too large to be dismissed as a bare wire with no coil in the circuit.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-708" title="EEVblog #708 - Free Energy Overunity is RUBBISH!">708</a></sup>

Surface-mount power inductors trade inductance against current. Higher inductance in a given case size comes with a lower rated current, so a 470 microhenry part in the same package is unusable where a 1 microhenry part rated at around 1.8 amps will do; case sizes such as 1210, 1812 and 2220 set the available range, and a part too small for the current is a failure waiting to happen.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1299" title="EEVblog #1299 - Dumpster Laptop REPAIR">1299</a></sup>

## Measurement

Most modern multimeters measure capacitance, but very few can measure inductance — that is the distinguishing capability of an LCR meter.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-115" title="EEVblog #115 - Extech 380193 LCR Meter Review">115</a></sup> Beyond L, C and R, a good LCR meter also yields quality factor, dissipation factor and ESR.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-115" title="EEVblog #115 - Extech 380193 LCR Meter Review">115</a></sup>

The underlying technique is the same across instruments. A known [[waveform]] is applied through a series [[current-shunt-resistor|shunt resistor]]; the voltage across the device under test and the current through it are measured, and because the generating micro knows the phase of its own test signal, the phase relationship is known too.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-81" title="EEVblog #81 - Smart Tweezers LCR Meter Review And Teardown">81</a></sup> From voltage, current and phase, every parameter follows — inductance, capacitance, reactance, series and parallel resistance, dissipation factor and quality factor.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-81" title="EEVblog #81 - Smart Tweezers LCR Meter Review And Teardown">81</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-757" title="EEVblog #757 - HP4263A LCR Meter Teardown">757</a></sup> The same method is what turns an impedance analyser instrument into an LCR meter.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-858" title="EEVblog #858 - Red Pitaya">858</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-2" title="EEVblog2 #858 - Red Pitaya Extended Cut">2</a></sup> Results are reported in series or parallel form, each paired with Q or D as a secondary reading.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-757" title="EEVblog #757 - HP4263A LCR Meter Teardown">757</a></sup> Accurate phase is the binding constraint: a scope intended for [[frequency-response|frequency response]] analysis needs very low phase error — on the order of ±70 picoseconds between channels — precisely because measuring inductance or capacitance depends on it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1119" title="EEVBlog #1119 - Designing a 1kV Isolated Oscilloscope">1119</a></sup>

Measured inductance is not a single fixed number. It changes with test frequency and with whether series or parallel mode is selected, and LCR meter accuracy specifications are typically met only in the middle of each range, degrading at the extremes.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-115" title="EEVblog #115 - Extech 380193 LCR Meter Review">115</a></sup> A handheld LCR meter reading 1,055 microhenries and 2.6 ohms series resistance at 1 kHz on a surface-mount inductor compares well against a DC [[resistance-measurement|resistance measurement]] of 2.5 ohms on the same part.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-115" title="EEVblog #115 - Extech 380193 LCR Meter Review">115</a></sup> Bench-grade tweezer meters quote figures such as 0.5% on capacitance and inductance with a measurement range topping out around 10 kHz.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-477" title="EEVblog #477 - Mailbag">477</a></sup> Cheap component testers degrade sharply with value: a 1 millihenry inductor reads reasonably, a 10 microhenry part suffers poor resolution, and a 1 microhenry part cannot be measured at all — the tester simply reports a resistor.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1020" title="EEVblog #1020 - Is A $7 LCR / Component Tester Any Good?">1020</a></sup> An auto-ranging tweezer meter with its probes shorted may report inductance as the primary parameter and resistance as a 0.02 ohm secondary, when resistance is plainly the dominant quantity.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1375" title="EEVblog #1375 - Mailbag: IEEE">1375</a></sup> An ordinary [[multimeter]] can be pressed into service for at least usable indications of reasonably sized inductors, provided units are tracked carefully.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1465" title="EEVblog 1465 - Your Multimeter Can Measure Inductors">1465</a></sup>

Using coil inductance as a position sensor — measuring the small difference between two coils as an iron core moves, by feeding a low-level signal through when the coil is not being driven — is technically conceivable but not a solution to be relied on.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1351" title="EEVblog #1351 - Mailbag">1351</a></sup>
