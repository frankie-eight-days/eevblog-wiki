Power factor correction (PFC) is circuitry added to a mains-powered product to make the [[current]] it draws follow the shape and phase of the [[mains-voltage|mains voltage]], so that the [[apparent-power|apparent power]] in volt-amps approaches the real power in watts.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1730" title="EEVblog 1730 - AC Basics Tutorial Part 8: Apparent, Reactive &amp; Real Power">1730</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1191" title="EEVblog #1191 - Digital Energy Savers BUSTED!">1191</a></sup> Without it, a switch-mode supply presents a badly non-sinusoidal load: the rectifier conducts only at the peaks of the mains [[waveform]] and refills the bulk [[capacitor]] in short, tall current spikes.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1413" title="EEVblog 1413 - Oscilloscope Current Probe TEARDOWN + DEMO">1413</a></sup> Those spikes carry no extra useful power but do produce real I²R losses in the cabling and in the entire distribution system upstream, right back to the generator.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1413" title="EEVblog 1413 - Oscilloscope Current Probe TEARDOWN + DEMO">1413</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1730" title="EEVblog 1730 - AC Basics Tutorial Part 8: Apparent, Reactive &amp; Real Power">1730</a></sup>

## Real, apparent and reactive power

A product whose [[voltage]] and current are out of phase, or whose current is distorted, dissipates less real power than the product of RMS volts and RMS amps suggests.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1730" title="EEVblog 1730 - AC Basics Tutorial Part 8: Apparent, Reactive &amp; Real Power">1730</a></sup> A typical modern [[oscilloscope]] with a non-corrected switching supply illustrates the gap directly: measured on a power analyser it drew about 9.1 W of real power against roughly 32 VA apparent.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1191" title="EEVblog #1191 - Digital Energy Savers BUSTED!">1191</a></sup> The same class of measurement on another instrument gave a [[power-factor|power factor]] of 0.55, and elsewhere 0.45 — figures that are entirely normal for an uncorrected product and are not a fault of the instrument.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1413" title="EEVblog 1413 - Oscilloscope Current Probe TEARDOWN + DEMO">1413</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1730" title="EEVblog 1730 - AC Basics Tutorial Part 8: Apparent, Reactive &amp; Real Power">1730</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1191" title="EEVblog #1191 - Digital Energy Savers BUSTED!">1191</a></sup>

The closer power factor falls toward zero, the more current the product takes from the whole upstream delivery chain.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1730" title="EEVblog 1730 - AC Basics Tutorial Part 8: Apparent, Reactive &amp; Real Power">1730</a></sup> Well-specified equipment therefore states its input rating in VA rather than watts; a mains rating given in watts is a reasonable hint that there is no PFC stage inside.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1730" title="EEVblog 1730 - AC Basics Tutorial Part 8: Apparent, Reactive &amp; Real Power">1730</a></sup>

## Who actually pays for poor power factor

Residential and most commercial tariffs meter real power in kilowatt-hours, so a domestic user is billed for watts and not for apparent power.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1191" title="EEVblog #1191 - Digital Energy Savers BUSTED!">1191</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-o2NxHu5Bsnk" title="Discussion with Shahriar from The Signal Path">•</a></sup> Correcting the power factor of household loads therefore does not reduce the bill, because no apparent-power meter has been installed.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1191" title="EEVblog #1191 - Digital Energy Savers BUSTED!">1191</a></sup> Industrial supply is different: large sites with heavy motor loads can be billed on apparent power, and the high circulating current forces heavier copper, so power factor correction there is a genuine economic concern.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1191" title="EEVblog #1191 - Digital Energy Savers BUSTED!">1191</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-870" title="EEVblog #870 - Shonky Z-Energeia Energy Saving Wipes">870</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-o2NxHu5Bsnk" title="Discussion with Shahriar from The Signal Path">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-847" title="EEVblog #847 - Mailbag">847</a></sup>

The cost does not vanish for domestic users, it is simply socialised. If every television had its PFC stage removed, the utility would need greater transmission capacity, larger conductors and more generation to carry the extra current.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1388" title="EEVblog 1388 -  Dumpster Diving 4K TV Murphy&#x27;s &quot;Repair&quot;">1388</a></sup> Bad power factor is an environmental and infrastructure cost even where it is not a line item on the bill.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1388" title="EEVblog 1388 -  Dumpster Diving 4K TV Murphy&#x27;s &quot;Repair&quot;">1388</a></sup>

## Passive correction

The classic passive fix is to place reactance of the opposite sign in parallel with the load. A factory full of motors is almost entirely inductive, so [[capacitance]] is added in parallel to cancel the phase shift and bring power factor as close to unity as possible.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1730" title="EEVblog 1730 - AC Basics Tutorial Part 8: Apparent, Reactive &amp; Real Power">1730</a></sup> Power stations and factories accordingly run large capacitor banks for this purpose.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1730" title="EEVblog 1730 - AC Basics Tutorial Part 8: Apparent, Reactive &amp; Real Power">1730</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1191" title="EEVblog #1191 - Digital Energy Savers BUSTED!">1191</a></sup> The converse case — a switch-mode supply that looks capacitive — calls for added [[inductance]] instead.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1730" title="EEVblog 1730 - AC Basics Tutorial Part 8: Apparent, Reactive &amp; Real Power">1730</a></sup>

Some correction is inherent rather than added. A supply with only a small amount of [[input-capacitance|input capacitance]] conducts over more of the mains cycle and therefore has a naturally good power factor; one [[led|LED]] driver of this type measured 0.95 at just over 24 W output.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1253" title="EEVblog #1253 - LED Flicker 2: Electric Boogaloo">1253</a></sup> Controller vendors advertise this as inherent PFC, which achieves the result through the converter topology rather than a dedicated stage, at a cost elsewhere in the design.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1253" title="EEVblog #1253 - LED Flicker 2: Electric Boogaloo">1253</a></sup> A UPS [[inverter]]-charger design obtained the same effect by drawing a sinusoidal, undistorted current from the line without any additional control circuitry, using the leakage reactances of the main power [[transformer]] together with the H-bridge switching devices.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-504" title="EEVblog #504 - UPS Tutorial &amp; Teardown">504</a></sup>

## Active correction

An active PFC stage sits between the [[bridge-rectifier|bridge rectifier]] and the bulk filter capacitor, and is recognisable as a switching element with a choke and a small controller chip in that position.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1388" title="EEVblog 1388 -  Dumpster Diving 4K TV Murphy&#x27;s &quot;Repair&quot;">1388</a></sup> It is a [[boost-converter|boost converter]] in operation: the switch chops current through the [[inductor]], which feeds the main filter capacitor through a [[diode]], shaping the input current to track the mains sinusoid.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1388" title="EEVblog 1388 -  Dumpster Diving 4K TV Murphy&#x27;s &quot;Repair&quot;">1388</a></sup>

Dedicated controllers for this have existed for decades. A 40 A bench switch-mode supply used a Motorola MC34262 in an eight-pin DIP together with a transformer and a handful of surrounding parts, feeding through a diode into series filter caps with voltage-sharing resistors, behind 3.3 µF 400 V bulk capacitors.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-272" title="EEVblog #272 - Manson 9400 40A 3-15V Switchmode PSU Teardown">272</a></sup> Modern LED driver controllers such as the TI TPS92314 fold PFC into an offline primary-side sensing part whose typical application [[schematic]] is otherwise near identical to a non-corrected equivalent.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1253" title="EEVblog #1253 - LED Flicker 2: Electric Boogaloo">1253</a></sup>

Because the stage runs at rectified mains potential, its switch is a high-voltage device. In one 100 W-class product the PFC [[mosfet|MOSFET]] was an N-channel part rated 18 A at 500 V with 0.27 Ω nominal on-[[resistance]] — arguably over-specified on current for the power involved, though 500 V is about the minimum sane rating on a 240 V mains.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1387" title="EEVblog 1387 - MOSFET Repair Replacement Search">1387</a></sup> Substituting such a part is comparatively forgiving: matching voltage, current and roughly similar RDS(on) is generally sufficient in a PFC application, whereas the long tail of MOSFET [[datasheet]] parameters matters far more in other roles.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1387" title="EEVblog 1387 - MOSFET Repair Replacement Search">1387</a></sup>

Removing the stage does not stop the product working. Taking the [[transistor]] out of a television supply and leaving the inductor and diode as a plain DC path let the set run normally — less efficiently, and with no power factor correction at all.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1388" title="EEVblog 1388 -  Dumpster Diving 4K TV Murphy&#x27;s &quot;Repair&quot;">1388</a></sup> With the new transistor fitted, standby consumption actually rose to 3.2 W and the corrected [[standby-power|standby power]] factor was still only 0.16, which is typical for low-power products in standby.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1388" title="EEVblog 1388 -  Dumpster Diving 4K TV Murphy&#x27;s &quot;Repair&quot;">1388</a></sup>

## Where it appears

Active PFC is standard on professional and high-power equipment: a 5100 W, 600 V laboratory supply carried a dedicated PFC board on the primary side behind three bridge rectifiers and a three-phase common-mode choke arrangement,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-814" title="EEVblog #814 - Keysight N8762A 600V 5100W PSU Teardown">814</a></sup> and a variable-[[frequency]] AC source was specified with electronic power factor correction alongside its true sinusoidal output.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-449" title="EEVblog #449 - Absopulse VFC500 Variable Frequency Converter Teardown">449</a></sup> Television and monitor supplies commonly include an active stage behind the common-mode chokes and ahead of the high-voltage DC caps.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-eCKRl_Txa18" title="Is This Dumpster Hisense TV Worth Repairing?">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1657" title="EEVblog 1657 - DUMPSTER Samsung 75&quot; QLED One Connect TV">1657</a></sup> Lower-cost consumer instruments frequently omit it entirely — one oscilloscope had a common-mode choke at the IEC inlet and no other suppression or power factor correction at all.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-384" title="EEVblog #384 - Agilent 4000X Oscilloscope Teardown">384</a></sup>

The economic threshold has moved sharply. PFC was once used only at 600 W and above; it is now found in 45 W adapters, because controller silicon has become cheap enough that above a certain power level a PFC front end costs less overall than omitting it, the savings in losses paying for the extra parts.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1032" title="EEVblog #1032 Part 1 - John Kenny Keysight Interview">1032</a></sup> A complete PFC function is available as a single small hybrid for around fifty cents, though a choke and a somewhat larger input filter are still required.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1032" title="EEVblog #1032 Part 1 - John Kenny Keysight Interview">1032</a></sup> The practical consequence is that almost any current product achieves a power factor of 0.98 or 0.99.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1032" title="EEVblog #1032 Part 1 - John Kenny Keysight Interview">1032</a></sup> Wide-bandgap devices push this further still: a gallium-nitride PFC board of 2.5 kW fits in a very small form factor at better than 99 % [[efficiency]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1557" title="EEVblog 1557 - Micsig SigOFIT Optical Fibre Probe + GaN Experiment">1557</a></sup>

There are countervailing design reasons to avoid a PFC front end. Distributing 48 V DC internally instead of running a 400 V-plus PFC rail simplifies safety qualification substantially, shortening development time and compliance testing.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1032" title="EEVblog #1032 Part 1 - John Kenny Keysight Interview">1032</a></sup> Bidirectional supplies for electric-vehicle test can go the other way and regulate power factor digitally to unity while returning energy to the line,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1032" title="EEVblog #1032 Part 1 - John Kenny Keysight Interview">1032</a></sup> and solid-state transformers can be made unity power factor, or variable if the utility asks for it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1753" title="EEVblog 1753 - Designing a 2000V Isolated Oscilloscope (Cleverscope)">1753</a></sup>

Regulation is often the driver rather than economics. A good power factor is a plausible requirement for public-sector lighting supply contracts, and LED driver silicon is marketed on high power factor for that reason.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1253" title="EEVblog #1253 - LED Flicker 2: Electric Boogaloo">1253</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1252" title="EEVblog #1252 - LED Panel Lighting Flicker Investigated">1252</a></sup> Measured drivers in that class returned power factors of 0.9 and better.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-GbADsyp9wM8" title="Failed Lucci Constant Current LED Driver Teardown">•</a></sup>

## Mains capacitor boxes

Consumer energy-saving boxes sold as electricity savers are, internally, a single power factor correction capacitor connected straight across active and neutral — in one dissected example a 3 µF part with nothing else of consequence in the enclosure.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1191" title="EEVblog #1191 - Digital Energy Savers BUSTED!">1191</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-847" title="EEVblog #847 - Mailbag">847</a></sup> Parallel capacitance is a real technique against inductive load, and the marketing copy correctly describes energy being interchanged between capacitive and inductive elements.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1191" title="EEVblog #1191 - Digital Energy Savers BUSTED!">1191</a></sup> The kernel of truth is what makes the product saleable.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1191" title="EEVblog #1191 - Digital Energy Savers BUSTED!">1191</a></sup>

It does not follow that the box saves money. On a domestic tariff billed in watts there is nothing to recover, and the device costs money rather than saving it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1191" title="EEVblog #1191 - Digital Energy Savers BUSTED!">1191</a></sup> Against a load that is already capacitive — which describes most modern switch-mode electronics — adding parallel capacitance makes the power factor worse, not better.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1191" title="EEVblog #1191 - Digital Energy Savers BUSTED!">1191</a></sup> Correction of this kind belongs at industrial scale, where the customer is billed for apparent power and where the capacitor banks are sized to the motor loads they offset.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1191" title="EEVblog #1191 - Digital Energy Savers BUSTED!">1191</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-870" title="EEVblog #870 - Shonky Z-Energeia Energy Saving Wipes">870</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-847" title="EEVblog #847 - Mailbag">847</a></sup> Products claiming the same benefit through non-electrical means, such as substances applied to cabling, have no mechanism at all.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-870" title="EEVblog #870 - Shonky Z-Energeia Energy Saving Wipes">870</a></sup>
