Overvoltage protection (OVP) is a circuit or [[firmware]] function that stops a supply rail from exceeding a set ceiling, either by shutting the source down or by clamping the excess.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1293" title="EEVblog #1293 - New Rohde and Schwarz NGP800 800W PSU">1293</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-509" title="EEVblog #509 - Rigol DP832 Lab Power Supply">509</a></sup> On a bench supply it exists as a settable limit independent of the [[output-voltage|output voltage]] setting, so that a knob bump, a wrong keystroke, or an internal failure cannot put more [[voltage]] on a load than the load can survive.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1293" title="EEVblog #1293 - New Rohde and Schwarz NGP800 800W PSU">1293</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-439" title="EEVblog #439 - Atten PPS3205T-3S Triple Output Power Supply Review">439</a></sup> Inside equipment it appears as a clamp, a trip [[comparator]], or a block of an integrated regulator, and it is one of the standard companions of overcurrent and overtemperature protection.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-90" title="EEVblog #90 - Linear and LDO regulators and Switch Mode Power Supply Tutorial">90</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1541" title="EEVblog1541 - What is this Blown SMD Component? Redux">1541</a></sup>

## Why a separate limit exists

The case for OVP on a bench supply is economic rather than theoretical: a prototype board being powered for the first time may represent tens or hundreds of thousands of dollars of parts and a project schedule that collapses if it is destroyed.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1293" title="EEVblog #1293 - New Rohde and Schwarz NGP800 800W PSU">1293</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-509" title="EEVblog #509 - Rigol DP832 Lab Power Supply">509</a></sup> The protection setpoint is deliberately separate from the output setting, so that a passer-by who twiddles the front panel — a manager, a marketing person, or simply someone who did not read the label — cannot raise the rail past the ceiling.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1293" title="EEVblog #1293 - New Rohde and Schwarz NGP800 800W PSU">1293</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-509" title="EEVblog #509 - Rigol DP832 Lab Power Supply">509</a></sup> Overvoltage and overcurrent limits are for this reason treated as lifesaver features when first powering a prototype or anything expensive.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-509" title="EEVblog #509 - Rigol DP832 Lab Power Supply">509</a></sup>

The conventional setpoint is a small margin above the nominal rail: a 3.3 V board is protected at 3.4 V, a 5 V board at something like 5.25 V or 5.5 V.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-509" title="EEVblog #509 - Rigol DP832 Lab Power Supply">509</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1293" title="EEVblog #1293 - New Rohde and Schwarz NGP800 800W PSU">1293</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1030" title="EEVblog #1030 - $20 DIY Bench Power Supply!">1030</a></sup> With the limit armed, an attempt to set or slide the output past it is refused — the output switches off, or the supply simply beeps and reverts to the previous value rather than delivering the higher voltage.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-509" title="EEVblog #509 - Rigol DP832 Lab Power Supply">509</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1691" title="EEVblog 1691 - Uni-T UDP6731 360W Bench PSU REVIEW">1691</a></sup> On a multi-channel supply the ceiling is set per channel.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-439" title="EEVblog #439 - Atten PPS3205T-3S Triple Output Power Supply Review">439</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1606" title="EEVblog 1606 - $45 AlienTek DP100 100W USB-C PSU REVIEW">1606</a></sup> Instruments that store presets may carry a separate overvoltage and overcurrent limit within each preset.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1606" title="EEVblog 1606 - $45 AlienTek DP100 100W USB-C PSU REVIEW">1606</a></sup>

## Software limits versus hardware trips

Most consumer and mid-range bench supplies implement OVP in firmware: the controller reads back the output and shuts it down, with no dedicated hardware crowbar behind it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1691" title="EEVblog 1691 - Uni-T UDP6731 360W Bench PSU REVIEW">1691</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1606" title="EEVblog 1606 - $45 AlienTek DP100 100W USB-C PSU REVIEW">1606</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1030" title="EEVblog #1030 - $20 DIY Bench Power Supply!">1030</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1265" title="EEVblog #1265 - $53 360W Lab Bench PSU!">1265</a></sup> The weakness is explicit — if the software fails, locks up, or otherwise misbehaves, a firmware-only limit will not save the load from overvoltage.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1265" title="EEVblog #1265 - $53 360W Lab Bench PSU!">1265</a></sup> Whether a given supply's protection is software or true hardware is not always documented and is a distinguishing question when evaluating one.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1173" title="EEVblog #1173 - Rohde &amp; Schwarz Power Supply Bonanza">1173</a></sup> Where the protection is genuinely independent, it is visible as discrete circuitry: hardware overvoltage protection trip and detect stages sitting alongside [[current]] monitors, done in hardware rather than software.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1550" title="EEVblog 1550 - Keysight E36731A Battery Emulator Teardown">1550</a></sup> Some supplies place dedicated overvoltage protection in the [[pwm|PWM]] control section purely to stop the output going berserk after an internal component failure.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-272" title="EEVblog #272 - Manson 9400 40A 3-15V Switchmode PSU Teardown">272</a></sup> The ideal a well-equipped supply aims at is protection that remains effective even when the regulating path itself has failed.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-439" title="EEVblog #439 - Atten PPS3205T-3S Triple Output Power Supply Review">439</a></sup>

Related architecture appears in instruments whose front panel and regulation are handled by different processors: overcurrent and overvoltage protection is the job of a local controller close to the output, not of the main applications [[processor]], for reasons of speed, consistency, and separation.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1550" title="EEVblog 1550 - Keysight E36731A Battery Emulator Teardown">1550</a></sup>

## Crowbars and clamps

The classic hardware response is a crowbar, which shorts the rail on overvoltage and clamps it down.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-905" title="EEVblog #905 - REPAIR: HP85 Vintage Computer">905</a></sup> It is a sacrificial defence: a crowbar that fires on, for example, a supply left switched to 110 V and plugged into 240 V may destroy itself, but it saves the rest of the equipment.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-905" title="EEVblog #905 - REPAIR: HP85 Vintage Computer">905</a></sup> Vintage linear supplies commonly carry one across the main rail.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-905" title="EEVblog #905 - REPAIR: HP85 Vintage Computer">905</a></sup>

A cheaper clamp is a Zener across the output — a 12 V Zener on the output of a 0–10 V bench supply prevents dangerously high voltages from reaching the load, though whether the Zener survives depends on the capability of whatever is driving it, and no single clamp caters for every scenario.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-233" title="EEVblog #233 - Lab Power Supply Design Part 6 - LT3080 Testing">233</a></sup> A transient voltage suppressor, sold variously as a TVS or transorb, does the same job with more energy capability; a 12 V part such as the SA12AG clamps output overvoltage while also providing reverse-polarity [[diode]] protection.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-238" title="EEVblog #238 - Power Supply Design Part 7">238</a></sup> In the design of the output stage of a DC-DC converter, the feedback divider itself can be used as a ceiling: lowering the upper [[feedback-resistor|feedback resistor]] sets an absolute maximum the converter can reach, an inherent safety feature rather than an added circuit.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-260" title="EEVblog #260 - Tracking Pre-Regulator Simulation in LTspice - PSU Part 13">260</a></sup>

At meter inputs the clamping elements are metal oxide varistors, chosen with nominal ratings above the meter's rated input — 560 V and 620 V parts appear in a 1000 V handheld front end, clamping anything above the meter's rated range.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1667" title="EEVblog 1667 - Reverse Engineering the Brymen BM2257 Multimeter LowZ Mode">1667</a></sup> Multimeters also carry dedicated overvoltage protection on the ohms path specifically.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-426" title="EEVBlog #426 - HP 3457A Multimeter Teardown">426</a></sup> High-voltage sections rely additionally on isolation slots milled through the [[pcb|PCB]], which appear even where the working voltage is modest.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1023" title="EEVblog #1023 - Rigol DL3021 Electronic Load Teardown">1023</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1530" title="EEVblog 1530 - Siglent SDS2000X HD 12 Bit Oscilloscope TEARDOWN">1530</a></sup>

## Integrated and embedded protection

Linear regulators, LDOs, and switching regulators routinely include overcurrent, overvoltage, and overtemperature protection on-die as added value.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-90" title="EEVblog #90 - Linear and LDO regulators and Switch Mode Power Supply Tutorial">90</a></sup> Boost regulators are sold with overvoltage protection and soft start as headline features.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-259" title="EEVblog #259 - PSU Rev C Schematic - Part 12">259</a></sup> [[battery|Battery]]-charge and power-path controllers integrate overvoltage and overcurrent detection along with charge detection and on-die temperature sensing.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1541" title="EEVblog1541 - What is this Blown SMD Component? Redux">1541</a></sup> Automotive H-bridge motor drivers add undervoltage and overvoltage protection, thermal protection, cross-conduction protection, and protection against loss of [[ground]] or VCC.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-413" title="EEVblog #413 - Mailbag">413</a></sup> Audio power stages signal these conditions on a fault interrupt line and disable their outputs when tripped.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1322" title="EEVblog #1322 - JBL LSR308 Monitor Speaker Repair (FULL)">1322</a></sup> Camera and consumer power sections show the function as a named block — a DC-DC converter followed by an overvoltage protector — in the block-partitioned [[schematic]] style used for complex systems.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1429" title="EEVblog 1429 - Sony RX100 Camera Troubleshooting Part 2">1429</a></sup>

The distinguishing feature of industrial-grade I/O is that every input and output is protected, typically with [[current-limiting|current limiting]] and overvoltage clamping.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-979" title="EEVblog #979 - Mailbag">979</a></sup> Vehicle body-controller nodes carry [[overcurrent-protection|overcurrent protection]], automotive transient protection, and overvoltage protection on all inputs and outputs.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1627" title="EEVblog 1627 - Electronex: Engineering Consulting with Xentronics">1627</a></sup> PC motherboards expose the function in firmware as anti-surge support, enabling undervoltage and overvoltage protection, and enable it by default.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-462" title="EEVblog #462 - Video Editing PC Assembly">462</a></sup> Even inexpensive device programmers advertise overvoltage and overcurrent protection as a way of protecting both the target device and the programmer.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-411" title="EEVblog #411 - MiniPro TL866 Universal Programmer Review">411</a></sup>

## Lithium cell protection

Small lithium-polymer packs carry a protection PCB whose devices provide overvoltage cutoff and, usually, undervoltage cutoff, switched by MOSFETs on the board.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-412" title="EEVblog #412 - Jawbone UP Pedometer Teardown">412</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-295" title="EEVblog #295 - $30 eBook Reader Teardown">295</a></sup> A cell protection circuit with overvoltage, overcurrent, and low-voltage functions should, in principle, make the cell impossible to kill — it will disconnect rather than allow the abuse.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1613" title="EEVblog 1613 - Audiophile REPAIR: $4500 Chord Hugo DAC">1613</a></sup> The presence of such a board is a basic quality indicator in a teardown of any lithium-powered device.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-295" title="EEVblog #295 - $30 eBook Reader Teardown">295</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-412" title="EEVblog #412 - Jawbone UP Pedometer Teardown">412</a></sup>

## Trip behaviour and recovery

A tripped limit may latch, requiring explicit operator action to clear: some supplies display an overvoltage protection fault and require a shift-key sequence on the OVP preset key to clear the OVP status and reset before the output can be re-enabled.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-439" title="EEVblog #439 - Atten PPS3205T-3S Triple Output Power Supply Review">439</a></sup> Latching can have awkward side effects — on a multi-channel supply, an armed or tripped limit on one channel may block turning on any channel at all.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-439" title="EEVblog #439 - Atten PPS3205T-3S Triple Output Power Supply Review">439</a></sup> Switching controllers more often respond by retreating into hiccup mode, restarting periodically and shutting down again on each attempt; the behaviour is common enough that it is documented in controller datasheets, and its presence during a repair indicates the switching stage is alive and receiving voltage but is meeting an overvoltage or overcurrent condition.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1726" title="EEVblog 1726 - REPAIR of an EV Charger (Unusual Twist!)">1726</a></sup> Hiccup is also specified as the deliberate overload response in AC power converters, alongside a stated output overvoltage protection threshold such as 280 V AC enforced by internal supply limiting.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-449" title="EEVblog #449 - Absopulse VFC500 Variable Frequency Converter Teardown">449</a></sup>

An unexplained output collapse under load can be mistaken for an overvoltage trip, and distinguishing a real protection event from an instability or design fault requires establishing that the trip conditions were actually met.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-315" title="EEVblog #315 - Korad KA3005P Review/FAIL">315</a></sup> Documentation is frequently inadequate on this point: manuals may say nothing about how the overcurrent and overvoltage protection operate, or whether the setpoints are settable at all.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-315" title="EEVblog #315 - Korad KA3005P Review/FAIL">315</a></sup>
