Standby power is the power a product continues to draw when it is nominally off or idle — the residual consumption of a soft-switched mains device waiting for a button press, or the sleep [[current]] of a [[battery]]-powered instrument.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-164" title="EEVblog #164 - Agilent Fly To The Moon">164</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-790" title="EEVblog #790 - Lecroy Wavejet 354 Touch Oscilloscope Teardown">790</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-60" title="EEVblog #60 - Fluke 117 Multimeter Review and Teardown">60</a></sup> Because it is drawn continuously rather than in bursts, a figure of a few watts dominates a product's lifetime energy use far more than its operating consumption does, and in battery products the standby figure is usually what sets shelf life.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1313" title="EEVblog #1313 - Bad Luck Mailbag">1313</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-320" title="EEVblog #320 - Mailbag Monday">320</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1287" title="EEVblog #1287 - Gatekeeper Shopping Trolley Lock Investigated">1287</a></sup> It is a routine measurement on any product with a soft power switch, and one where measured values across otherwise comparable equipment span more than three orders of magnitude.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-790" title="EEVblog #790 - Lecroy Wavejet 354 Touch Oscilloscope Teardown">790</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1659" title="EEVblog 1659 - Quick 861 Pro Hot Air Station REVIEW">1659</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-842" title="EEVblog #842 - Rohde &amp; Schwarz HMO1202 Oscilloscope Teardown">842</a></sup>

## Where it comes from

A device with a real mechanical mains switch — a clunking switch, often with a plastic actuating rod running back to the mains board at the rear of the chassis — genuinely disconnects and draws nothing when off.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-164" title="EEVblog #164 - Agilent Fly To The Moon">164</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-187" title="EEVblog #187 - Tektronix TDS2024C Oscilloscope Teardown">187</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-475" title="EEVblog #475 - GW Instek GDS-2000A Oscilloscope Teardown">475</a></sup> A soft power button cannot do this: something must stay alive to sense the press, so a small auxiliary supply runs continuously.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-378" title="EEVblog #378 - Dumpster Diving Teardown Repair">378</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1032" title="EEVblog #1032 Part 5 - John Kenny Keysight Interview">1032</a></sup> The same architecture appears in televisions, where a small always-on rail keeps just enough circuitry powered for the infrared receiver to detect a remote command.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1032" title="EEVblog #1032 Part 5 - John Kenny Keysight Interview">1032</a></sup>

The presence of a mechanical switch is not by itself proof of zero standby. The Agilent InfiniiVision 2000 series had a full mechanical lever-arm switch running back to the mains board and still drew about 6.5 watts with the switch off.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-164" title="EEVblog #164 - Agilent Fly To The Moon">164</a></sup> The failure was noticed because the instrument stayed warm after being switched off.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-164" title="EEVblog #164 - Agilent Fly To The Moon">164</a></sup>

Auxiliary loads that are not part of the switching logic inflate the figure further. A [[frequency-counter]] fitted with an ovenised oscillator draws 17 watts on standby, because the oven and its [[cooling-fan]] are held powered whenever the instrument is plugged in — behaviour that is intentional, since an oscillator kept at temperature is the point of the option.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-647" title="EEVblog #647 - Agilent 53131A Frequency Counter Oven Upgrade">647</a></sup> At the other extreme, one [[oscilloscope]]'s eight watts of standby went largely to running a heartbeat [[led]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-800" title="EEVblog #800 - Siglent 1000X Oscilloscope Teardown">800</a></sup>

## Measured figures in test equipment

Standby consumption in bench instruments varies enormously and correlates poorly with price or brand:

- Agilent InfiniiVision 2000/3000X: about 6.5 watts, at 247 V mains and roughly 256 mA.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-164" title="EEVblog #164 - Agilent Fly To The Moon">164</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-187" title="EEVblog #187 - Tektronix TDS2024C Oscilloscope Teardown">187</a></sup>
- Siglent SDS1000X: 8.8 watts, 17.4 VA apparent.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-797" title="EEVblog #797 - Siglent SDS1000X Oscilloscope Review">797</a></sup>
- Siglent 1000X: around eight watts.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-800" title="EEVblog #800 - Siglent 1000X Oscilloscope Teardown">800</a></sup>
- Siglent SDS2000X: almost 7 watts standby, about 40 watts operating.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-864" title="EEVblog #864 - Siglent SDS2000X Series Oscilloscope Teardown">864</a></sup>
- Tektronix MDO4000: 4.8 watts, [[power-factor]] around 0.2 and nearly 24 VA.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-199" title="Tektronix Mixed Domain Oscilloscope MDO4000 Review - EEVblog #199">199</a></sup>
- Tektronix TDS2024C: zero, by way of a mechanical mains switch.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-187" title="EEVblog #187 - Tektronix TDS2024C Oscilloscope Teardown">187</a></sup>
- Uni-T UPO2104CS: 2.13 watts, against 26 watts operating.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1038" title="EEVblog #1038 - Uni-T UPO2104CS Oscilloscope Teardown">1038</a></sup>
- LeCroy WaveJet 354: 1.6 watts, about 7 VA.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-790" title="EEVblog #790 - Lecroy Wavejet 354 Touch Oscilloscope Teardown">790</a></sup>
- Keysight HD3 with the Megazoom V [[asic]]: 1.1 watts.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1638" title="EEVblog 1638 - NEW Keysight Megazoom V ASIC 1GHz HD3 Oscilloscope!">1638</a></sup>
- Rohde & Schwarz HMO1202: half a watt, 6.3 VA, against 18 watts operating.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-842" title="EEVblog #842 - Rohde &amp; Schwarz HMO1202 Oscilloscope Teardown">842</a></sup>
- Fluke 45: just over 1 watt, 8 VA, with the [[transformer]] and output rectifier and filter capacitors left energised.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-791" title="EEVblog #791 - Ebay Fluke 45 Multimeter Teardown">791</a></sup>

Figures in the 5-to-9 watt range are treated as design failures rather than acceptable trade-offs: a six-and-a-half watt standby is the kind of number expected from a 1970s or 1980s VCR, not from modern instrumentation.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-164" title="EEVblog #164 - Agilent Fly To The Moon">164</a></sup> Sub-watt standby in a mains-powered instrument is achievable and is the benchmark against which the rest are judged.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-842" title="EEVblog #842 - Rohde &amp; Schwarz HMO1202 Oscilloscope Teardown">842</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1659" title="EEVblog 1659 - Quick 861 Pro Hot Air Station REVIEW">1659</a></sup>

## Power factor and apparent power

Standby power factor is almost always poor, because the small auxiliary supply is lightly loaded and non-linear. A scope drawing half a watt real still presents 6.3 VA; one drawing 1.6 watts presents about 7 VA at a power factor of 0.23; one drawing 4.8 watts presents nearly 24 VA.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-842" title="EEVblog #842 - Rohde &amp; Schwarz HMO1202 Oscilloscope Teardown">842</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-790" title="EEVblog #790 - Lecroy Wavejet 354 Touch Oscilloscope Teardown">790</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-199" title="Tektronix Mixed Domain Oscilloscope MDO4000 Review - EEVblog #199">199</a></sup> A hot air station measured 0.07 watts in soft-button standby yet still drew significant apparent power.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1659" title="EEVblog 1659 - Quick 861 Pro Hot Air Station REVIEW">1659</a></sup> The same instruments show much better power factor once operating — 0.64 to 0.86 is typical — because the supply is then properly loaded.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-790" title="EEVblog #790 - Lecroy Wavejet 354 Touch Oscilloscope Teardown">790</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-164" title="EEVblog #164 - Agilent Fly To The Moon">164</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-864" title="EEVblog #864 - Siglent SDS2000X Series Oscilloscope Teardown">864</a></sup>

Domestic consumers are generally billed on real power and not on apparent power, while commercial customers often are.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-971" title="EEVblog #971 - Zero Standby Power TV - BUSTED!">971</a></sup> The generation and distribution system, however, must still be sized for the apparent power, so a poor standby power factor imposes a system cost even where nobody is billed for it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-971" title="EEVblog #971 - Zero Standby Power TV - BUSTED!">971</a></sup>

## Battery-powered standby

In battery products the relevant quantity is sleep current, and the design target is that standby consumption should be negligible against the cell's self-discharge — that is, the product should last its battery's shelf life.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-320" title="EEVblog #320 - Mailbag Monday">320</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1287" title="EEVblog #1287 - Gatekeeper Shopping Trolley Lock Investigated">1287</a></sup>

A handheld [[multimeter]] drawing 75 microamps in sleep mode gives more than a year of standby from an alkaline 9 V cell, making auto-off nearly as good as switching the instrument off.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-60" title="EEVblog #60 - Fluke 117 Multimeter Review and Teardown">60</a></sup> A handheld [[lcr-meter]] specified at 2 microamps powered off measured 0.5 microamps, comfortably inside spec.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-137" title="EEVblog #137 - BK Precision 879B Handheld LCR Meter Review">137</a></sup> Another instrument settling at 55 microamps standby, against a nominal 800 mAh alkaline 9 V battery, works out to roughly 14,000 hours — effectively the shelf life of the cell.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-320" title="EEVblog #320 - Mailbag Monday">320</a></sup> Around 10 microamps is a reasonable figure to assume as typical standby consumption when designing a representative load.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-hSkaZEgrZkY" title="Alkaline Battery Discharge Testing Part 3">•</a></sup>

Low-power programmable logic makes similar numbers available in more complex designs: the Lattice iCE40 LP1K used in a smartwatch, with 1280 logic cells, 64K of RAM and a PLL, has a 21 microamp standby figure.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-761" title="EEVblog #761 - Pebble Time Smartwatch Teardown">761</a></sup> At the extreme, an Amstrad NC100 notepad measured on the order of 10 nanoamps — effectively nothing.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-385" title="EEVblog #385 - Amstrad NC100 Notepad Repair">385</a></sup>

Where standby is the dominant load, the arithmetic is unforgiving in the other direction too. A battery-powered shredder mechanism has roughly a month of standby per 50 mW of consumption before the cell is exhausted, leaving nothing for the motor; multi-year receiver standby is achievable, but only by deliberately reducing consumption.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1136" title="EEVblog #1136 - Banksy Artwork Shredder - Part 2 (Still Trolling!)">1136</a></sup> A shopping trolley lock running a very low-power [[microcontroller]] from a large lithium primary should manage 5 to 10 years, since only the lock and unlock motor drive takes significant current.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1287" title="EEVblog #1287 - Gatekeeper Shopping Trolley Lock Investigated">1287</a></sup>

Software can wreck an otherwise sound standby design. A navigation utility running on a Nokia E71 kept a process alive with the phone in standby, producing a repeating square-wave current pulse of several seconds high and several seconds low, which raised average standby consumption to about 0.18 watts and cut endurance to roughly two days against an advertised 480 hours.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-80" title="EEVblog #80 - Nokia E71 + Garmin Mobile XT = Embedded Hell">80</a></sup>

## Consumer products and aggregate load

Consumer equipment measurements cluster around the one-watt mark. Mini PCs measure 1 W and 1.5 W in standby; a desktop video-editing machine measured about 0.4 watts; an 86-inch [[lcd]] television measured 1.3 watts before its backlight came on; an inkjet printer measured 100 milliwatt standby.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1656" title="EEVblog 1656 - MAILBAG: ElectroPermanent Magnet, DIN PSU&#x27;s, Sponge, Beelink EQ14 N150 PC">1656</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1649" title="EEVblog 1649 - Mailbag: M5stack, Beelink Mini PC, ZOYI LCR, ToughOn Battery">1649</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-462" title="EEVblog #462 - Video Editing PC Assembly">462</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-Ia4xB9wk80o" title="Dumpster Diving 86&quot; LG 4K LCD TV">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1421" title="EEVblog 1421 - Epson Dumpster Hack">1421</a></sup> A Yamaha receiver achieved 0.1 watts using a novel and convoluted low-[[power-supply]] topology built around a half-wave rectified tap with a feedback path through Q1.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-379" title="EEVBlog #379 - Yamaha RX-V557 Receiver Fix">379</a></sup> Another product measured 0.12 watts against a manual figure of about 0.1 watts, which confirmed the mains input and [[fuse]] were intact and pointed the fault at the soft power switch circuitry instead.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-378" title="EEVblog #378 - Dumpster Diving Teardown Repair">378</a></sup> Half a watt is a fair expectation for a well-designed television standby supply.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-915" title="EEVblog #915 - Dumpster Dive LCD TV Salvage">915</a></sup>

Individually small figures aggregate badly. A household audit put whole-house standby at around 100 watts with everything switched off, possibly as low as 90, reducible to 80 or 90 by removing the Wi-Fi and NBN modem.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1505" title="EEVblog 1505 - 120W Home Phantom Power? Audit Time!">1505</a></sup> The contributors are the usual soft-sense devices — printer, games console, DVD player, network hub, television, sound bar, streaming stick — each drawing a watt or so and jumping around too much to read a stable figure.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1505" title="EEVblog 1505 - 120W Home Phantom Power? Audit Time!">1505</a></sup> A well-equipped laboratory was found to be throwing away about a kilowatt in standby alone, much of it in bench supplies and in RF equipment holding oven-controlled crystals at temperature.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-s2KkgI-kyK0" title="The Signal Path Lab">•</a></sup> A whole-house energy monitor with sufficient resolution can be used at night, with everything nominally off, to attribute the residual draw device by device by unplugging plug packs one at a time.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-877" title="EEVblog #877 - Solar Analytics Home Energy Monitoring Installation">877</a></sup>

## Regulation and design practice

Energy [[efficiency]] legislation — Energy Star, and the MEPS regulations — makes it illegal in some countries to sell chargers and similar products that fail to meet a specified standby power figure and efficiency, which can rule out a product concept outright at the proposal stage.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-n4NBUruLyoo" title="The 1000th Video - The Lost Tape">•</a></sup> Consumer-grade brick switching supplies are designed against these rules and are commonly specified below one watt in standby.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1032" title="EEVblog #1032 Part 5 - John Kenny Keysight Interview">1032</a></sup>

Test and measurement has historically had an easier time of it, and instruments built around a mains transformer face a hard floor: with the transformer energised and the supplies on its output engaged, there is a limit to how low standby can go.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1032" title="EEVblog #1032 Part 5 - John Kenny Keysight Interview">1032</a></sup> The consumer architecture — a small always-on supply feeding only the wake-up circuitry, with the power button wired separately from all other front-panel buttons — is migrating into instrumentation and can bring standby down to around 100 milliwatts.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1032" title="EEVblog #1032 Part 5 - John Kenny Keysight Interview">1032</a></sup> The commercial pressure that drives this is partly ergonomic rather than environmental: large mechanical power switches take up panel space and require a plastic rod running fore and aft, so manufacturers prefer soft switches and must then engineer the standby figure down.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1032" title="EEVblog #1032 Part 5 - John Kenny Keysight Interview">1032</a></sup>

A standby indicator LED is a design choice in its own right and can be badly judged. An instrument whose standby LED is bright enough to glare across a darkened lab is behaving like a consumer product rather than professional equipment.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-199" title="Tektronix Mixed Domain Oscilloscope MDO4000 Review - EEVblog #199">199</a></sup>

## Zero-standby claims

Claims of eliminating standby power entirely warrant scrutiny about where the loss actually sits. A sensor-driven [[voltage]] detector IC — a five-pin SOT-23 part designated UBM20, requiring no supply of its own and drawing its operating power from the sensor signal — was demonstrated waking a television from the infrared signal of a standard remote control at a distance, giving a set with no continuous standby draw.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-971" title="EEVblog #971 - Zero Standby Power TV - BUSTED!">971</a></sup> The demonstration is misleading as a product case, because the television's own standby consumption sits alongside the quiescent draw of the plug pack feeding the arrangement; the chip removes the former while the latter, at around 4 VA for a plug pack under no load, remains.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-971" title="EEVblog #971 - Zero Standby Power TV - BUSTED!">971</a></sup> Conventional standby supplies in this application already reach the order of five to ten milliwatts, so energy harvesting is not solving the loss that dominates in mains-powered products.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-971" title="EEVblog #971 - Zero Standby Power TV - BUSTED!">971</a></sup>

The same reasoning applies to folk beliefs about mains outlets themselves consuming power when switched on with nothing plugged into them, which they do not.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-CYm-4gbl1Zc" title="ElectroBOOM Stalks Sydney">•</a></sup>
