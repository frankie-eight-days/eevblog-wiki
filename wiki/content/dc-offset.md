DC offset is a steady (DC) [[voltage]] component superimposed on a signal, which shifts the signal's average value away from zero relative to the reference [[ground]]. It matters in two directions: as an unwanted error term that corrupts measurements, wastes instrument [[dynamic-range|dynamic range]], and can physically damage driven devices; and as a deliberate, useful quantity added by oscilloscopes, probes, and signal generators to position signals or bias circuits.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-685" title="EEVblog #685 - What Is Oscilloscope AC Trigger Coupling?">685</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1417" title="EEVblog 1417 - AC Basics Tutorial Part 1: Alternating Current">1417</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1735" title="EEVblog 1735 - Power Rail Probing &amp; Oscilloscope DC Offset EXPLAINED">1735</a></sup>

## Effect on RMS and noise measurement

A plain RMS measurement does not remove DC offset; by definition the DC component is included in the root-mean-square result, so even a residual offset of a couple of hundred microvolts at the 1 mV/div range can dominate a low-level [[noise]] measurement.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1223" title="EEVblog #1223 - Oscilloscope Standard Deviation Noise Measurement">1223</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1521" title="EEVblog 1521 - CMRR Full Edit">1521</a></sup> The correct quantity is standard deviation, also labelled AC RMS on some instruments, which removes any DC offset component and is the appropriate measure for noise and for [[common-mode-rejection-ratio|CMRR]] measurements.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1223" title="EEVblog #1223 - Oscilloscope Standard Deviation Noise Measurement">1223</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1521" title="EEVblog 1521 - CMRR Full Edit">1521</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1355" title="EEVblog #1355 - NEW Siglent SDS1104X-U $399 4CH Oscilloscope Teardown">1355</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1744" title="EEVblog 1744 - NEW Micsig DP700 High Voltage Differential Probe">1744</a></sup> Switching the [[oscilloscope]] input to [[ac-coupling|AC coupling]] does not necessarily fix the problem, because the residual offset often originates after the input AC coupling [[capacitor]], inside the input amplifiers, and it drifts with temperature and time.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1223" title="EEVblog #1223 - Oscilloscope Standard Deviation Noise Measurement">1223</a></sup>

## Input coupling and triggering

AC input coupling switches in a series capacitor that removes the DC component, passing only frequencies from roughly a few tens of hertz upward.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-685" title="EEVblog #685 - What Is Oscilloscope AC Trigger Coupling?">685</a></sup> Trigger coupling interacts with DC offset separately: with DC trigger coupling, every probed signal that carries a different DC offset requires the [[trigger-level|trigger level]] to be re-adjusted manually, whereas AC trigger coupling removes the DC component at the trigger path so a single trigger point works across signals of differing offset.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-685" title="EEVblog #685 - What Is Oscilloscope AC Trigger Coupling?">685</a></sup>

## Dynamic range and power-rail probing

Measuring [[ripple]] on a [[power-rail|power rail]] forces DC coupling, because the rail voltage itself is part of the measurement; a [[5-volt-rail|5 V rail]] therefore appears as 5 V of DC offset, and without offset correction the signal of interest occupies only a small fraction of the ADC range.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1735" title="EEVblog 1735 - Power Rail Probing &amp; Oscilloscope DC Offset EXPLAINED">1735</a></sup> Oscilloscopes differ in how much input offset they can apply, and not all have the range required for a given rail; using a 10:1 probe attenuates a 5 V rail to 500 mV at the scope input, so only 500 mV of offset is needed, a range essentially any oscilloscope can provide.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1735" title="EEVblog 1735 - Power Rail Probing &amp; Oscilloscope DC Offset EXPLAINED">1735</a></sup> Vendors also disagree on the sign convention of the offset readout: Keysight and Rohde & Schwarz display the offset as a positive voltage when the trace is moved down, while Siglent and Rigol display it as negative, reflecting an input-referred view in which a compensating negative voltage is being added.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1735" title="EEVblog 1735 - Power Rail Probing &amp; Oscilloscope DC Offset EXPLAINED">1735</a></sup>

Offset can instead be removed in the probe. Power-rail probes exist specifically to take out the DC offset before the signal reaches the scope, preserving the full dynamic range of a 12- or 14-bit converter; a low-cost example provides ±24 V of offset, while high-end active probes have offered ±12 V or ±20 V built into the probe rather than the oscilloscope.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1733" title="EEVblog 1733 - Mailbag: $100 2GHz Power Rail Probe, Retro Chip Tester">1733</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1735" title="EEVblog 1735 - Power Rail Probing &amp; Oscilloscope DC Offset EXPLAINED">1735</a></sup> The same principle appears in digitizer design, where a DC offset stage lets a converter digitize only a narrow window such as 2.1 to 2.2 V at full resolution.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-207" title="CleverScope USB Oscilloscope - EEVblog #207 (2 of 3)">207</a></sup> Optical-fibre probes drift with temperature and therefore include built-in [[calibration]] signal generators that null out offsets, with trim resolution on the order of 0.2 mV per step; calibration should be run before critical measurements.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1557" title="EEVblog 1557 - Micsig SigOFIT Optical Fibre Probe + GaN Experiment">1557</a></sup>

## Calibration and drift

Oscilloscope front ends exhibit residual DC offset, particularly on the most sensitive ranges such as 500 µV/div or 2 mV/div, and instruments provide self-calibration routines — run with all inputs disconnected — that generate internal test signals and correct channel offset and gain.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-369" title="EEVblog #369 - Rigol DS2000 Oscilloscope Playing Around">369</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-480" title="EEVblog #480 - 300MHz Owon SDS Oscilloscope">480</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-487" title="EEVblog #487 - Tekway DST1102B Oscilloscope Review">487</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-704" title="EEVBlog #704 - Rigol DS1054Z Oscilloscope Features Review">704</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-OBOgiMA89Ks" title="My Keysight 1000X Oscilloscope FAILED :-(">•</a></sup> Self-calibration has cleared gross out-of-box offsets of several divisions and recurring drift on older instruments, and can simply be re-run whenever offset reappears.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-369" title="EEVblog #369 - Rigol DS2000 Oscilloscope Playing Around">369</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1515" title="EEVblog 1515 - Dumpster Tektronix TDS540D 500MHz Oscilloscope LCD Upgrade">1515</a></sup> Offset that appears identically on both channels of a scope, or only on particular ranges such as 200 mV/div and 500 mV/div while other ranges remain correct, indicates a common calibration or configuration fault rather than a per-channel hardware failure, and user calibration can fail outright in such cases.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-OBOgiMA89Ks" title="My Keysight 1000X Oscilloscope FAILED :-(">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1418" title="EEVblog 1418 - The Most EMBARRASSING Repair!">1418</a></sup>

## Generating and exploiting DC offset

Function and arbitrary [[waveform]] generators expose DC offset as a standard output parameter alongside amplitude and load [[impedance]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-143" title="EEVblog #143 - Agilent 2000 X Series Infiniivision Oscilloscope Review">143</a></sup> A generator's DC offset or DC mode, typically produced by a 16-bit DAC, can serve as a precision DC [[voltage-source|voltage source]] with steps as fine as 0.1 mV — finer than many bench power supplies — but the output carries its own residual offset at the zero setting, observed from about 25 µV on a good unit to roughly 500 µV (0.5 mV) on a poorer one.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1560" title="EEVblog 1560 - TIP: Use Your Arb Gen as a High Resolution DC Voltage Source!">1560</a></sup>

Deliberate DC offset is fundamental to single-supply circuit design: biasing an amplifier input at a fraction of the supply [[via]] a [[resistor-divider|resistor divider]] shifts the signal up from ground so it does not clip without a split supply.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-713" title="EEVblog #713 - Voice Recognition - 1980&#x27;s Style">713</a></sup> In composite amplifiers, the DC-precision stage is selected for ultra-low DC offset, low drift, and low noise to handle the DC and low-[[frequency]] path while a faster amplifier handles the high-frequency path.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1609" title="EEVblog 1609 - Composite Amplifier Tutorial + Practical Demo">1609</a></sup>

## DC offset as a fault and damage mechanism

Sustained DC offset damages devices that require AC drive. "DC offsets on an [[lcd|LCD]] will eventually kill them": a segment driven from a single 5 V logic source sits at a 2.5 V average relative to the common pin, and correct drive requires the average value relative to the common pin to be zero, achieved by driving the segment both positive and negative; burst or duty-cycle tricks lower the average but do not eliminate the DC.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1045" title="EEVblog #1045 - How To Drive an LCD">1045</a></sup> Offset also degrades contrast — the same peak-to-peak drive can vanish or come good purely by shifting the DC level.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1045" title="EEVblog #1045 - How To Drive an LCD">1045</a></sup>

Unwanted offset shows up as a diagnostic symptom in many instruments. Clamp meters require a zero function to null DC offset before measuring, and a healthy unit should be out by only a few least-significant digits.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-dv0B_WqL7w4" title="UniT 210E Current Clamp Teardown + Brymen Comparison">•</a></sup> [[current|Current]] probes provide degauss/auto-zero cycles and an offset (trace position) control to null the DC component and compensate offset in the measured waveform.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1413" title="EEVblog 1413 - Oscilloscope Current Probe TEARDOWN + DEMO">1413</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-296" title="EEVblog #296 - AIM-TTi I-Prober 520 Current Probe Review">296</a></sup> A [[multimeter]]'s pulse-width measurement was observed to become inaccurate in the presence of a high DC offset of 2.5 V while remaining correct at low offsets.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-249" title="EEVblog #249 - Agilent U1272A Multimeter Review">249</a></sup> Retro composite-video outputs can carry a significant DC offset that a series coupling capacitor removes.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1021" title="EEVblog #1021 - Retro Radio Shack TRS-80 Color Computer">1021</a></sup> An unexpected DC offset on an audio power output is evidence of a fault such as [[oscillation]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1072" title="EEVblog #1072 - KRK Rokit 6 Studio Monitor Speaker Repair">1072</a></sup> A silicon change to a widely used dual op-amp introduced an offset problem serious enough that a sustained [[offset-voltage|offset voltage]] could damage the oscilloscope front ends the part is used in.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1752" title="EEVblog 1752 - Texas Instruments SCREWED UP the NE5532!">1752</a></sup> Low-cost instruments have shipped with fixed offsets of several hundred millivolts and no means to adjust them, verifiable against a reference oscilloscope.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1582" title="EEVblog 1582 - FNIRSI DPOX180H 180MHz DPO for $123?">1582</a></sup>
