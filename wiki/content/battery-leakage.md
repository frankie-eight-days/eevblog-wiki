Battery leakage is the escape of [[electrolyte]] from a primary cell into the equipment it powers. In alkaline cells the electrolyte is potassium hydroxide, and once outside the cell it reacts with atmospheric carbon dioxide to form potassium carbonate, the white crystalline growth that appears on corroded [[battery]] terminals.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1274" title="EEVblog #1274 - Long Term Alkaline Battery Leakage Testing">1274</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1296" title="EEVblog #1296 - Alkaline Battery Leakage Testing 2 - Electric Boogaloo">1296</a></sup> The residue is caustic, eats away battery terminals and [[pcb|PCB]] tracks, and is the single most common reason a piece of otherwise sound equipment is found dead after long storage.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1274" title="EEVblog #1274 - Long Term Alkaline Battery Leakage Testing">1274</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1497" title="EEVblog 1497 - RIP Fluke. Thanks Energizer. NOT.">1497</a></sup> It is common enough in infrequently used instruments that a [[multimeter]] left with cells installed should be treated as a candidate for it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1636" title="EEVblog 1636 - Ultimate Multimeter Beginners Guide!">1636</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1497" title="EEVblog 1497 - RIP Fluke. Thanks Energizer. NOT.">1497</a></sup>

## Where a cell leaks and why

An [[alkaline-battery|alkaline cell]]'s outer can is the positive terminal and is sealed metal; there is no seal at the positive end.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-H-yOP-KSMC4" title="4K AA Alkaline Battery Leakage">•</a></sup> The only seal is the rubber one at the negative end, so that is where electrolyte escapes.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1296" title="EEVblog #1296 - Alkaline Battery Leakage Testing 2 - Electric Boogaloo">1296</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-VZuebPVrzI8" title="Fluke 1AC Voltage Detection Stick Repair">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1274" title="EEVblog #1274 - Long Term Alkaline Battery Leakage Testing">1274</a></sup> Leakage apparently originating at the positive terminal does occur, but it is the rare case: the liquid has left the negative seal, run down the outside of the can, and pooled at the other end according to how the cell happened to be stored.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-H-yOP-KSMC4" title="4K AA Alkaline Battery Leakage">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1508" title="EEVblog 1508 - We FINALLY Got Alkaline Battery LEAKAGE!">1508</a></sup> Storage orientation therefore governs how far the damage spreads and what it reaches.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-EcdjlD2rbuw" title="HORRIFIC 4K Alkaline Battery Leakage :-(">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-VZuebPVrzI8" title="Fluke 1AC Voltage Detection Stick Repair">•</a></sup>

Escaped electrolyte is normally found in its final crystalline state; catching it while still liquid is unusual, because it crystallises quickly.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1349" title="EEVblog #1349 - Energizer Battery Leakage - MADE IN USA!">1349</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1497" title="EEVblog 1497 - RIP Fluke. Thanks Energizer. NOT.">1497</a></sup> The liquid phase has no detectable smell.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1349" title="EEVblog #1349 - Energizer Battery Leakage - MADE IN USA!">1349</a></sup>

Draining a cell close to zero and leaving it there provokes leakage. A Duracell discharged fully and left in place for a matter of hours or a day longer than its companions leaked, while the companions did not.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-789" title="EEVblog #789 - Batteriser Monkey BUSTED!">789</a></sup> Published work has been cited to the effect that cells discharged at high [[current]] and then left standing are the most likely to leak, which is why deliberate leakage attempts discharge to roughly 90 percent so that internal pressure can build.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-hSkaZEgrZkY" title="Alkaline Battery Discharge Testing Part 3">•</a></sup> Manufacturer advisories against mixing old with new cells, or mixing brands, rest on a related mechanism: when a series string discharges, the still-good cells can reverse-charge the ones that die first.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1274" title="EEVblog #1274 - Long Term Alkaline Battery Leakage Testing">1274</a></sup>

## What the damage looks like

The electrolyte creeps. In a [[voltage]] detection stick it ran down the outside of the cell, entered the board through a [[via]], spread underneath an IC and accumulated there, damaging a pad — damage invisible from the outside of the board and consistent with the fault, since the circuit is high [[impedance]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-VZuebPVrzI8" title="Fluke 1AC Voltage Detection Stick Repair">•</a></sup> It attacks more than metal: it has been observed eating away a plastic battery post entirely,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1564" title="EEVblog 1564 - Agilent/Keysight U1273AX Multimeter Repair">1564</a></sup> lifting [[solder-mask|solder mask]] off copper,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1497" title="EEVblog 1497 - RIP Fluke. Thanks Energizer. NOT.">1497</a></sup> and removing the printed markings from [[resistor]] bodies.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-p8T4Dtc6OKk" title="The CRUSTIEST bit of electronics EVER!">•</a></sup> On an Acorn Archimedes A3000 a leaked battery left green corrosion through the chip contacts and into the keyboard flat-flex [[connector]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-507" title="EEVblog #507 - The First ARM Processor Computer - Acorn Archimedes A3000">507</a></sup>

Damage is not always proportional to appearance. A leak that looks unremarkable may have seeped into tracks,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1497" title="EEVblog 1497 - RIP Fluke. Thanks Energizer. NOT.">1497</a></sup> while a board covered in visible residue can turn out to have nothing wrong with it beyond dirty contacts, with the contacts themselves still working.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1564" title="EEVblog 1564 - Agilent/Keysight U1273AX Multimeter Repair">1564</a></sup> The diagnostic rule is that when a product with battery leakage is faulty, the leakage is the first suspect, but it still has to be confirmed against the actual failure — in one Fluke 3000 repair the leakage had not reached the display circuitry at all, ruling it out as the cause.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1564" title="EEVblog 1564 - Agilent/Keysight U1273AX Multimeter Repair">1564</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-qmcSEJehqZM" title="Energizer Battery Leak Fluke 3000 Multimeter &quot;Repair&quot;">•</a></sup>

Because the residue continues to attack metal, the standard treatment is to neutralise it with a mild acid — white vinegar, or lemon juice — applied with a cotton bud, followed by [[isopropyl-alcohol|isopropyl alcohol]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1497" title="EEVblog 1497 - RIP Fluke. Thanks Energizer. NOT.">1497</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-VZuebPVrzI8" title="Fluke 1AC Voltage Detection Stick Repair">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-2QZ8GWwzXXY" title="Tektronix Multimeter Repair-O-Thon">•</a></sup> Left uncleaned, the rot continues.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-j_eaXfmRB8Q" title="Alkaline Battery Leakage Testing - Part 1">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-VZuebPVrzI8" title="Fluke 1AC Voltage Detection Stick Repair">•</a></sup>

## Brand reputation versus controlled testing

Anecdotal culprits are named confidently and inconsistently: every Duracell leaks, every Energizer leaks, this brand leaks and that one does not.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1274" title="EEVblog #1274 - Long Term Alkaline Battery Leakage Testing">1274</a></sup> Duracell attracts the reputation strongly enough to be nicknamed accordingly,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-2QZ8GWwzXXY" title="Tektronix Multimeter Repair-O-Thon">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1508" title="EEVblog 1508 - We FINALLY Got Alkaline Battery LEAKAGE!">1508</a></sup> but leaking Energizers are equally well documented, including cells only weeks or months old sent through the post inside a device.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-rpGRqlI_VkQ" title="Energizer Battery Leakage Followup">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1497" title="EEVblog 1497 - RIP Fluke. Thanks Energizer. NOT.">1497</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1349" title="EEVblog #1349 - Energizer Battery Leakage - MADE IN USA!">1349</a></sup> A long-term controlled experiment was begun in March 2019 with seven brands, discharged in several different ways, specifically to test whether any brand bias exists.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1508" title="EEVblog 1508 - We FINALLY Got Alkaline Battery LEAKAGE!">1508</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-j_eaXfmRB8Q" title="Alkaline Battery Leakage Testing - Part 1">•</a></sup>

The result after ten months on a continuously connected 47 ohm load was zero leakage and zero crystalline growth across all brands, including the ones with the worst reputation, and the same for the half-drained set.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1274" title="EEVblog #1274 - Long Term Alkaline Battery Leakage Testing">1274</a></sup> A null result of this kind admits two readings: either the brands are genuinely fine, or the test conditions were wrong.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1274" title="EEVblog #1274 - Long Term Alkaline Battery Leakage Testing">1274</a></sup> A repeat with a different discharge methodology followed, in the knowledge that results would not be available for six to nine months.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1296" title="EEVblog #1296 - Alkaline Battery Leakage Testing 2 - Electric Boogaloo">1296</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1350" title="EEVblog #1350 - Fake Energizer Batteries?">1350</a></sup> Only after roughly two years did any of the original cells fail; Panasonic came out worst, and essentially none of the Duracells or Energizers in the test failed at all.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1508" title="EEVblog 1508 - We FINALLY Got Alkaline Battery LEAKAGE!">1508</a></sup>

The experiments also disposed of one popular hypothesis. Since cells in a product sit under spring pressure from the holder contacts, physical tension on the end of the cell was proposed as the trigger; none of the cells held in battery holders leaked, so on this small scale the mechanism can be discounted.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1508" title="EEVblog 1508 - We FINALLY Got Alkaline Battery LEAKAGE!">1508</a></sup>

Statistically the exercise is difficult. What the test is really hunting is the tail of the manufacturing distribution — outlying faults in the pressure vents — so with a failure rate on the order of 1 percent, hundreds of cells per type would be needed to make a leak likely at all.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1296" title="EEVblog #1296 - Alkaline Battery Leakage Testing 2 - Electric Boogaloo">1296</a></sup> A later attempt built a circuit supplied by a correspondent who reported that it made batteries leak every time, within 30 to 40 days, on multiple brands; the working suspicion was that a pulsed rather than constant load is what matters.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1653" title="EEVblog 1653 - Alkaline Battery Leakage Testing 2 - Electric Boogaloo">1653</a></sup> That attempt also failed to produce leakage.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-9O-fyw8CyIY" title="Dumpster Diving Monitors vs Chair">•</a></sup> Meanwhile the original cells from the first test did eventually leak, but only many years later.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1653" title="EEVblog 1653 - Alkaline Battery Leakage Testing 2 - Electric Boogaloo">1653</a></sup>

## Other chemistries

Carbon-zinc dry cells contain no potassium hydroxide, so they cannot produce the characteristic potassium carbonate growth; if such a cell leaks the substance is something else, and a cell that does contain KOH is not a proper carbon-zinc cell.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1508" title="EEVblog 1508 - We FINALLY Got Alkaline Battery LEAKAGE!">1508</a></sup> Lithium thionyl chloride primaries are liquid cells in a sealed container, so in principle they can leak, but as parts designed for 20-plus-year service they effectively do not without serious abuse.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-WWMXJLhPVdA" title="34,000 Hour Battery Life Fluke 37 Multimeter">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1371" title="EEVblog #1371 - A 10,000hr Battery Life Bench Multimeter?">1371</a></sup> Lithium coin and backup cells in instruments are routinely found intact after a decade or more,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-755" title="EEVblog #755 - Fluke PM2812 PSU Ebay Score">755</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-783" title="EEVblog #783 - Dumpster Dive Power Macs">783</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-620" title="EEVblog #620 - Repair: Stanford Research SR650 Programmable Filter">620</a></sup> though replacement as a matter of course is the sensible move when returning vintage gear to service.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-755" title="EEVblog #755 - Fluke PM2812 PSU Ebay Score">755</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-620" title="EEVblog #620 - Repair: Stanford Research SR650 Programmable Filter">620</a></sup>

## Practical consequences

The operative rule is not to leave alkaline cells in equipment that is used infrequently, and specifically not in expensive test gear.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-2Y1tVvllklc" title="9V Duracell Alkaline Battery Leakage">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1497" title="EEVblog 1497 - RIP Fluke. Thanks Energizer. NOT.">1497</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-FgkD8K0Ssdc" title="Fluke 287 Repair - My Arse">•</a></sup> Discovering leakage in one meter is grounds for systematically checking the rest.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1497" title="EEVblog 1497 - RIP Fluke. Thanks Energizer. NOT.">1497</a></sup> The cost is not theoretical: leakage has written off rare prototype instruments, with the back cover stuck fast to the cells,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-EcdjlD2rbuw" title="HORRIFIC 4K Alkaline Battery Leakage :-(">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1668" title="EEVblog 1668 - HORRIFIC Alkaline Battery Leakage in Rare Prototype Multimeter">1668</a></sup> rendered vintage systems beyond economical repair,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-u6Qd9KfQOzQ" title="EEVblog Bunker Rummage + CLEANUP">•</a></sup> and generated enough demand for replacement battery doors — corroded or with the contacts snapped off — to justify stocking them as a spare part.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1670" title="EEVblog 1670 - BM786 Multimeter BATTERY LEAKAGE REPAIR + GIVEAWAY">1670</a></sup>

Not all battery-adjacent faults are leakage. Excess drain from a meter's battery can equally be a leaky [[capacitor]] across the battery terminals, and a [[diode]] plus two capacitors across the supply are the components to examine first in that case.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1449" title="EEVblog 1449 - What Causes Excess Battery Drain? (BM235)">1449</a></sup> Conversely, a cheap instrument may be undermined by leakage before it is ever used, simply from sitting in storage with its cell installed.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-872" title="EEVblog #872 - Mailbag">872</a></sup>
