A series connection joins components end to end so that the same [[current]] flows through every element in the chain while their voltages add.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-932" title="EEVblog #932 - How Does A HV Differential Probe Work?">932</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-212" title="EEVblog #212 - DIY Decade Resistance Substitution Boxes">212</a></sup> It is the standard way of building a useful [[voltage]] out of devices that individually produce or withstand only a small one — cells, solar cells, LEDs, capacitors, thermoelectric pellets — and equally the standard way of building an adjustable total from fixed steps, as in a decade [[resistance]] box.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-212" title="EEVblog #212 - DIY Decade Resistance Substitution Boxes">212</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1586" title="EEVblog 1586 -  Stranger Things Decade Resistor Box + Repair">1586</a></sup> Its defining weakness follows directly from the shared current path: every element carries the whole current, so the chain is only as good as its worst member.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1217" title="EEVblog #1217 - My Home Solar Power System FAILED!">1217</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1426" title="EEVblog 1426 - WOW! This Problem DROPS Solar Output by 20% !">1426</a></sup>

## Adding voltages

Stacking cells is the most common application. Two lithium thionyl chloride cells at 3.6 V nominal give 7.2 V, comfortably more than the 6.5 V a meter needs.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-WWMXJLhPVdA" title="34,000 Hour Battery Life Fluke 37 Multimeter">•</a></sup> A 9 V [[alkaline-battery|alkaline battery]] is internally a series stack of smaller cells spot welded together.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-518" title="EEVblog #518 - 9V Alkaline Battery Construction">518</a></sup> Model aircraft packs follow the same pattern, a three-cell pack being three cells in series for 11.1 V nominal.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-397" title="EEVblog #397 - Turnigy Accucell 6 Charger Teardown">397</a></sup> [[battery|Battery]] holders provide the series link mechanically, with a terminal plate joining adjacent cells.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-292" title="EEVblog #292 - Johnny B. Goode">292</a></sup> Coin cells are stacked the same way where 3.3 V is insufficient and 5 V is wanted.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1286" title="EEVblog #1286 - Mailbag">1286</a></sup> Supercapacitors are stacked to reach a rail voltage: two 2.7 V parts must be in series to run anything above 2.7 V, such as a 3.3 V or [[5-volt-rail|5 V rail]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1511" title="EEVblog 1511 - Solar Analytics System FAIL! (+ Dave gets ZAPPED!)">1511</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-WFVfbu1Xz3A" title="Solar Analytics 4G Solar Monitor TEARDOWN">•</a></sup> Stacking supercaps to reach 3.2 V for a memory [[lcd|LCD]] and its [[microcontroller]] halves the available capacity, which can make the arrangement useless — 100 µAh at 2 µA is only 50 hours.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1242" title="EEVblog #1242 - Memory LCD+Supercaps+Low Power Design">1242</a></sup>

[[solar-power-system|Photovoltaics]] are built series-first at every level. A silicon [[solar-cell|solar cell]] is effectively a [[diode]] producing about 0.6 V, and a typical residential panel strings 60 of them in a 6-by-10 arrangement to give roughly 36 to 40 V.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1426" title="EEVblog 1426 - WOW! This Problem DROPS Solar Output by 20% !">1426</a></sup> Panels are then strung in series into an array: twelve panels produce a nominal 450 V DC, which is dangerous in its own right.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1426" title="EEVblog 1426 - WOW! This Problem DROPS Solar Output by 20% !">1426</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1217" title="EEVblog #1217 - My Home Solar Power System FAILED!">1217</a></sup> The cells within a panel are connected by a single silver strip zigzagging down and back so that the two terminals emerge at opposite corners.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1385" title="EEVblog 1385 - 8kW Home Solar Power System EXPANSION!">1385</a></sup> Solar shingles have traditionally been installed in the same series configuration.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-938" title="EEVblog #938 - Tesla Solar Roofs -  Are They Viable?">938</a></sup> Small amorphous cells behave identically at small scale, three in series giving 3.4 V.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-48" title="EEVblog #48 - Solar Power Hope">48</a></sup>

Thermoelectric modules are a special case: the bismuth telluride pellets are electrically in series but thermally in parallel, forming a long series chain of PN junctions across the metallized ceramic substrate [[via]] printed conductive traces.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-664" title="EEVblog #664 - Peltier TEG Energy Harvesting Experiments">664</a></sup> A module may contain 50 or 100 pellets in series.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-664" title="EEVblog #664 - Peltier TEG Energy Harvesting Experiments">664</a></sup> Thermoelectric harvesters can likewise be strung to raise [[output-voltage|output voltage]], but stringing does not create energy that is not there: at roughly 2 µW per square centimetre, a handful of elements still yields only single-digit to ten microwatts.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1383" title="EEVblog 1383 - Human Battery Energy Harvesting BUSTED">1383</a></sup>

LEDs are almost always driven in series rather than parallel. A series string needs a higher [[compliance-voltage|compliance voltage]] but a much lower current, so the wiring can be thinner and the board easier to manufacture — around 50 mA for a laptop [[backlight]] string rather than the far higher current a parallel arrangement would draw.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-pKV_JiauAE4" title="Dumpster Dive - HP Envy Touch PC Repair">•</a></sup> The same logic drives DIY lighting: six 6 V [[led|LED]] strips in series need a 36 V supply.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1372" title="EEVblog #1372 - DIY PCB Photography Light Box">1372</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-6ZH2KeplSrs" title="DIY Product/PCB Photography Light Box - Part 3">•</a></sup> Where a panel is too large for one string, it is broken into parallel strings of twelve LEDs each, driven at constant current because constant voltage would be unworkable.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1343" title="EEVblog #1343 - DEAD Deck Cree LED&#x27;s">1343</a></sup> A single indicator LED gets a [[series-resistor|series resistor]] in the lead, a 2k2 costing about 2 mA extra from the battery.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-182" title="EEVblog #182 - Rode Videomic Shotgun Microphone Hack">182</a></sup>

Bench instruments are stacked the same way. Two 30 V [[power-supply|power supply]] modules wired in series give a 60 V output, and three would give 90 V.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-755" title="EEVblog #755 - Fluke PM2812 PSU Ebay Score">755</a></sup> Two channels of a 30 V, 3 A supply in series reach 60 V, enough to test a 48 V input regulator.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-895" title="EEVblog #895 - BEC Pro Model Airplane Regulator Testing">895</a></sup> A 0 to 100 V, 2 A per channel supply becomes 0 to 200 V at 2 A in series, or 0 to 100 V at 4 A in parallel.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1402" title="EEVblog 1402 - Rohde &amp; Schwarz NGA100 PSU Teardown + GIVEAWAY">1402</a></sup> Not every instrument permits it — a battery emulator limited to a single solar-panel pole cannot have multiple ones put in series.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-Y2rcx4vKxlc" title="Unboxing the Keysight E36731A DC Power Supply, Electronic Load, and Battery Emulator">•</a></sup> [[transformer|Transformer]] primaries use the same trick in reverse: a split primary wired in parallel gives the 115 V configuration, and a jumper change putting the two coils in series gives 240 V, doubling the measured primary resistance from 45 Ω.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1189" title="EEVblog #1189 - Ebay $140 6.5 digit DMM Bargain!?">1189</a></sup>

## Adding voltage ratings

Series-connected passives share the applied voltage, so their ratings add. Where a part above 1 kV is hard to obtain, three 1 kV capacitors in series give a 3 kV total, at the cost of a [[capacitance]] divided by three.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-932" title="EEVblog #932 - How Does A HV Differential Probe Work?">932</a></sup> DMM input networks whack [[resistor]] arrays in series for extra standoff, each element contributing a couple of hundred volts up to a 1000 V rating.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1382" title="EEVblog 1382 - Keysight EDU34450A 5.5 digit Bench Multimeter TEARDOWN">1382</a></sup> A defibrillator [[capacitor]] bank uses five 400 V working-voltage capacitors in series for a 2000 V maximum.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-909" title="EEVblog #909 - Heart Defibrillator Teardown">909</a></sup> Zener diodes add likewise: a 30 V and a 20 V part in series give a total 50 V drop.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-952" title="EEVblog #952 - Nixie Tube Display Project - Part 3">952</a></sup> Series MOSFETs appear where standoff rather than current handling is the requirement, drain connecting to source along a P-channel pair and the same for the N-channel pair.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1005" title="EEVblog #1005 - Keithley 2302 Battery Simulator Teardown">1005</a></sup> The same principle extends to non-isolated converters: eight LLC stages in series produce an isolated 800 V assembly even though no individual stage is isolated between primary and secondary.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1737" title="EEVblog 1737 - Alex Lidow: Inventor of the Power MOSFET">1737</a></sup>

## The weakest-link problem

Because current is forced through every element, one bad member takes down the whole chain. If a single panel in a twelve-panel string fails, the string dies and the array produces nothing.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1217" title="EEVblog #1217 - My Home Solar Power System FAILED!">1217</a></sup> Shading has the same effect without any failure: the string current is still forced through the shaded cell, and a very slight, diffuse shadow — from an [[antenna]] pole or even a guy wire — is enough to upset a string [[inverter]]'s entire array.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1426" title="EEVblog 1426 - WOW! This Problem DROPS Solar Output by 20% !">1426</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-NoIjTK249D0" title="How a SMALL SHADOW can drop Solar Power Output by 20%!">•</a></sup> Panels therefore carry bypass diodes, typically three per panel, to route current around a shaded section.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1426" title="EEVblog 1426 - WOW! This Problem DROPS Solar Output by 20% !">1426</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-938" title="EEVblog #938 - Tesla Solar Roofs -  Are They Viable?">938</a></sup>

Batteries in series fail in a nastier way. Cells from the same packet differ slightly in capacity and ESR, so discharging a long series string drives the weakest cell into reverse charge once it is exhausted.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-hSkaZEgrZkY" title="Alkaline Battery Discharge Testing Part 3">•</a></sup> Discharging thirteen packs of two cells at a constant 100 mA is attractive because it tests everything at once, but with unknown and mismatched capacities across brands the result is unreliable — a string expected to read about 26 V read zero while 44 mA still flowed.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1296" title="EEVblog #1296 - Alkaline Battery Leakage Testing 2 - Electric Boogaloo">1296</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-RFb3TwWzza0" title="Alkaline Battery Leakage Test Setup">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-XDjyY48u0PU" title="Alkaline Battery Discharge Testing Part 2">•</a></sup> Discharging in small matched groups, two at a time, avoids the problem at the cost of time.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-hSkaZEgrZkY" title="Alkaline Battery Discharge Testing Part 3">•</a></sup> The same mismatch is why multi-cell packs need balancing: an unbalanced cell in a three-cell pack shortens the pack's life or ends it violently, so a charger buffers and taps each cell node individually.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-397" title="EEVblog #397 - Turnigy Accucell 6 Charger Teardown">397</a></sup> Production packs apply this at both levels, four parallel strings in series for a 12.8 V pack with the management system tapping each string.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-8P8Af5SR57U" title="Goal Zero Yeti 400 Lithium Battery Follow--up">•</a></sup> Series supercapacitors get balance resistors across them for the same reason.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-WFVfbu1Xz3A" title="Solar Analytics 4G Solar Monitor TEARDOWN">•</a></sup> Series capacitors carry a poor reputation in industry on these grounds, though they remain a legitimate engineering trade-off.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-WFVfbu1Xz3A" title="Solar Analytics 4G Solar Monitor TEARDOWN">•</a></sup>

## Series as a construction technique

Decade resistance boxes are wired entirely in series, one decade cascading into the next, so the highest decade dominates and each lower switch trims the total in finer steps.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-212" title="EEVblog #212 - DIY Decade Resistance Substitution Boxes">212</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-510" title="EEVblog #510 - Mailbag">510</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1586" title="EEVblog 1586 -  Stranger Things Decade Resistor Box + Repair">1586</a></sup> Because the elements are in series, the physical order of the decades on the board is irrelevant.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-212" title="EEVblog #212 - DIY Decade Resistance Substitution Boxes">212</a></sup> Within a single decade using a dual-wipe switch, four resistors sit between contacts and a fifth returns to the common, snaking the whole set into one series path.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1586" title="EEVblog 1586 -  Stranger Things Decade Resistor Box + Repair">1586</a></sup> Capacitance substitution boxes invert the arrangement, using [[parallel-connection|parallel connection]] because capacitances add in parallel.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-510" title="EEVblog #510 - Mailbag">510</a></sup>

Series connection also serves as a deliberate load or measurement technique. Putting the two filaments of a lamp in series across the outer terminals makes a convenient 24 V, four-and-a-half to five amp load.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-gqzZHbEfWDU" title="KoradFollowup Charles">•</a></sup> A current-limiting circuit is inserted in series with an existing supply to measure its effect on output, against a 1630 lux benchmark.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-392" title="EEVblog #392 - 555 LED PWM Hack">392</a></sup> With two supply channels in series, the input powers of both must be summed to get the true total.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-895" title="EEVblog #895 - BEC Pro Model Airplane Regulator Testing">895</a></sup> A thermal cutout switch is placed in series with the load it protects.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1694" title="EEVblog 1694 - Scarlet Rae Teardown + Design Discussion with Xentronics">1694</a></sup> And where a device is a two-terminal series element regardless of pin count, as with a dual-anode part whose anodes are internally in series, the pinout distinction stops mattering.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1049" title="EEVblog #1049 - Mailbag">1049</a></sup>
