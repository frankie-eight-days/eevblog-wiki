A service manual is the document a manufacturer produces for the people who repair its equipment, as distinct from the operating manual written for its users. At its most complete it contains schematics, board overlays, a theory of operation, disassembly procedures, test point voltages, troubleshooting flow charts, alignment and [[calibration]] procedures, and a [[bill-of-materials]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-116" title="EEVblog #116 - Retro Teardown - Tandy Radio Shack TRS-80 Model 100">116</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1376" title="EEVblog 1376 - Tandy 102 Vintage Computer REPAIR">1376</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1662" title="EEVblog 1662 - Vintage Tandy 200 Portable Computer Claytons Repair/Teardown">1662</a></sup> Whether such a document exists and can be obtained is often the single largest factor in whether a piece of equipment is economically repairable at all, which is why the first step in a repair is frequently to download the manual before any measurement is taken.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-536" title="EEVblog #536 - HP35670A DSA Repair">536</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-502" title="EEVblog #502 - $19 Hameg Analog Oscilloscope">502</a></sup>

## What a full manual contains

The defining section of a thorough manual is the theory of operation: a narrative explanation of how the circuit actually works, block by block, rather than a bare [[schematic]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1662" title="EEVblog 1662 - Vintage Tandy 200 Portable Computer Claytons Repair/Teardown">1662</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-116" title="EEVblog #116 - Retro Teardown - Tandy Radio Shack TRS-80 Model 100">116</a></sup> It is normally accompanied by a system block diagram separated into boards, timing diagrams, and flow charts.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-ZBVyJaLAZDY" title="Scanning Vintage Computer Service Manuals - Tandy 1100FD Laptop Computer">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1452" title="EEVblog 1452 - Fluke PM3370B Combiscope REPAIR">1452</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1662" title="EEVblog 1662 - Vintage Tandy 200 Portable Computer Claytons Repair/Teardown">1662</a></sup> The TRS-80 Model 100 documentation included the complete theory of operation, all schematics, the bill of materials and the [[pcb-layout]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-116" title="EEVblog #116 - Retro Teardown - Tandy Radio Shack TRS-80 Model 100">116</a></sup> The Apple IIc manual ran to roughly 560 pages and included the ROM dump listing.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-788" title="EEVblog #788 - Apple IIC Teardown">788</a></sup>

Disassembly instructions are the other section with no substitute. A 1985 camcorder manual carried hand-drawn diagrams showing the removal of each individual nut and screw, internal photographs identifying every part, exploded views, and multi-page foldout schematics annotated with test point voltages and signal flow.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-381" title="EEVblog #381 - How Do Ultrasonic Delay Lines Work?">381</a></sup> A 2013 Sony amplifier manual carried detailed disassembly flow diagrams covering the same ground.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1602" title="EEVblog 1602 - Sony Amplifier REPAIR ADVENTURE">1602</a></sup> Mixing consoles and cameras receive comparable treatment, with complex disassembly procedures documented step by step.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-840" title="EEVblog #840 - Yamaha M3000 Mixing Console Teardown">840</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1429" title="EEVblog 1429 - Sony RX100 Camera Troubleshooting Part 2">1429</a></sup> Without such a section, disassembly of an unfamiliar assembly degenerates into removing fasteners one at a time and testing for movement after each, with a real risk of breaking heat stakes or plastic catches.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-n_0gccBLEfU" title="Pointless Lenovo Legion 5i Keyboard Upgrade?">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1527" title="EEVblog 1527 - Toshiba T1000LE DOS Vintage Laptop Repair HELL">1527</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1587" title="EEVblog 1587 - Dumpster FAX Teardown">1587</a></sup>

Schematics are not always the deepest content. Some manuals show the internal operation of individual chips alongside the board schematic, with test point voltages marked around them.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-381" title="EEVblog #381 - How Do Ultrasonic Delay Lines Work?">381</a></sup> Others provide a simplified schematic of a single stage — sufficient, for example, to establish that a sealed output stage contains four MOSFETs and two power transistors without removing the seal pads.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-718" title="EEVblog #718 - Keithley 2400 SMU Teardown">718</a></sup> Where full schematics are absent, a block diagram of the analog input circuitry plus the calibration procedure may still be present.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-478" title="EEVblog #478 - Keithley 2015 THD Multimeter Teardown">478</a></sup> Manuals for large industrial supplies sometimes omit schematics entirely while retaining the theory of operation.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1381" title="EEVblog 1381 - Argon Ion Laser 10kW PSU Teardown">1381</a></sup>

## Use in fault-finding

The most immediate use of a manual is locating and interpreting test points. Boards frequently carry no [[silkscreen]] designators at all, so the component overlay in the manual is the only way to identify a part physically.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-905" title="EEVblog #905 - REPAIR: HP85 Vintage Computer">905</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-536" title="EEVblog #536 - HP35670A DSA Repair">536</a></sup> Test point identifiers such as TP240 are meaningless without the manual's table.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-536" title="EEVblog #536 - HP35670A DSA Repair">536</a></sup> On one dynamic signal analyser the manual supplied a chart of connector pin assignments, allowing the ±18 V rails to be measured at 18.3 V and −19.3 V with the board powered outside its normal position.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-538" title="EEVblog #538 - HP35670A DSA Repair - Part 2">538</a></sup> On a Tektronix 2465B every [[voltage]] test point turned out to be brought to a single 14-pin DIP socket, J119, a fact discoverable only from the manual.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1203" title="EEVblog #1203 - REPAIR: Tektronix 2465B Oscilloscope">1203</a></sup>

Manuals also decode diagnostic output. An ADC gateway error message was documented as being derived from the instrument's own source output, which redirected the investigation away from the [[asic]] named in the message.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-540" title="EEVblog #540 - HP35670A DSA Repair - Part 3">540</a></sup> Elsewhere a reset procedure was documented only as holding a particular DIP switch for two seconds.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-710" title="EEVblog #710 - Intercom System Repair">710</a></sup> Rated operating limits appear too: one receiver was specified to operate from about 4.5 V to 6 V.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-573" title="EEVblog #573 - Mailbag">573</a></sup> Test point voltages given in the manual can also mislead when a measured value sits close to the documented one and the fault lies elsewhere, which is a known hazard of the rule that troubleshooting begins with measuring voltages.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-379" title="EEVBlog #379 - Yamaha RX-V557 Receiver Fix">379</a></sup>

Calibration and alignment sections identify the specific adjustment to touch and, critically, where it is. A vertical gain adjustment specified as R145 could not be found by inspection; the manual's adjustment location diagrams placed it on a board other than the expected vertical board.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-208" title="Tektronix 2225 Oscilloscope Teardown and Calibration - EEVblog #208">208</a></sup> The general practice is to adjust only the named control and leave the others alone when the rest of the instrument is in calibration.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-208" title="Tektronix 2225 Oscilloscope Teardown and Calibration - EEVblog #208">208</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-196" title="Tektronix 2225 Analog Oscilloscope - EEVblog #196">196</a></sup>

A comprehensive manual can also be misapplied. A 70-page troubleshooting guide invites exhaustive sequential testing when stepping back and reasoning about the most probable failure — for instance, that an ASIC reporting a fault may simply be unpowered — reaches the answer faster.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-538" title="EEVblog #538 - HP35670A DSA Repair - Part 2">538</a></sup> Manuals are not infallible: in one [[oscilloscope]] manual two [[capacitor]] positions were transposed, a documented error that had been independently rediscovered by others.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1203" title="EEVblog #1203 - REPAIR: Tektronix 2465B Oscilloscope">1203</a></sup>

## Gaps, errors and partial coverage

Even excellent manuals have holes. One analyser's manual omitted the CRT unit entirely, covering only the circuitry the instrument's own manufacturer designed.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-523" title="EEVblog #523 - REPAIR: HP 35660A Dynamic Signal Analyser">523</a></sup> A bench supply manual carried schematics for almost all boards but not for the mains [[power-supply]], the switch mode base board, or the rear board.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-755" title="EEVblog #755 - Fluke PM2812 PSU Ebay Score">755</a></sup> Another manual provided a full schematic and [[pcb]] overlay but no bill of materials cross-reference, so the part number of the front end op-amp could not be determined from it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-529" title="EEVblog #529 - HP 35660A DSA Upgrade Investigation">529</a></sup> A 1,500-page photocopier manual proved so large and so complex that the power supply section could not readily be located within it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1629" title="EEVblog 1629 - Dumpster Find: A3 Colour Photocopier!">1629</a></sup> Fault codes are sometimes undocumented, including flashing-[[led]] codes that turn out to correspond to readable fault registers in the driver chip.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1322" title="EEVblog #1322 - JBL LSR308 Studio Monitor Speaker REPAIR">1322</a></sup>

Where the exact model's manual is unavailable, a related model is often substituted: a B-revision manual for an A-revision [[lcr-meter]], a sibling model for a Sony amplifier, or a regional variant's schematics.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-757" title="EEVblog #757 - HP4263A LCR Meter Teardown">757</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1602" title="EEVblog 1602 - Sony Amplifier REPAIR ADVENTURE">1602</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1246" title="EEVblog #1246 - Dumpster TV to Retro RGB Monitor Conversion">1246</a></sup> This carries risk, as regional and generational variants can differ substantially even under the same model number, and a manual for a later machine may simply not match the earlier one.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1246" title="EEVblog #1246 - Dumpster TV to Retro RGB Monitor Conversion">1246</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1348" title="EEVblog #1348 - World&#x27;s First IBM Compatible PC - The Compaq Portable">1348</a></sup>

At the other extreme, some equipment has no schematic, no block diagram, and not even typical test voltages or test procedures in what documentation exists.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1527" title="EEVblog 1527 - Toshiba T1000LE DOS Vintage Laptop Repair HELL">1527</a></sup> For a high-power supply with no test point voltages marked on the hardware and no obtainable manual or schematic, the alternative is [[reverse-engineering]] the whole assembly.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-814" title="EEVblog #814 - Keysight N8762A 600V 5100W PSU Teardown">814</a></sup> Complicated [[relay]] and interlock wiring in appliances becomes disproportionately difficult to trace for the same reason.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1ngqB4mxZOI" title="Smeg Oven Goes Clunkedy Clunk + Capacitor Go Poop">•</a></sup>

## Design that reduces dependence on the manual

The preferred design practice is one where the manual is not needed for basic servicing: marked voltage test points on the silkscreen, component designators printed on the board, and connector pins labelled with their function.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1185" title="EEVblog #1185 - Dumpster Tek 500MHz TDS540D Scope">1185</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1203" title="EEVblog #1203 - REPAIR: Tektronix 2465B Oscilloscope">1203</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-814" title="EEVblog #814 - Keysight N8762A 600V 5100W PSU Teardown">814</a></sup> One instrument routed a slot in the board so that a probe could reach test points from the reverse side, which is evidence that servicing was considered during design.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-378" title="EEVblog #378 - Dumpster Diving Teardown Repair">378</a></sup> Modular construction serves the same end, since boards can be swapped and isolated without documentation.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-755" title="EEVblog #755 - Fluke PM2812 PSU Ebay Score">755</a></sup> A related documentation technique is capturing signature [[waveform|waveforms]] from a known-good golden board and recording them in the service manual, so a technician probes each point and compares against the documented trace.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src--ON1AdmAMOY" title="Vintage Tek Museum: Curve Bug Signal Debugger">•</a></sup>

## Availability

Availability varies by manufacturer and era rather than by price of the equipment. Full service manuals with schematics have been freely downloadable for old Tektronix scopes, Hameg analog scopes, HP and Agilent instruments, Acorn machines, and Stanford Research gear, the last being noted for full schematics as standard.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-jmb9ICnI8xI" title="Dumpster Tektronix 475 Oscilloscope Repair - Part 1">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-502" title="EEVblog #502 - $19 Hameg Analog Oscilloscope">502</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-523" title="EEVblog #523 - REPAIR: HP 35660A Dynamic Signal Analyser">523</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-507" title="EEVblog #507 - The First ARM Processor Computer - Acorn Archimedes A3000">507</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-358" title="EEVblog #358 - Electronex 2012">358</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-450" title="EEVblog #450 - Ebay Unboxing">450</a></sup> Some manufacturers put the schematics and theory of operation in the ordinary user manual rather than a separate service document.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1012" title="EEVblog #1012 - Best Bargain Ebay Bench Meter? - Fluke 8842A">1012</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1017" title="EEVblog #1017 - Enter The World Of Atto Amps">1017</a></sup> Sony has continued to produce comprehensive service manuals well past the vintage era, including for inexpensive consumer equipment from the late 1990s, published by its quality engineering department.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1243" title="EEVblog #1243 - Sony Boombox REPAIR and Teardown">1243</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-lIohAMnsDcs" title="eevBLAB 94 - Why Dont Companies DO THIS Anymore?">•</a></sup> Siglent releases service manuals for its scopes but without schematics.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-jmb9ICnI8xI" title="Dumpster Tektronix 475 Oscilloscope Repair - Part 1">•</a></sup> Tesla published a full service manual with theory of operation and circuits for the Roadster.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1581" title="EEVblog 1581 - Tesla Roadster now OPEN SOURCE?">1581</a></sup>

Where the manufacturer does not publish, the material circulates through third-party archives, forums, and paid download sites of variable honesty — one paid service returned a file-not-found after payment, another manual cost five dollars and turned out to be for the wrong model revision.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1246" title="EEVblog #1246 - Dumpster TV to Retro RGB Monitor Conversion">1246</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1429" title="EEVblog 1429 - Sony RX100 Camera Troubleshooting Part 2">1429</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-752" title="EEVblog #752 - Original Sony Walkman TPS-L2 Teardown">752</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1672" title="EEVblog 1672 - JBL REPAIR: You&#x27;ll never guess the fault!">1672</a></sup> Third-party publishers have historically filled the gap, notably the Sams Photofact series covering CB and amateur radio equipment and other categories.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1364" title="EEVblog #1364 - Compaq Portable PSU REPAIR">1364</a></sup> Community effort also matters: schematics have been reconstructed from photographs of a manual taken for an auction listing, and a Tandy 1100FD manual missing from the internet for a decade was scanned to searchable OCR and released as 199 pages.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-777" title="EEVblog #777 - Keithley 177 Microvolt DMM Repair">777</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-ZBVyJaLAZDY" title="Scanning Vintage Computer Service Manuals - Tandy 1100FD Laptop Computer">•</a></sup>

Against this, the practical buying rule for second-hand equipment is to confirm the service manual is obtainable before purchase, and to check from the schematic that the design uses off-the-shelf parts.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-502" title="EEVblog #502 - $19 Hameg Analog Oscilloscope">502</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-756" title="EEVblog #756 - Mailbag">756</a></sup>

## Restriction and the repair debate

Restricting service documentation is a recognised barrier to independent repair, alongside withholding spare parts, diagnostic software, and warranty terms voided by opening a product.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-b1wshlHie3c" title="Right to Repair - Shane Rattenbury ACT Minister for Consumer Affairs">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1407" title="EEVblog 1407 - Right to Repair with iFixit Founder Kyle Wiens">1407</a></sup> Copyright has been the usual instrument: Apple sent copyright threats to those posting its service manuals online, removing that information from the internet, and Toshiba Australia issued a takedown against a site sharing manuals on the grounds that they were copyright material for authorised technicians only.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1407" title="EEVblog 1407 - Right to Repair with iFixit Founder Kyle Wiens">1407</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-k4zrmTsZj_s" title="Australia Right to Repair Summit - Session 1 of 4">•</a></sup> iFixit was founded expressly to rewrite the service information that had been taken offline.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1407" title="EEVblog 1407 - Right to Repair with iFixit Founder Kyle Wiens">1407</a></sup>

Survey evidence puts numbers on the problem: nearly half of respondents reported situations with limited or no access to tools or manuals, and about a quarter reported the same for spare parts.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-qDWLTSfrq_E" title="2021 Australia Right to Repair Summit - Session 2 of 4">•</a></sup> Access to technical information and service manuals ranked among the principal technological restrictions identified.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-qDWLTSfrq_E" title="2021 Australia Right to Repair Summit - Session 2 of 4">•</a></sup> In automotive repair the hard-copy manual once available at a local parts store has been displaced by computerised systems, with information as basic as the correct oil blend proving difficult to obtain despite the liability consequences of getting it wrong.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-qDWLTSfrq_E" title="2021 Australia Right to Repair Summit - Session 2 of 4">•</a></sup>

Regulatory responses have centred on making service information a condition rather than a courtesy. France's repairability score, rated 1 to 10, factors in both parts availability and service information availability, which prompted the release of service manuals for flagship smartphones in French but not in other markets.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-k4zrmTsZj_s" title="Australia Right to Repair Summit - Session 1 of 4">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1407" title="EEVblog 1407 - Right to Repair with iFixit Founder Kyle Wiens">1407</a></sup> Proposals under consideration include a positive obligation on manufacturers to supply spare parts, diagnostic equipment and repair manuals to independent repairers.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-xiXucGYuQ6M" title="Australian Right to Repair Inquiry with Productivity Commissioner Paul Lindwall">•</a></sup> Voluntary undertakings to make software and repair manuals available have been used to argue that legislation was unnecessary, after which the underlying problem persisted.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-k4zrmTsZj_s" title="Australia Right to Repair Summit - Session 1 of 4">•</a></sup> Manufacturer [[resistance]] is not purely commercial in its stated form: releasing schematics creates a worldwide support obligation, since manuals must be written, translated into multiple languages, and backed by staff able to answer questions about them.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1032" title="EEVblog #1032 Part 2 - John Kenny Keysight Interview">1032</a></sup>

The historical baseline is that this information used to ship with the product. Televisions carried the circuit diagram inside the back cover so an independent technician could troubleshoot at reasonable cost, and early Apple computers came with full service information and code listings.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-Y0SNOsQOBYk" title="Right to Repair - Random Answers">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-moa3YBdLepg" title="eevBLAB 88 - Right To Repair Questions &amp; Answers">•</a></sup> Manufacturer self-service repair programmes that provide an official repair manual and genuine parts represent a partial return to that position.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-GHbo4v8pahc" title="eevBLAB 97 - Is Apple Serious About Right To Repair? (The Verge)">•</a></sup>
