Solar panel efficiency is the fraction of incident sunlight a [[solar-panel|photovoltaic module]] converts to electrical power, measured against the standard reference irradiance of 1,000 watts per square meter.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1591" title="EEVblog 1591 - MORE Colas Wattway Solar Cycleways in the Netherlands!">1591</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1386" title="EEVblog 1386 - 295W Inverter vs 370W Solar Panel - WTF?">1386</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-sJS2paVW-kc" title="Renewable Energy vs Fossil Fuels Presentation">•</a></sup> A hypothetical 100% efficient panel of one square meter would deliver 1,000 watts under that condition.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-sJS2paVW-kc" title="Renewable Energy vs Fossil Fuels Presentation">•</a></sup> Real silicon modules land around 20 to 22%, which equates to roughly 200 to 220 watts peak per square meter.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1591" title="EEVblog 1591 - MORE Colas Wattway Solar Cycleways in the Netherlands!">1591</a></sup> Because capture area is fixed by whatever roof, vehicle body, or land is available, [[efficiency]] per unit area is the figure that governs almost every practical decision about a [[solar-power-system|photovoltaic]] installation.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1575" title="EEVblog 1575 - TIPA: The World&#x27;s Most Efficient Solar Panel - BUSTED">1575</a></sup>

## Typical and best-case figures

Bulk production cells sit near 19%, while manufacturers such as Panasonic and SunPower reach 21 to 22%, with the best modules approaching 24%.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1025" title="EEVblog #1025 - Minute Physics + Bill Nye Promoting Rayton Solar">1025</a></sup> A representative high-output residential panel is 400 watts at 22.6% efficiency in an area of 1.76 square meters.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1480" title="EEVblog 1480 EXTENDED VERSION - LightYear Zero + Aptera Solar EV">1480</a></sup> Panels improve year on year while getting cheaper, so claims of a large future efficiency advantage tend to be overtaken by ordinary commercial parts before the claimed product exists.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1025" title="EEVblog #1025 - Minute Physics + Bill Nye Promoting Rayton Solar">1025</a></sup> Twenty-five percent is about the maximum a PV cell can be expected to reach.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1591" title="EEVblog 1591 - MORE Colas Wattway Solar Cycleways in the Netherlands!">1591</a></sup>

Space-rated panels beat terrestrial ones: quad-junction gallium arsenide modules reach around 460 watts per square meter, against roughly 250 watts per square meter for a typical rooftop panel.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-wTzQg1N-SLI" title="eevBLAB 127 - Starlink as a Satellite Energy Utility?">•</a></sup> That advantage is irrelevant to grid economics, because launch cost consumes it many times over — putting panels and batteries on each house is at least 21 times more cost-effective than orbiting them and beaming the power down.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-wTzQg1N-SLI" title="eevBLAB 127 - Starlink as a Satellite Energy Utility?">•</a></sup>

## Physical limits

The Shockley-Queisser limit sets a ceiling on what a single-junction [[solar-cell|solar cell]] can achieve.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1575" title="EEVblog 1575 - TIPA: The World&#x27;s Most Efficient Solar Panel - BUSTED">1575</a></sup> A module three times more efficient than the state of the art, at 60%, is physically impossible.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1575" title="EEVblog 1575 - TIPA: The World&#x27;s Most Efficient Solar Panel - BUSTED">1575</a></sup>

A persistent misreading holds that because a panel is about 20% efficient it must reflect the other 80% of the light, so recapturing that reflection through internal mirrors would multiply output — summing 20%, 16%, 12.8% and 10.2% to reach 59%.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1575" title="EEVblog 1575 - TIPA: The World&#x27;s Most Efficient Solar Panel - BUSTED">1575</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1575" title="EEVblog 1575 - TIPA: The World&#x27;s Most Efficient Solar Panel - BUSTED">1575</a></sup> The unused 80% is not sitting in a reflected beam waiting to be caught, and designs built on that premise do not work.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1575" title="EEVblog 1575 - TIPA: The World&#x27;s Most Efficient Solar Panel - BUSTED">1575</a></sup> Any such scheme still has to win on output per unit of capture area, which is what actually matters.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1575" title="EEVblog 1575 - TIPA: The World&#x27;s Most Efficient Solar Panel - BUSTED">1575</a></sup>

## Temperature

Silicon panel efficiency falls as cell temperature rises, at a rough rule of thumb of about 0.5% per degree Celsius.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1630" title="EEVblog 1630 - Solar Panels for Water Pre-Heating?">1630</a></sup> Ratings are given at 25 °C, a temperature panels almost never operate at during a cloudless summer day; at 50 °C output is around 90% of the nominal figure.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-BtQUuD6QRMw" title="Solar Panel Temperature Dependency">•</a></sup> Frame temperatures in peak summer are too hot to touch, generally around 50 °C, with 75 °C or more unlikely in practice.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-BtQUuD6QRMw" title="Solar Panel Temperature Dependency">•</a></sup>

The [[temperature-coefficient|temperature coefficient]] invites the idea of water-cooling panels, with piping bonded to the panel backs feeding a swimming pool, and such products exist.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1630" title="EEVblog 1630 - Solar Panels for Water Pre-Heating?">1630</a></sup> The gain is only about 5%, which does not justify snaking plumbing across a 28-panel array.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1630" title="EEVblog 1630 - Solar Panels for Water Pre-Heating?">1630</a></sup>

## Losses in real installations

Well-designed systems get their efficiency from geometry and siting: panels angled optimally, kept out of shade, fitted with thin high-transmission front glass, and placed close to the load so distribution losses stay small.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-632" title="EEVblog #632 - Solar Roadways Are RUBBISH!">632</a></sup> Large solar farms take the same approach with deliberately engineered power infrastructure.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-632" title="EEVblog #632 - Solar Roadways Are RUBBISH!">632</a></sup>

Shading is severe and disproportionate. A single guy wire supporting a television [[antenna]] dropped the efficiency of the array beneath it by 20%.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1534" title="EEVblog 1534 - Solar Freakin&#x27; RAILways! Sun-Ways DEBUNKED">1534</a></sup> Overhead structures such as catenary poles and wires guarantee shading for some hours every day.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1534" title="EEVblog 1534 - Solar Freakin&#x27; RAILways! Sun-Ways DEBUNKED">1534</a></sup>

Vibration causes micro-cracks in the silicon, which reduce efficiency and can cause outright panel failure; this is why panels must be transported carefully.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1534" title="EEVblog 1534 - Solar Freakin&#x27; RAILways! Sun-Ways DEBUNKED">1534</a></sup> A railway track with modules suspended on arms in free air, coupled through metal rods, is among the highest-vibration environments possible.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1534" title="EEVblog 1534 - Solar Freakin&#x27; RAILways! Sun-Ways DEBUNKED">1534</a></sup>

Soiling matters less on angled rooftop panels than is often assumed. Rain does a reasonable job of washing them, and performance degradation from dirt on a domestic roof array is hard to measure at all.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-5x8UVZnwyL8" title="New Enphase Solar Power System - 1st Day">•</a></sup> Seasonal variation dominates instead: a winter day may yield around 60% of what the panels are capable of, with solar insolation itself varying by perhaps 10% day to day.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-5x8UVZnwyL8" title="New Enphase Solar Power System - 1st Day">•</a></sup>

## Panel selection

Where physical area is fixed, buy the highest-output panels that the budget allows, stopping short of bleeding-edge parts that carry a price premium.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1386" title="EEVblog 1386 - 295W Inverter vs 370W Solar Panel - WTF?">1386</a></sup> Older designs of 325 or 340 watts occupy exactly the same footprint as a modern higher-wattage panel, so choosing them buys no space and simply gives less power.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1386" title="EEVblog 1386 - 295W Inverter vs 370W Solar Panel - WTF?">1386</a></sup> The higher-output panel is more efficient on every day, and particularly on the majority of days when the array is nowhere near clipping the [[inverter]], so a lower inverter rating is not a reason to downgrade the panels.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1386" title="EEVblog 1386 - 295W Inverter vs 370W Solar Panel - WTF?">1386</a></sup> Microinverters are a related constraint: they are designed for efficient panels that generate substantial power, and pairing them with low-output modules is a mismatch.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1363" title="EEVblog #1363 - Solar FREAKIN&#x27; Roadways Gets NEW FUNDING!">1363</a></sup>

## Horizontal and integrated installations

Panels laid flat in a road surface are a worked example of every efficiency loss at once. The [[solar-roadways|Solar Roadways]] SR3 panel occupies 27% of the area of a typical 320-watt, 1.5 square meter consumer panel; at parity it would produce 87 watts, but it is rated at 44 watts — half the efficiency per square meter.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-935" title="EEVblog #935 - Solar Roadways Installation BUSTED!">935</a></sup> The deficit is expected, given no angle, thick protective glass and its extra losses, plus dirt and wear.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-935" title="EEVblog #935 - Solar Roadways Installation BUSTED!">935</a></sup> The textured anti-skid surface reduces incoming light further, to the point that a bridge installation became more efficient as its grip surface wore away.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-4GUygSdf92A" title="Solar Freakin&#x27; Bridges! - ANOTHER Solar Roadways FAIL">•</a></sup>

Field data confirms the shortfall. Against a comparable rooftop installation 30 kilometers away, over 11 months of similar weather and irradiation, a Colas Wattway road ran anywhere from 150% to over 500% behind depending on the month, an overall loss of 288% — roughly a third the efficiency of an average home installation at some non-optimal fixed angle, let alone a tracked commercial array.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1047" title="EEVblog #1047 - Solar Roadways FINALLY BUSTED! (Colas Wattway)">1047</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1047" title="EEVblog #1047 - Solar Roadways FINALLY BUSTED! (Colas Wattway)">1047</a></sup> A later Wattway claim of 148 watts peak per square meter still sits well under the 200-plus watts per square meter a regular panel delivers.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1591" title="EEVblog 1591 - MORE Colas Wattway Solar Cycleways in the Netherlands!">1591</a></sup> Road grime is cumulative: tire rubber wear and embedded dirt let progressively less light through, and [[adhesive]] degrades under UV alongside the vibration.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1591" title="EEVblog 1591 - MORE Colas Wattway Solar Cycleways in the Netherlands!">1591</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1047" title="EEVblog #1047 - Solar Roadways FINALLY BUSTED! (Colas Wattway)">1047</a></sup> Panels that cost more, produce less, and last less carry an embodied energy cost one to two orders of magnitude higher over the full life cycle than a conventional panel.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1591" title="EEVblog 1591 - MORE Colas Wattway Solar Cycleways in the Netherlands!">1591</a></sup>

The alternative is unglamorous and available now: roofing over an existing car park with angled panels lasts 20 years, shelters the vehicles, gives optimal efficiency, and costs very little using existing technology.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1234" title="EEVblog #1234 - MORE Epic Solar Roadways FAIL!">1234</a></sup> Angled roof mounting also avoids the shadows that cross a road surface late in the afternoon and the grit that a road accumulates.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1356" title="EEVblog #1356 - The USA finally gets Solar Roadways! LOL!">1356</a></sup> Rooftop panels are free of shading and vibration, and last 20 years with essentially no intervention.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1534" title="EEVblog 1534 - Solar Freakin&#x27; RAILways! Sun-Ways DEBUNKED">1534</a></sup>

Building-integrated products pay a similar penalty. Solar shingles measured against conventional modules delivered 60 to 70% of the capacity of regular rooftop panels, partly because appearance requirements force manufacturing compromises such as a film that hides the cells when viewed at an angle.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-938" title="EEVblog #938 - Tesla Solar Roofs -  Are They Viable?">938</a></sup>

## Vehicle-mounted arrays

Solar area on a car is small and badly oriented, so the arithmetic is unforgiving. Five square meters of body panels scaled from a 400-watt residential module gives an absolute ceiling of 1,185 watts, before accounting for the curvature of the bodywork; around 1 kilowatt is the realistic maximum, and a sound back-of-the-envelope assumption knocks at least 25% off for body shape alone.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1480" title="EEVblog 1480 EXTENDED VERSION - LightYear Zero + Aptera Solar EV">1480</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1480" title="EEVblog 1480 EXTENDED VERSION - LightYear Zero + Aptera Solar EV">1480</a></sup> Manufacturers that genuinely used the world's most efficient cells would advertise the fact, so silence on the point implies the cells are no better than an ordinary residential panel.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1480" title="EEVblog 1480 EXTENDED VERSION - LightYear Zero + Aptera Solar EV">1480</a></sup>

The manufacturer's own marketing figures can be reversed to check. A claimed 20 kilometers of range added in two hours, on a car consuming 10.5 kWh per 100 kilometers, implies 2.1 kWh captured, or 1.05 kWh per hour, which across 5 square meters is 210 watts per square meter.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1480" title="EEVblog 1480 EXTENDED VERSION - LightYear Zero + Aptera Solar EV">1480</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1480" title="EEVblog 1480 EXTENDED VERSION - LightYear Zero + Aptera Solar EV">1480</a></sup> That sits just under the 235 watts per square meter of the residential comparison panel, making it a reasonable claim — for the perfect time of day in midsummer.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1480" title="EEVblog 1480 EXTENDED VERSION - LightYear Zero + Aptera Solar EV">1480</a></sup>
