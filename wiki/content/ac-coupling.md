AC coupling is the insertion of a series [[capacitor]] into a signal path so that the AC component of a signal passes while any [[dc-offset]] is blocked. It is used both as a measurement mode in instruments (oscilloscopes, multimeters, counters) and as a circuit-design technique wherever a signal riding on an unwanted DC level must be passed between stages at different bias points, such as amplifier inputs and outputs, video lines, and RF chains.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-600" title="EEVblog #600 - OpAmps Tutorial - What is an Operational Amplifier?">600</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-594" title="EEVblog #594 - How To Measure Power Supply Ripple &amp; Noise">594</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-598" title="EEVblog #598 - Sony DME7000 Video Multi Effects Processor Teardown">598</a></sup> Its principal measurement benefit is that removing the DC content lets a small AC variation — power-supply ripple, for example — be examined at high vertical sensitivity instead of being pushed off screen by the DC level.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-594" title="EEVblog #594 - How To Measure Power Supply Ripple &amp; Noise">594</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-224" title="EEVblog #224 - Lab Power Supply Design - Part 3">224</a></sup>

## Implementation in instruments

In an [[oscilloscope]] front end, AC coupling is implemented as a physical capacitor switched into the signal path by a [[relay]] — mechanical or solid-state — which simply shorts the capacitor out again when DC coupling is selected.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1223" title="EEVblog #1223 - Oscilloscope Standard Deviation Noise Measurement">1223</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1761" title="EEVblog 1761 - Micsig MHO14 Tablet Oscilloscope TEARDOWN">1761</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1540" title="EEVblog 1540 - The $80 Zotek ZT-702S Osciloscope Multimeter REVIEW">1540</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1717" title="EEVblog 1717 - Rigol&#x27;s INSANE New $999 350MHz Oscilloscope: TEARDOWN">1717</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1723" title="EEVblog 1723 - AlienTek DM40 Multimeter/Oscilloscope Review">1723</a></sup> The switching element is frequently a Cosmo or Fujitsu solid-state relay located immediately after the BNC input and termination network, and the user often hears an audible click when toggling coupling or 50-ohm termination.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1563" title="EEVblog 1563 - New $389 12bit Rigol DHO800 Scope TEARDOWN">1563</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1717" title="EEVblog 1717 - Rigol&#x27;s INSANE New $999 350MHz Oscilloscope: TEARDOWN">1717</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-620" title="EEVblog #620 - Repair: Stanford Research SR650 Programmable Filter">620</a></sup> A high-value [[resistor]] to ground, typically 10 MΩ, is placed on the downstream side of the coupling capacitor to establish the DC reference for the now-floating node.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1129" title="EEVblog #1129 - Creating a Nice Readable Schematic">1129</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-675" title="EEVblog #675 - How To Reverse Engineer A Rigol DS1054Z">675</a></sup> Instruments commonly provide separate 1 MΩ and 50 Ω input paths, with the AC/DC coupling selection applied on the 1 MΩ path.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1639" title="EEVblog 1639 - Keysight InfiniiVision HD3 Oscilloscope Teardown">1639</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1503" title="EEVblog 1503 - Rigol HDO4000 12bit Oscilloscope TEARDOWN">1503</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1545" title="EEVblog 1545 - R&amp;S MXO4 TEARDOWN. The World&#x27;s Fastest Oscilloscope!">1545</a></sup>

In classic [[analog-oscilloscope]] front ends, the high-[[frequency]] amplifier chain may itself be permanently AC coupled and only able to amplify high-frequency content, with the DC component tapped off separately and recombined, because the [[waveform]] must be biased inside the front-end amplifier for vertical position control.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-675" title="EEVblog #675 - How To Reverse Engineer A Rigol DS1054Z">675</a></sup>

Multimeters implement AC coupling in the AC volts function: the input signal passes through a series capacitor so that the DC component is removed before the RMS converter, which is why a meter's AC range inherently performs an AC-only (standard deviation) measurement.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1223" title="EEVblog #1223 - Oscilloscope Standard Deviation Noise Measurement">1223</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1667" title="EEVblog 1667 - Reverse Engineering the Brymen BM2257 Multimeter LowZ Mode">1667</a></sup> A high-[[voltage]]-rated part may be used for this — for example, a 0.22 µF 1000 V peak capacitor directly coupling the AC input into the [[resistor-divider|resistive divider]] and true-RMS converter.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-731" title="EEVblog #731 - Keithley DMM7510 7.5 Digit Multimeter Teardown">731</a></sup>

Vintage analog multimeters carried the same idea under the confusing name "output" terminal: despite the label, it was an input identical to the regular volts/ohms/amps jack but with a large series capacitor that removed any DC content.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1067" title="EEVblog #1067 - Analog vs Digital Multimeters!">1067</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-634" title="EEVblog #634 - Analog Multimeter Teardowns">634</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-648" title="EEVblog #648 - Mailbag">648</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-899" title="EEVblog #899 - Extreme Mailbag!">899</a></sup>

## Measurement practice

For power-supply ripple and noise measurements, AC coupling is the standard setup: the DC content must be removed so the channel can be run at a few millivolts per division, in combination with [[bandwidth]] limiting.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-594" title="EEVblog #594 - How To Measure Power Supply Ripple &amp; Noise">594</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-224" title="EEVblog #224 - Lab Power Supply Design - Part 3">224</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-855" title="EEVblog #855 - Ceramic Capacitor Piezoelectric Effect">855</a></sup> With a 500 mV ripple input, for instance, a [[linear-regulator]] under test showed almost identical input and output ripple at 10 kHz — roughly 60 mV peak-to-peak — a comparison only visible because both channels were AC coupled at 50 mV per division.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1116" title="EEVBlog #1116 - How to Remove Power Supply Ripple">1116</a></sup> AC coupling is likewise used to check whether a low-dropout regulator oscillates as it is pulled below its [[dropout-voltage]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-972" title="EEVblog #972 - Operating Chips Outside Their Spec">972</a></sup>

For power-rail probing the opposite rule applies. AC coupling must not be used, because a [[power-rail]] carries meaningful low-frequency content that the coupling capacitor strips away; DC coupling with a probe or front end that can offset the rail's DC voltage is required to use the full dynamic range of the ADC.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1735" title="EEVblog 1735 - Power Rail Probing &amp; Oscilloscope DC Offset EXPLAINED">1735</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1733" title="EEVblog 1733 - Mailbag: $100 2GHz Power Rail Probe, Retro Chip Tester">1733</a></sup> Dedicated power-rail probes exist precisely because ordinary active probes often lack input AC coupling options and cannot handle the DC offset.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1733" title="EEVblog 1733 - Mailbag: $100 2GHz Power Rail Probe, Retro Chip Tester">1733</a></sup>

A DC offset that originates *after* the instrument's coupling capacitor — internal front-end offset — is not removed by selecting AC coupling, so a residual offset can persist on screen even in AC mode; self-[[calibration]] removes such offsets.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1223" title="EEVblog #1223 - Oscilloscope Standard Deviation Noise Measurement">1223</a></sup> AC coupling can also introduce its own display artifacts, such as overshoot on captured digital packets.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-762" title="EEVblog #762 - How Secure Are Electronic Safe Locks?">762</a></sup>

A software equivalent exists: the standard-deviation measurement on a DC-coupled channel is the AC RMS value, effectively AC coupling the signal in software while still displaying the DC offset, whereas the RMS measurement by definition includes the DC component (AC+DC mode).<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1223" title="EEVblog #1223 - Oscilloscope Standard Deviation Noise Measurement">1223</a></sup>

## AC trigger coupling

Trigger coupling is a separate control from input coupling: AC trigger coupling removes the DC component only in the trigger path.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-699" title="EEVblog #699 - Rigol DS1054Z Oscilloscope Jitter Fix Testing">699</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-685" title="EEVblog #685 - What Is Oscilloscope AC Trigger Coupling?">685</a></sup> Its practical value appears when probing unknown circuits whose signals sit on different, unknown DC offsets — with DC trigger coupling the trigger level must be re-adjusted for every new signal, while AC trigger coupling lets a single trigger point near zero work across all of them.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-685" title="EEVblog #685 - What Is Oscilloscope AC Trigger Coupling?">685</a></sup> Some oscilloscopes have exhibited severe trigger jitter specifically in AC trigger-coupling mode — on the Rigol DS1000Z and DS2000 series the jitter was inherent to the mode and could not be removed, while other scopes, including the older DS1052E, triggered cleanly.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-683" title="EEVblog #683 - Rigol DS1000Z &amp; DS2000 Oscilloscope Jitter Problems">683</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-699" title="EEVblog #699 - Rigol DS1054Z Oscilloscope Jitter Fix Testing">699</a></sup>

## Circuit design applications

AC coupling appears throughout analog and RF design:

- **Amplifiers.** Op-amp configurations are normally DC coupled, but any of them can be AC coupled by adding capacitors on the inputs and outputs.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-600" title="EEVblog #600 - OpAmps Tutorial - What is an Operational Amplifier?">600</a></sup> Valve and audio stages are conventionally AC coupled at the output.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-837" title="EEVblog #837 - Reverse Engineering A Valve Headphone Amplifier">837</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1243" title="EEVblog #1243 - Sony Boombox REPAIR and Teardown">1243</a></sup>
- **Microphones and audio.** An electret microphone is biased from the rail — 2.2 kΩ being the most common value — and the signal is then AC coupled into the amplifier; line inputs and headphone stages are coupled the same way.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-713" title="EEVblog #713 - Voice Recognition - 1980&#x27;s Style">713</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-271" title="EEVblog #271 - Zoom H1 Recorder Teardown">271</a></sup> A node after a coupling capacitor needs a defined DC reference, such as a pull-high resistor, or it floats and depends on leakage paths.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-267" title="EEVblog #267 - Voltage Detection Stick Teardown">267</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-611" title="EEVblog #611 - Electret Microphone Design">611</a></sup>
- **Video.** Composite and analog video signals are routinely AC coupled into processing ICs, a series cap being a reliable tell on a board that a trace carries video.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-598" title="EEVblog #598 - Sony DME7000 Video Multi Effects Processor Teardown">598</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-969" title="EEVblog #969 - Vintage $80k NLE Video Editor Teardown">969</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1021" title="EEVblog #1021 - Retro Radio Shack TRS-80 Color Computer">1021</a></sup>
- **RF.** Spectrum-analyser and signal-generator inputs are AC coupled (e.g., through a series capacitor ahead of the first switch or attenuator), and coupling capacitors bridge breaks in shielding between RF blocks.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-892" title="EEVblog #892 - Siglent SSA3021X Spectrum Analyser Teardown">892</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1109" title="EEVblog #1109 - Spectrum Analyser Design Walk-through">1109</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-823" title="EEVBlog #823 - Rigol DSG815 RF Signal Generator Teardown">823</a></sup> A common pattern powers a remote RF device — amplifier, transducer, receiver — through an [[inductor]] that is open circuit to RF, while the signal is tapped off through an AC coupling capacitor.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-940" title="EEVblog #940 - Mailbag">940</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-827" title="EEVblog #827 - Mailbag">827</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-956" title="EEVblog #956 - Countersurveillance Monitor Teardown">956</a></sup> EMC probes and line-[[impedance]] stabilization networks AC couple conducted noise off a power line into a [[spectrum-analyzer]] while the inductor presents a fixed 50 Ω impedance to the source.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1178" title="EEVblog #1178 - Build a $10 DIY EMC Probe">1178</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-548" title="EEVblog #548 - EMC Pre-Compliance Conducted Emissions Testing">548</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-546" title="EEVblog #546 - Mailbag Monday">546</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-993" title="EEVblog #993 - Mailbag">993</a></sup>
- **High-speed digital.** [[usb]] 3.0 SuperSpeed differential pairs carry series AC coupling capacitors on the board, and Ethernet termination networks are often AC coupled to ground.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-340" title="EEVblog #340 - USB 3.0 Eye Diagram Measurement">340</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-934" title="EEVblog #934 - Raspberry Pi Supercomputer Cluster PART 1">934</a></sup>
- **Detection and feedback.** Coupling capacitors feed sampled RF back to a [[microcontroller]] for transmit-verification in an EPIRB, couple energy off a resonant oscillator for rectification in energy-harvesting front ends, and provide capacitive pickup of ambient fields.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-368" title="EEVblog #368 - EPIRB Teardown">368</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-664" title="EEVblog #664 - Peltier TEG Energy Harvesting Experiments">664</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-441" title="EEVblog #441 - How To Track Down Common Mode Noise">441</a></sup>

## Component selection and biasing

Ceramic capacitors are acceptable for AC coupling at input, interstage, and output positions in typical audio-frequency circuits.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-827" title="EEVblog #827 - Mailbag">827</a></sup> Electrolytics remain hard to avoid where very low frequencies must pass, because of the large [[capacitance]] required in a small volume, despite their unreliability.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-33" title="EEVblog #33 1of2 - Capacitor Tutorial (Electrolytic, Tantalum, &amp; Plastic Film)">33</a></sup> Where a DC path must coexist with the block — for example injecting a signal onto a [[coaxial-cable|coax]] line that also carries DC power — the DC feed must be lifted or the generator itself AC coupled, since the coupling capacitor otherwise blocks the injected signal or the DC upsets the source.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-966" title="EEVblog #966 - CPM700 Counter Surveillance Monitor Repair">966</a></sup>

## Limitations

Because AC coupling is a high-pass function, it discards low-frequency information along with the DC, which is precisely why it is unsuitable for power-rail integrity work.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1735" title="EEVblog 1735 - Power Rail Probing &amp; Oscilloscope DC Offset EXPLAINED">1735</a></sup> The absence of AC coupling in a low-cost instrument is regarded as a serious deficiency: a pocket oscilloscope offering DC-only coupling was judged practically useless for general signal viewing.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-359" title="EEVblog #359 - QDSO Pocket Oscilloscope Review">359</a></sup> Conversely, flawed AC-coupling implementations — the Rigol trigger-path jitter being the notable example — can make the mode unusable even when present.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-683" title="EEVblog #683 - Rigol DS1000Z &amp; DS2000 Oscilloscope Jitter Problems">683</a></sup>

## AC coupling in photovoltaic systems

In residential solar and [[battery]] systems, "AC coupling" has a distinct meaning: a second, independent PV system — typically microinverters — connects onto the AC bus rather than into the hybrid [[inverter]]'s DC side.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1682" title="EEVblog 1682 - Enphase Microinverter FAIL &amp; Warranty Install">1682</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-gAu8CvMjDrU" title="15kWh AERL LFP Home Storage Battery - First Analysis">•</a></sup> Because each [[microinverter]] feeds the common AC bus and is individually addressed by serial number, the hybrid inverter has no direct communication with that system; instead a [[current]] clamp on the grid connection detects excess power flowing toward the grid, and the inverter diverts that surplus into [[battery-charging]] rather than exporting it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-gAu8CvMjDrU" title="15kWh AERL LFP Home Storage Battery - First Analysis">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1634" title="EEVblog 1634 - 15kWh AERL LFP Home Storage Battery Install!">1634</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1682" title="EEVblog 1682 - Enphase Microinverter FAIL &amp; Warranty Install">1682</a></sup> On Deye hybrid inverters this behaviour is enabled as an "AC couple on grid side" mode, located in the generator-port settings, with configurable start and stop battery-capacity thresholds (e.g., 95% and 100%).<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1634" title="EEVblog 1634 - 15kWh AERL LFP Home Storage Battery Install!">1634</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-gAu8CvMjDrU" title="15kWh AERL LFP Home Storage Battery - First Analysis">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-BXVgk-uoxn8" title="Hoymiles Solar Microinverter Install + Deye Gen Connection">•</a></sup> The feature allows, for example, roughly 2 kW of otherwise-exported energy from a separate Enphase microinverter system to be added to the battery charge rate on top of the inverter's own array contribution.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-gAu8CvMjDrU" title="15kWh AERL LFP Home Storage Battery - First Analysis">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1719" title="EEVblog 1719 - 75kWh Home Storage Battery Expansion!">1719</a></sup> On at least one Deye configuration, enabling a microinverter (generator-port) input disables the grid-side AC coupling option, so the two cannot necessarily operate together.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-BXVgk-uoxn8" title="Hoymiles Solar Microinverter Install + Deye Gen Connection">•</a></sup>
