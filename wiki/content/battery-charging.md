Battery charging is the controlled delivery of energy into a secondary cell, governed by a profile that the cell chemistry dictates rather than by whatever the source happens to supply. For lithium-ion and lithium-polymer cells the profile is a two-stage constant-[[current]] then constant-[[voltage]] sequence, and getting the terminal voltage wrong is dangerous rather than merely inefficient.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-919" title="EEVblog #919 - How To Charge Li-Ion/LiPo Batteries With A Power Supply">919</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-176" title="EEVblog #176 - Lithium Ion/Polymer Battery Charging Tutorial">176</a></sup> Almost every portable instrument, tool, camera, and e-reader carries the charger inside it, which makes charging circuitry one of the most common blocks encountered in teardown and repair work, and one of the most common places for a product to fail.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1672" title="EEVblog 1672 - JBL REPAIR: You&#x27;ll never guess the fault!">1672</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1370" title="EEVblog #1370 - Kindle Paperwhite REPAIR">1370</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1433" title="EEVblog 1433 - More Adventures in Sony RX100 Repair (Part 3)">1433</a></sup>

## The constant-current / constant-voltage profile

Two numbers are needed to charge a lithium cell: its nominal charging voltage, which is critical, and its charge current.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-919" title="EEVblog #919 - How To Charge Li-Ion/LiPo Batteries With A Power Supply">919</a></sup> The overwhelming majority of cells terminate at 4.2 V, though the figure shifts slightly with chemistry and manufacturing.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-919" title="EEVblog #919 - How To Charge Li-Ion/LiPo Batteries With A Power Supply">919</a></sup> The characteristic curve plotted for such a cell is cell voltage against time or against capacity, the x-axis running from zero to 100% of capacity — the quantity called C — or equivalently from zero to some number of hours.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-176" title="EEVblog #176 - Lithium Ion/Polymer Battery Charging Tutorial">176</a></sup>

Charging begins in constant current. As the cell fills, its effective electrochemistry and internal [[resistance]] change until the terminal voltage reaches the set point, at which the charger crosses over into constant voltage and the current begins to fall away.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1688" title="EEVblog 1688 - Constant Current Sources EXPLAINED + DEMO">1688</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-357" title="EEVblog #357 - USB Supply Power-up Testing">357</a></sup> Termination is typically at a current threshold rather than a time: around 10% of the charge current, so a cell charged at roughly 1.7 A might cut off near 170 mA.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-919" title="EEVblog #919 - How To Charge Li-Ion/LiPo Batteries With A Power Supply">919</a></sup> The crossover is directly observable — a cell sitting below the set voltage is still in constant current, while a cell pinned exactly at 4.2 V with falling current has already switched to constant voltage.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-357" title="EEVblog #357 - USB Supply Power-up Testing">357</a></sup> A partly charged cell, for example one resting at 3.81 V, may skip the constant-current phase almost entirely and drop straight into constant voltage.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-357" title="EEVblog #357 - USB Supply Power-up Testing">357</a></sup>

Charge current is conventionally expressed relative to capacity. A 2600 mAh cell charged at 1 A is not even at 1C, and could take a couple of amps if wanted.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1613" title="EEVblog 1613 - Audiophile REPAIR: $4500 Chord Hugo DAC">1613</a></sup> At a 1 A rate, cells of around 2700 mAh charge in roughly three hours.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-259" title="EEVblog #259 - PSU Rev C Schematic - Part 12">259</a></sup>

## Charging from a bench supply

Because a lab [[power-supply|power supply]] provides independent constant-voltage and constant-current limits, it implements the lithium charging profile directly: set the maximum [[output-voltage|output voltage]] to 4.200 V and the [[current-limit|current limit]] to the desired charge rate.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-919" title="EEVblog #919 - How To Charge Li-Ion/LiPo Batteries With A Power Supply">919</a></sup> Supply accuracy is not the limiting factor — a 0.05% instrument such as the Rigol DP835 is an order of magnitude better than the application requires.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-919" title="EEVblog #919 - How To Charge Li-Ion/LiPo Batteries With A Power Supply">919</a></sup> The same procedure applies unchanged to 18650 cells and to LiPo pouch cells of the kind found in remote-control equipment.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-919" title="EEVblog #919 - How To Charge Li-Ion/LiPo Batteries With A Power Supply">919</a></sup> Done incorrectly, charging lithium cells this way can be quite dangerous.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-919" title="EEVblog #919 - How To Charge Li-Ion/LiPo Batteries With A Power Supply">919</a></sup>

The technique generalises to other chemistries by changing the [[compliance-voltage|compliance voltage]]. A nickel pack is charged by setting maximum compliance at 1.4 V per cell — 8.4 V for a six-cell pack — with a current limit of around half an amp.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-XpZVIWdXliY" title="Fluke OneTouch Network Tester Battery Restoration Attempt">•</a></sup> A single small rechargeable cell can be nursed at 1.5 V compliance and 1 mA of constant current.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-0018phUFjuQ" title="Citizen EcoDrive Watch Battery Replacement">•</a></sup> A 30 V, 500 mA tool pack accepting charge shows constant current at 500 mA with the compliance voltage sitting at 28.98 V and climbing, which is the signature of a pack that is genuinely taking a charge.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1760" title="EEVblog 1760 - Today in the Dumpster (July 12th, 2026)">1760</a></sup>

Nickel chemistries are less forgiving of a dumb profile than the two-step lithium sequence. Nickel-metal-hydride charging is exothermic, and charging such cells properly calls for temperature sensing.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-176" title="EEVblog #176 - Lithium Ion/Polymer Battery Charging Tutorial">176</a></sup> Modern integrated lithium chargers fold the same idea in: a [[battery]] running too hot is charged at a slower rate in accordance with the JEDEC standards.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1694" title="EEVblog 1694 - Scarlet Rae Teardown + Design Discussion with Xentronics">1694</a></sup>

One hazard specific to bench charging is what happens at the supply rather than the battery. Disconnecting the input voltage from a linear supply while a charged battery remains connected to its output feeds voltage backwards into the regulator, a scenario common enough with traditional linear supplies to warrant deliberate testing.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-233" title="EEVblog #233 - Lab Power Supply Design Part 6 - LT3080 Testing">233</a></sup>

## Charger silicon

Dedicated charger ICs are jellybean parts, and a premium one is a defensible choice rather than an extravagance in a product whose failure mode is thermal.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1694" title="EEVblog 1694 - Scarlet Rae Teardown + Design Discussion with Xentronics">1694</a></sup> Identified charge controllers include the BQ24773 in a portable speaker,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1672" title="EEVblog 1672 - JBL REPAIR: You&#x27;ll never guess the fault!">1672</a></sup> a Microchip part paired with battery reverse-polarity protection,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1619" title="EEVblog 1619 - Mailbag: Featuring Don McKenzie">1619</a></sup> and a Linear Technology charging circuit sited immediately next to the charge [[connector]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-571" title="EEVblog #571 - Sennheiser EW100 G3 Wireless Microphone Teardown">571</a></sup> Charging is also frequently absorbed into a power management unit: the MC13892 companion to an ARM Cortex [[processor]] carries built-in battery charging plus coulomb counting for state-of-charge estimation, a real-time clock, and the DC-to-DC converters and regulators for the processor cores.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-189" title="iRiver Story HD eBook Reader TEARDOWN - EEVblog #189">189</a></sup>

Charger parts differ in their termination features. A later-generation part replacing the LT1512 added end-of-charge control with a selectable minimum current ratio — that is, a programmable termination threshold.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-259" title="EEVblog #259 - PSU Rev C Schematic - Part 12">259</a></sup> At the crude end, the charger in a rectifying toothbrush base is a single SOT-23 switch with no series [[current-limiting|current limiting]] at all, connecting the rectified voltage from the power coupling coil straight through to the battery, with the [[microcontroller]]'s ADC measuring [[battery-voltage|battery voltage]] during charging to decide what to do.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-284" title="EEVblog #284 - Braun Toothbrush Teardown">284</a></sup> Adjustable linear regulators are also pressed into service as chargers in their application notes, distinguished from the ordinary regulator configuration by an added [[series-resistor|series resistor]] in the output leg.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1438" title="EEVblog 1438 - The TOP 5 Jellybean Regulators &amp; References">1438</a></sup>

Where charging circuitry lives is a board-level design decision with thermal consequences. Charging two 18650 cells from a 12 V rail through a linear pass element throws the difference away as heat; moving the charging circuitry onto the main board and adding a switching pre-regulator removes the [[heat-sink|heat sink]] altogether.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-258" title="EEVblog #258 - PSU Housing Design - Part 11">258</a></sup>

## Multi-cell and series-pack considerations

A single-cell product sidesteps the whole problem of multi-cell charging.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-913" title="EEVblog #913 - Mailbag">913</a></sup> Where several packs are present, a charger may handle them one at a time: in principle packs could be paralleled and the charge split between them, but packs that are paralleled to charge cannot simultaneously be in series to power the product, so a device that charges while running must keep them in series.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1460" title="EEVblog1460 - REPAIRING a LED Studio Light with a DUMPSTER LAPTOP!">1460</a></sup> Separate charge circuits per battery are also used, as in a portable DAC with two independently charged cells.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1613" title="EEVblog 1613 - Audiophile REPAIR: $4500 Chord Hugo DAC">1613</a></sup>

In a large series pack, keeping all cells at roughly the same voltage matters, and cells are sourced matched from the same batch to that end.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-923" title="EEVblog #923 - World&#x27;s Fastest Solar Electric Car">923</a></sup> Balancing is visible during recovery charging of a nickel pack, where individual cells converging toward the same voltage indicates the pack is coming back.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-XpZVIWdXliY" title="Fluke OneTouch Network Tester Battery Restoration Attempt">•</a></sup>

## Deeply discharged packs and under-voltage lockout

The most common charging failure in a dead device is not a dead cell but a protection circuit latched off. A pack that has been drawn down below its threshold trips under-voltage lockout, after which the product's own charger will not restart it — a device left on its charger for days will simply flash and refuse, which is a design oversight when the product is a standalone power bank.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1707" title="EEVblog 1707 - REPAIR: Goal Zero Yeti 400 Lithium Battery">1707</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-8P8Af5SR57U" title="Goal Zero Yeti 400 Lithium Battery Follow--up">•</a></sup> The remedy is to bypass the internal charging circuitry and apply constant current directly across the cell terminals until the pack rises above lockout: a GPS unit sitting at 2.5 V and refusing external [[usb|USB]] charge went straight into constant current at 400 mA from a bench supply and recovered.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-KTr-44n0bbU" title="Garmin GPS Battery Kickstarting">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-XpZVIWdXliY" title="Fluke OneTouch Network Tester Battery Restoration Attempt">•</a></sup> The behaviour is then confirmed the ordinary way, by watching the pack transition into constant voltage with the charge current tapering.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-KTr-44n0bbU" title="Garmin GPS Battery Kickstarting">•</a></sup>

Under-voltage lockout, in the cases where it acts before the cell is damaged, is what saves the pack rather than what breaks it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-8P8Af5SR57U" title="Goal Zero Yeti 400 Lithium Battery Follow--up">•</a></sup> A pack that is merely flat, rather than latched, will still misbehave in circuit: an uncharged battery can load down the rail feeding the processor enough to cause repeated resets, which reads as a hiccuping power supply until the battery has taken on enough charge to stop being a burden on the rest of the circuit.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1662" title="EEVblog 1662 - Vintage Tandy 200 Portable Computer Claytons Repair/Teardown">1662</a></sup>

## Diagnosing charging from the outside

Charge state is readable from input current without opening anything. A camera drawing 150 to 180 mA on USB that collapses to about 1 mA within five seconds is not charging its battery; it has recognised a full or unreachable cell and dropped to standby, whereas a sustained draw of 150 to 160 mA is genuine charging.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1433" title="EEVblog 1433 - More Adventures in Sony RX100 Repair (Part 3)">1433</a></sup> An e-reader drawing 460 mA is putting essentially all of it into the battery, since an e-ink display needs no power to hold an image and the device only wakes to scan the [[touchscreen]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1370" title="EEVblog #1370 - Kindle Paperwhite REPAIR">1370</a></sup> Where the product is supposed to charge only while switched off, a cell that recovers no faster with the charger plugged in localises the fault to the charging path rather than the battery.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1433" title="EEVblog 1433 - More Adventures in Sony RX100 Repair (Part 3)">1433</a></sup>

Indicator behaviour is a second, weaker channel: an amber [[led|LED]] going green on completion,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-205" title="Amazon Kindle 4 Review &amp; Unboxing - EEVblog #205">205</a></sup> a battery symbol flashing fast for charging and slow for discharging,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1719" title="EEVblog 1719 - 75kWh Home Storage Battery Expansion!">1719</a></sup> or a state-of-charge readout that reports the battery as too low and shuts the product down even while current is demonstrably flowing into it — which points at a second fault in the charging chip alongside the failed cell.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1370" title="EEVblog #1370 - Kindle Paperwhite REPAIR">1370</a></sup> A charger that begins a charge cycle with no battery installed is unambiguously faulty.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1460" title="EEVblog1460 - REPAIRING a LED Studio Light with a DUMPSTER LAPTOP!">1460</a></sup>

Some designs expose the battery directly enough to be charged through an unexpected port. A portable power station whose nominal 12 V output is connected straight across a 12.8 V pack, with no 12 V regulator in between, will charge that pack when 12 V is applied to the output.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src--ir61ARd5T4" title="WTF is Wrong with my Rigol DL3021 Electronic Load?">•</a></sup> Other designs invite disassembly instead, with the pack contacts sitting directly across the cells so the internal charging circuitry can be bypassed.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-XpZVIWdXliY" title="Fluke OneTouch Network Tester Battery Restoration Attempt">•</a></sup>

## Charging from the mains supply

Backup power systems face the problem of pushing large amounts of energy into large packs without dedicated charging hardware. One line-interactive UPS solves it by running its [[inverter]] backwards: the H-bridge MOSFETs' substrate body diodes rectify power fed back through the [[transformer]], energy is stored in the winding inductances, and large bulk [[capacitance]] filters the result to charge the batteries.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-504" title="EEVblog #504 - UPS Tutorial &amp; Teardown">504</a></sup> With the H-bridge devices as the only power semiconductors present, this is the only route by which enough power could reach the packs.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-504" title="EEVblog #504 - UPS Tutorial &amp; Teardown">504</a></sup> The approach is documented in US patent 5,302,858, "Method and apparatus for providing battery charging in a backup power system".<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-504" title="EEVblog #504 - UPS Tutorial &amp; Teardown">504</a></sup>

At the other end of the scale, a NiMH fast charger routes current down through a ladder of MOSFETs and into the battery, and the same shunt reads current in one direction during charge and the opposite direction during discharge, since the pack's positive terminal sources current on discharge.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-812" title="EEVblog #812 - Varta 15min NiMH Charger Part 2">812</a></sup>

## Grid-scale and vehicle charging

Domestic storage inverters make charge scheduling a tariff-arbitrage problem. A hybrid inverter can be configured for time-of-use offsets between grid, battery, and load, supports flexible dynamic battery charging across different chemistries, and accepts solar, wind, generator, and [[microinverter]] inputs.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1628" title="EEVblog 1628 - Home Solar Power Re-Install + Upgrade + FAIL">1628</a></sup> Where a network has excess solar and offers a free-power window — for instance three hours from 11:00 a.m. to 2:00 p.m. — charging the house battery inside that window is worth doing regardless of what the sun is doing.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-HctGMxWPWRE" title="Deye Hybrid Inverter Time Sync Issue UPDATE + Battery News">•</a></sup> The binding constraint is charge power rather than storage: a 25 kWh battery limited to a 5 kW maximum charge rate can absorb at most 15 kWh in a three-hour window, which is roughly overnight supply.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-HctGMxWPWRE" title="Deye Hybrid Inverter Time Sync Issue UPDATE + Battery News">•</a></sup> Raising the inverter from 5 kW to 8 kW exists specifically to fill more of that window, and the same window is used to charge an electric vehicle.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-kKfZgAyFu8Q" title="Solis Hybrid Inverter">•</a></sup> Accounting across AC-coupled sources is not straightforward: 0.6 kW from one array plus 0.08 kW from a microinverter does not obviously produce 1.24 kW into the battery.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-BXVgk-uoxn8" title="Hoymiles Solar Microinverter Install + Deye Gen Connection">•</a></sup>

Electric vehicles charge in the other direction as well. [[regenerative-braking|Regenerative braking]] returns energy to the pack whenever the driver lifts off the accelerator, which is the desired behaviour in about 95% of lift-off events; disabling regen restores true coasting, limited only by tyre rolling resistance and aerodynamic drag.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1432" title="EEVBlog 1432 - Owning an ELECTRIC CAR for 12 Months &amp; 17,000km">1432</a></sup>

## Harvested energy and charging arithmetic

Claims that ambient energy can charge a phone collapse under a straightforward energy balance. A device that charges a phone from 30% to full in about 90 minutes is doing so from an internal lithium-ion battery that was charged conventionally beforehand — functionally no different from a pair of AA cells and a 5 V generator.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-55" title="EEVblog #55 - RCA Airnergy WiFi Charging Free Energy Harvesting Marketing BS">55</a></sup> A thermoelectric watch harvesting on the order of 0.77 mWh per day would need 961 days, about 2.6 years, to fill a 740 mWh cell from flat, and that figure ignores losses in the DC-to-DC converter and the charging process entirely.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-945" title="EEVblog #945 - Thermal Powered Smartwatches Are GIMMICKS!">945</a></sup> Phone-to-phone charging over NFC fails on a second [[ground]] beyond the power budget: no circuitry exists in NFC-equipped phones to route received energy back into the battery, so even the tens of milliwatts theoretically available cannot be stored.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1099" title="EEVblog #1099 - BattBump Kickstarter REDACTED EDITION!">1099</a></sup>

Harvesting that is engineered rather than asserted works, and is specified accordingly: a solar-powered meter with a genuine rechargeable cell has its charge rate quoted for a given illuminance in lux.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1608" title="EEVblog 1608 - Mailbag">1608</a></sup>

## Charging interfaces

The charging connector is a frequent point of failure and of deliberate lock-in. Designing proper in-camera charging circuitry and then fitting a connector that resembles micro-USB closely enough to invite the mistake, while requiring the manufacturer's own cable, negates the benefit of the charging design entirely.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-578" title="EEVblog #578 - Proprietary Connector Rant - Panasonic LF1">578</a></sup> Proprietary multi-contact barrel jacks with an inner ring in addition to the outer ring and tip serve the same purpose.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-Cn3DVQGmF9A" title="Dyson Battery Failure">•</a></sup> Worn or marginal DC jacks are a diagnostic hazard in their own right, requiring physical pressure before any current flows.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-XpZVIWdXliY" title="Fluke OneTouch Network Tester Battery Restoration Attempt">•</a></sup>

Alternatives exist. One handheld meter dispenses with a DC jack and charges through its ground and milliamp banana jacks, marked as such on the front panel, running a three-minute self-test on connection to determine whether the installed cell is an alkaline primary or a rechargeable before starting a charge and reporting a time to completion.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-56" title="EEVblog #56 - Agilent U1253A OLED Multimeter Review &amp; Teardown">56</a></sup> Docking contacts serve the same role in mobile robots, where returning to a charging station and charging unattended is the whole point of the product.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-980" title="EEVblog #980 - RoboMaid Automated Vacuum Cleaner Teardown">980</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-429" title="EEVblog #429 - TurtleBot &amp; CineMoco">429</a></sup>

Charging hardware is also treated carelessly in the field. A mains tool charger visibly melted and well past its test date, left permanently plugged in and in daily use to recharge tool packs, is the ordinary state of such equipment on a work site.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-YaTldV7-uXo" title="When Safety Goes Out The Window">•</a></sup>
