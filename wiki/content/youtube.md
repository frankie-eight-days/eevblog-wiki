YouTube is the video hosting, distribution and discovery platform on which independent technical publishing overwhelmingly runs. It is the world's second biggest search engine after Google and, by orders of magnitude, the biggest video search engine, and no competitor comes close on the combination of community, search and content delivery.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-Gwp645Vv8Bs" title="eevBLAB #41 - VidMe Is Shutting Down!">•</a></sup> For a technical creator it is simultaneously the only viable audience-building venue and a single point of failure: the platform controls monetisation, discovery ranking, copyright adjudication and account existence, with limited transparency and no guaranteed recourse.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src--oiPAhdGdnM" title="Filling out the Youtube Creator Survey">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-wjBV0JSV3xs" title="eevBLAB #69 - The Problem With Bitchute">•</a></sup>

## Discovery and ranking

Views do not arrive mainly through subscriptions. On a large engineering channel the split runs roughly 42% from suggested videos appearing alongside other people's content, 27% from browse features, and around 8% from search.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-Gwp645Vv8Bs" title="eevBLAB #41 - VidMe Is Shutting Down!">•</a></sup> Older material continues to accumulate views indefinitely once indexed, producing a steady baseline independent of new uploads.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-200" title="EEVblog #200 - Retrospective">200</a></sup>

Ranking responds strongly to keywords in the title, and an established, highly ranked channel publishing a title-focused video on a specific search term will typically land on the first page of results within minutes of publication.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-tzro0ZylTpw" title="Why Youtube SEO Title Keywords Matter (uBeam)">•</a></sup> This makes title choice a deliberate technical act: a rebuttal or debunking video only reaches the people researching a claim if it ranks against the same keyword as the promotional material it answers.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-tzro0ZylTpw" title="Why Youtube SEO Title Keywords Matter (uBeam)">•</a></sup>

The algorithm optimises for watch time rather than view count, so audience retention — the average proportion of a video actually watched — governs how aggressively a video is recommended.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1v2_h-_YHEE" title="eevBLAB #71: Audience Retention + My Editing Process">•</a></sup> Total watch time divided by views shows that viewers will typically spend only about 10 to 15 minutes on a piece of content, and the most successful channels run videos of roughly five to eight minutes.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-g3y_Vr8ZJ7w" title="The Signal Path Discussion - Youtube &amp; Bell Labs (1/3)">•</a></sup> The consequence is that short-form polish is poorly rewarded relative to its production cost, while long-form content that holds attention is favoured.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1v2_h-_YHEE" title="eevBLAB #71: Audience Retention + My Editing Process">•</a></sup>

Title and thumbnail are the two elements that determine whether a video is opened at all; descriptions are frequently not read.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-943" title="EEVblog #943 - How EEVblog Does Video Editing">943</a></sup> Thumbnails and titles can be A/B tested, with up to three variants served at random and click-through compared, which is why the same video can appear with different artwork on successive page refreshes.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-BmcoJqMxT6g" title="eevBLAB 142 - The Inevitable Future of AI Youtube Thumbnails">•</a></sup>

Early uploads were capped at ten minutes, which forced multi-part publication of longer material.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1198" title="EEVblog #1198 - 10 Years On Youtube!">1198</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-599" title="EEVblog #599 - 5 years of the EEVblog">599</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-T5cRvs44LcE" title="EEVblog Survey Results - 2021">•</a></sup> Mobile viewing grew to something over 30% of watching, which broke overlay annotations and prompted the end screen feature as a replacement.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-cmANDi1AcTs" title="eevBLAB #26 - Youtube End Screen Feature FAIL">•</a></sup>

Access is not universal. The platform is blocked in China by the government.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-606" title="EEVblog #606 - Mailbag">606</a></sup> Delivery has also been degraded at the ISP level: one Australian carrier throttled YouTube specifically, capping it to an essentially fixed rate for all its users while leaving other traffic alone.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-9iDRynyBl0c" title="Proof that Telstra Bigpond is Throttling Youtube Bandwidth in Australia?">•</a></sup> Upload throughput is separately capped somewhere in the path, averaging around 800 kB/s regardless of the connection's nominal upstream capacity, so a faster local link does not produce faster uploads.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-500" title="EEVblog #500 - Live Show">500</a></sup>

## Upload, encoding and processing

Once a video is uploaded it cannot be edited. The start and end can be trimmed, but nothing can be cut from or inserted into the middle.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-cmANDi1AcTs" title="eevBLAB #26 - Youtube End Screen Feature FAIL">•</a></sup> Correcting an error therefore requires deleting and re-uploading, which discards all accumulated views, ratings and comments.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-cmANDi1AcTs" title="eevBLAB #26 - Youtube End Screen Feature FAIL">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-8C0G_UUyUg0" title="eevBLAB #34 - A Proposal For Youtube">•</a></sup> This is the structural reason for post-hoc correction mechanisms such as annotations and, later, end screens and pinned notes.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-cmANDi1AcTs" title="eevBLAB #26 - Youtube End Screen Feature FAIL">•</a></sup>

After upload the file is processed frame by frame, and a low-quality version is served first while higher-quality renditions are still being generated; playback during this window looks markedly worse than the finished result.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-74" title="EEVblog #74 - Video Blogging Tools">74</a></sup> Uploading at 4K causes the platform to process the 1080p rendition at a higher bit rate than it would use for a natively uploaded 1080p file, so an upscaled 4K master can yield a better-looking 1080p delivery.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1096" title="EEVblog #1096 - ANENG Q1 Multimeter Teardown (Now in 4K!)">1096</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-q95mfnmMdwA" title="eevBLAB #16 - 4K Render Youtube Experiment - 1080p Version">•</a></sup> Whatever is supplied, the platform re-encodes it, and delivered quality is always lossy relative to the exported master.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-q95mfnmMdwA" title="eevBLAB #16 - 4K Render Youtube Experiment - 1080p Version">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-jYJz1QlPagM" title="TEST: Constant Qp Test - Using VBR">•</a></sup>

Recommended upload bit rates are around 8 Mbps for 1080p30 and around 24 Mbps for 4K, typically encoded variable-bit-rate with a ceiling well above the average — for example 8 Mbps average with a 20 Mbps maximum.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-wq0Y-yoCHhI" title="TEST #2: Constant Qp Test - Using Qp">•</a></sup> Because masters at broadcast bit rates produce very large files — a 20-minute 26 Mbps render lands around 3.4 GB, and a half-hour teardown around 4.7 GB — a two-step workflow is standard: render from the editor, then transcode to a smaller distribution file before upload and archiving.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-943" title="EEVblog #943 - How EEVblog Does Video Editing">943</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-726" title="EEVblog #726 - Dual Xeon Video Editing Machine Build">726</a></sup> The motivation is upload time and long-term archive size across a library of many hundreds of videos, not playback quality on the platform itself, which handles large files without difficulty.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-726" title="EEVblog #726 - Dual Xeon Video Editing Machine Build">726</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-jYJz1QlPagM" title="TEST: Constant Qp Test - Using VBR">•</a></sup>

Higher frame rates carry a delivery cost. When a browser, machine and connection can sustain 50 or 60 fps the platform will serve it and consume the extra [[bandwidth]] automatically, with no option to fall back to 1080p30.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-686" title="EEVblog #686 - Mailbag">686</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-698" title="EEVblog #698 - GPU Video Rendering">698</a></sup>

Playlists are weakly implemented: a video can be assigned to only one playlist as its official playlist, and reassignment between playlists is awkward, which makes curating a large back catalogue into topic series impractical.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-400" title="EEVblog #400 - 400th Live Show">400</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-ZTwVQmUm6NY" title="eevBLAB 95 - Why Are Youtube Playlists So BAD?">•</a></sup>

## Embedding and tracking

The standard embed code tracks visitors on the host site by default. Privacy-enhanced mode is an opt-in checkbox that is disabled unless explicitly selected; with it off, merely loading a page containing an embedded player sets a cookie in the visitor's browser, whether or not the video is ever played.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-KRzqd2fg_0o" title="eevBLAB #47 - Youtube Are Watching You">•</a></sup>

## Monetisation

Ad revenue comes through AdSense, in several placements including overlays on the video itself.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-62" title="EEVblog #62 - The Drivetime Experiment">62</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-OU08zwBH-wo" title="eevBLAB #13 - Buying Youtube DISLIKES! - The Batteriser">•</a></sup> For a large engineering channel it is not sufficient as a sole income: over roughly fifteen years of full-time operation, ad revenue alone never covered a full-time living in any single year, and the shortfall is made up by direct product sales, merchandise, Patreon, website and forum advertising, and sponsorships.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-jp-pSRQ7fLE" title="eevBLAB 134 - The End of OG Youtube?">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-gT8gzX2CPQA" title="eevBLAB 114 - DHL Emergency Situation Surcharge is BS">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-rEOBcUW2Nf8" title="eevBLAB #39 - Youtube Alternatives">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1688" title="EEVblog 1688 - Constant Current Sources EXPLAINED + DEMO">1688</a></sup> Revenue per view has trended down over years even where absolute revenue held roughly flat, and views themselves decline as the same finite viewing hours are spread across an ever-growing number of channels.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-jp-pSRQ7fLE" title="eevBLAB 134 - The End of OG Youtube?">•</a></sup>

Creators outside the United States are subject to a 24% US withholding charge on relevant earnings, applied per creator depending on individual circumstances.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-sHDJKiQIncU" title="eevBLAB 85 - Youtube TAX!">•</a></sup>

Premium subscription revenue is shared with creators, with well over half passed through, allocated according to what each subscriber actually watched rather than to subscriptions alone — a viewer who watches a channel without subscribing still generates a share for it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-jp-pSRQ7fLE" title="eevBLAB 134 - The End of OG Youtube?">•</a></sup>

Advertising placement is not a creator entitlement. A self-rating questionnaire asks creators to classify their own content against advertiser-friendly guidelines, and the accompanying terms state that YouTube has the right, not the obligation, to display ads.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-bHJGUTc43kg" title="New Youtube Self-Rating Monetization Feature">•</a></sup> Categories such as analysis or opinion around serious injuries, or historical discussion of atrocity, can trigger demonetisation.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-bHJGUTc43kg" title="New Youtube Self-Rating Monetization Feature">•</a></sup> Ad format changes have been imposed unilaterally, including expansions of mid-roll eligibility that affect viewers as much as creators.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-dCLxiN_dpjU" title="eevBLAB #76 - HUGE Youtube Ad Changes will impact YOU!">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-ZdbMuuu9trg" title="eevBLAB 115 -  New Youtube Ad Changes FORCED on YOU!">•</a></sup> The 2017 demonetisation wave established the pattern: advertisers are entitled to choose what their money sits beside, but the resulting sweeps caught large amounts of unobjectionable content.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-Gwp645Vv8Bs" title="eevBLAB #41 - VidMe Is Shutting Down!">•</a></sup>

Sponsorship is a parallel income stream with its own market. VPN vendors and similar advertisers employ in-house staff and talent agencies to approach creators, targeting by channel size, and often bundle deliverables such as one video cross-posted to several social platforms plus travel to the sponsor's headquarters.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-GiN9d6NnTDA" title="eevBLAB #67 - Shilling NordVPN &amp; Youtube Sponsorships">•</a></sup> Offers should be benchmarked against trade-media advertising rate cards rather than against a creator's own monthly platform revenue; a $2,000 offer can be a substantial fraction of monthly ad income while still being far below what equivalent trade advertising costs.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-kEp4k8TGzeI" title="eevBLAB #27 - Paid Blog &amp; Review Offers">•</a></sup>

By CPM, alternative platforms have at times paid better. A decentralised competitor returned $2.53 US CPM, about 1.42 times the per-thousand-views rate of the main platform, though on an order of magnitude fewer views — $458 a month against roughly $3,300.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-wU3FAIaAF0I" title="eevBLAB #75 - Income Shootout! LBRY vs Youtube!">•</a></sup>

## Copyright enforcement

Content ID lets rights holders, particularly large corporations, register a reference library; every subsequently uploaded video is automatically matched against it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-6IzVrEk72ww" title="eevBLAB #56 - Another Bogus Copyright Claim">•</a></sup> The failure mode is that entities which upload material they do not own have that material enter the reference database anyway, after which the system automatically issues claims against the legitimate owner.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-6IzVrEk72ww" title="eevBLAB #56 - Another Bogus Copyright Claim">•</a></sup> A claim can demand the disputed video's monetisation or block it outright, and the notice identifies the claimant but not the source recording, so the accused creator cannot inspect what the match was made against.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-6IzVrEk72ww" title="eevBLAB #56 - Another Bogus Copyright Claim">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-gBKEvHhRvSs" title="eevBLAB 139 - The Youtube Copyright System is BROKEN">•</a></sup> Claims routinely land on years-old videos.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-gBKEvHhRvSs" title="eevBLAB 139 - The Youtube Copyright System is BROKEN">•</a></sup> Early false matches on ordinary speech were trivially disputed; later claims from aggregators are contested, re-asserted and upheld against creators who own the material outright, with an appeal risking a channel strike.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-b_Syp2uyqtE" title="eevBLAB #37 - Youtube Copyright Trolls">•</a></sup>

## Content restriction and platform-wide interventions

Restricted Mode, intended to shield institutional and child viewers from questionable material, was applied so bluntly that comments were disabled across effectively every channel on the platform including the platform's own, while purely technical channels also had videos hidden.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-0Yr8sF6osyM" title="eevBLAB #31 - Youtube Restricted Mode Comments WTF?!">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-hrj9IAHWKkw" title="eevBLAB #32 - Youtube Restricted Mode Followup">•</a></sup>

The COPPA settlement — $170 million paid to the FTC over tracking of children's viewing — shifted the compliance burden onto creators.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-Ywj-FcVNhec" title="eevBLAB #68 - Youtube COPPA Adpocalypse 3.0">•</a></sup> Every video and channel must be marked as child-directed or not, and if a video is later deemed kid-directed and was not so marked, the creator, not the platform doing the tracking, faces a fine of over $42,000 per video.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-Ywj-FcVNhec" title="eevBLAB #68 - Youtube COPPA Adpocalypse 3.0">•</a></sup> Comments were separately removed wholesale from all children's content.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-JvOnvDJbKsc" title="eevBLAB 89 - Youtube is DEAD - Dislike Count REMOVED!">•</a></sup>

Public dislike counts were made private across all videos in 2021, on the stated grounds of reducing coordinated dislike attacks and protecting small creators.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-JvOnvDJbKsc" title="eevBLAB 89 - Youtube is DEAD - Dislike Count REMOVED!">•</a></sup> The dislike button still functions and still tunes personal recommendations, but the count is no longer visible.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-JvOnvDJbKsc" title="eevBLAB 89 - Youtube is DEAD - Dislike Count REMOVED!">•</a></sup> The engineering objection is that creators already had per-video and per-channel controls to disable ratings entirely, so the platform-wide removal solved a problem creators could already solve while destroying a signal with genuine diagnostic value: a heavy dislike ratio is a primary public indicator that a video's claims are disputed or have been debunked, and small channels are not the ones subject to targeted dislike attacks in the first place.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-JvOnvDJbKsc" title="eevBLAB 89 - Youtube is DEAD - Dislike Count REMOVED!">•</a></sup> Competing platforms retained visible dislike counters.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-JvOnvDJbKsc" title="eevBLAB 89 - Youtube is DEAD - Dislike Count REMOVED!">•</a></sup> A browser plugin subsequently reconstructed approximate dislike counts from extrapolated plugin-user data, which is statistically weakest on low-view videos and blind to app-based viewing.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-6H5UymGEmvs" title="Return Youtube Dislike Count Plugin TESTED - Is it accurate?">•</a></sup>

Enforcement is opaque and asymmetric. Channels are terminated or restricted without a specific cited cause or link to the offending material, live streaming can be revoked indefinitely after a single strike, including false flags, and an established repair channel with years of history and nearly two million views was deleted outright.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src--oiPAhdGdnM" title="Filling out the Youtube Creator Survey">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1163" title="EEVblog #1163 - Xmas Mailbag">1163</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-W0mMOHrftgU" title="Youtube DELETED Jordan Pier&#x27;s Electronics Repair Channel!">•</a></sup>

## Vote and view manipulation

Dislikes, likes, views and subscribers are purchasable commodities. Vendors openly sell them — for example 2,000 views for $4, delivered within half a day — and many use paid humans who briefly open a video and interact, making the traffic hard to distinguish from organic engagement.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-OU08zwBH-wo" title="eevBLAB #13 - Buying Youtube DISLIKES! - The Batteriser">•</a></sup> Targeted dislike campaigns against specific debunking videos are visible in a channel's own analytics as sharp anomalies against an otherwise stable baseline.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-OU08zwBH-wo" title="eevBLAB #13 - Buying Youtube DISLIKES! - The Batteriser">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-MA5cMpHz3o4" title="eevBLAB #49 - Paid Dislikes! (AGAIN!) - BattBump">•</a></sup> View metrics are also distorted by unrelated infrastructure: after a change to an ad-blocker-related list in August, desktop views stopped being counted even when the video was watched, while revenue was unaffected — a problem for creators mainly because view counts are the currency used to negotiate sponsorships.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-XlEjL8tWM6U" title="The Youtube Aug10th View Drop Theory">•</a></sup>

## Comment spam and account compromise

Comment sections are attacked automatically within seconds of publication. Pornographic link bots reliably occupy the first several comment slots on every upload, operating from an effectively unlimited pool of generated accounts with numeric name suffixes; reporting them individually is a whack-a-mole with no effect.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-AuFSMpFzAnw" title="eevBLAB 117 - The Youtube Bot Comment Problem">•</a></sup> Cryptocurrency spam floods the comments within a minute of release, especially on finance- or crypto-adjacent topics, suggesting keyword triggering from the title or description.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-cejuuK4v_HY" title="Youtube Comment Crypto Spam SCAM">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-nVtefye9Yz4" title="eevBLAB 107 - WARNING: Youtube Telegram Comment SCAM">•</a></sup>

A distinct impersonation scam uses accounts mimicking the channel owner to reply to genuine commenters, directing them to Telegram or, in earlier variants, WhatsApp, with a prize pretext.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-nVtefye9Yz4" title="eevBLAB 107 - WARNING: Youtube Telegram Comment SCAM">•</a></sup> The impersonating accounts are identifiable by randomly generated usernames and a handful of subscribers who are themselves bots.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-nVtefye9Yz4" title="eevBLAB 107 - WARNING: Youtube Telegram Comment SCAM">•</a></sup>

Channel hijacking is the most serious threat. The vector abuses a genuine platform feature: any user can share a private video with any other, which generates a legitimate notification email from youtube.com.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-KbFogh7XqBQ" title="eevBLAB 129 - WARNING: New Youtube Creator Channel Hijack Scam">•</a></sup> Attackers use this to deliver a convincing AI-generated video purporting to come from a creator support team, warning that monetisation will be suspended unless new terms are accepted within seven days, and directing the target to download an infected document that then takes over the channel.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-KbFogh7XqBQ" title="eevBLAB 129 - WARNING: New Youtube Creator Channel Hijack Scam">•</a></sup> The tells are the sending channel's implausible name and negligible subscriber count, and the fact that the platform states it will never contact creators or share information through a private video.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-KbFogh7XqBQ" title="eevBLAB 129 - WARNING: New Youtube Creator Channel Hijack Scam">•</a></sup> Generative video makes such lures materially more convincing than earlier text phishing.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-KbFogh7XqBQ" title="eevBLAB 129 - WARNING: New Youtube Creator Channel Hijack Scam">•</a></sup> Crypto-scam hijacks of this kind have hit channels at every scale, including a 15-million-subscriber channel whose entire channel group was suspended as a containment measure so that content could later be restored.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-qegYDD1A8VA" title="eevBLAB 110 - Linus Tech Tips HACKED!">•</a></sup>

## Machine-generated content

Automatically generated content has become a structural problem rather than a fringe one. Clickbait-titled spam channels in the space, science and technology categories accumulate large view counts and rank well in search on those keywords.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-7O-QckjCXNo" title="eevBLAB 99 - AI SPAM BOT Youtube Space/Science/Tech Channels? - WTF">•</a></sup> Generation tooling operates at a scale that makes moderation by volume infeasible: one such tool alone claimed roughly 150 million videos generated, and even a small fraction of that reaching video platforms represents tens of millions of uploads.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-W6T2TGwc-p8" title="AI Slop of the Day - Award Winner (Multimeters)">•</a></sup>

Disclosure exists but is one-sided. Creators are required to mark AI-generated content or risk channel termination, and the platform applies a visible tag, but there is no corresponding viewer-side control — no setting to exclude AI content from recommendations — so tagged material still appears in feeds.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-W6T2TGwc-p8" title="AI Slop of the Day - Award Winner (Multimeters)">•</a></sup>

The platform has also applied generative tooling to creators' own work without opt-in, including speech-model dubbing of a creator's voice into other languages and into a different gender's voice, and auto-generated AI descriptions retroactively attached to a subset of existing videos.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-8Mfw0X5jY3A" title="eevBLAB 123 - MORE Youtube AI BS! - AI Description Summaries">•</a></sup> A one-click AI thumbnail generator analyses the channel and the [[current]] video's metadata to synthesise artwork; results range from usable to substituting an unrelated person for the creator.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-BmcoJqMxT6g" title="eevBLAB 142 - The Inevitable Future of AI Youtube Thumbnails">•</a></sup>

Policy has oscillated. A stated commitment to demonetise channels using AI to produce their content — on the reasoning that a platform premised on people making content for other people has no business monetising the alternative — was followed within about three months by deep integration of the platform's own AI generation tools into short-form uploads.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-SVP7oPLCNuc" title="eevBLAB 130 - Youtube Are FINALLY Demonetising AI Slop Channels?">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-jp-pSRQ7fLE" title="eevBLAB 134 - The End of OG Youtube?">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-T6yQZpOVe-A" title="eevBLAB 133 - YouTube Just Self Destructed (AI Shorts Integration)">•</a></sup> The demonetisation announcement also never addressed whether recommendation would be adjusted, which is a separate lever from monetisation.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-SVP7oPLCNuc" title="eevBLAB 130 - Youtube Are FINALLY Demonetising AI Slop Channels?">•</a></sup>

## Alternative platforms

Leaving the platform entirely is treated as channel suicide regardless of channel size, because it remains the only place to find and build an audience at scale, and because more than half of all traffic arrives through its search and suggestion surfaces rather than through subscriptions.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-wjBV0JSV3xs" title="eevBLAB #69 - The Problem With Bitchute">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-rEOBcUW2Nf8" title="eevBLAB #39 - Youtube Alternatives">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-Gwp645Vv8Bs" title="eevBLAB #41 - VidMe Is Shutting Down!">•</a></sup> The defensible position is redundancy rather than migration: publish everywhere, treat the main platform as primary, and accept that no alternative will carry the audience.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-jp-pSRQ7fLE" title="eevBLAB 134 - The End of OG Youtube?">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-8ajM3kkKwNs" title="eevBLAB #81: Google BANNED LBRY Android App from Play Store">•</a></sup>

Mirroring is largely automatic. Several alternatives pull videos directly from the source channel including description and thumbnail, typically appearing an hour or two after publication; the decentralised LBRY/Odysee stack pulls full 1080p, whereas BitChute pulls only a 480p version, which is inadequate for screen captures and detailed teardown footage where fine detail is the content.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-wU3FAIaAF0I" title="eevBLAB #75 - Income Shootout! LBRY vs Youtube!">•</a></sup> Odysee became the de facto alternative venue for electronics, engineering and science channels, in contrast to other alternative platforms whose content is almost entirely political.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-G5aKdUed5so" title="eevBLAB 116 - The SEC Killed LBRY Inc. What happens to Odysee?">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-E_ChC3eY0zI" title="eevBLAB #84 - 2021 is the Year of Alternatives">•</a></sup>

Competitors face a structural rather than a technical barrier. Content delivery is a solved and contestable problem; community and search are not, and the incumbent's position there is the same kind of position Google holds in web search.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-Gwp645Vv8Bs" title="eevBLAB #41 - VidMe Is Shutting Down!">•</a></sup> Buying creators away does not transfer their audiences, and supporting multiple platforms properly — reading and answering comments on each — is itself close to a full-time job.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-Gwp645Vv8Bs" title="eevBLAB #41 - VidMe Is Shutting Down!">•</a></sup> VidMe, founded in 2014 to offer revenue sharing to small and niche creators at a time when only one major platform provided it, shut down without delivering a distinguishable advantage in search, categorisation or discovery.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-Gwp645Vv8Bs" title="eevBLAB #41 - VidMe Is Shutting Down!">•</a></sup> The ecosystem risk extends beyond the video platform itself: the same parent company removed a competing platform's Android application from its app store without warning.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-8ajM3kkKwNs" title="eevBLAB #81: Google BANNED LBRY Android App from Play Store">•</a></sup>

## Channel operation as a discipline

The channel began on 4 April 2009, recorded on a 320-by-240 webcam at a computer desk rather than in a lab, uploaded to a personal account and posted to an electronics forum, under the then-current ten-minute limit.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1198" title="EEVblog #1198 - 10 Years On Youtube!">1198</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-599" title="EEVblog #599 - 5 years of the EEVblog">599</a></sup> The format — single-take, unscripted, episodic and regular — was chosen because regular episodic engineering content did not exist at the time.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1198" title="EEVblog #1198 - 10 Years On Youtube!">1198</a></sup> The unedited single-take side format exists specifically to bypass the editing, rendering, transcoding and podcast-conversion pipeline and produce content quickly.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-9_qhlFc-MVU" title="eevBLAB #1 - The EEVblog Is Hiring">•</a></sup>

Growth was linear rather than viral, and the operative variable is diligence: persistent output over many years, with no single video responsible for the audience.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-yb73J7dTg_w" title="eevBLAB 86 - On The Importance Of Diligence + NEWS">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-231" title="EEVblog #231 - 2011">231</a></sup> The same reasoning is applied to engineering careers — published, documented, visible work is what distinguishes a candidate, and video is one medium for it among several, with written documentation a fully adequate substitute for anyone uncomfortable on camera.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-KJEmBtvZrPU" title="eevBLAB #78 - Engineering Student Job Advice">•</a></sup>

Cumulative watch time is the measure that captures reach: for a single mid-sized technical channel, aggregate viewing amounted to roughly 53 years of continuous human attention.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-g3y_Vr8ZJ7w" title="The Signal Path Discussion - Youtube &amp; Bell Labs (1/3)">•</a></sup> Advanced and esoteric topics are underserved precisely because their audience is smaller, which trades reach against the value of covering material nobody else covers.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-g3y_Vr8ZJ7w" title="The Signal Path Discussion - Youtube &amp; Bell Labs (1/3)">•</a></sup>
