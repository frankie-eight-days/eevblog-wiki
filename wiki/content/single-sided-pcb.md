A single-sided [[pcb]] carries copper on one face only, with the components mounted on the bare side and their leads passing through to be soldered on the copper side. It is the cheapest form of printed circuit board there is, and it remains cheaper to manufacture than a double-sided board even now that double-sided fabrication has become inexpensive.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1108" title="EEVblog #1108 - Casio CT-X700 Keyboard Teardown">1108</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-LAbWsqS51Tk" title="When you REALLY don&#x27;t want to pay for a Double Sided PCB">•</a></sup> That cost gap is the entire reason the technology survives: it is still the default in high-volume consumer goods, in white goods, and above all in switch-mode power supplies, where a saving of a few cents per unit outweighs every inconvenience it creates for the layout engineer.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1111" title="EEVblog #1111 - World&#x27;s First Microcontroller &amp; Electronic Game">1111</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1108" title="EEVblog #1108 - Casio CT-X700 Keyboard Teardown">1108</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-187" title="EEVblog #187 - Tektronix TDS2024C Oscilloscope Teardown">187</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-770" title="EEVblog #770 - Rockband 3 Stratocaster Guitar Teardown">770</a></sup>

## Why it persists

The economics are brutally simple. A board with copper on one side needs one imaging and etching pass and no through-hole plating, so it undercuts a double-sided board on price at any volume where the difference matters.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1108" title="EEVblog #1108 - Casio CT-X700 Keyboard Teardown">1108</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-187" title="EEVblog #187 - Tektronix TDS2024C Oscilloscope Teardown">187</a></sup> Appliance manufacture is the clearest case: even inside expensive equipment, the [[power-supply]] board will typically be single-sided, because it was built down to the last cent.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1ngqB4mxZOI" title="Smeg Oven Goes Clunkedy Clunk + Capacitor Go Poop">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-289" title="EEVblog #289-1 - VCR Teardown &amp; Handifax Followup">289</a></sup> The pattern shows up across televisions, microwave ovens, washing machines and similar goods,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1108" title="EEVblog #1108 - Casio CT-X700 Keyboard Teardown">1108</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1111" title="EEVblog #1111 - World&#x27;s First Microcontroller &amp; Electronic Game">1111</a></sup> in plasma TV supplies,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-52" title="EEVblog #52 - Panasonic Plasma TV&#x27;s Suck (and a Teardown)">52</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-725" title="EEVblog #725 - LG Plasma TV Teardown">725</a></sup> in PC and instrument supplies,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-462" title="EEVblog #462 - Video Editing PC Assembly">462</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-630" title="EEVblog #630 - How To: Soniq LCD TV Troubleshooting Repair - Part 1">630</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-607" title="EEVblog #607 - Agilent B2912A Source Measure Unit SMU Teardown">607</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1265" title="EEVblog #1265 - $53 360W Lab Bench PSU!">1265</a></sup> in the supply of a bench [[oscilloscope]],<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-187" title="EEVblog #187 - Tektronix TDS2024C Oscilloscope Teardown">187</a></sup> and in the cheapest toys and novelties, where the whole product has to reach the absolute lowest possible cost.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-292" title="EEVblog #292 - Johnny B. Goode">292</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-afErPINq8qc" title="Mini Arcade Game Console Repair OOPSIE!">•</a></sup>

The material usually goes with the process. Single-sided boards are frequently phenolic-based rather than fiberglass, a substrate summarised as "none of that FR4 rubbish".<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1ngqB4mxZOI" title="Smeg Oven Goes Clunkedy Clunk + Capacitor Go Poop">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1562" title="EEVblog 1562 - THE 1980&#x27;s Boombox TEARDOWN - Sharp GF-7600">1562</a></sup> Phenolic single-sided construction appears in 1970s and 1980s consumer gear as a matter of course,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1111" title="EEVblog #1111 - World&#x27;s First Microcontroller &amp; Electronic Game">1111</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-443" title="EEVblog #443 - Mailbag">443</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-936" title="EEVblog #936 - Mailbag">936</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-991" title="EEVblog #991 - Mailbag">991</a></sup> and it was still being used in low-cost boards subcontracted out for plasma TV drivers.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-446" title="EEVblog #446 - Dumpster Diving Samsung Plasma TV">446</a></sup> The combination has real electrical consequences: a cheap phenolic single-sided board offers no [[ground-plane]] and no controlled-[[impedance]] routing, which is disqualifying for high-speed work such as [[usb]] 2.0 signalling.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-12" title="EEVblog #12 Part 2 of 2 - Shanghai Special - Dodgy USB Hubs">12</a></sup>

## Jumper links

Routing a circuit with one copper layer eventually runs out of room, and the standard escape is a jumper link — a wire or a zero-ohm [[resistor]] that hops one trace over another on the component side. Links are the visible signature of the technique, and vintage consumer boards carry them in large numbers.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-256" title="EEVblog #256 - Yamaha PSR-80 Keyboard Teardown">256</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-378" title="EEVblog #378 - Dumpster Diving Teardown Repair">378</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-303" title="EEVblog #303 - Photocopier Extreme Teardown">303</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-142" title="EEVblog #142 - Electronics Magazine Memories">142</a></sup> The trade is made deliberately: one speaker board used what amounted to an entire reel of zero-ohm jumpers rather than pay for a double-sided PCB, because the manufacturer still judged the links cheaper.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-LAbWsqS51Tk" title="When you REALLY don&#x27;t want to pay for a Double Sided PCB">•</a></sup> The same reasoning scales up to heavy [[current]], where a single-sided power board may carry bus-bar-sized links.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-378" title="EEVblog #378 - Dumpster Diving Teardown Repair">378</a></sup>

Some designs avoid discrete links entirely by other means. A keyboard controller used screen-printed carbon overlaid on the copper, so the carbon runs served both as membrane contact pads and as the jumpers, which was cheaper than adding gold flash and individual link components — and cheaper still than moving to a double-sided board for layout reasons alone.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1108" title="EEVblog #1108 - Casio CT-X700 Keyboard Teardown">1108</a></sup> An infrared remote used the same construction: ordinary copper traces under [[solder-mask]], with carbon printed on top making contact with exposed copper pads.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-505" title="EEVblog #505 - IR Learning Remote Control Hack">505</a></sup>

## The craft of one-layer layout

Getting a design onto one layer with no links is treated as the "holy grail of PCB design".<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-353" title="EEVblog #353 - Mailbag">353</a></sup> It is a genuine skill rather than a formality: fitting an [[led]] display, op-amps, trimmers and through-hole resistors onto one side is not a trivial task,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1171" title="EEVblog #1171 - Yihau WEP SMD Rework Station Meltdown!">1171</a></sup> and boards that achieve it with only a couple of links, or none, are noted as good work.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-964" title="EEVblog #964 - Mailbag">964</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-928" title="EEVblog #928 - Jaycar DMX RGB LED Lights Teardown">928</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-606" title="EEVblog #606 - Mailbag">606</a></sup> The difficulty is compounded when the mechanical designers dictate the outline, leaving too little board area to route what the cost engineer has insisted must be a single layer.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1171" title="EEVblog #1171 - Yihau WEP SMD Rework Station Meltdown!">1171</a></sup> Tilting a chip a few degrees to free up two more escape routes is one of the moves that results.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1171" title="EEVblog #1171 - Yihau WEP SMD Rework Station Meltdown!">1171</a></sup>

The constraint reaches into other details. [[silkscreen]] designators are placed outside the component outlines so they remain readable once parts are fitted, which matters especially for a kit.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-244" title="EEVblog #244 - How To Lay Out A PCB - PSU Design Part 9">244</a></sup> Some manufacturers silkscreen the entire copper pattern onto the component side, so the traces on the far face can be followed without turning the board over.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1562" title="EEVblog 1562 - THE 1980&#x27;s Boombox TEARDOWN - Sharp GF-7600">1562</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-g2kCBfUa9H0" title="Will this $5 Oscilloscope Work?">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-502" title="EEVblog #502 - $19 Hameg Analog Oscilloscope">502</a></sup> Surface-mount parts can still be used: they are glued to the copper side, run through a [[wave-soldering]] machine, and appear as wave-soldered SMD on what is otherwise a through-hole single-sided board.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1ngqB4mxZOI" title="Smeg Oven Goes Clunkedy Clunk + Capacitor Go Poop">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-443" title="EEVblog #443 - Mailbag">443</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1278" title="EEVblog #1278 - Mailbag">1278</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1243" title="EEVblog #1243 - Sony Boombox REPAIR and Teardown">1243</a></sup>

Modern multilayer practice inherits the discipline. Routing an SMD board is essentially a single-sided design problem, because surface-mount parts do not provide a [[via]] at every pin the way through-hole parts do; the goal is to get as many signals as possible onto the top layer and fill in power and the remainder on the other layers.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1323" title="EEVblog #1323 - PCB Layout Review &amp; Analysis">1323</a></sup>

## Servicing and reverse engineering

A single-sided board is markedly easier to work on. All the copper and all the joints are visible from one face, so solder joints can be inspected directly<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1301" title="EEVblog #1301 - Arcade Machine Repair">1301</a></sup> and a circuit can be traced out without flipping between sides.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-378" title="EEVblog #378 - Dumpster Diving Teardown Repair">378</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-502" title="EEVblog #502 - $19 Hameg Analog Oscilloscope">502</a></sup> [[reverse-engineering]] is easier for the same reason: there are no traces running under components on the top side that would have to be exposed by desoldering parts.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1313" title="EEVblog #1313 - Bad Luck Mailbag">1313</a></sup> Repair of a consumer amplifier is simplified when the board turns out to be single-sided rather than double-sided.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1072" title="EEVblog #1072 - KRK Rokit 6 Studio Monitor Speaker Repair">1072</a></sup>

It also introduces a characteristic fault mode. Because every ground crossing has to jump over other traces through a link, a single bad link can break a ground connection in a way that presents as a dry joint or a missing net, and tracing an unbuzzable ground pin back through the links is a recognised diagnostic path.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-XUyjRm1Upjs" title="That moment you realise the Schematic &amp; PCB don&#x27;t match">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1394" title="EEVblog 1394 - Onkyo Receiver Repair - Part 1">1394</a></sup> The absence of plated-through holes also blocks a common modification technique: the vias do not go through to the other side, so a wire cannot simply be soldered to the back of a via, and hacking into a trace means drilling or scraping solder mask.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-505" title="EEVblog #505 - IR Learning Remote Control Hack">505</a></sup>

## Single-sided load

A related but distinct property is single-sided *load* — all components populated on one face, whatever the number of copper layers. This is chosen for manufacturing cost, since only one placement and [[soldering]] pass is needed,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-189" title="iRiver Story HD eBook Reader TEARDOWN - EEVblog #189">189</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1447" title="EEVblog 1447 - EXPENSIVE Fluke vs CHEAPER Brymen - Teardown">1447</a></sup> and in professional equipment it is also chosen for access and serviceability, keeping the assembly reachable rather than sandwiched.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-598" title="EEVblog #598 - Sony DME7000 Video Multi Effects Processor Teardown">598</a></sup> A comparison of two multimeters found the cheaper-to-construct arrangement was the single-sided load, with the more feature-packed instrument requiring double-sided load.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1447" title="EEVblog 1447 - EXPENSIVE Fluke vs CHEAPER Brymen - Teardown">1447</a></sup> Populating both faces also costs height: moving a phone design from two-sided to one-sided population removed roughly 30 to 40 percent of the board thickness, before counting the shielding that had to be doubled up as well.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-243" title="EEVblog #243 - Vintage Brick Mobile Phone Teardown">243</a></sup> Partial exceptions are common, such as an otherwise single-sided load with only the MOSFETs mounted on the reverse.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-397" title="EEVblog #397 - Turnigy Accucell 6 Charger Teardown">397</a></sup>

## Historical and hobbyist significance

PCBs were expensive enough in the era of electronics magazines that essentially everything published was single-sided.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-142" title="EEVblog #142 - Electronics Magazine Memories">142</a></sup> A double-sided project was hard to publish, because magazines printed PCB artwork for readers to photocopy and etch their own boards, which only works for one layer.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1619" title="EEVblog 1619 - Mailbag: Featuring Don McKenzie">1619</a></sup> Fitting a design into that constraint drove architecture: a RAM stack was the classic workaround for the difficulty of laying out memory on one layer.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-142" title="EEVblog #142 - Electronics Magazine Memories">142</a></sup>

Dave Jones set single-sided construction as a hard design goal for a 32-channel PC-based logic analyser precisely so readers could build it themselves, and that constraint forced the choice of Lattice in-system-programmable devices to cut the pin count enough to fit; the finished board needed only a few jumper links.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-747" title="EEVblog #747 - PC Based Logic Analyser Project">747</a></sup> A contemporaneous competing design used double-sided plated-through boards, a difference driven by cost — a tooling fee for a plated-through double-sided board ran to hundreds of dollars, against the five dollars such a board costs now.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1326" title="EEVblog #1326 - How Engineering Minds Think Alike">1326</a></sup> Kits still use single-sided boards to hold the price down.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-464" title="EEVblog #464 - Mailbag">464</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-353" title="EEVblog #353 - Mailbag">353</a></sup>

For one-off and low-volume work that calculus has now inverted. Unless a design is being built in the millions, there is no longer a large cost advantage in going single-sided, which frees the designer to use the bottom layer as a ground plane instead — even on a layout that could almost have been squeezed onto one side with about a dozen jumper links.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-244" title="EEVblog #244 - How To Lay Out A PCB - PSU Design Part 9">244</a></sup> In production, though, the old rule holds: "single-sided is still cheaper in production".<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-LAbWsqS51Tk" title="When you REALLY don&#x27;t want to pay for a Double Sided PCB">•</a></sup>
