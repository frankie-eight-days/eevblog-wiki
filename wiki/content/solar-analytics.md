Solar Analytics is an Australian home energy monitoring product that logs household [[solar-power-generation|solar production]] and consumption using [[current]] transformers clamped onto circuits inside the switchboard, with the data sent to a cloud service over its own 4G link.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-877" title="EEVblog #877 - Solar Analytics Home Energy Monitoring Installation">877</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1628" title="EEVblog 1628 - Home Solar Power Re-Install + Upgrade + FAIL">1628</a></sup> Its defining property is independence: it has no connection to the inverters it sits alongside, so it measures what is actually flowing in the wiring rather than what an [[inverter]] reports about itself.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-k2_mJtAeaog" title="SNEAKY Enphase Solar Monitoring Modification">•</a></sup> That independence makes it the reference against which inverter-side monitoring is checked, and lets a single installation report the combined output of solar arrays that have no knowledge of each other.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1626" title="EEVblog1626 - YOU HAD ONE JOB! (Solar Power Install FAIL)">1626</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1628" title="EEVblog 1628 - Home Solar Power Re-Install + Upgrade + FAIL">1628</a></sup>

## Measurement hardware and installation

The sensors are split-core magnetic current transformers that the wires simply pass through, so no circuit has to be broken to add a channel.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1385" title="EEVblog 1385 - 8kW Home Solar Power System EXPANSION!">1385</a></sup> The clamps are directional, and the installer has to get the orientation right — otherwise the sign of the measured power is wrong, and correct installation is only confirmed later by comparing the reading against another source.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1390" title="EEVblog 1390 - NEGATIVE Household Solar Consumption? WHY?">1390</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-M4IiR4vW0aY" title="I don&#x27;t have a problem. I only have 4 Solar Monitoring Systems">•</a></sup> The current transformers used in this class of home monitoring equipment, including this one and the Zappi EV charger, are typically 1000:1 ratio devices.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1505" title="EEVblog 1505 - 120W Home Phantom Power? Audit Time!">1505</a></sup>

A monitoring channel is defined by where its clamp sits. The consumption clamp goes on the main incoming conductor so it sees current flowing both into and out of the property; whether the property is importing or exporting is read from the direction of flow.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1390" title="EEVblog 1390 - NEGATIVE Household Solar Consumption? WHY?">1390</a></sup> The physical position along that main run makes no difference to the reading.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-k2_mJtAeaog" title="SNEAKY Enphase Solar Monitoring Modification">•</a></sup> Production clamps go on the AC output of each inverter.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-k2_mJtAeaog" title="SNEAKY Enphase Solar Monitoring Modification">•</a></sup> The installation grew from three channels to six, though the ability to name and configure the new hardware channels was not immediately exposed in the account.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-WtGlolw-BxA" title="Reclaim Heat Pump Hot Water Solar Energy Usage UPDATE">•</a></sup> The unit is fed from its own way in the switchboard, and the 4G [[antenna]] constrains what covers can be fitted over the solar section of the board.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-E0Iz7HscYTQ" title="Electroboom Style ELCB Test &amp; (Hager) Failure">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1651" title="EEVblog 1651 - Home Electrical Switchboard UPGRADE!">1651</a></sup> The property is single phase, with a 60 A incoming [[fuse]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1651" title="EEVblog 1651 - Home Electrical Switchboard UPGRADE!">1651</a></sup>

## Summing two independent solar systems

The house carries two entirely separate generation systems: an original 3 kW array on a Sunny Boy SMA string inverter, and a later 5 kW array of 14 Enphase microinverters reporting to an Enphase Envoy.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1390" title="EEVblog 1390 - NEGATIVE Household Solar Consumption? WHY?">1390</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-k2_mJtAeaog" title="SNEAKY Enphase Solar Monitoring Modification">•</a></sup> Neither inverter system knows the other exists.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-eHCzTC9lku8" title="The DOWNSIDE of my Home Solar Storage Battery">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1628" title="EEVblog 1628 - Home Solar Power Re-Install + Upgrade + FAIL">1628</a></sup> The Solar Analytics installation bridges them by wiring two production current transformers in parallel onto a single input channel, so the channel reads the sum of both arrays' output.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-k2_mJtAeaog" title="SNEAKY Enphase Solar Monitoring Modification">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1385" title="EEVblog 1385 - 8kW Home Solar Power System EXPANSION!">1385</a></sup> The same paralleling trick was applied on the consumption side, after which the system had enough information to do the addition and subtraction needed to derive true household consumption, a figure that had previously been unknown.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1390" title="EEVblog 1390 - NEGATIVE Household Solar Consumption? WHY?">1390</a></sup> Channels are finite, so this came at a cost: the air conditioner had previously had its own clamp, and that measurement was sacrificed to free the channel, the air conditioner load folding into the total instead.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1390" title="EEVblog 1390 - NEGATIVE Household Solar Consumption? WHY?">1390</a></sup>

Total-system figures recorded this way include a 5.3 kW combined peak on a clear day before [[battery]] installation, 5.8 kW after a two-string inverter re-install, and 3.82 kW on a heavily overcast day of which only 2.3 kW came from the Enphase side.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-eHCzTC9lku8" title="The DOWNSIDE of my Home Solar Storage Battery">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-I7wMkLJgnko" title="Home Solar Power System Re-install is now completely working!">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1628" title="EEVblog 1628 - Home Solar Power Re-Install + Upgrade + FAIL">1628</a></sup> Cumulative totals across the life of the installation reached 30 MWh produced, 18 MWh exported and 37 MWh consumed.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1385" title="EEVblog 1385 - 8kW Home Solar Power System EXPANSION!">1385</a></sup>

## Negative consumption with two production sources

Where two independent generation sources feed a switchboard and the monitoring system's clamps only see one of them, calculated consumption can go negative.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1390" title="EEVblog 1390 - NEGATIVE Household Solar Consumption? WHY?">1390</a></sup> The mechanism is a simple summing error at the node. With the house drawing 1 A and each of two arrays generating 2 A, 4 A arrives at the busbar, 1 A goes into the house and 3 A flows out to the grid; a monitor that knows about only one 2 A source subtracts wrongly and reports a negative household consumption.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1390" title="EEVblog 1390 - NEGATIVE Household Solar Consumption? WHY?">1390</a></sup> This is a common outcome of two-system installations and does not occur with a single system.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1390" title="EEVblog 1390 - NEGATIVE Household Solar Consumption? WHY?">1390</a></sup> It appeared on both the 3 kW system's monitoring and on the Enphase side.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1390" title="EEVblog 1390 - NEGATIVE Household Solar Consumption? WHY?">1390</a></sup> Paralleling transformers onto one channel fixes it, and Enphase's software carries a dedicated setting for the same situation.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1390" title="EEVblog 1390 - NEGATIVE Household Solar Consumption? WHY?">1390</a></sup>

## Sampling resolution

Long-term logged data is stored at five-minute intervals, with a live view available down to roughly five seconds.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-k2_mJtAeaog" title="SNEAKY Enphase Solar Monitoring Modification">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-WtGlolw-BxA" title="Reclaim Heat Pump Hot Water Solar Energy Usage UPDATE">•</a></sup> Elsewhere the logging interval is given as 15 minutes, with a live mode available for short periods.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-lndRXed2ylk" title="AGL Energy Smart Meter DODGY?">•</a></sup> Either way the consequence is the same: a load switched on and off inside one logging interval can fail to appear in the stored data at all.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-lndRXed2ylk" title="AGL Energy Smart Meter DODGY?">•</a></sup> Five-minute logging is also too coarse to resolve the brief consumption spikes that drive residual grid import.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-b9K0YoPBGek" title="Why Isn&#x27;t My Electricity Bill Zero with a Home Battery?">•</a></sup> This resolution is nonetheless better than the Enphase Envoy's, which was the deciding argument for keeping both systems rather than retiring one: the Envoy was reassigned to measure only its own production while Solar Analytics kept whole-house production and consumption.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-k2_mJtAeaog" title="SNEAKY Enphase Solar Monitoring Modification">•</a></sup>

## What a battery does to the production measurement

Adding a DC-coupled hybrid inverter and storage battery breaks the production channel. Because the clamps sit on the AC side downstream of the inverter, and the battery charges and discharges before that point, the monitor cannot distinguish current originating at the panels from current originating at the battery — it treats the battery as simply another solar system connected to the AC grid.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-eHCzTC9lku8" title="The DOWNSIDE of my Home Solar Storage Battery">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-gAu8CvMjDrU" title="15kWh AERL LFP Home Storage Battery - First Analysis">•</a></sup> Two visible artefacts follow. Overnight, the production trace continues as if the sun were still up, because the battery is discharging into the house.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-b9K0YoPBGek" title="Why Isn&#x27;t My Electricity Bill Zero with a Home Battery?">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-7WbO1lnsLC8" title="Some Interesting Home Solar Data">•</a></sup> During the day, the characteristic curved solar profile flattens into a plateau, because excess generation above household demand is being absorbed into the battery rather than reaching the clamps.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-gAu8CvMjDrU" title="15kWh AERL LFP Home Storage Battery - First Analysis">•</a></sup> The stored production figure is therefore no longer correct in any useful sense, and the ability to measure genuine total solar production is lost.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-gAu8CvMjDrU" title="15kWh AERL LFP Home Storage Battery - First Analysis">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-eHCzTC9lku8" title="The DOWNSIDE of my Home Solar Storage Battery">•</a></sup> Consumption and per-circuit measurement remain fully valid, which is what the system is still used for.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-gAu8CvMjDrU" title="15kWh AERL LFP Home Storage Battery - First Analysis">•</a></sup> Dave Jones's stated advice from this experience is not to install two separate solar systems as he did, an arrangement accumulated piecemeal over years.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-eHCzTC9lku8" title="The DOWNSIDE of my Home Solar Storage Battery">•</a></sup>

The same data reveals why a battery does not produce a zero electricity bill. Consumption periodically exceeds production by small amounts even overnight with nobody home, averaging around one kilowatt hour per day of grid import.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-b9K0YoPBGek" title="Why Isn&#x27;t My Electricity Bill Zero with a Home Battery?">•</a></sup> The cause is the control loop inside the on-grid inverter, which cannot respond fast enough to abrupt load steps such as fridge and freezer compressors starting; the shortfall during those transients is drawn from the grid.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-b9K0YoPBGek" title="Why Isn&#x27;t My Electricity Bill Zero with a Home Battery?">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-7WbO1lnsLC8" title="Some Interesting Home Solar Data">•</a></sup> When production and consumption traces overlay exactly, no grid energy is being imported at all — the battery is covering the entire load.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-lndRXed2ylk" title="AGL Energy Smart Meter DODGY?">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-uE0x8YR7nMg" title="Enphase Envoy S FAILING?">•</a></sup>

## Diagnostic use

Because it is independent of both inverter manufacturers, the system serves as the arbiter when inverter-side monitoring is unavailable or suspect. When the Enphase Envoy failed to boot, it was the only way to determine whether the microinverters were still producing without physically putting a [[current-clamp|current clamp]] on the output.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-uE0x8YR7nMg" title="Enphase Envoy S FAILING?">•</a></sup> During a botched install where the installer claimed the microinverters were not connected, the independent record showed no produced energy whatsoever on the day in question.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1626" title="EEVblog1626 - YOU HAD ONE JOB! (Solar Power Install FAIL)">1626</a></sup> It also caught a hybrid inverter dropping to zero grid [[voltage]] and going off grid.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-OouUyaUR250" title="Deye Hybrid Inverter Grid Failure">•</a></sup>

Per-circuit clamps on hot water, pool and EV charging turn it into a load-profiling tool: the [[heat-pump-hot-water-system|heat pump hot water system]]'s daily ramp is visible as a distinct step, pool pump timing can be shifted to align with generation, and EV charging in solar-following mode can be seen tracking the production curve almost exactly, including stopping and restarting as shade passed.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-WtGlolw-BxA" title="Reclaim Heat Pump Hot Water Solar Energy Usage UPDATE">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-5x8UVZnwyL8" title="New Enphase Solar Power System - 1st Day">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-EYx46kRv2Bw" title="Myenergi Zappi 7kW EV Charger Installation &amp; Testing">•</a></sup> Live production readings cross-check against other monitors — 1.9 kW read simultaneously on the Zappi and on Solar Analytics.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-M4IiR4vW0aY" title="I don&#x27;t have a problem. I only have 4 Solar Monitoring Systems">•</a></sup> Of four separate solar monitoring systems on the property, this is the one used most, because it is the only one giving the combined picture.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-M4IiR4vW0aY" title="I don&#x27;t have a problem. I only have 4 Solar Monitoring Systems">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1511" title="EEVblog 1511 - Solar Analytics System FAIL! (+ Dave gets ZAPPED!)">1511</a></sup>

The service also pushes automated underperformance alerts, notifying the owner that the system is generating less than expected and that the installing company has already been contacted; the listed candidate causes include incorrect system details, incorrect device wiring, an actual system fault, inverter fault, overvoltage, shading and seasonal change.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-uuLwmQ7oLo8" title="Solar Power System Monitor ALERT!">•</a></sup>

## Software and hardware shortcomings

The web interface auto-scales the y-axis with no manual override and no full-screen mode, which makes comparing days awkward.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-dKOmNkVLUAA" title="Home Solar Upgrade Power Consumption">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-7WbO1lnsLC8" title="Some Interesting Home Solar Data">•</a></sup> A software change at one point corrupted the y-axis by an order of magnitude, an error reproduced independently by another user.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-vDtxmikcYuc" title="Who Screwed With The Solar Analytics Code?">•</a></sup>

The 4G monitoring unit itself has failed. One stopped logging abruptly at 7 p.m. with the L3 [[led|LED]] flashing to indicate loss of 4G connection.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1628" title="EEVblog 1628 - Home Solar Power Re-Install + Upgrade + FAIL">1628</a></sup> A teardown of a failed unit found a [[supercapacitor]] failure that burnt through the board, destroying a [[diode]] and reflowing a nearby [[resistor]] into the wrong orientation; the balance resistor across the two series supercapacitors was missing from the assembly.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-WFVfbu1Xz3A" title="Solar Analytics 4G Solar Monitor TEARDOWN">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1511" title="EEVblog 1511 - Solar Analytics System FAIL! (+ Dave gets ZAPPED!)">1511</a></sup> The manufacturer reported not having seen this failure mode in over 70,000 installed units.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-WFVfbu1Xz3A" title="Solar Analytics 4G Solar Monitor TEARDOWN">•</a></sup> Restoring service after a failure required the company to re-enable the account, with a substantial unexplained delay.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-WtGlolw-BxA" title="Reclaim Heat Pump Hot Water Solar Energy Usage UPDATE">•</a></sup>

The product is sold on a subscription with a money-back guarantee tied to achieving a stated level of savings.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-877" title="EEVblog #877 - Solar Analytics Home Energy Monitoring Installation">877</a></sup> After a five-year plan and a renewal, the subscription was ultimately cancelled at 95 dollars, with the search moving to an alternative — preferably open source — for the function that actually mattered, monitoring individual circuits.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-k2_mJtAeaog" title="SNEAKY Enphase Solar Monitoring Modification">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-wQbCsk7UAFc" title="eevBLAB 141 - Solar Analytics is a RIP OFF!">•</a></sup>
