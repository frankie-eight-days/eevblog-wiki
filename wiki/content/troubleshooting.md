Troubleshooting is the practice of locating a fault in a circuit or piece of equipment by measurement and inference rather than by guesswork, and it is treated as a discipline distinct from design.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-65" title="EEVblog #65 - Umm, I Design Computers">65</a></sup> Formal training in electronics design does not confer it: "troubleshooting is an art in itself", and an engineer confronted with unfamiliar gear starts from square one no matter how many years of design experience they carry.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-65" title="EEVblog #65 - Umm, I Design Computers">65</a></sup> Familiarity with a specific machine dominates general qualification — someone intimately acquainted with a particular piece of equipment, formally educated or not, will out-diagnose a stranger to it every time.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-65" title="EEVblog #65 - Umm, I Design Computers">65</a></sup> The counterpart is that it is a core hobbyist competence: the ability to answer the question of what to do when a working thing stops working is one of the main practical arguments for learning electronics fundamentals rather than assembling modules and writing code.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-zyuRcsM0gjI" title="eevBLAB #10 - Why Learn Basic Electronics?">•</a></sup>

## The golden rule: measure voltages

The first and most frequently invoked rule of troubleshooting is stated as a commandment — "thou shalt test voltages".<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-65" title="EEVblog #65 - Umm, I Design Computers">65</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-538" title="EEVblog #538 - HP35670A DSA Repair - Part 2">538</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-536" title="EEVblog #536 - HP35670A DSA Repair">536</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-803" title="EEVblog #803 - HP1740A Analog Oscilloscope">803</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-710" title="EEVblog #710 - Intercom System Repair">710</a></sup> It applies before theory, before the [[service-manual|service manual]], and before any suspicion of a specific component: the rails are checked first, and work proceeds outward from there.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-65" title="EEVblog #65 - Umm, I Design Computers">65</a></sup> On a repair where local on-card regulation was suspected, the service manual was set aside entirely and every supply [[voltage]] was accessed through the [[ribbon-cable|ribbon cable]] so the regulators could be measured directly.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-538" title="EEVblog #538 - HP35670A DSA Repair - Part 2">538</a></sup> The rule covers [[ground]] pins as well as supply pins, because a chip with a missing VCC or a missing ground connection can continue to appear to work — parasitically powered through its ESD protection diodes — producing a fault that is extremely difficult to reason about unless the pin voltages are actually measured.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-831" title="EEVblog #831 - Power A Micro With No Power Pin!">831</a></sup>

The rule is invoked as much in the breach as in the observance. On a mains heater repair, failing to measure the rail voltage out of the front-end [[bridge-rectifier|diode bridge]] cost roughly ten to fifteen minutes; the measurement would immediately have shown about half the voltage needed to drive the 24 V [[relay]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1481" title="EEVblog 1481 - Dodgy Dangerous Heater REPAIR">1481</a></sup> On an [[oscilloscope]] repair, the absence of marked, accessible test points on the top of the board meant voltages were not checked straight away, and the search detoured through the crystal and the reset lines before the fault was found.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-536" title="EEVblog #536 - HP35670A DSA Repair">536</a></sup> Deferring the measurement did not fail because the theory was wrong; it failed because the cheap test was skipped in favour of expensive ones.

## Symptoms as evidence

Symptoms are treated as directional evidence: they determine where attention is spent next.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1452" title="EEVblog 1452 - Fluke PM3370B Combiscope REPAIR">1452</a></sup> A high-[[frequency]] hum heard at shutdown on a combiscope ruled out a failed high-ESR electrolytic on the primary side, because that failure mode does not produce that symptom, and redirected the search to something charging up and arcing over on the high-voltage side.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1452" title="EEVblog 1452 - Fluke PM3370B Combiscope REPAIR">1452</a></sup> Where two identical signal chains are both dead, a single shared cause — a common front end, or a supply feeding both over the [[coaxial-cable|coax]] — is far more probable than two independent failures, and is tested first.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-966" title="EEVblog #966 - CPM700 Counter Surveillance Monitor Repair">966</a></sup>

Probability governs the search order generally: rather than exhaustively eliminating every possibility, the odds are played, and a low-probability candidate such as a failing ceramic [[decoupling-capacitor|decoupling capacitor]] is not ruled out but is not chased first either.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-398" title="EEVblog #398 - Lecroy 9384C Oscilloscope Repair">398</a></sup> On a failed 4K television, the symptoms were narrowed to identify the highest-probability chip, which was then reflowed as a test of that hypothesis.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1154" title="EEVblog #1154 - Surprising 4K Dumpster TV Fault">1154</a></sup> The same logic prioritises effort by cost of investigation: on a tablet reader with both a charging fault and a suspect [[touchscreen]] controller, the charging problem was attacked first — the easy fault before the rabbit hole of fine connections in the touch controller — on the expectation that the second symptom might be downstream of the first.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1370" title="EEVblog #1370 - Kindle Paperwhite REPAIR">1370</a></sup>

An older technique for chains of signal-processing stages is signal injection: a tone, historically around 1 kHz, is fed in at successive points while the output is monitored, and the stage at which the signal stops is the faulty one.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-966" title="EEVblog #966 - CPM700 Counter Surveillance Monitor Repair">966</a></sup> [[reference-waveform|Reference waveform]] comparison serves the same narrowing function on a modern instrument — capturing one point and overlaying another establishes whether two nodes are behaving identically, and a match is as informative a result as a difference.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1324" title="EEVblog #1324 - Oscilloscope Reference Waveforms are USEFUL">1324</a></sup> Comparison against a known-good unit is the first move where one is available, starting with visual inspection.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-884" title="EEVblog #884 - EEVBlog BM235 Multimeter REPAIR">884</a></sup>

## False leads

A fault that produces a plausible but wrong signature is described as leading the investigator up the garden path.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-230" title="EEVblog #230 - ArduCopter ArduPilot Troubleshooting">230</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-379" title="EEVBlog #379 - Yamaha RX-V557 Receiver Fix">379</a></sup> On a receiver repair, the measured voltages were close enough to the service manual's figures at the relevant test point that the supply was cleared, while the actual culprit was a metallized polyester [[capacitor]] — an unusual failure that the correct application of the golden rule did not catch.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-379" title="EEVBlog #379 - Yamaha RX-V557 Receiver Fix">379</a></sup> On a flight controller, a shorted [[5-volt-rail|5 V rail]] was pursued through the FTDI chip and then across the board before the real source was isolated.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-230" title="EEVblog #230 - ArduCopter ArduPilot Troubleshooting">230</a></sup> On a bench supply investigation, a theory that a regulator was overheating and dropping out to cause [[processor]] resets was nearly abandoned when the 5 V rail failed to collapse, sag to 3 V, or show gross [[ripple]] as expected.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-512" title="EEVblog #512 - Rigol DP832 Bad Design Investigation">512</a></sup>

Failure to find anything broken is itself an unsatisfying outcome, because a component that has visibly failed both confirms the diagnosis and completes the repair.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1428" title="EEVblog 1428 - Sony RX100 IV Camera Repair and then WTF!">1428</a></sup> Intermittent and mechanical faults are the hardest class: an intermittent [[multimeter]] rotary switch problem that may live in the plastics or the contacts can consume days or weeks, and characterising it properly calls for a population of units — on the order of fifty — and a jig that mechanically agitates them to reproduce the fault.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-IoRks5bJw8Y" title="Brymen BM786 Switch Fault Investigation">•</a></sup> Some sessions end without a result; an hour spent eliminating candidates on an oscilloscope with a missing 3.3 V rail is a normal outcome rather than an anomaly.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-398" title="EEVblog #398 - Lecroy 9384C Oscilloscope Repair">398</a></sup> There is a class boundary too: once a device boots and outputs correct data but hangs in self-test, the remaining problem is software or configuration and requires a different class of troubleshooting.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1061" title="EEVblog #1061 - Data IO Programmer REPAIR - Part 1">1061</a></sup>

## Documentation, test points and design for repair

Design choices determine how tractable a fault will be. Test points belong at the top of a board so the unit can be diagnosed while it remains in the chassis.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1237" title="EEVblog #1237 - Old School Teardown: HP3785A Jitter Analyser">1237</a></sup> Front-panel diagnostic or programming connectors, accessible only once the cover is off, exist so rack-mounted equipment can be serviced or reconfigured without reaching the inaccessible rear panel.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-598" title="EEVblog #598 - Sony DME7000 Video Multi Effects Processor Teardown">598</a></sup> Built-in self-test with fault LEDs that indicate which section has failed is a direct aid at the bench.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-569" title="EEVblog #569 - Tour of an Analog TV Transmission Facility">569</a></sup> Conversely, a secondary board that must be desoldered before the main board is reachable prevents injecting a signal and measuring the result simultaneously, and makes the job harder.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-884" title="EEVblog #884 - EEVBlog BM235 Multimeter REPAIR">884</a></sup> White and black [[solder]] masks obscure the traces, complicating the [[reverse-engineering|reverse engineering]] that troubleshooting without a [[schematic]] requires.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-330" title="EEVblog #330 - Medela Swing Teardown &amp; Repair">330</a></sup>

Older service manuals are valued for supporting fault-finding directly rather than merely describing the product: theory of operation, complete schematics, DC bias voltages printed on the circuit diagrams, timing diagrams, flow charts, marked voltage and frequency test points, alignment procedures, and symptom-indexed entries specific enough to name a pin — a read error on a floppy disk traced to IC1 pin 6 low.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-752" title="EEVblog #752 - Original Sony Walkman TPS-L2 Teardown">752</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-ZBVyJaLAZDY" title="Scanning Vintage Computer Service Manuals - Tandy 1100FD Laptop Computer">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1410" title="EEVblog 1410 - Mailbag">1410</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1376" title="EEVblog 1376 - Tandy 102 Vintage Computer REPAIR">1376</a></sup> Instruments with an internal fault log and an extensive manual troubleshooting section allow a coded error to be tracked down step by step.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-536" title="EEVblog #536 - HP35670A DSA Repair">536</a></sup> Without a schematic, the alternative is tracing the board out and reverse engineering it, which is often not worth the time.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1035" title="EEVblog #1035 - Flaming DIY Power Supply!">1035</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-710" title="EEVblog #710 - Intercom System Repair">710</a></sup>

## Learning by breaking

Troubleshooting is held to be the most efficient way to learn electronics, to the point that a project failing on first build is regarded as the desirable outcome: "I hope your next project you build up doesn't work and you have to troubleshoot it", because that is where the learning happens.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-500" title="EEVblog #500 - Live Show">500</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-zyuRcsM0gjI" title="eevBLAB #10 - Why Learn Basic Electronics?">•</a></sup> Beginner kits and books are judged partly on whether they include troubleshooting procedures for when a circuit does not work — including the possibility that the build is correct and the contacts or [[breadboard]] are at fault rather than the wiring.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1710" title="EEVblog 1710 - Mailbag: Tennismatic, Breadboards, Books &amp; Boost Converters">1710</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-27aG9xhfk6s" title="See Blocks Review">•</a></sup> Investigating an unfamiliar board also teaches component identification, since the process forces the question of what an unrecognised part actually is and how to deduce it from context.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1461" title="EEVblog 1461 - The MOSFET Search CHALLENGE">1461</a></sup>

A precondition is having a definite failure to work with: "troubleshooting is easy if you've got something to troubleshoot", whereas a vague complaint that something is not quite right offers no starting point beyond measuring the rails and working down, which can take days or weeks.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-400" title="EEVblog #400 - 400th Live Show">400</a></sup> Time pressure and distraction degrade the process — troubleshooting while simultaneously filming is acknowledged to make it harder, and to contribute to replacing parts that should not have been replaced.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1322" title="EEVblog #1322 - JBL LSR308 Monitor Speaker Repair (FULL)">1322</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1520" title="EEVblog 1520 - Troubleshooting a Faulty BM786 Multimeter">1520</a></sup>

Two working heuristics recur. When a device is suspected of a [[solder-joint|bad solder joint]] on a fine-pitch package, "When in doubt, reflow."<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-366" title="EEVblog #366 - USB PSU Troubleshooting">366</a></sup> And instructions, assumptions and appearances are all to be verified by measurement rather than believed — do not assume something is overloaded, measure it, and double check.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-804" title="EEVblog #804 - HP1740A Oscilloscope Repair - Part 2">804</a></sup>
