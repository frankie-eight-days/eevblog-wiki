A capacitor is two conductive plates separated by a dielectric — an insulator, whether air, mica, ceramic, plastic film or the epoxy resin of a circuit board — with no direct electrical connection between them.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1406" title="EEVblog 1406 - DC Fundamentals Part 7: DC Circuit Transients Fundamentals">1406</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-486" title="EEVblog #486 - Does Current Flow Through A Capacitor?">486</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1117" title="EEVblog #1117 - PCB Power Plane Capacitance">1117</a></sup> It stores energy in the electric field across that dielectric, the complement of the [[inductor]], which stores energy in a [[magnetic-field]] in a coil and its core.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1409" title="EEVblog 1409 - The DANGERS of Inductor Back EMF">1409</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1406" title="EEVblog 1406 - DC Fundamentals Part 7: DC Circuit Transients Fundamentals">1406</a></sup> Because it stores rather than dissipates, it introduces time into a circuit: charge takes time to build and time to leave, and that single property underpins timing, filtering, energy buffering, coupling and phase shift across essentially all of electronics.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1406" title="EEVblog 1406 - DC Fundamentals Part 7: DC Circuit Transients Fundamentals">1406</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1470" title="EEVblog 1470 - AC Basics Tutorial Part 3 - Complex Numbers are EASY!">1470</a></sup>

The governing relation is Q equals C times V, charge in coulombs equalling [[capacitance]] times [[voltage]], from which the [[current]] relation I equals C dV/dt follows directly.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1406" title="EEVblog 1406 - DC Fundamentals Part 7: DC Circuit Transients Fundamentals">1406</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-486" title="EEVblog #486 - Does Current Flow Through A Capacitor?">486</a></sup> Increasing the dielectric constant of the material between the plates increases the capacitance; air has a dielectric constant of one and most other materials are higher.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1406" title="EEVblog 1406 - DC Fundamentals Part 7: DC Circuit Transients Fundamentals">1406</a></sup> Capacitance is not a property reserved for components: any two conductors in any space form a capacitor, including the contacts of an open switch, adjacent wires, a [[pcb-trace]] and the [[ground-plane]] beneath it, and the input of every logic gate.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-486" title="EEVblog #486 - Does Current Flow Through A Capacitor?">486</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1085" title="EEVblog #1085 - Bypass Capacitors Visualised!">1085</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1728" title="EEVblog 1728 - AC Basics Tutorial Part 6: Impedance, Conductance, Susceptance, Admittance">1728</a></sup>

## Charge, discharge, and the short-circuit rule

The foundational rule for transient analysis is that a fully discharged capacitor behaves as a [[short-circuit]] at the instant voltage is applied. If there is no charge on the plates there can be no volts across them regardless of the capacitance value, so current at time zero is at maximum and then tapers off as the voltage rises, a mirror image of the voltage curve.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1406" title="EEVblog 1406 - DC Fundamentals Part 7: DC Circuit Transients Fundamentals">1406</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-486" title="EEVblog #486 - Does Current Flow Through A Capacitor?">486</a></sup> Inductors are the exact inverse: at time zero they are an open circuit.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1406" title="EEVblog 1406 - DC Fundamentals Part 7: DC Circuit Transients Fundamentals">1406</a></sup> Once charged, a capacitor holds its voltage after the source is removed, and a capacitor cannot change its voltage instantaneously.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1406" title="EEVblog 1406 - DC Fundamentals Part 7: DC Circuit Transients Fundamentals">1406</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-483" title="EEVblog #483 - Microcontroller Voltage Inverter Tutorial">483</a></sup>

This rule has direct practical consequences. Plugging a mains cable into a supply with large DC capacitors after the rectifier produces a spark because the capacitors are momentarily a short and the only limiting [[impedance]] is the connectors, wiring and the equivalent [[resistance]] of the [[bridge-rectifier]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1406" title="EEVblog 1406 - DC Fundamentals Part 7: DC Circuit Transients Fundamentals">1406</a></sup> It is also why capacitors interfere with [[continuity-testing]]: applying the meter's voltage to a capacitor in circuit makes it act as a short and produce a false beep, which is why fast-latching continuity functions are a liability when probing for shorts on a populated board.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1671" title="EEVblog 1671 - Beware of Multimeter Continuity Latching">1671</a></sup> Small capacitors charge too quickly to register at all on a slow, non-latching continuity function.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1671" title="EEVblog 1671 - Beware of Multimeter Continuity Latching">1671</a></sup> The same effect explains the low-impedance measurement mode found on many multimeters, which shorts out capacitively coupled ghost voltages so that only real voltages are read.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-CYm-4gbl1Zc" title="ElectroBOOM Stalks Sydney">•</a></sup>

## Does current flow through a capacitor

No electrons cross the dielectric. Charge accumulates on the plates, equal and opposite, setting up an electric field between them; electrons flow into one plate and out of the other, and in a real part a negligible leakage current sneaks through the insulator.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-486" title="EEVblog #486 - Does Current Flow Through A Capacitor?">486</a></sup> Yet every basic formula treats current as flowing through the capacitor in a series circuit, and it can be measured doing so.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-486" title="EEVblog #486 - Does Current Flow Through A Capacitor?">486</a></sup> The resolution is Maxwell's displacement current: as the electric field between the plates varies, it creates a changing magnetic field, and that term — required to complete the theory of electromagnetism and electromagnetic wave propagation — is the equivalent current through the dielectric.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-486" title="EEVblog #486 - Does Current Flow Through A Capacitor?">486</a></sup> In practical electronics, current flows through a capacitor, with the word current understood to mean electric current and displacement current together; the question of whether it really does is a physics question rather than an engineering one.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-486" title="EEVblog #486 - Does Current Flow Through A Capacitor?">486</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-ItoRt1buLkM" title="Initial reaction to the Veritasium video &quot;The Big Misconception About Electricity&quot;">•</a></sup>

The inverse relationship with inductors falls out of the same picture: the greater the rate of change of the electric field, the more current a capacitor passes, while the greater the rate of change of the magnetic field, the less current an inductor passes.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-486" title="EEVblog #486 - Does Current Flow Through A Capacitor?">486</a></sup> Put simply, capacitors block DC and pass AC or changing currents.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-486" title="EEVblog #486 - Does Current Flow Through A Capacitor?">486</a></sup>

## AC behaviour

Introducing a capacitor or inductor into a circuit causes phase shifts between current and voltage.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1470" title="EEVblog 1470 - AC Basics Tutorial Part 3 - Complex Numbers are EASY!">1470</a></sup> In a capacitor the current leads the voltage; in an inductor the voltage leads the current, conventionally remembered with the CIVIL mnemonic, in which C is the capacitor, L the inductor, V the voltage and I the current.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1660" title="EEVblog 1660 - AC Basics Tutorial Part 4: Resistors, Capacitors, Inductors">1660</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1469" title="EEVblog 1469 - AC Basics Tutorial Part 2 - Phasors">1469</a></sup> The physical reason is the short-circuit rule: at switch-on the current goes immediately to maximum while the voltage is still zero and has to build up on the plates.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1660" title="EEVblog 1660 - AC Basics Tutorial Part 4: Resistors, Capacitors, Inductors">1660</a></sup>

Every capacitor presents a reactance measured in ohms, XC, equal to 1/ωC at an angle of minus 90 degrees — anti-phase with inductive reactance.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1470" title="EEVblog 1470 - AC Basics Tutorial Part 3 - Complex Numbers are EASY!">1470</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1728" title="EEVblog 1728 - AC Basics Tutorial Part 6: Impedance, Conductance, Susceptance, Admittance">1728</a></sup> In complex form these reactances combine with resistance into impedance, and a circuit's current cannot be found without first converting each capacitor and inductor into its reactance.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1728" title="EEVblog 1728 - AC Basics Tutorial Part 6: Impedance, Conductance, Susceptance, Admittance">1728</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1661" title="EEVblog 1661 - AC Basics Tutorial Part 5: Time Domain vs Frequency Domain">1661</a></sup> Ideal capacitors and inductors dissipate no real power at all; the energy shuttled to and from them is reactive power, designated Q and measured in volt-amps reactive.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1730" title="EEVblog 1730 - AC Basics Tutorial Part 8: Apparent, Reactive &amp; Real Power">1730</a></sup> The sinusoid is the only [[waveform]] not distorted in shape by passing through capacitors and inductors, which is why a filter built from them returns a [[sine-wave]] for a sine wave in.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1417" title="EEVblog 1417 - AC Basics Tutorial Part 1: Alternating Current">1417</a></sup>

Deliberate use of reactive current is the basis of [[power-factor]] correction: adding parallel capacitance compensates a large inductive load such as a factory full of motors, pulling the power factor toward unity, and power stations and factories install banks of capacitors for exactly this.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1730" title="EEVblog 1730 - AC Basics Tutorial Part 8: Apparent, Reactive &amp; Real Power">1730</a></sup> The consumer energy-saver products sold on this principle are simply a mains-rated capacitor, in one case a 3 microfarad 450 V part, wired straight across active and neutral; domestic customers are billed for real power, so the device saves nothing and costs a fraction of a watt.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1191" title="EEVblog #1191 - Digital Energy Savers BUSTED!">1191</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-847" title="EEVblog #847 - Mailbag">847</a></sup>

## The real component: ESR, ESL and resonance

A real capacitor is not just a capacitance. It has an [[equivalent-series-resistance]], the ESR, and also an equivalent series [[inductance]], the ESL, arising from lead and construction inductance — making it an RLC circuit rather than an ideal element.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-859" title="EEVblog #859 - Bypass / Decoupling Capacitor Tutorial">859</a></sup> Even a surface-mount part has resistance in its end caps and terminations, contributing I squared R losses.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1401" title="EEVblog 1401 - DC Fundamentals Part 6: DC Power, Efficiency, &amp; Maximum Power Transfer Theory">1401</a></sup> The ESL may be only picohenries, but at some [[frequency]] it dominates: capacitive reactance falls with frequency until it meets the rising inductive reactance at the self-resonant frequency, above which the part no longer behaves as a capacitor.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-859" title="EEVblog #859 - Bypass / Decoupling Capacitor Tutorial">859</a></sup> This is why bypassing uses several values and types rather than one large capacitor.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-859" title="EEVblog #859 - Bypass / Decoupling Capacitor Tutorial">859</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1323" title="EEVblog #1323 - PCB Layout Review &amp; Analysis">1323</a></sup>

## Bypass and decoupling

A [[bypass-capacitor]] stores charge locally so that when a chip switches and demands a sudden gulp of current, that current comes from the adjacent capacitor rather than from the far side of the [[pcb]] through long series inductance.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-859" title="EEVblog #859 - Bypass / Decoupling Capacitor Tutorial">859</a></sup> The further the capacitor sits from the chip, the greater the loop inductance and the worse the behaviour at high switching frequencies, so the goal is a low-impedance, low-inductance supply element.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-859" title="EEVblog #859 - Bypass / Decoupling Capacitor Tutorial">859</a></sup> Current is drawn on every transition even with no resistive load at all, because traces and gate inputs present capacitance — sometimes only a few picofarads — and at fast edges that capacitance is effectively a short circuit.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1085" title="EEVblog #1085 - Bypass Capacitors Visualised!">1085</a></sup> A common starting rule of thumb, as recommended by [[fpga]] vendors, is 100 nF and 10 nF per device power pin, with ESR and self-resonance considered where the design warrants it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1323" title="EEVblog #1323 - PCB Layout Review &amp; Analysis">1323</a></sup> The power and ground planes of a multilayer board form a capacitor in their own right, the fiberglass-epoxy laminate acting as the dielectric.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1117" title="EEVblog #1117 - PCB Power Plane Capacitance">1117</a></sup>

## Types, ratings and construction

Electrolytic capacitors use aluminium foil with a liquid [[electrolyte]] in a metal can and are poor performers in almost every respect, but they provide bulk capacitance cheaply.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-33" title="EEVblog #33 1of2 - Capacitor Tutorial (Electrolytic, Tantalum, &amp; Plastic Film)">33</a></sup> Their electrolyte can leak, and the scored vent in the top relieves pressure build-up.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1636" title="EEVblog 1636 - Ultimate Multimeter Beginners Guide!">1636</a></sup> Solid types can be identified by the absence of the vents that wet electrolytics carry.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1606" title="EEVblog 1606 - $45 AlienTek DP100 100W USB-C PSU REVIEW">1606</a></sup> Class Y capacitors are used between either mains line and protective earth; mains-rated film types are self-healing, so a small internal overload sparks, extinguishes itself and destroys a minuscule amount of capacitance rather than shorting or exploding.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-33" title="EEVblog #33 1of2 - Capacitor Tutorial (Electrolytic, Tantalum, &amp; Plastic Film)">33</a></sup>

Ceramics are divided into classes with very different behaviour. Class II dielectrics show pronounced voltage dependency: applying a larger excitation to a Y5V part visibly changes its charging characteristic, making such parts unsuitable for timing applications where an RC charging to a threshold sets the interval.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-626" title="EEVblog #626 - Ceramic Capacitor Voltage Dependency">626</a></sup> Capacitors in general are not high-tolerance devices.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-626" title="EEVblog #626 - Ceramic Capacitor Voltage Dependency">626</a></sup> Volumetric [[efficiency]] has advanced enormously — a 0.1 microfarad part once required a 1210 case and is now available in 0201 or 01005 sizes, with 1 microfarad in 0105 under development.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-9V99J22aiLE" title="Full Discussion - Film Capacitor Failures with Ron Demcko from AVX">•</a></sup> A further trick is stacking two chip capacitors at the factory to produce a through-hole part, leveraging the volume manufacturing of the standard parts to reach higher values where board area is not the constraint.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1525" title="EEVblog 1525 - Commisioned Mailbag">1525</a></sup>

Two further non-idealities matter in precision work. Dielectric absorption gives a capacitor a memory of its previous charge, so that a shorted capacitor may not stay at zero volts, the effect depending on the dielectric.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-490" title="EEVblog #490 - Peak Detector Circuit">490</a></sup> And capacitors, like inductors, are microphonic: vibration couples into them, and an audible high-pitched squeal from a [[power-supply]] can originate in its capacitors or inductors.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1607" title="EEVblog 1607 - PSU Switching Noise Reduction via Vibration">1607</a></sup>

## Failure and diagnosis

Capacitors are among the components that cannot be screened by electrical test at manufacture: a fine crack invisible even under magnification will pass every test and fail later in the field, giving ceramics a long-term failure rate rather than a detectable defect.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-684" title="EEVblog #684 - Ness SMT Manufacturing &amp; Assembly Factory Tour">684</a></sup> Electrolytics dry out with age, ESR rises and ripple exceeds the design limit, which can present as a system-level fault such as a machine rebooting under peak current load.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-eSx8MeY6V48" title="CPU Thermal Ghost - Part 2">•</a></sup> Bulging is the usual visual giveaway and bulged or leaking caps are worth looking for as soon as a lid comes off, but a capacitor can be electrically dead without any bulge.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-523" title="EEVblog #523 - REPAIR: HP 35660A Dynamic Signal Analyser">523</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-365" title="EEVblog #365 - ESR Meter Bad Cap Monitor Repair">365</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src--bPGrSSaXhE" title="Goldshell LBC LBRY ASIC Miner Teardown &amp; Troubleshooting">•</a></sup>

ESR measurement is the most direct test. A 3300 microfarad part that should read around 0.06 to 0.1 ohms reading 0.9 ohms is finished, while another that has visibly leaked can still measure 0.07 ohms and be electrically sound.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-763" title="EEVblog #763 - Dumpster Plasma TV Bad Cap Repair">763</a></sup> Current draw is another discriminator: a failed capacitor drawing 240 milliamps where the specification allows an order of magnitude less is unambiguous.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-365" title="EEVblog #365 - ESR Meter Bad Cap Monitor Repair">365</a></sup> Capacitance loss can be dramatic and subtle at once — a 22 nanofarad film capacitor that fell to 500 picofarads still produced a nominal output voltage while corrupting the timing that depended on it, and a 220 nanofarad mains dropper losing capacitance raised its series impedance until there was insufficient current to hold a Zener in regulation and the 24 V [[relay]] it fed would no longer pull in.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-379" title="EEVBlog #379 - Yamaha RX-V557 Receiver Fix">379</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1482" title="EEVblog 1482 - Mains Capacitor Zener Regulator Circuit">1482</a></sup> Leakage matters as much as value: a tantalum measuring correctly on capacitance but drawing 150 microamps of leakage at 6 volts, in series with a 1 megohm [[resistor]], pulled a node to mid-rail.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1245" title="EEVblog #1245 - A Most Excellent REPAIR: IBM PCjr Keyboard">1245</a></sup>

Recapping an entire unit rather than hunting the single bad part is defensible practice when intermittent breakdown is suspected, since a voltage-dependent breakdown in one capacitor may not reproduce on the bench.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1714" title="EEVblog 1714 - Presonus Eris Speaker REPAIR (Capacitor Pop Noise)">1714</a></sup> Where multiple capacitors are replaced at once, counting the parts removed and comparing against the parts fitted catches the omissions that produce a dead channel afterwards.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1714" title="EEVblog 1714 - Presonus Eris Speaker REPAIR (Capacitor Pop Noise)">1714</a></sup> Assembly also matters: [[soldering]] a capacitor directly across large-mass pins surrounded by copper forces a great deal of heat into the part, and the resulting damage can end with the capacitor on fire when a supply capable of a kilowatt delivers 20 amps into it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1035" title="EEVblog #1035 - Flaming DIY Power Supply!">1035</a></sup> Mechanically, tall capacitors are secured with adhesive so they do not vibrate loose in equipment destined for rough service, and their height must be accounted for against exposed vias on any board mounted flush above them.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-WFhk1aRqLD8" title="AllPowers R1500 1500W 1150Wh LiFeP04 Battery Teardown + Review">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1262" title="EEVblog #1262 - Designing a Flex PCB + uSupply Update">1262</a></sup>

Before any of this, large capacitors must be discharged. A television supply designed without a bleed resistor can sit at 150 V DC long after being unplugged, decaying only through the 4 megohm load of a probe; a meter's low-impedance function will drain a modest capacitor safely.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1388" title="EEVblog 1388 -  Dumpster Diving 4K TV Murphy&#x27;s &quot;Repair&quot;">1388</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-663" title="EEVblog #663 - Compucorp 322G Calculator Teardown">663</a></sup> The energies involved elsewhere are considerably higher — a capacitor-discharge supply for ocean bottom profiling charges a large capacitor to 4.2 or 4.3 kilovolts and dumps it through SCRs in under a millisecond, and an electric fence energiser stores its joules in 230 microfarad 900 volt capacitors.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-84" title="EEVblog #84 - High Energy Multimeter Destruction">84</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1292" title="EEVblog #1292 - $2 Meter vs JVA Electric Fence Controller!">1292</a></sup> An airbag controller's reservoir capacitor is enclosed in its own protective cage with compliance in the leads, so that it survives the crash long enough to fire the charges and commit the crash data to [[eeprom]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-517" title="EEVblog #517 - Car Airbag Controller Teardown">517</a></sup>

## Measurement

An [[lcr-meter]] distinguishes capacitance from inductance by the sign of the phase angle: negative series reactance is capacitive, positive is inductive.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1473" title="EEVblog 1473 - How Your LCR Meter Works">1473</a></sup> A good capacitor reads close to minus 90 degrees with a dissipation factor around 0.001.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-757" title="EEVblog #757 - HP4263A LCR Meter Teardown">757</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-847" title="EEVblog #847 - Mailbag">847</a></sup> Capacitance functions on general-purpose multimeters are useful for troubleshooting supplies — a 100 microfarad part reading 95 microfarads is fine — but ranges are limited, and pushing beyond a meter's stated maximum yields a slow and badly wrong reading rather than an overrange indication.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1636" title="EEVblog 1636 - Ultimate Multimeter Beginners Guide!">1636</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1692" title="EEVblog 1692 - $130 Fluke 17B MAX Multimeter REVIEW">1692</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1335" title="EEVblog #1335 - Miniware $70 DT71 LCR Tweezer REVIEW">1335</a></sup>

In-circuit measurement works better than expected, but only within limits. Surrounding components — regulators, ESD protection diodes inside ICs, parallel resistances — mean a capacitor is rarely measured alone, and it is these that confuse the meter rather than the capacitor itself.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1473" title="EEVblog 1473 - How Your LCR Meter Works">1473</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1474" title="EEVblog 1474 - Can You Measure Capacitors IN Circuit?">1474</a></sup> The key variable is the meter's excitation level: with a 10 microfarad capacitor at 100 Hz and the range resistor the meter selects, the signal is a few hundred millivolts peak-to-peak, too small to turn on any active device in circuit, and the measurement is good.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1474" title="EEVblog 1474 - Can You Measure Capacitors IN Circuit?">1474</a></sup> Large values at low frequency therefore measure well in circuit even with 100 ohms in parallel, whereas a 100 nanofarad part in the same circuit cannot be measured at all because the signal clips on the surrounding silicon.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1474" title="EEVblog 1474 - Can You Measure Capacitors IN Circuit?">1474</a></sup> Raising the frequency makes matters worse rather than better for large values, since the signal level collapses: at 100 Hz a 220 microfarad capacitor reads correctly, at 1 kHz the signal is buried in common-mode noise, and at 10 kHz it is hopeless.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1474" title="EEVblog 1474 - Can You Measure Capacitors IN Circuit?">1474</a></sup> Anything with tens of ohms around it is unmeasurable, and multiple capacitors charging in parallel will produce wandering readings.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1474" title="EEVblog 1474 - Can You Measure Capacitors IN Circuit?">1474</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1649" title="EEVblog 1649 - Mailbag: M5stack, Beelink Mini PC, ZOYI LCR, ToughOn Battery">1649</a></sup> Where a lead can be freed — by lifting it, or by pulling a socketed IC so one terminal goes nowhere — the ambiguity disappears.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-777" title="EEVblog #777 - Keithley 177 Microvolt DMM Repair">777</a></sup> Two capacitors in parallel read as their sum, which is worth buzzing out before drawing conclusions from a doubled value.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1527" title="EEVblog 1527 - ## Capacitors as circuit elements

Feeding a constant current into a capacitor produces a linear voltage ramp, the basis of the op-amp integrator and of the dual-slope integrating technique used in precision multimeters.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-600" title="EEVblog #600 - OpAmps Tutorial - What is an Operational Amplifier?">600</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-485" title="EEVblog #485 - Agilent TrueVolt 34461A Multimeter Teardown">485</a></sup> A [[diode]] and a capacitor make a peak detector, but the capacitor value is a genuine trade-off: too small and droop degrades the measurement, too large and the [[op-amp]]'s slew rate limits how fast it can charge, so speed and hold time pull in opposite directions.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-490" title="EEVblog #490 - Peak Detector Circuit">490</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-490" title="EEVblog #490 - Peak Detector Circuit">490</a></sup> In a 555, the timing capacitor charges through the resistor network to the two-thirds threshold and is dumped by the discharge pin, and the same part discharged through a second steering diode gives independent control of the [[pwm]] duty cycle.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-555" title="EEVblog #555 - 555 Timer Kit">555</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-392" title="EEVblog #392 - 555 LED PWM Hack">392</a></sup> The recommended 10 nF capacitor from pin five to ground is not optional decoration.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-160" title="EEVblog#160 - 555 Timer Easter Egg?">160</a></sup>

Switched-capacitor circuits use capacitors as charge carriers rather than filters, producing voltage doublers and inverters from a [[microcontroller]] pin, with output ripple set by the capacitor values and [[switching-frequency]] and the values chosen from the discharge rate of the load.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-483" title="EEVblog #483 - Microcontroller Voltage Inverter Tutorial">483</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-473" title="EEVBlog #473 - Microcontroller Voltage Doubler">473</a></sup> A charge pump inside an IC needs its external capacitor to function at all.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-5v8qX9PrjnA" title="Brymen BM257 vs BM235 Multimeter IR Serial Protocol">•</a></sup> The Cockcroft-Walton multiplier stacks the same principle: because each stage sees only the input swing, the capacitors and diodes need only be rated for the input voltage rather than the multiplied output, though output voltage sags with load depending on capacitor value and switching frequency.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-469" title="EEVblog #469 - Cockcroft-Walton Multiplier">469</a></sup> Energy harvesting stores what it scavenges in capacitors before delivering it in a usable burst.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-664" title="EEVblog #664 - Peltier TEG Energy Harvesting Experiments">664</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-MCyLO-1grEk" title="WiGL Wireless Power BUSTED AGAIN!">•</a></sup> The same buffering keeps a solar calculator alive under 20 lux, at the cost of a turn-on delay proportional to how much capacitance is fitted.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1094" title="EEVblog #1094 - Casio FX260 Solar II Calculator Tritium Nuclear Battery Experiments">1094</a></sup>

At high frequency the capacitor need not be a component at all. In distributed-element filters, a pad on the board is the capacitor and the trace leading to it is the inductor, their values set by geometry, controlled impedance and the distance to the ground plane, producing an LC low-pass or band-pass structure with no discrete parts fitted.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-391" title="EEVblog #391 - Rigol DSA815 Spectrum Analyser Teardown">391</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-823" title="EEVBlog #823 - Rigol DSG815 RF Signal Generator Teardown">823</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-470" title="EEVblog #470 - Agilent N9344C 20GHz Spectrum Analyser Teardown">470</a></sup> Two pads facing each other across the board are a capacitor whether or not that was the intent, which is how a signal crosses a gap that appears unconnected.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-470" title="EEVblog #470 - Agilent N9344C 20GHz Spectrum Analyser Teardown">470</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1415" title="EEVblog 1415 - Reverse Engineering the DP10007 Differential Probe">1415</a></sup>

Capacitors also serve as sensors and transducers. A pyroelectric infrared element is a capacitor whose ferroelectric dielectric produces a charge displacement, and hence a voltage, in response to thermal energy, feeding an RC network with a time constant of around one second.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-275" title="EEVblog #275 - PIR Sensor Teardown &amp; Tutorial">275</a></sup> A condenser microphone capsule is a polarised capacitor — 200 V for a measurement microphone, perhaps 60 to 90 V for a studio capsule — in which a pressure wave closes the plate spacing, raising the capacitance while the charge is preserved, so the voltage must fall.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-609" title="EEVblog #609 - Condenser Microphone Design Tutorial">609</a></sup> A ceramic piezo bender is likewise just a capacitor, and can be driven to emit sound as a continuity test.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1400" title="EEVblog 1400 - Hard Drive Micro Actuators are AMAZING!">1400</a></sup> Placing a capacitor in series is the whole of [[ac-coupling]], as in the AC-coupled input range that older analog meters labelled output.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1067" title="EEVblog #1067 - Analog vs Digital Multimeters!">1067</a></sup> Series strings of capacitors are used for safety as well as ratings: a high-voltage differential probe uses several 6.8 pF 1 kV ceramics in series across a chain of 1 megohm resistors, so a single failure does not compromise the isolation.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-932" title="EEVblog #932 - How Does A HV Differential Probe Work?">932</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-932" title="EEVblog #932 - How Does A HV Differential Probe Work?">932</a></sup>

## Selection and design practice

Capacitor choice is usually a systems decision rather than a component one. Output capacitance stabilises a control loop and cleans a transient — replacing 22 microfarads with 100 nanofarads on a lab supply output turns a clean response into a badly degraded one, and removing it entirely is worse still — while a large-value output capacitor gives a smooth switch-on that a small one or none at all will overshoot.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-224" title="EEVblog #224 - Lab Power Supply Design - Part 3">224</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-225" title="EEVblog #225 - Lab Power Supply Design Part 4 - PWM Control">225</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-577" title="EEVblog #577 - Precision 1A Current Source Part 2">577</a></sup> Cheap supplies sometimes simply add output capacitance until the loop is stable.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1174" title="EEVblog #1174 - Rohde &amp; Schwarz PSU Teardowns">1174</a></sup>

Where a design has freedom, a resistor is generally the cheaper and more versatile way to set an RC constant than a high-value capacitor, so a 10 megohm resistor with a small capacitor is preferred over 100 kilohms with tens of microfarads.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-471" title="EEVblog #471 - Overload Detector Circuit Design">471</a></sup> Component consolidation is worth doing deliberately: substituting a value already in the [[bill-of-materials]] for a nearby one that is not — a 10 microfarad where 4.7 would do — reduces part count.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-232" title="EEVblog #232 - Lab Power Supply Design Part 5">232</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1307" title="EEVblog #1307 - TUTORIAL: PCB BOM Consolidation">1307</a></sup> A design carrying 26 different capacitor types consumes half the reel capacity of a pick-and-place machine that supports only 40, which is immaterial for a hundred boards and decisive for a hundred thousand.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1307" title="EEVblog #1307 - TUTORIAL: PCB BOM Consolidation">1307</a></sup> Higher temperature ratings are a genuine benefit where the parts are actually to specification rather than merely stamped with a number.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-400" title="EEVblog #400 - 400th Live Show">400</a></sup>

Finally, notation. Values are written in nanofarads rather than as fractional microfarads because decimal points are lost in printouts — 10 nF rather than 0.01 µF — a convention that dates only from the nanofarad's adoption in schematics in the early to mid 1980s.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1129" title="EEVblog #1129 - Creating a Nice Readable Schematic">1129</a></sup>
