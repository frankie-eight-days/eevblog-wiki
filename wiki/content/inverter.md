The word *inverter* names two entirely different components. In digital electronics it is the single-input logic gate that produces the complement of its input: a zero in gives a one out, a one in gives a zero out.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-7bVnsXHO6Uw" title="EEVacademy | Digital Design Series Part 1 - Introduction To Digital Logic">•</a></sup> In power electronics it is the circuit that converts DC into AC, generating a mains-like or motor-drive [[waveform]] from a [[battery]], a solar array, or a DC bus.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-179" title="Mitsubishi iMiEV Electric Car Test Drive - EEVblog #179">179</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-504" title="EEVblog #504 - UPS Tutorial &amp; Teardown">504</a></sup> Both meanings recur constantly — the logic inverter because it is one of the small set of gates from which every other digital function can be built,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-7bVnsXHO6Uw" title="EEVacademy | Digital Design Series Part 1 - Introduction To Digital Logic">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-hFvqEfZfMtA" title="EEVacademy | Digital Design Series Part 2 - Digital Logic Boolean &amp; Demorgan&#x27;s Theorems">•</a></sup> and the power inverter because it sits at the heart of every solar installation, UPS, portable power station, and electric drivetrain.

## The logic inverter

The logic inverter has one input and one output and only two possible input states, so its [[truth-table]] is trivial: the output is always the opposite of the input.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-7bVnsXHO6Uw" title="EEVacademy | Digital Design Series Part 1 - Introduction To Digital Logic">•</a></sup> Its name describes its function — it inverts the logical sense of the input, though polarity is not the correct term for what it does.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-7bVnsXHO6Uw" title="EEVacademy | Digital Design Series Part 1 - Introduction To Digital Logic">•</a></sup>

In [[schematic]] notation the inversion is drawn as a small circle on the gate output, and in Boolean algebra it is written as a bar over the inverted term.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-7bVnsXHO6Uw" title="EEVacademy | Digital Design Series Part 1 - Introduction To Digital Logic">•</a></sup> Adding an inverter to the C input of a gate is expressed by barring C alone; inverting the whole output function is expressed by a bar drawn across the entire expression.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-7bVnsXHO6Uw" title="EEVacademy | Digital Design Series Part 1 - Introduction To Digital Logic">•</a></sup> The NAND gate is exactly an AND gate with an inverter on its output, and building one from a discrete AND chip and a discrete inverter chip produces an identical function to buying a NAND chip, because that extra inversion is all the NAND package contains beyond the AND.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-7bVnsXHO6Uw" title="EEVacademy | Digital Design Series Part 1 - Introduction To Digital Logic">•</a></sup>

Two inversions in series cancel: feeding a signal through one inverter and then another returns the original signal, which is the circuit-level statement of the double-bar identity in Boolean algebra.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-hFvqEfZfMtA" title="EEVacademy | Digital Design Series Part 2 - Digital Logic Boolean &amp; Demorgan&#x27;s Theorems">•</a></sup> Feeding a variable and its inverse into an AND gate can never produce a high output, because the inverter guarantees the two inputs are never simultaneously high — a single-variable theorem demonstrated directly from the gate structure.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-hFvqEfZfMtA" title="EEVacademy | Digital Design Series Part 2 - Digital Logic Boolean &amp; Demorgan&#x27;s Theorems">•</a></sup>

## Universality and gate substitution

Any two-input NAND gate becomes an inverter when its inputs are tied together, and the same trick works on a NOR gate.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-hFvqEfZfMtA" title="EEVacademy | Digital Design Series Part 2 - Digital Logic Boolean &amp; Demorgan&#x27;s Theorems">•</a></sup> This is the basis of building any logic function from a single gate type: tie the inputs of NOR gates together to make inverters, invert the inputs of another NOR, and a NAND emerges — and the construction runs in the opposite direction just as well.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-hFvqEfZfMtA" title="EEVacademy | Digital Design Series Part 2 - Digital Logic Boolean &amp; Demorgan&#x27;s Theorems">•</a></sup> Any combinatorial circuit, up to the complexity of a processor, can be built from nothing but NAND gates or nothing but NOR gates.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-hFvqEfZfMtA" title="EEVacademy | Digital Design Series Part 2 - Digital Logic Boolean &amp; Demorgan&#x27;s Theorems">•</a></sup> Equivalently, the combination of AND gates and inverters is sufficient to create any other gate or complete digital system.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-7bVnsXHO6Uw" title="EEVacademy | Digital Design Series Part 1 - Introduction To Digital Logic">•</a></sup>

Commercial parts reflect this. A 74HC86 exclusive-OR is internally made from inverters, AND gates and NOR gates plus a further inverter, and each of those constituent gates could in turn be synthesised from NANDs.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-hFvqEfZfMtA" title="EEVacademy | Digital Design Series Part 2 - Digital Logic Boolean &amp; Demorgan&#x27;s Theorems">•</a></sup> Where two NOR gates appear with their inputs shorted, they are functioning as inverters on the inputs of a following gate, and the circuit can be rewritten with bars over the input variables.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-hFvqEfZfMtA" title="EEVacademy | Digital Design Series Part 2 - Digital Logic Boolean &amp; Demorgan&#x27;s Theorems">•</a></sup>

Inverter count is one of the things Boolean simplification trades against. A circuit reduced by the distributive law may go from five two-input gates and three inverters to four two-input gates and three inverters — one whole gate eliminated for identical function.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-hFvqEfZfMtA" title="EEVacademy | Digital Design Series Part 2 - Digital Logic Boolean &amp; Demorgan&#x27;s Theorems">•</a></sup>

## Inverters in logic design practice

Inverters appear in synthesised combinatorial logic wherever a product term requires an input in its complemented form. Realising an output that is high when A is 0 and B is 1 needs an inverter on A to convert that 0 into the 1 the AND gate requires, while B feeds straight through.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-rfaCFEqk05M" title="EEVacademy | Digital Design Series Part 3 - Designing Combinatorial Digital Logic Circuits">•</a></sup> A different row of the same truth table needs the inverter on B instead.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-rfaCFEqk05M" title="EEVacademy | Digital Design Series Part 3 - Designing Combinatorial Digital Logic Circuits">•</a></sup> The same applies to expressions reduced by Karnaugh map: a simplified term of B AND NOT C is drawn as an AND gate with an inverter on the C input.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-lJ3q9RHIatU" title="EEVacademy | Digital Design Series Part 5 - Karnaugh Maps">•</a></sup>

Whether a signal path contains an inverter or merely a buffer is diagnostic information when reading a timing diagram. A clock input drawn through a plain buffer, with no inversion bubble, indicates positive-edge triggering; a NOT on that path, or a matching pair of NOTs, tells the reader which edge the logic actually responds to.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1249" title="EEVblog #1249 - TUTORIAL: Timing Diagrams Explained">1249</a></sup> Some counter chips provide both a negative-going and a positive-going clock input on the same package, removing the need to add an inverter ahead of the clock pin — the unused input is simply tied to ground.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-801" title="EEVblog #801 - How To Design A Digital Clock">801</a></sup>

Two inverters in series make a serviceable buffer, and this is a standard remedy for a board driving long or heavily loaded lines: add an input buffer, preferably with Schmitt-trigger inputs, and a separate set of output drivers rather than loading a single clock line directly.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1365" title="EEVblog #1365 - Viewer PCB &amp; Circuit Design Review">1365</a></sup> A six-gate package can cover both roles, three gates as input buffers and three as output drivers.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1365" title="EEVblog #1365 - Viewer PCB &amp; Circuit Design Review">1365</a></sup> Leaving a spare inverter on a board with its input tied off and its input and output brought out to pads is good practice, because a bodge wire can then press it into service; Schmitt versions are preferable, and not only for slow rise and fall times but for noisy signals generally.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1611" title="EEVblog 1611 - Top 5 Jellybean 7400 &amp; 4000 Logic">1611</a></sup>

Inverters also serve as small output drivers. An inverter can drive an [[led]] directly once a threshold circuit has established the timing condition, lighting the LED when its input goes low.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-471" title="EEVblog #471 - Overload Detector Circuit Design">471</a></sup> In a non-contact [[voltage]] detection stick, the detected signal is double-inverted or buffered before driving the output [[transistor]] that drives the LED.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-267" title="EEVblog #267 - Voltage Detection Stick Teardown">267</a></sup>

## Inverters inside analog and mixed-signal circuits

Almost every [[microcontroller]] or logic chip with X1 and X2 crystal pins contains an inverter connected between those two pins.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1614" title="EEVblog 1614 - Circuit Design TIP: Crystal Oscillators">1614</a></sup> The consequence is that X1 is a digital input, so an external oscillator or any other signal generated elsewhere in the circuit can be driven straight into it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1614" title="EEVblog 1614 - Circuit Design TIP: Crystal Oscillators">1614</a></sup>

The inverter is also the mechanism behind the voltage doubling seen in multiplexed [[lcd]] drive. Taking a 74HC04 inverter powered from 5 V and feeding it a 5 V peak-to-peak square wave gives a 5 V peak-to-peak output measured to ground, but probing differentially between input and output yields 10 V peak to peak, because the two nodes are in opposite phase and the measurement is a difference function.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1046" title="EEVblog #1046 - Mysterious Digital Voltage Doubling (LCD design)">1046</a></sup> An LCD segment connected across the input and output of an inverter sees exactly this doubling.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1046" title="EEVblog #1046 - Mysterious Digital Voltage Doubling (LCD design)">1046</a></sup> The same principle is applied deliberately when driving a segment: an inverter placed in the segment path, fed the same 100 Hz signal, inverts the phase relative to the common pin and changes the resultant waveform across the display.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1045" title="EEVblog #1045 - How To Drive an LCD">1045</a></sup>

CMOS Schmitt triggers are built around this same primitive. Beyond the threshold-setting transistor network, the output stage of a typical CMOS Schmitt trigger consists of a further inverter followed by an output driver pair.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-941" title="EEVblog #941 - Schmitt Trigger Tutorial">941</a></sup> At the process level, the transistor cost is visible in old-school gate data sheets: a NOR gate implemented conventionally amounts to an inverter and some NAND structure with output inversion, and the transistor count for a single gate is substantial.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-555" title="EEVblog #555 - 555 Timer Kit">555</a></sup> Early integrated logic was similarly sparse — DTL chips containing at most a handful of gate structures, a couple of gates or some inverters,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-561" title="EEVblog #561 - Mailbag">561</a></sup> and Apollo-era LVDC chips in which an entire package might hold an inverter made from a couple of diodes and a transistor.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-638" title="EEVblog #638 - Apollo Saturn V LVDC Testing">638</a></sup> Discrete inverter packages remain common in modern instruments; a 74HC04 turns up in the RF section of a 20 GHz spectrum analyser.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-470" title="EEVblog #470 - Agilent N9344C 20GHz Spectrum Analyser Teardown">470</a></sup> Programmable logic tooling exposes the same vocabulary, letting each device pin be defined as a buffer, an inverter, an open output, or tied to VCC.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-747" title="EEVblog #747 - PC Based Logic Analyser Project">747</a></sup>

## DC-to-AC power inverters

A power inverter converts DC into AC. In an electric vehicle conversion it takes the 320 V DC traction pack and produces three-phase AC for a permanent-magnet AC motor.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-179" title="Mitsubishi iMiEV Electric Car Test Drive - EEVblog #179">179</a></sup> The topology in a UPS is a step-up conversion that generates either a sinusoidal or a square-wave output,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-504" title="EEVblog #504 - UPS Tutorial &amp; Teardown">504</a></sup> typically implemented as an H bridge; at high power each leg of the bridge is unlikely to be a single [[mosfet]] and will instead use several devices in parallel.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-504" title="EEVblog #504 - UPS Tutorial &amp; Teardown">504</a></sup> A traditional UPS chains a [[transformer]]-rectifier stage converting AC to DC, a battery charger, the battery, the inverter H bridge, and an output transformer feeding the load.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-504" title="EEVblog #504 - UPS Tutorial &amp; Teardown">504</a></sup>

Standby UPS designs pass mains straight through to the output [[via]] a static switch and only start the inverter when input power fails, which introduces a switchover interval of anywhere from tens of milliseconds to around 100 ms.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-504" title="EEVblog #504 - UPS Tutorial &amp; Teardown">504</a></sup> This is a real design constraint for equipment intended to run downstream: products that do not produce continuous output AC exhibit a gap where the waveform simply stops before the source changes over, and programmable AC sources can be used to simulate those switchover times when designing an inverter or a product meant to work with one.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1698" title="EEVblog 1698 - Awesome Ebay AC Power Source: GW Instek APS-7050">1698</a></sup>

Inverters carry a quiescent penalty. A portable lithium power station draws 11 W with its inverter switched on and the output disconnected — a substantial idle [[current]] for a switching stage doing no useful work.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1707" title="EEVblog 1707 - REPAIR: Goal Zero Yeti 400 Lithium Battery">1707</a></sup> Conversion loss also argues for bypassing the inverter where possible: drawing DC directly from a battery pack's DC outputs incurs fewer internal losses than stepping the pack voltage up to 240 V AC, so usable watt-hours measured at the mains output fall short of what the DC path would deliver.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-WFhk1aRqLD8" title="AllPowers R1500 1500W 1150Wh LiFeP04 Battery Teardown + Review">•</a></sup>

In portable power stations the inverter is generally packaged with the charger as one modular assembly that lifts out of the enclosure,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1499" title="EEVblog 1499 - EcoFlow Delta Pro 3.6kWh Portable Battery TEARDOWN!">1499</a></sup> and the same unit handles bidirectional flow: charging from mains, solar MPPT or an EV connector in one direction, and producing 240 V mains output from the battery in the other.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1499" title="EEVblog 1499 - EcoFlow Delta Pro 3.6kWh Portable Battery TEARDOWN!">1499</a></sup> Physical access to the cells is often gated behind the inverter assembly, which must be removed before the battery management board underneath can be reached.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-8P8Af5SR57U" title="Goal Zero Yeti 400 Lithium Battery Follow--up">•</a></sup> The inverter section may be walled off by insulation tied into the mounting screws, making it awkward to open without cutting through.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-WFhk1aRqLD8" title="AllPowers R1500 1500W 1150Wh LiFeP04 Battery Teardown + Review">•</a></sup>

Behaviour during source changeover is a legitimate quality criterion for such units. A portable station's internal inverter should continue to operate regardless of which source is currently feeding it, whether that is the internal battery or an external input, and momentary dropouts of its 234 V RMS output under a fridge load represent a genuine fault rather than expected behaviour.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-5JT8M8v_zNg" title="EcoFlow Delta Pro - RTFM Dummy!">•</a></sup>

## Grid-connected and hybrid solar inverters

In a grid-connected solar installation the inverter converts panel DC into the 240 V required by the mains and pushes it onto the grid; production monitoring is normally measured after the inverter, on the wire carrying its output.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1390" title="EEVblog 1390 - NEGATIVE Household Solar Consumption? WHY?">1390</a></sup> The monitoring platform is independent of the inverter used — string inverters and microinverters alike present the same measurable production.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1390" title="EEVblog 1390 - NEGATIVE Household Solar Consumption? WHY?">1390</a></sup>

Inverter capacity is a hard ceiling on a system. A 3 kW string inverter that is already maxed out cannot absorb additional panels even though it carries a second string input, so expanding the array requires a larger inverter with two string inputs.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-dlOtqDPCO1o" title="Solar Pergola Upgrade">•</a></sup> The same ceiling applies to [[battery-charging]]: a 5 kW inverter caps grid charging at 5 kW during a cheap-energy window, and that limit comes from the inverter rather than from available solar or grid capacity.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-aANZNwmnC_4" title="AERL Lithium Home Storage Battery - Cloud Data">•</a></sup> Where microinverters are undersized relative to the panels — 295 W units on 370 W panels — the system clips, and the array can never deliver more than roughly 4.13 kW no matter the conditions.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-k2_mJtAeaog" title="SNEAKY Enphase Solar Monitoring Modification">•</a></sup> Clipping of this kind does not distort the AC waveform or the [[power-factor]]; the inverter still produces a clean continuous [[sine-wave]] regardless of where the DC-side power is limited.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-BtQUuD6QRMw" title="Solar Panel Temperature Dependency">•</a></sup>

Hybrid inverters add islanding and backup functions. On grid failure the hybrid inverter automatically isolates the grid side so the inverter does not feed power back into the grid, while providing a separate emergency output capable of running fridges and freezers, and it performs that isolation without the owner having to manually isolate the house.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-5JT8M8v_zNg" title="EcoFlow Delta Pro - RTFM Dummy!">•</a></sup> A backup port on a hybrid inverter can power the majority of a house, and in pass-through mode the inverter supplements its own output with grid power up to a pass-through limit — 50 A on one such installation, which was observed drawing 1.3 kW from the grid to cover a load beyond its own capability.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1719" title="EEVblog 1719 - 75kWh Home Storage Battery Expansion!">1719</a></sup> Battery configuration in these systems is typically done on the inverter rather than on the battery modules, which are self-contained and need only be plugged in; a Cat6 comms cable between batteries and inverter allows the inverter to self-detect the pack, and a two-pole DC circuit breaker sits between the battery cabinet and the inverter.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1634" title="EEVblog 1634 - 15kWh AERL LFP Home Storage Battery Install!">1634</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-eldXifbNm2A" title="Talk with Peter Watkinson from AERL about LFP Battery Storage">•</a></sup>

## Maximum power point tracking and shading

The inverter's maximum power point tracker determines how badly partial shading hurts an array, and the effect can be far larger than the shaded area alone would suggest. Light shading across a couple of cells that should have cost around 6% of a string's output instead produced a 15 to 20% drop, and the cause was the string inverter's MPPT being thrown off rather than the cell shading itself.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1426" title="EEVblog 1426 - WOW! This Problem DROPS Solar Output by 20% !">1426</a></sup> Extending the shadow to cover three whole panels added only a further 10%, confirming that the tracking behaviour, not the shaded area, dominated the loss.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1426" title="EEVblog 1426 - WOW! This Problem DROPS Solar Output by 20% !">1426</a></sup> Shading is therefore a property of the panel-and-inverter combination, not of the panels alone.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1426" title="EEVblog 1426 - WOW! This Problem DROPS Solar Output by 20% !">1426</a></sup>

This is the argument for microinverters: with one inverter per panel, maximum power point tracking is performed per panel rather than across an entire series string of twelve panels, so a shaded panel does not drag down the rest.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1426" title="EEVblog 1426 - WOW! This Problem DROPS Solar Output by 20% !">1426</a></sup> Functionally an inverter and a [[microinverter]] are effectively the same thing — the distinction is one per panel versus one per string.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-BtQUuD6QRMw" title="Solar Panel Temperature Dependency">•</a></sup> String arrays with a central inverter remain the cheaper solution, so the choice is a genuine trade-off rather than a clear win either way.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1426" title="EEVblog 1426 - WOW! This Problem DROPS Solar Output by 20% !">1426</a></sup> Mixing inverter technologies across two systems on one house complicates monitoring considerably, since production data from the two platforms does not combine cleanly.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-k2_mJtAeaog" title="SNEAKY Enphase Solar Monitoring Modification">•</a></sup>

## Reliability, losses and system cost

Inverters are not typically the weak point of a solar installation. A quality inverter would be expected to run without trouble for a further five years after its first five, alongside panels that appear highly reliable, making ten years a reasonable expectation.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1086" title="EEVblog #1086 - 5 Year Solar Power Results - Payback?">1086</a></sup> Even a budget system with cheaper panels and a cheaper inverter should comfortably last five years, at a payback period of just over three.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1086" title="EEVblog #1086 - 5 Year Solar Power Results - Payback?">1086</a></sup> When a system stops producing, the inverter is often not the culprit: an installation showing zero output from an array rated at 456 volts had a fully functional inverter, with the fault lying upstream in the isolator or the string.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1217" title="EEVblog #1217 - My Home Solar Power System FAILED!">1217</a></sup> Underperformance diagnostics list inverter fault as only one candidate among incorrect system details, incorrect device wiring, array faults, overvoltage, shading and seasonal change.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-uuLwmQ7oLo8" title="Solar Power System Monitor ALERT!">•</a></sup> [[mains-voltage]] rise during the day, which does occur, was ruled out as a cause of one such underperformance because the inverter showed no sign of shutting down or limiting on voltage.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1426" title="EEVblog 1426 - WOW! This Problem DROPS Solar Output by 20% !">1426</a></sup>

For energy-yield estimation, a combined figure of around 10% for cable and inverter losses is a reasonable ballpark.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-681" title="EEVblog #681 - More Solar Roadways BULLSHIT!">681</a></sup> In installed system cost, inverters, wiring and associated infrastructure form a large fraction of the total; in one road-embedded installation the modules were 40% of installed cost against a typical 15% for a conventional commercial system, leaving inverters, cabling and ducting to make up roughly 60%.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1047" title="EEVblog #1047 - Solar Roadways FINALLY BUSTED! ## Other uses of the term

The word also attaches to a range of smaller conversion circuits. A gas plasma display in a vintage calculator is driven by its own inverter on the [[power-supply]] board, connected by a separate cable to the main board.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-663" title="EEVblog #663 - Compucorp 322G Calculator Teardown">663</a></sup> LCD modules commonly carry driver and inverter circuitry directly on the flex.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-511" title="EEVblog #511 - Rigol DP832 Power Supply Teardown">511</a></sup> In a vintage portable [[oscilloscope]], the inverter forms part of the regulation circuitry, with its transformer housed in a separate can and wired across to the board.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-628" title="EEVblog #628 - Tektronix 213 Vintage Portable Oscilloscope Teardown">628</a></sup> Where a design needs a negative rail, an inverter is one option; using two batteries to form plus and minus rails avoids needing one at all.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1613" title="EEVblog 1613 - Audiophile REPAIR: $4500 Chord Hugo DAC">1613</a></sup>

Vehicle and aircraft power systems use inverters alongside rectifiers to derive the various AC and DC buses, and a 747 simulator reproduces that arrangement in kind.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1268" title="EEVblog #1268 - DIY Boeing 747 Cockpit Simulator Full Tour">1268</a></sup> Some trucks carry an inverter buffered by a large supercapacitor tied to the alternator, so that plugging in an oversized load does not cause problems downstream.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-9V99J22aiLE" title="Full Discussion - Film Capacitor Failures with Ron Demcko from AVX">•</a></sup> Surplus and clearance channels carry inverters in quantity, including petrol-generator units in the 2 kW class, generally sold with a fault note attached describing symptoms such as no 240 volt output or no power output.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-927" title="EEVblog #927 - Insane Jaycar Dumpster Sale!">927</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-v676ER-580E" title="Jaycar Warehouse Sale Visit">•</a></sup>

Inverter design as a discipline sits outside the range of subjects covered: Dave Jones has stated plainly that power electronics of this kind, including motors and inverters, is not an area he has knowledge of or interest in, and that despite frequent requests he will not produce a tutorial on designing inverters.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-400" title="EEVblog #400 - 400th Live Show">400</a></sup>
