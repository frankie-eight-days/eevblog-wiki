A rotary encoder converts shaft rotation into a stream of digital transitions rather than into a varying [[resistance]], which is what makes it the knob of choice once a front panel is under [[microcontroller]] control.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-225" title="EEVblog #225 - Lab Power Supply Design Part 4 - PWM Control">225</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-238" title="EEVblog #238 - Power Supply Design Part 7">238</a></sup> Where an analogue design would need a [[potentiometer]] per adjustment, a digital design puts an encoder on the same panel position and lets [[firmware]] decide what a click means, so the same knob can set [[voltage]], [[current]], [[time-base|timebase]] or a menu selection.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-225" title="EEVblog #225 - Lab Power Supply Design Part 4 - PWM Control">225</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-232" title="EEVblog #232 - Lab Power Supply Design Part 5">232</a></sup> The cost argument is stark: decent 10-turn pots run five to ten dollars or more each and a supply needs one for voltage and one for current, while encoders are around 50 cents, under a dollar.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-225" title="EEVblog #225 - Lab Power Supply Design Part 4 - PWM Control">225</a></sup>

## Signals and decoding

The common part is an incremental quadrature type: two outputs, conventionally called the A and B phase, sharing a [[ground]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-238" title="EEVblog #238 - Power Supply Design Part 7">238</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-294" title="EEVblog #294 - Mailbag">294</a></sup> Direction is recovered from the phase relationship — turning one way produces transitions on one channel and not the other, and reversing swaps which channel leads.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-k1C0qz8fR_U" title="A Totally Pointless Video About Toggle Switches (Haefely ESD Tester)">•</a></sup> Two extra pins beyond the ground and phase outputs indicate a push type, giving three functions from one control.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-294" title="EEVblog #294 - Mailbag">294</a></sup> Pushable encoders are routine on instrument panels.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-360" title="EEVblog #360 - Rigol DS2000 Oscilloscope Teardown">360</a></sup>

Firmware normally wants an interrupt on knob movement rather than a polling loop, so the encoder lines belong on pins with interrupt capability. In a bench supply design the four signals from two encoders were taken directly to microcontroller pins for exactly this reason, after an [[i2c|I2C]] expander route was rejected because the interrupt lines could not be arranged as needed; the AVR used offered pin-change interrupts on very nearly every pin.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-238" title="EEVblog #238 - Power Supply Design Part 7">238</a></sup> Writing the quadrature decoder by hand is something of an art, and in practice a library is used.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1573" title="EEVblog 1573 - TEARDOWN: How a Rotary (Angle) Pulse Encoder Works">1573</a></sup> With the decoding working, the knob drives the setpoint in defined increments — 10 mV per step on the voltage control and 1 mA per step on the [[current-limit|current limit]] in one such supply.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-240" title="EEVblog #240 - Power Supply Design Part 8">240</a></sup>

An alternative is to move the decoding off the main [[processor]] entirely. An I2C encoder board carries its own micro, accepts a standard-pinout encoder plugged onto it, and the boards can be chained — either through pin headers or butt-soldered directly to each other — into a grid.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1110" title="EEVblog #1110 - Mailbag">1110</a></sup> For one or two encoders the effort is not worth it, since running the lines back to the project's own controller is straightforward; the payoff comes with large arrays of controls, where per-encoder wiring multiplies and the software has to keep reading all of them in real time.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1110" title="EEVblog #1110 - Mailbag">1110</a></sup>

## Contact, optical, and wear

Most cheap encoders are contact types, with a brush or wiper sweeping over surface contacts.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1573" title="EEVblog 1573 - TEARDOWN: How a Rotary (Angle) Pulse Encoder Works">1573</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-Bh3gcBr0DKs" title="How to do Lifecycle Testing">•</a></sup> Optical versions avoid the wiping contact and are marketed on life expectancy; one 12-bit scope advertised detented optical encoders rather than electrical wipe contacts as a headline specification.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1501" title="EEVblog 1501 - Rigol HDO4000 Low Noise 12bit Oscilloscope Unboxing &amp; First Impression">1501</a></sup> Hybrid arrangements exist too, with a photo sensor detecting a cutout as an end stop alongside metal contacts for the incremental signal.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1244" title="EEVblog #1244 - Mailbag">1244</a></sup>

Rated life is the number that surprises people. Pulling data sheets for contact rotary encoders from the major manufacturers — C&K, Panasonic, CUI — shows some parts rated in the millions of cycles, but the majority, including parts costing around $5 each, are guaranteed for only 30,000 to 50,000 cycles; roughly 400 types were listed under 100,000 cycles.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-Bh3gcBr0DKs" title="How to do Lifecycle Testing">•</a></sup> Encoders on instrument front panels get real wear and tear and will almost certainly eventually wear out, depending on how often the machine is used.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-475" title="EEVblog #475 - GW Instek GDS-2000A Oscilloscope Teardown">475</a></sup> Dust and accumulated crud in the mechanism make it worse.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-Bh3gcBr0DKs" title="How to do Lifecycle Testing">•</a></sup>

## Feel and detents

Because the encoder is the part of the instrument the user physically touches, its mechanical quality dominates the perceived quality of the whole front panel. Detent strength is the main variable. A well-regarded retro scope used large, heavyweight encoders requiring a fair bit of force to turn.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-591" title="EEVblog #591 - Agilent 54622D Retro Mixed Signal Osciloscope Review &amp; Teardown">591</a></sup> A fluted metal shaft on a surface-mount encoder soldered straight down to the [[pcb|PCB]] produced similarly well-defined indents.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-842" title="EEVblog #842 - Rohde &amp; Schwarz HMO1202 Oscilloscope Teardown">842</a></sup> At the other end, one budget scope's encoders had only a small indent and a feathery touch, so an extra count was easily skipped and the setting could change without reaching the next indent, leaving a constant sense of impending [[overshoot]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-704" title="EEVBlog #704 - Rigol DS1054Z Oscilloscope Features Review">704</a></sup> Another had encoders with no indents and poor feel throughout.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-480" title="EEVblog #480 - 300MHz Owon SDS Oscilloscope">480</a></sup> Detents are not universally wanted — one review found the absence of indents on the secondary controls preferable.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-797" title="EEVblog #797 - Siglent SDS1000X Oscilloscope Review">797</a></sup>

Skipping is not always mechanical. On one 1 GHz scope, spinning the knob by the same amount produced different amounts of change, consistent with missed encoder readings in the software decoding rather than a bad contact.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1220" title="EEVblog #1220 - Siglent SDS5000X 1GHz Oscilloscope Review">1220</a></sup>

The encoder also shapes the user interface around it. Where a panel has soft labels along a non-touch screen with no physical buttons beneath them, every selection has to be driven through the knob, which makes the navigation non-obvious.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1691" title="EEVblog 1691 - Uni-T UDP6731 360W Bench PSU REVIEW">1691</a></sup>

Outside instrumentation, an encoder on a brushless motor shaft allows a cheap motor to be used as a precise servo; the [[calibration]] is specific to that motor, so the encoder has to stay fitted.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1049" title="EEVblog #1049 - Mailbag">1049</a></sup> A camcorder control bus can be reverse engineered by watching the data change as an encoder-driven zoom control is moved, revealing codes that depend on zoom level rather than simply on direction.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-297" title="EEVblog #297 - Canon LANC Bus Reverse Engineering">297</a></sup>

A high-security electronic lock is an instructive case. The rotating dial drives an encoder whose signals pass through a zebra elastomeric strip and ribbon cables to a microcontroller inside the secure part of the lock, which counts turns in each direction and shows a two-digit number on an [[lcd|LCD]], simulating the look and feel of a mechanical tumbler.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1484" title="EEVblog 1484 - Kaba Mas X-09 High Security Electronic Lock Teardown">1484</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1483" title="EEVblog 1483 - Holy Mailbag Bomb Batman!">1483</a></sup> The dial spins continuously with no end stop, and the entry gesture is to spin to a number, pause, then reverse direction.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1483" title="EEVblog 1483 - Holy Mailbag Bomb Batman!">1483</a></sup> Deliberately, the only things on the outside of the door are the encoder and the display, with no electronics that could offer an attack path into the microcontroller.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1484" title="EEVblog 1484 - Kaba Mas X-09 High Security Electronic Lock Teardown">1484</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1483" title="EEVblog 1483 - Holy Mailbag Bomb Batman!">1483</a></sup>

## Mechanical integration

Encoders drive the mechanical stack-up of a front panel. In a single-board scope design, allowing for button travel plus the encoder height made the stack difficult enough that every comparable earlier product had used multiple boards.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-PcxEO3fA_Ls" title="Tektronix 2 Series Discussion with Andy Tedd from Tektronix">•</a></sup> A portable soldering station ended up with the encoder manually wired over rather than reflowed as intended, a concession to the same class of constraint.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1646" title="EEVblog 1646 - iFixIt FixHub Portable USB Soldering Station REVIEW">1646</a></sup> Serviceability deserves equal weight: mechanical switches and encoders are the parts most likely to need replacing over the life of a long-lived, high-value instrument, and a construction that requires [[desoldering]] an entire switch and plate assembly to reach one encoder makes that repair far harder than it needs to be.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1572" title="EEVblog 1572 - Haefely 16kV ESD Tester TEARDOWN + Repair Part 1">1572</a></sup>

## The angle pulse encoder

Not every encoder produces quadrature. A German-made unit from EBE, fitted as the voltage-setting knob of an early-1990s 16 kV ESD tester, instead presents two single-pole double-throw sections, each of which responds to one direction of rotation only: rotating one way toggles one section between its two pins while the other stays still, and reversing swaps which section moves.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1593" title="EEVblog 1593 - 16kV ESD Tester REPAIR - Part 2">1593</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1573" title="EEVblog 1573 - TEARDOWN: How a Rotary (Angle) Pulse Encoder Works">1573</a></sup> The two sections count at different rates, the outer pin having a higher count than the inner.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-k1C0qz8fR_U" title="A Totally Pointless Video About Toggle Switches (Haefely ESD Tester)">•</a></sup> Internally there is no wiping contact at all — a shaped inner cam forces a springy contact back and forth between a normally-closed and a normally-open post as the shaft turns, which makes the mechanism dependent on tight tolerance and on the metal retaining its springiness.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1573" title="EEVblog 1573 - TEARDOWN: How a Rotary (Angle) Pulse Encoder Works">1573</a></sup> The failed unit showed no significant wear on its teeth and an intact arm, pointing at loss of spring force rather than abrasion as the failure mode.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1573" title="EEVblog 1573 - TEARDOWN: How a Rotary (Angle) Pulse Encoder Works">1573</a></sup> The scheme is easier to decode in software than quadrature, at the cost of needing the double-throw contacts.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1573" title="EEVblog 1573 - TEARDOWN: How a Rotary (Angle) Pulse Encoder Works">1573</a></sup>

Failure of such a part is diagnosable in place: with the board accessible, a continuity tester on the encoder pins should beep as the shaft is rotated in a given direction, and a section that stays open under rotation is dead.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1572" title="EEVblog 1572 - Haefely 16kV ESD Tester TEARDOWN + Repair Part 1">1572</a></sup> Here one contact was well and truly open, the fault presenting as a voltage knob that would only count down and never up.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1572" title="EEVblog 1572 - Haefely 16kV ESD Tester TEARDOWN + Repair Part 1">1572</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1593" title="EEVblog 1593 - 16kV ESD Tester REPAIR - Part 2">1593</a></sup> The part carried no part number and an unfamiliar six-pin footprint, and a wide public appeal failed to identify it — a genuine "special snowflake" for which no modern equivalent pinout was found.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1572" title="EEVblog 1572 - Haefely 16kV ESD Tester TEARDOWN + Repair Part 1">1572</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-k1C0qz8fR_U" title="A Totally Pointless Video About Toggle Switches (Haefely ESD Tester)">•</a></sup> It was eventually traced by someone who knew the correct German search term, still available at around 93 euros, single-source, with new old stock offered by a German eBay seller.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-k1C0qz8fR_U" title="A Totally Pointless Video About Toggle Switches (Haefely ESD Tester)">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1593" title="EEVblog 1593 - 16kV ESD Tester REPAIR - Part 2">1593</a></sup> The fallback, had no replacement been available, was a small microcontroller converting a conventional rotary encoder or a pair of toggle switches into the same alternating single-pole double-throw action, since the instrument's firmware expected that behaviour.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1573" title="EEVblog 1573 - TEARDOWN: How a Rotary (Angle) Pulse Encoder Works">1573</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1593" title="EEVblog 1593 - 16kV ESD Tester REPAIR - Part 2">1593</a></sup> The repair of a $10,000 instrument came down to the cost of that one encoder.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1593" title="EEVblog 1593 - 16kV ESD Tester REPAIR - Part 2">1593</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-k1C0qz8fR_U" title="A Totally Pointless Video About Toggle Switches (Haefely ESD Tester)">•</a></sup>

## Absolute and process-control variants

Where a shaft angle must be reported rather than counted, the output can be an analogue current instead of pulses. An Australian-made rotational sensor puts the shaft on a ball bearing, processes the angle in a microcontroller in real time, and converts it to the 4-20 mA process control standard — a current loop being used precisely because such a reading is not readily upset by the wiring between sensor and receiver.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-DX_f0Cg6pHo" title="How a 4-20mA Process Control Current Loop Rotational Sensor Works">•</a></sup> The cable enters through a screw-in, dust- and waterproof sealed interface.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-DX_f0Cg6pHo" title="How a 4-20mA Process Control Current Loop Rotational Sensor Works">•</a></sup> Weatherproofing around the shaft itself is handled with a rubber seal in other sealed encoder assemblies.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1483" title="EEVblog 1483 - Holy Mailbag Bomb Batman!">1483</a></sup>
