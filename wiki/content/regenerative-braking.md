Regenerative braking is the use of an electric vehicle's traction motor as a generator during deceleration, so that the vehicle's kinetic energy is returned to the [[battery]] instead of being dissipated.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-7nUgmEuyKIU" title="Hyundai IONIQ Regen Braking vs Coasting Test">•</a></sup> In an internal combustion vehicle the same energy is converted to heat in the friction brakes and lost entirely.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-7nUgmEuyKIU" title="Hyundai IONIQ Regen Braking vs Coasting Test">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1600" title="EEVblog 1600 - Rouute: Road Based Energy BUSTED">1600</a></sup> The practical consequences are twofold: measurably extended range, and friction brakes that are barely used across the life of the vehicle.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-7nUgmEuyKIU" title="Hyundai IONIQ Regen Braking vs Coasting Test">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-5IV_oRAupeo" title="Hyundai IONIQ EV Zero Brake Wear After 12 Months!">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1339" title="EEVblog #1339 - Looking at an Electric Bus!">1339</a></sup>

## Operating principle and the coasting alternative

When the accelerator is released, the motor is driven by the wheels and [[current]] flows back into the pack.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-7nUgmEuyKIU" title="Hyundai IONIQ Regen Braking vs Coasting Test">•</a></sup> The instrument cluster represents this on a bidirectional bar graph: bars in the positive direction mean energy is leaving the battery under acceleration, and bars in the negative direction mean charge is being returned, whether from braking or from a descent.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-7nUgmEuyKIU" title="Hyundai IONIQ Regen Braking vs Coasting Test">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-5rZk8mNvYUc" title="2020 Hyundai IONIQ AC Charger FAIL?">•</a></sup> Some implementations also report the recovered energy in range terms, for instance several hundred metres of range added at a single stop.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-h9MvrP9JDrs" title="Hyundai IONIQ 2020 Review after 12 Month/17,000km">•</a></sup> On the Mitsubishi iMiEV the equivalent function is selected by moving the gear lever from eco into B, which drives the econo gauge into the charge region on a descent, though on a shallow hill the change in deceleration is barely perceptible.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-179" title="Mitsubishi iMiEV Electric Car Test Drive - EEVblog #179">179</a></sup>

The complement to regeneration is coasting. Turning regeneration off entirely leaves the vehicle to roll on when the accelerator is released, losing energy only to tyre rolling [[resistance]], wheel bearings and aerodynamic drag.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-7nUgmEuyKIU" title="Hyundai IONIQ Regen Braking vs Coasting Test">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1432" title="EEVBlog 1432 - Owning an ELECTRIC CAR for 12 Months &amp; 17,000km">1432</a></sup> In a low-drag body — the IONIQ's drag coefficient is around 0.23 — there is very little left to slow the car, so it simply keeps going.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-7nUgmEuyKIU" title="Hyundai IONIQ Regen Braking vs Coasting Test">•</a></sup> Which of the two is preferable is scenario-dependent rather than settled, which is why the comparison is worth measuring rather than assuming: regeneration against coasting, over a mountainous route and over ordinary flat suburban driving.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-7nUgmEuyKIU" title="Hyundai IONIQ Regen Braking vs Coasting Test">•</a></sup>

## Levels, selection and driver control

Regeneration strength is a driver-selectable quantity. The Hyundai IONIQ exposes it through steering-wheel paddles: pulling the right paddle enters a fixed manual mode with discrete levels, of which level zero is regeneration off and level three is maximum.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-7nUgmEuyKIU" title="Hyundai IONIQ Regen Braking vs Coasting Test">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-XmBW_MV-TBU" title="Hyundai IONIQ Electric EV First Drive!">•</a></sup> Holding the left paddle down applies full regeneration and will pull the car to a standstill regardless of the level otherwise selected.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-XmBW_MV-TBU" title="Hyundai IONIQ Electric EV First Drive!">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-7nUgmEuyKIU" title="Hyundai IONIQ Regen Braking vs Coasting Test">•</a></sup> There is also an automatic mode that varies the level without driver input, and in eco mode with level three selected the car performs the regeneration on lift-off without any paddle use at all.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1432" title="EEVBlog 1432 - Owning an ELECTRIC CAR for 12 Months &amp; 17,000km">1432</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1337" title="EEVblog #1337 - I Bought a Fully Electric Elite Car!">1337</a></sup>

Even at maximum fixed level, lift-off regeneration alone does not quite bring the car to a complete stop; the paddle hold, or a light touch of the brake pedal at the very end, closes the gap.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-7nUgmEuyKIU" title="Hyundai IONIQ Regen Braking vs Coasting Test">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-XmBW_MV-TBU" title="Hyundai IONIQ Electric EV First Drive!">•</a></sup> Modulating the accelerator to arrive at a stop line without touching the brake is a skill in its own right — released fully, the car often decelerates too early, so a small amount of pedal is held to stretch the stop out.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-7nUgmEuyKIU" title="Hyundai IONIQ Regen Braking vs Coasting Test">•</a></sup> This is the basis of one-pedal driving, in which the brake pedal is reserved for exceptions.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-7nUgmEuyKIU" title="Hyundai IONIQ Regen Braking vs Coasting Test">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-h9MvrP9JDrs" title="Hyundai IONIQ 2020 Review after 12 Month/17,000km">•</a></sup> In general driving Dave Jones leaves regeneration engaged permanently, on fixed level three or on auto, and rarely touches the brakes.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-7nUgmEuyKIU" title="Hyundai IONIQ Regen Braking vs Coasting Test">•</a></sup> The engineering argument for defaulting to it is that in roughly 95% of lift-off events the driver wants to decelerate anyway, so the deceleration may as well be recovered.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1432" title="EEVBlog 1432 - Owning an ELECTRIC CAR for 12 Months &amp; 17,000km">1432</a></sup>

Two interactions matter for safety and for measurement. Under strong regeneration the vehicle applies the brake lights even though the brake pedal has not been pressed, since a following driver needs the same warning regardless of which mechanism is decelerating the car.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-7nUgmEuyKIU" title="Hyundai IONIQ Regen Braking vs Coasting Test">•</a></sup> Engaging cruise control disables regeneration, which rules it out during any consumption comparison.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-7nUgmEuyKIU" title="Hyundai IONIQ Regen Braking vs Coasting Test">•</a></sup>

## Measured recovery

Over a mixed motorway and suburban route with regeneration held at full for the whole trip, average consumption came to 10.6 kWh per 100 km, with instantaneous figures ranging from the low eights to 12 or 13.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-7nUgmEuyKIU" title="Hyundai IONIQ Regen Braking vs Coasting Test">•</a></sup> Motorway sections with a slight downhill component ran at 9.7 and then 8.8 kWh per 100 km; around 10 kWh per 100 km is a typical low figure for suburban driving.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-7nUgmEuyKIU" title="Hyundai IONIQ Regen Braking vs Coasting Test">•</a></sup>

Recovery is at its most dramatic on sustained descents, where the energy being reclaimed is gravitational potential rather than the kinetic energy of individual stops. A descent from Blackheath in the Blue Mountains down to Penrith consumes zero net energy, the regeneration on the way down offsetting all traction energy used.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1600" title="EEVblog 1600 - Rouute: Road Based Energy BUSTED">1600</a></sup> Regeneration is an efficient means of recovering that energy — efficient enough that the alternative of harvesting it from the road surface under the tyres offers nothing that the vehicle is not already capturing for itself.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1600" title="EEVblog 1600 - Rouute: Road Based Energy BUSTED">1600</a></sup>

In fleet service the recovery fraction is substantial. An electric city bus operation reports regeneration returning on the order of 30 to 40 percent, with up to 30 percent seen on a single trip; hilly routes yield more.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1339" title="EEVblog #1339 - Looking at an Electric Bus!">1339</a></sup> Close stop spacing was expected to be a problem, on the assumption that the bus would never reach enough speed between stops to regenerate meaningfully, and the outcome was the opposite of that expectation.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1339" title="EEVblog #1339 - Looking at an Electric Bus!">1339</a></sup>

## Effect on friction brakes

Because the friction brakes are seldom commanded, pad wear is nearly eliminated. Even with regeneration switched off and the vehicle in coast mode, regeneration still handles the deceleration, and the discs engage only under hard braking.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-5IV_oRAupeo" title="Hyundai IONIQ EV Zero Brake Wear After 12 Months!">•</a></sup> After twelve months of use an IONIQ front pad showed effectively no wear.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-5IV_oRAupeo" title="Hyundai IONIQ EV Zero Brake Wear After 12 Months!">•</a></sup> Electric buses still require brake inspections, but show significantly less pad wear than their diesel equivalents, attributable to regeneration.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1339" title="EEVblog #1339 - Looking at an Electric Bus!">1339</a></sup> On the iMiEV, pad life was expected in the region of 30,000 to 40,000 km, brakes and fluids being close to the whole of the remaining service schedule.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-179" title="Mitsubishi iMiEV Electric Car Test Drive - EEVblog #179">179</a></sup>

## Beyond cars

The same mechanism appears on smaller vehicles. A brushless hub-motor electric bicycle kit provides regeneration through a switch in each of the supplied brake levers, with the amount of regeneration programmable in the controller so the rider can dial up as much or as little braking effect as wanted.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-338" title="EEVblog #338 - DIY Electric Bike">338</a></sup>
