Solar roadways are road, cycleway, or pavement surfaces built from [[solar-power-system|photovoltaic]] panels protected by a load-bearing glass, resin, or polymer top layer, intended to generate electricity from land already used as roadway. The concept matters as a recurring case study in engineering feasibility analysis: multiple heavily promoted trials in the Netherlands, France, China, Germany, and the United States were built between 2014 and the early 2020s, and every one of them underperformed, degraded, or was removed, confirming predictions made from simple back-of-the-envelope energy and cost calculations before any were constructed.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-681" title="EEVblog #681 - More Solar Roadways BULLSHIT!">681</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1047" title="EEVblog #1047 - Solar Roadways FINALLY BUSTED! (Colas Wattway)">1047</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1233" title="EEVblog #1233 - The Demise Of Solar Roadways">1233</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1603" title="EEVblog 1603 - Colas Wattway Solar Roadways is DEAD">1603</a></sup> The consistent measured result across all installations is roughly one-half to one-third the energy yield of an ordinary rooftop or solar-farm installation at several times the cost, before accounting for the severe durability and maintenance problems of driving on glass.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-850" title="EEVblog #850 - French Wattway Solar Roadways BUSTED!">850</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1047" title="EEVblog #1047 - Solar Roadways FINALLY BUSTED! (Colas Wattway)">1047</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1603" title="EEVblog 1603 - Colas Wattway Solar Roadways is DEAD">1603</a></sup>

## Concept and claimed features

The archetypal proposal, from the US company Solar Roadways founded by Scott and Julie Brusaw in 2006, was to replace roadways, parking lots, sidewalks, driveways, and bike paths with interlocking hexagonal glass panels containing solar cells, [[led|LED]] lighting for lane markings and signage, pressure sensors, wireless communications, and internal heating elements intended to keep the surface free of snow and ice.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1738" title="EEVblog 1738 - Solar Freakin&#x27; Roadways NEW FUNDING!">1738</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-sJS2paVW-kc" title="Renewable Energy vs Fossil Fuels Presentation">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1050" title="EEVblog #1050 - MORE Solar Roadways LOL!">1050</a></sup> The company claimed the panels could support 250,000 lb vehicles and that converting all roads in the lower 48 US states could produce over three times the country's annual electricity consumption.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1738" title="EEVblog 1738 - Solar Freakin&#x27; Roadways NEW FUNDING!">1738</a></sup> Other implementations took different forms: the Dutch SolaRoad project used large prefabricated concrete modules with cells embedded under a translucent gritty top layer, and the French Colas Wattway used thin (~7 mm) resin-polymer-covered cells glued directly onto an existing road surface, the most engineering-credible approach of the three.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-850" title="EEVblog #850 - French Wattway Solar Roadways BUSTED!">850</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1047" title="EEVblog #1047 - Solar Roadways FINALLY BUSTED! (Colas Wattway)">1047</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1276" title="EEVblog #1276 - Solar Roadways Surface TESTED!">1276</a></sup>

## Measured energy yield

Real test data from the major trials converge on the same figure: about half the output of a comparable conventional installation.

- A first-principles estimate for the Solar Roadways concept, using a high-end panel laid flat with realistic system losses, gives a best case of 380 Wh/m²/day, equivalent to roughly 9 US cents of electricity per square meter per day, or about $33/m²/year.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-632" title="EEVblog #632 - Solar Roadways Are RUBBISH!">632</a></sup>
- The 70 m × 1.75 m (122.5 m²) SolaRoad cycleway in the Netherlands was specified by its own developers at 50 kWh/m²/year (137 Wh/m²/day); its first full week produced 100 kWh, or 117 Wh/m²/day, closely matching that expectation.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-681" title="EEVblog #681 - More Solar Roadways BULLSHIT!">681</a></sup> Over its six-month trial it generated 3,000 kWh, or 24.5 kWh/m², against an average of 47.2 kWh/m² from nearby rooftop systems monitored over the identical period — almost exactly double.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-743" title="EEVblog #743 - Solar Roadways Test Results">743</a></sup>
- The 1 km Colas Wattway installation in Normandy (2,800 m² of panels, 336 kW nominal, €5 million) delivered 82 kWh/m²/year against 146 kWh/m²/year for ordinary consumer rooftop systems — 56% of rooftop output — and averaged 288% less per unit area than a modest home installation over 11 months of measured data.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-850" title="EEVblog #850 - French Wattway Solar Roadways BUSTED!">850</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1047" title="EEVblog #1047 - Solar Roadways FINALLY BUSTED! (Colas Wattway)">1047</a></sup> It was projected to produce 150,000 kWh/year but produced 80,000 kWh in 2018 and 40,000 kWh in 2019, and a cumulative 366 MWh over roughly eight years — about 61 MWh/year, enough for only a handful of average households.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1603" title="EEVblog 1603 - Colas Wattway Solar Roadways is DEAD">1603</a></sup>
- Solar Roadways' own SR4 installation in Sandpoint, Idaho (30 panels, 1.5 kW nominal) produced 0.46 kWh on a day when a nearby angled 4.4 kW rooftop array produced 10.85 kWh — a 13:1 advantage to the rooftop per unit of installed capacity on the same day.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1269" title="EEVblog #1269 - Solar Roadways SR4 DATA Hilarity!">1269</a></sup>
- A later Dutch solar cycleway installation achieved only 275 full-load hours, 70% less yield than regular solar panels of the same capacity.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1556" title="EEVblog 1556 - SolaRoad Solar Cycleway FAILS, AGAIN!">1556</a></sup>
- The Peachtree Corners, Georgia installation was expected to produce about 1,300 kWh/year feeding an EV charger — less than half the annual output of a comparably sized 3 kW home rooftop array.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1356" title="EEVblog #1356 - The USA finally gets Solar Roadways! LOL!">1356</a></sup>

## Loss mechanisms

The shortfall is not incidental; it follows directly from the configuration. Panels laid horizontal rather than tilted lose roughly 20–30% of yield relative to angled mounting; reference data for the Wattway location show a flat 336 kW system producing 288 MWh/year against 345 MWh/year tilted, a 22% penalty from orientation alone.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-743" title="EEVblog #743 - Solar Roadways Test Results">743</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1047" title="EEVblog #1047 - Solar Roadways FINALLY BUSTED! (Colas Wattway)">1047</a></sup> The thick textured protective glass or resin layer required to survive traffic attenuates light by a further 10–15%, and an opacity test on an actual recovered fragment of the Dutch surface confirmed that covering a cell with the road-surface material destroys most of its output.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-743" title="EEVblog #743 - Solar Roadways Test Results">743</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1276" title="EEVblog #1276 - Solar Roadways Surface TESTED!">1276</a></sup> [[ground|Ground]]-embedded panels lack the [[airflow]] beneath a rooftop array, so they run hotter, and photovoltaic [[efficiency]] falls with temperature.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-743" title="EEVblog #743 - Solar Roadways Test Results">743</a></sup> On top of this come shading by vehicles, dirt, grime, and leaf litter, which also create non-uniform illumination across panels that perform best under uniform light.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-632" title="EEVblog #632 - Solar Roadways Are RUBBISH!">632</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1603" title="EEVblog 1603 - Colas Wattway Solar Roadways is DEAD">1603</a></sup>

## Durability failures

Driving on solar panels proved to be the decisive problem, exactly as predicted.

- The original 2014 Krommenie SolaRoad cycleway strip (90 m × 1.7 m) had to be redone in 2016 after surface damage; a recovered fragment shows the gritty resin top layer peeling away from the roughly 3 mm base.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1276" title="EEVblog #1276 - Solar Roadways Surface TESTED!">1276</a></sup>
- The Wattway road in Normandy deteriorated within three years: panels cracked, delaminated, and splintered, sections were declared unrepairable and demolished, and the surface was noisy enough to require a 50 km/h speed limit. A 400 m section was rebuilt in 2019, and the installation was being removed entirely by 2024.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1603" title="EEVblog 1603 - Colas Wattway Solar Roadways is DEAD">1603</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1234" title="EEVblog #1234 - MORE Epic Solar Roadways FAIL!">1234</a></sup>
- The SolaRoad heavy-traffic bus-lane pilot in South Holland (100 m, opened 7 March) was closed within days after surface damage; the official finding was that "the [[solar-panel|solar panel]] and the top layer release from the underlying concrete of the road element", causing distortion and progressive failure, and that repair was not possible, so the province discontinued the pilot.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1234" title="EEVblog #1234 - MORE Epic Solar Roadways FAIL!">1234</a></sup>
- The 2 km solar highway in Jinan, China, built with "transparent concrete", was damaged within days of opening in late December, with investigators concluding the panels were broken by rocks and debris carried in tire treads or thrown from vehicles.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1050" title="EEVblog #1050 - MORE Solar Roadways LOL!">1050</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1233" title="EEVblog #1233 - The Demise Of Solar Roadways">1233</a></sup>
- A solar bridge installation in Hengalow showed its resin grip coating completely worn away across entire patches under traffic.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-4GUygSdf92A" title="Solar Freakin&#x27; Bridges! - ANOTHER Solar Roadways FAIL">•</a></sup>

Vehicle loading also induces microcracking in the cells and flexing that lifts panel edges and admits water.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1603" title="EEVblog 1603 - Colas Wattway Solar Roadways is DEAD">1603</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1234" title="EEVblog #1234 - MORE Epic Solar Roadways FAIL!">1234</a></sup> Colas Wattway's managing director conceded in 2019 that "Our system is not mature on long-distance traffic," announcing a retreat to small 3–9 m² modules for powering CCTV cameras, bus-shelter lighting, and e-bike charging instead of roads.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1603" title="EEVblog 1603 - Colas Wattway Solar Roadways is DEAD">1603</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1234" title="EEVblog #1234 - MORE Epic Solar Roadways FAIL!">1234</a></sup>

## Economics

The cost structure is as unfavorable as the energy yield. A rooftop system runs about €230/m² installed, while a road-based system starts at €300/m² and carries maintenance burdens rooftops do not have; on measured output the rooftop is at least four times more energy-productive per euro, the difference that makes rooftop solar pay back in roughly six years while a solar road can never pay for itself.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-681" title="EEVblog #681 - More Solar Roadways BULLSHIT!">681</a></sup> Applying the Dutch developers' own verified figures, the SolaRoad prototype's revenue works out to about €10/m²/year against its construction cost — a nominal payback on the order of 1,500 years for the €3 million, 70 m prototype.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-681" title="EEVblog #681 - More Solar Roadways BULLSHIT!">681</a></sup> Wattway's management admitted costs around €6 per watt-peak with a best-case aspiration of €3/Wp; the Normandy road ultimately delivered one-third the capacity of a conventional installation for nine times the cost, a figure later revised to fifteen times on the observation that the €5 million spent on the 0.336 MW road could have built a 5 MW solar farm producing reliably for decades.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-850" title="EEVblog #850 - French Wattway Solar Roadways BUSTED!">850</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-935" title="EEVblog #935 - Solar Roadways Installation BUSTED!">935</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1047" title="EEVblog #1047 - Solar Roadways FINALLY BUSTED! (Colas Wattway)">1047</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1603" title="EEVblog 1603 - Colas Wattway Solar Roadways is DEAD">1603</a></sup> Solar Roadways' SR3 panels cost about $2,000 per 44 W panel — 22 times the cost per watt of commercial rooftop solar — and even a hypothetical tenfold cost reduction would leave them four to five times more expensive, before maintenance and the road-specific infrastructure of cable pits, drainage, and wiring.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-935" title="EEVblog #935 - Solar Roadways Installation BUSTED!">935</a></sup>

Ancillary electrical loads make the picture worse. By Solar Roadways' own measurements, the LED lane markers and supporting circuitry consume about a quarter of the energy the tiles produce per lane-mile, for LEDs not visible in daylight.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-935" title="EEVblog #935 - Solar Roadways Installation BUSTED!">935</a></sup>

## The snow-melting claim

The flagship Solar Roadways promise of heating the panels to keep roads snow- and ice-free fails on basic latent-heat arithmetic. Snow at about 10 lb/ft³ covering a 4.4 ft² tile to a depth of six inches is 22 lb of ice per tile, requiring about 930 Wh per tile merely to transition to water — 27.9 kWh for the 30-tile Sandpoint array. That array is a 1.5 kW system, so even eight hours of full-sun maximum output supplies less than half the energy required for the phase change alone, in a location and season where actual insolation is far lower.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1269" title="EEVblog #1269 - Solar Roadways SR4 DATA Hilarity!">1269</a></sup> In practice the panels had to be cleared by shoveling, and the heating elements produced only small melt holes in the snow cover.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1050" title="EEVblog #1050 - MORE Solar Roadways LOL!">1050</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-TL94sJ3Ykgs" title="Merry Solar Roadways Christmas!">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-wnp3FnAGiyw" title="Portable Kinetic Molecular Heater SCAM!">•</a></sup>

## Engineering assessment

The consistent engineering judgment, borne out by every built trial, is that the concept takes existing photovoltaic technology — already a marginally economic proposition even in optimal installations — and places it in its worst possible configuration: flat, shaded, soiled, covered by lossy protective layers, thermally isolated, and mechanically abused by traffic.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-632" title="EEVblog #632 - Solar Roadways Are RUBBISH!">632</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1047" title="EEVblog #1047 - Solar Roadways FINALLY BUSTED! (Colas Wattway)">1047</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1234" title="EEVblog #1234 - MORE Epic Solar Roadways FAIL!">1234</a></sup> No amount of development changes the comparison, because conventional rooftop and ground-mount solar improves in cost and efficiency in parallel, preserving the gap indefinitely.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-681" title="EEVblog #681 - More Solar Roadways BULLSHIT!">681</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1356" title="EEVblog #1356 - The USA finally gets Solar Roadways! LOL!">1356</a></sup> The correct deployment of the same panels is on rooftops, on poles, as canopies over car parks and canals, or in solar farms; the frequently cited counter-example is the 32 km South Korean cycleway covered by an elevated solar canopy, which keeps panels clean, angled, unmolested by vehicles, and maintainable for decades while sheltering the path beneath.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1356" title="EEVblog #1356 - The USA finally gets Solar Roadways! LOL!">1356</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-681" title="EEVblog #681 - More Solar Roadways BULLSHIT!">681</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-743" title="EEVblog #743 - Solar Roadways Test Results">743</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-sJS2paVW-kc" title="Renewable Energy vs Fossil Fuels Presentation">•</a></sup> Niche decorative applications such as park lighting were the only use ever conceded as potentially defensible, and even there a panel on a pole beside the path outperforms one embedded in it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-681" title="EEVblog #681 - More Solar Roadways BULLSHIT!">681</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-743" title="EEVblog #743 - Solar Roadways Test Results">743</a></sup>
