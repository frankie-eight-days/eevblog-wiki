A decoupling capacitor is a [[capacitor]] placed between a device's power pin and [[ground]] to supply the short, sharp [[current]] demands the chip makes as it switches, so that those demands are met locally instead of being drawn down the power distribution network.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1383" title="EEVblog 1383 - Human Battery Energy Harvesting BUSTED">1383</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1365" title="EEVblog #1365 - Viewer PCB &amp; Circuit Design Review">1365</a></sup> The power source supplies the average current; the decoupling capacitors supply the peaks.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1383" title="EEVblog 1383 - Human Battery Energy Harvesting BUSTED">1383</a></sup> Where that local reservoir is absent or too far away, the transient current has to travel through trace and cable [[impedance]], and the resulting rail collapse and [[ringing]] produce real logic failures — spurious clocking of a [[shift-register|shift register]], for example.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1365" title="EEVblog #1365 - Viewer PCB &amp; Circuit Design Review">1365</a></sup> The terms decoupling and bypassing are used interchangeably for the same components.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1522" title="EEVblog 1522 - BM786 Multimeter Repair PART 2">1522</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1081" title="EEVblog #1081 - Are Bypass Capacitors REALLY needed? (Muntzing)">1081</a></sup>

## Values and why several are used in parallel

Datasheets typically recommend 0.1 µF — 100 nF — with multiple capacitors, one per power pin, and it is standard practice to run several in parallel.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1129" title="EEVblog #1129 - Creating a Nice Readable Schematic">1129</a></sup> It is not unusual to see a 10 µF, a 1 µF and a 0.1 µF grouped on a single supply pin, the point of the combination being that each part presents its low impedance over a different band of frequencies.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1129" title="EEVblog #1129 - Creating a Nice Readable Schematic">1129</a></sup> A pairing of a 47 µF and a 10 nF divides the work explicitly: the large part absorbs heavy current bursts, the small part handles the high-[[frequency]] switching, and together they hold a reasonably smooth impedance from around 100 kHz up to a few hundred megahertz.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-859" title="EEVblog #859 - Bypass / Decoupling Capacitor Tutorial">859</a></sup>

Below the ceramics, the board itself contributes. Power and ground on adjacent inner layers form a plate capacitor spanning the whole board, and that distributed [[capacitance]] is a real, measurable decoupling element rather than a theoretical one.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1117" title="EEVblog #1117 - PCB Power Plane Capacitance">1117</a></sup>

Manufacturer part numbers set a floor rather than a rule. The 74HC595 [[datasheet]] calls for a 10 nF capacitor on each chip, while the ULN2803 specifies no capacitors at all.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1365" title="EEVblog #1365 - Viewer PCB &amp; Circuit Design Review">1365</a></sup> Around a single 5 V logic supply the difference between 10 nF, 100 nF, 1 µF and 10 µF is often immaterial to whether the circuit works.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1365" title="EEVblog #1365 - Viewer PCB &amp; Circuit Design Review">1365</a></sup>

## Bulk decoupling and current gulps

Small ceramics do not cover loads that draw hundreds of milliamps in a step. A board switching relays or driving similar loads warrants local bulk decoupling of a couple of hundred microfarads — either one capacitor per chip or a single bulk part for the board — so the charge is gulped locally.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1365" title="EEVblog #1365 - Viewer PCB &amp; Circuit Design Review">1365</a></sup> Forcing that current to come from an upstream board is poor practice, and it becomes acutely bad when the two are joined by a long thin [[ribbon-cable|ribbon cable]], whose impedance the transient must cross.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1365" title="EEVblog #1365 - Viewer PCB &amp; Circuit Design Review">1365</a></sup> The same reasoning drives star power distribution, in which digital, analog and high-power branches radiate from one central point that carries its own bulk decoupling capacitor.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1365" title="EEVblog #1365 - Viewer PCB &amp; Circuit Design Review">1365</a></sup>

Large programmable logic shows the effect at scale. Big [[fpga|FPGAs]] take substantial gulps of current at power-up and demand massive, layout-critical bypassing.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1323" title="EEVblog #1323 - PCB Layout Review &amp; Analysis">1323</a></sup> Xilinx devices on telecom switch-fabric cards were ringed with bulk decoupling in the form of 47 µF 16 V parts.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1019" title="EEVblog #1019 - Mailbag">1019</a></sup> A Spartan 6 carried a dense field of 0402 capacitors on the underside of its footprint, packed that small purely to fit them in.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-475" title="EEVblog #475 - GW Instek GDS-2000A Oscilloscope Teardown">475</a></sup> The requirement is not uniform even between devices on one board: on an [[oscilloscope]]'s main board a secondary [[asic|ASIC]] ran heavy-duty ceramic bypass capacitors while the larger acquisition ASIC beside it used ordinary 100 nF parts distributed around its perimeter.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-148" title="EEVblog #148 - Agilent 3000 X Series Infiniivision Oscilloscope Teardown">148</a></sup> Conversely a small FPGA with only two VCC pins plus VCCIO does not need much decoupling at all.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1323" title="EEVblog #1323 - PCB Layout Review &amp; Analysis">1323</a></sup> Vendors publish device-specific decoupling and bypass filtering guidance for their programmable logic, and it is worth following.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1323" title="EEVblog #1323 - PCB Layout Review &amp; Analysis">1323</a></sup>

## Package parasitics and placement

The [[inductance]] of the capacitor and its connection sets the useful upper frequency, which is why unusually wide, short surface-mount packages exist — these are low-inductance capacitors built specifically for decoupling duty.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-859" title="EEVblog #859 - Bypass / Decoupling Capacitor Tutorial">859</a></sup>

Physical proximity is the whole point of the component, and this discipline carries into the [[schematic]] as well as the layout: decoupling capacitors should be drawn physically adjacent to the pins they serve, with a short local net rather than a long line running across the sheet.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1129" title="EEVblog #1129 - Creating a Nice Readable Schematic">1129</a></sup> Whether the ground symbol sits between two capacitors or off to one side is a matter of taste and changes nothing.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1129" title="EEVblog #1129 - Creating a Nice Readable Schematic">1129</a></sup> Decoupling capacitors are not to be scattered across a board the way [[ac-coupling|AC coupling]] capacitors legitimately can be.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-446" title="EEVblog #446 - Dumpster Diving Samsung Plasma TV">446</a></sup> Occasionally the placement is so critical that the capacitor must sit directly on the pins of the device.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-208" title="Tektronix 2225 Oscilloscope Teardown and Calibration - EEVblog #208">208</a></sup> Older through-hole boards took a distinctive approach: bulk decoupling capacitors made as long bars that lay underneath a row of DIP chips, with leads entering the same holes as the chips' own power and ground pins, custom-built for the pitch of that particular row.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-668" title="EEVblog #668 - Mailbag + FLIR TG165">668</a></sup> Generic versions sized to fit under standard DIP packages were once readily available.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-668" title="EEVblog #668 - Mailbag + FLIR TG165">668</a></sup> On boards from before controlled-impedance practice, decoupling capacitors were placed one per chip and laid out in neat repeating lines alongside a single large [[ground-plane|ground plane]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-410" title="EEVblog #410 - Microtek 8086 In-Circuit Emulator Teardown">410</a></sup>

## Regulators, converters and analog supplies

Decoupling on a regulator is a stability requirement, not merely [[noise]] hygiene. A linear or low-dropout regulator — and the charging ICs built around similar internal circuitry — will oscillate without the capacitance the datasheet specifies; the control loop is simply unstable until the recommended [[output-capacitance|output capacitance]] is present.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-176" title="EEVblog #176 - Lithium Ion/Polymer Battery Charging Tutorial">176</a></sup> Many parts are correspondingly trivial to apply: capacitance in, capacitance out, and little else.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-643" title="EEVblog #643 - Mailbag">643</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-158" title="EEVblog #158 - AVR ISP MK2 + LM317 Regulator Tutorial">158</a></sup> Modern regulators tolerate a wide ESR window, in one case roughly 0.1 Ω to 5 Ω, needing only a 1 µF capacitor on the input and another on the output.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-6" title="EEVblog #6 - Part 1 of 2 - Meterman 37XR Multimeter review">6</a></sup>

Where power comes from a source with limited peak capability — a tiny [[boost-converter|boost converter]], or an energy-harvesting element that can supply only the average power — the product's internal decoupling is what actually services the pulse loads, and its adequacy determines whether the rail sags when a motor or transmitter switches on.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-751" title="EEVblog #751 - How To Debunk A Product (The Batteriser)">751</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1383" title="EEVblog 1383 - Human Battery Energy Harvesting BUSTED">1383</a></sup> Decoupling also shapes what a supply-[[current-measurement|current measurement]] looks like: the slow charging ramp visible at the start of a [[duty-cycle|duty cycle]] is the decoupling capacitance filling before the circuit begins its operation.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-662" title="EEVblog #662- How &amp; Why to use Integration on an Oscilloscope">662</a></sup>

## Diagnosis and failure

Decoupling capacitors are useful landmarks when reverse-engineering an unfamiliar board: wide traces studded with capacitors and large numbers of vias are almost certainly power rails, which gives a starting point when probing blind.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1520" title="EEVblog 1520 - Troubleshooting a Faulty BM786 Multimeter">1520</a></sup> They are also a routine early suspect in a dead board, since a shorted ceramic pulls a rail down, and checking every decoupling capacitor for a short is a standard first sweep.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1522" title="EEVblog 1522 - BM786 Multimeter Repair PART 2">1522</a></sup> The failure mode can be more dramatic than a [[short-circuit|short circuit]] alone: a decoupling capacitor that failed short has set a board alight, which is precisely the scenario FR-4's flammability rating exists to contain.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-993" title="EEVblog #993 - Mailbag">993</a></sup> Odd, marginal behaviour in working equipment — logic that half-responds rather than failing outright — can also point back to decoupling on the affected board.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1203" title="EEVblog #1203 - REPAIR: Tektronix 2465B Oscilloscope">1203</a></sup>

## How much is actually necessary

Specifying one [[bypass-capacitor|bypass capacitor]] per chip is time-honoured engineering practice, followed regardless of whether the trace impedances and loop paths of a given layout genuinely demand it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1081" title="EEVblog #1081 - Are Bypass Capacitors REALLY needed? (Muntzing)">1081</a></sup> Whether every one of them is required is a separate question from whether fitting them is correct, and manufacturers frequently leave decoupling footprints on a board unpopulated.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-219" title="Amazon Kindle Fire Tablet Teardown - EEVblog #219">219</a></sup> In circuits where the load is trivial and the supply local, a decoupling capacitor may be removable without consequence.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1681" title="EEVblog 1681 - Mailbag: Nice DIY CC Load,  Amazing Proto Boards, Ian Johnston">1681</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1111" title="EEVblog #1111 - World&#x27;s First Microcontroller &amp; Electronic Game">1111</a></sup> The prevailing engineering judgment nonetheless favours fitting them by default, because the faults that follow from omitting adequate local decoupling are intermittent, localised and difficult to trace back to their cause.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1365" title="EEVblog #1365 - Viewer PCB &amp; Circuit Design Review">1365</a></sup>
