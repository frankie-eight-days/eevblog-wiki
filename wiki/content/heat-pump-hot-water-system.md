A heat pump hot water system heats a storage tank of domestic water using a vapour-compression refrigeration cycle rather than a burner or a resistive element, moving heat from ambient air into the water instead of generating it directly.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1517" title="EEVblog 1517 - Heat Pump Hot Water Install &amp; Analysis">1517</a></sup> Because it moves heat rather than making it, it delivers several times more thermal energy than the electrical energy it consumes, which makes it the most energy-efficient of the common domestic hot water options.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1517" title="EEVblog 1517 - Heat Pump Hot Water Install &amp; Analysis">1517</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1630" title="EEVblog 1630 - Solar Panels for Water Pre-Heating?">1630</a></sup> In a home with rooftop solar, the tank doubles as a thermal [[battery]]: a low-power load that can be timed to run entirely on excess generation, storing the energy as hot water rather than exporting it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-iavM2IqueM8" title="Heat Pump Hot Water System vs Gas Hot Water">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1517" title="EEVblog 1517 - Heat Pump Hot Water Install &amp; Analysis">1517</a></sup>

## Operating principle

Cold mains water enters the bottom of the tank; water is drawn from the bottom into the heat pump unit, and the heated output is returned to the top of the tank.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1517" title="EEVblog 1517 - Heat Pump Hot Water Install &amp; Analysis">1517</a></sup> The mechanism is the same as an air conditioner or reverse-cycle air conditioner — the unit is physically comparable in size to a small split-system air conditioner head.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1517" title="EEVblog 1517 - Heat Pump Hot Water Install &amp; Analysis">1517</a></sup> Delivered water leaves the heat pump at roughly 60–63 °C.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1517" title="EEVblog 1517 - Heat Pump Hot Water Install &amp; Analysis">1517</a></sup> The elevated outlet temperature is not arbitrary: keeping the tank hot suppresses Legionella growth.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1517" title="EEVblog 1517 - Heat Pump Hot Water Install &amp; Analysis">1517</a></sup> Control is by a [[temperature-sensor|temperature sensor]] whose set point determines when heat input stops.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1517" title="EEVblog 1517 - Heat Pump Hot Water Install &amp; Analysis">1517</a></sup>

Modern high-[[efficiency]] units use CO2 as the refrigerant, which yields a higher coefficient of performance than earlier refrigerants.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1517" title="EEVblog 1517 - Heat Pump Hot Water Install &amp; Analysis">1517</a></sup> Coefficients of performance well above four are achievable, and manufacturer claims run up to about five.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1517" title="EEVblog 1517 - Heat Pump Hot Water Install &amp; Analysis">1517</a></sup> A measured installation drawing 2.83 kWh per day of electrical energy delivered the equivalent of 10 to 12 kWh of heat energy — a ratio, not a violation of energy conservation, since electrical energy in and heat energy out are not the same quantity.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1517" title="EEVblog 1517 - Heat Pump Hot Water Install &amp; Analysis">1517</a></sup>

## Power draw and duty cycle

The defining electrical characteristic is low peak power over a long run time. A 415 L residential unit draws about 1 kW while running.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1517" title="EEVblog 1517 - Heat Pump Hot Water Install &amp; Analysis">1517</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-gAu8CvMjDrU" title="15kWh AERL LFP Home Storage Battery - First Analysis">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1634" title="EEVblog 1634 - 15kWh AERL LFP Home Storage Battery Install!">1634</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1651" title="EEVblog 1651 - Home Electrical Switchboard UPGRADE!">1651</a></sup> Measured averages sit slightly under that figure, around 950 W approaching winter.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-wQbCsk7UAFc" title="eevBLAB 141 - Solar Analytics is a RIP OFF!">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-5x8UVZnwyL8" title="New Enphase Solar Power System - 1st Day">•</a></sup> Run time is seasonal: roughly two and a half to three hours in winter and an hour and a half to two hours in summer, for a total of about 2.5 to 3 kWh per day.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1630" title="EEVblog 1630 - Solar Panels for Water Pre-Heating?">1630</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1517" title="EEVblog 1517 - Heat Pump Hot Water Install &amp; Analysis">1517</a></sup> Manufacturer specifications give 3 kWh to heat a full 315 L delivery, varying with inlet water temperature — cold climates degrade the coefficient of performance, which matters less in a temperate location such as Sydney.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-pXtSybs9QRs" title="Instant Electric Hot Water Heater vs Heat Pump Hot Water Tank System">•</a></sup> A single hot bath the previous night is enough to lengthen the following day's run measurably.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1630" title="EEVblog 1630 - Solar Panels for Water Pre-Heating?">1630</a></sup>

Because the running load is around 1 kW, a dedicated 20 A circuit is far more than the unit needs, though that is how such systems are wired at the switchboard.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1651" title="EEVblog 1651 - Home Electrical Switchboard UPGRADE!">1651</a></sup> The unit's nameplate may read 2.3 kW even where measured consumption is about half that.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1517" title="EEVblog 1517 - Heat Pump Hot Water Install &amp; Analysis">1517</a></sup>

## Comparison with gas and with resistive elements

Replacing an old high-efficiency gas storage system with a heat pump cut daily [[energy-consumption|energy consumption]] from 12.8 kWh to about 1.7 kWh — a factor of roughly seven and a half.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-iavM2IqueM8" title="Heat Pump Hot Water System vs Gas Hot Water">•</a></sup> Manufacturer figures for the same comparison give 6,100 kWh per year for gas against 1,100 kWh per year for the heat pump.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-pXtSybs9QRs" title="Instant Electric Hot Water Heater vs Heat Pump Hot Water Tank System">•</a></sup> On pure energy consumption, gas is worse than even a straight resistive element electric hot water system, which needs the full 10 to 12 kWh per day of electrical energy to achieve the same temperature rise.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-iavM2IqueM8" title="Heat Pump Hot Water System vs Gas Hot Water">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1517" title="EEVblog 1517 - Heat Pump Hot Water Install &amp; Analysis">1517</a></sup> Against gas, the heat pump wins without contest on energy consumption.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1517" title="EEVblog 1517 - Heat Pump Hot Water Install &amp; Analysis">1517</a></sup>

Instantaneous electric water heaters lose on peak power rather than on total energy. A single-phase instant unit draws around 7 kW while running, against 1 kW peak for the heat pump, which is precisely the wrong characteristic in a home with solar and battery storage where the objective is to minimise peak [[current]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-pXtSybs9QRs" title="Instant Electric Hot Water Heater vs Heat Pump Hot Water Tank System">•</a></sup> The heat pump's [[thermal-mass|thermal mass]] converts a large intermittent demand into a small scheduled one: 1 kW for three hours fills a 315 L tank with energy that can then be drawn on over the day.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-pXtSybs9QRs" title="Instant Electric Hot Water Heater vs Heat Pump Hot Water Tank System">•</a></sup> Instantaneous systems retain an advantage only under severe space constraints.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-pXtSybs9QRs" title="Instant Electric Hot Water Heater vs Heat Pump Hot Water Tank System">•</a></sup>

Solar thermal collectors were once common on Australian rooftops but have largely fallen out of use.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-pXtSybs9QRs" title="Instant Electric Hot Water Heater vs Heat Pump Hot Water Tank System">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1630" title="EEVblog 1630 - Solar Panels for Water Pre-Heating?">1630</a></sup> They cannot deliver consistently hot water year-round and therefore still require a boosting system behind them, and the roof area they occupy is better dedicated to [[solar-power-system|photovoltaic]] panels, whose output can serve every electrical load rather than water heating alone.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1630" title="EEVblog 1630 - Solar Panels for Water Pre-Heating?">1630</a></sup>

## Integration with solar generation

Units intended for solar self-consumption include a timer restricting operation to daylight hours — one such timer works only between 10:00 a.m. and 4:00 p.m.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1517" title="EEVblog 1517 - Heat Pump Hot Water Install &amp; Analysis">1517</a></sup> Because the load is only about 1 kW, even a poor overcast day leaves an 8 kW nominal array with enough surplus to cover it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1517" title="EEVblog 1517 - Heat Pump Hot Water Install &amp; Analysis">1517</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-iavM2IqueM8" title="Heat Pump Hot Water System vs Gas Hot Water">•</a></sup> Setting the start time too late risks overlapping with other daytime loads such as a pool pump, doubling instantaneous consumption; moving the start earlier separates them.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-5x8UVZnwyL8" title="New Enphase Solar Power System - 1st Day">•</a></sup>

The share of household generation consumed is small. At the start of summer, water heating took about one sixteenth — roughly 6% — of the daily output of an 8 kW system serving a family of four.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-WtGlolw-BxA" title="Reclaim Heat Pump Hot Water Solar Energy Usage UPDATE">•</a></sup> In midwinter the same household's peak generation falls to around four to four and a half kilowatts, and on a good winter day producing 30 kWh the hot water system accounted for about four and a half to five kWh.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-WtGlolw-BxA" title="Reclaim Heat Pump Hot Water Solar Energy Usage UPDATE">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1630" title="EEVblog 1630 - Solar Panels for Water Pre-Heating?">1630</a></sup> The magnitude only becomes intuitive when the consumption trace is compared as area under the curve against the generation trace.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-WtGlolw-BxA" title="Reclaim Heat Pump Hot Water Solar Energy Usage UPDATE">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-5HikvxaQ_Z8" title="Heat Pump Hot Water Standby Energy Usage">•</a></sup>

Deliberately oversizing the tank is defensible on these grounds: a 415 L tank in place of a 175 L one gives substantially greater heat capacity — a larger thermal sink — for little or no additional energy, since standing losses did not correlate measurably with the increase.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1517" title="EEVblog 1517 - Heat Pump Hot Water Install &amp; Analysis">1517</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-iavM2IqueM8" title="Heat Pump Hot Water System vs Gas Hot Water">•</a></sup>

## Cost, reliability and noise

A top-of-the-range installation cost $5,500 including installation, disconnection of the gas service and repiping, against an annual saving of $722, giving a payback period of about five years.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-iavM2IqueM8" title="Heat Pump Hot Water System vs Gas Hot Water">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1517" title="EEVblog 1517 - Heat Pump Hot Water Install &amp; Analysis">1517</a></sup> State and federal rebates of roughly $1,000 apply in Australia, and in some states government rebates bring the installed price close to zero.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-iavM2IqueM8" title="Heat Pump Hot Water System vs Gas Hot Water">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1517" title="EEVblog 1517 - Heat Pump Hot Water Install &amp; Analysis">1517</a></sup> Systems at half that price exist, and rising gas prices shorten the payback further.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-pXtSybs9QRs" title="Instant Electric Hot Water Heater vs Heat Pump Hot Water Tank System">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1517" title="EEVblog 1517 - Heat Pump Hot Water Install &amp; Analysis">1517</a></sup>

Cheap bottom-of-the-line units are a false economy — they break down, to the point that some installers refuse to fit them.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1517" title="EEVblog 1517 - Heat Pump Hot Water Install &amp; Analysis">1517</a></sup> The engineering case for paying more rests on the same reasoning applied to air conditioners: a heat pump water heater is mechanically the same class of machine as a reverse-cycle air conditioner, and units of that kind routinely run 10 to 20 years without failure.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1517" title="EEVblog 1517 - Heat Pump Hot Water Install &amp; Analysis">1517</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1630" title="EEVblog 1630 - Solar Panels for Water Pre-Heating?">1630</a></sup> Domestic units are quiet, rated around 36 dB and barely audible standing directly in front of one at full output; the [[noise]] objection applies to the much larger pool heat pumps, not to hot water units.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1517" title="EEVblog 1517 - Heat Pump Hot Water Install &amp; Analysis">1517</a></sup>

## Measurement caveat

Third-party energy monitoring platforms have misreported this load. One reported 79 to 85 W for a heat pump hot water system that actually draws about 1 kW — an order of magnitude error, mirrored on the same platform by a pool heat pump reported at 200 W and an EV charger reported at 400 to 500 W, below the vehicle's minimum charge rate.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-vDtxmikcYuc" title="Who Screwed With The Solar Analytics Code?">•</a></sup> Verifying a claimed daily consumption figure needs no elaborate instrumentation: a kilowatt-hour-per-day number from a simple mains energy logger is sufficient.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-iavM2IqueM8" title="Heat Pump Hot Water System vs Gas Hot Water">•</a></sup>
