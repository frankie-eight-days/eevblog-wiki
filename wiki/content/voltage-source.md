A voltage source is a circuit element that establishes a potential difference across its terminals, and along with the [[current]] source it forms one of the two primitive energy sources of DC circuit theory.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1397" title="EEVblog 1397 - DC Fundamentals Part 2: DC Voltage &amp; Current Sources (Thevenin &amp; Norton Theorems)">1397</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1688" title="EEVblog 1688 - Constant Current Sources EXPLAINED + DEMO">1688</a></sup> It sits immediately after [[voltage]], current and [[resistance]] in the order in which electronics is usually learned, and it underpins Thevenin and Norton equivalents, mesh and nodal analysis, and the maximum power transfer theorem.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1397" title="EEVblog 1397 - DC Fundamentals Part 2: DC Voltage &amp; Current Sources (Thevenin &amp; Norton Theorems)">1397</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1401" title="EEVblog 1401 - DC Fundamentals Part 6: DC Power, Efficiency, &amp; Maximum Power Transfer Theory">1401</a></sup> The single most consequential fact about it is negative: the ideal voltage source of the [[schematic]] symbol does not exist, and every real one carries an internal series resistance that must be accounted for.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1397" title="EEVblog 1397 - DC Fundamentals Part 2: DC Voltage &amp; Current Sources (Thevenin &amp; Norton Theorems)">1397</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1401" title="EEVblog 1401 - DC Fundamentals Part 6: DC Power, Efficiency, &amp; Maximum Power Transfer Theory">1401</a></sup>

## Symbol and physical realisations

The conventional symbol is a circle enclosing the letter V, with the voltage value written alongside and the positive terminal marked at the top; the alternative is the [[battery]] symbol, whose stacked long and short bars denote a multi-cell battery.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1688" title="EEVblog 1688 - Constant Current Sources EXPLAINED + DEMO">1688</a></sup> Either way the element represents a potential source of energy.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1688" title="EEVblog 1688 - Constant Current Sources EXPLAINED + DEMO">1688</a></sup>

The category is defined by behaviour rather than construction, and the physical realisations are diverse: an electrochemical battery, a linear or switch-mode [[power-supply]], a lab supply, a [[solar-cell]], a Peltier device, a generator, or triboelectric effects.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1397" title="EEVblog 1397 - DC Fundamentals Part 2: DC Voltage &amp; Current Sources (Thevenin &amp; Norton Theorems)">1397</a></sup> Generators push the discussion into AC, which is treated separately from the DC case.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1397" title="EEVblog 1397 - DC Fundamentals Part 2: DC Voltage &amp; Current Sources (Thevenin &amp; Norton Theorems)">1397</a></sup>

## Non-ideality and source resistance

An ideal voltage source would hold its terminal voltage at any current draw. Because no such source exists, the correct model of a practical source is the ideal element in series with a [[resistor]], and analysis that omits that resistor is analysis of something that cannot be built.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1397" title="EEVblog 1397 - DC Fundamentals Part 2: DC Voltage &amp; Current Sources (Thevenin &amp; Norton Theorems)">1397</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1401" title="EEVblog 1401 - DC Fundamentals Part 6: DC Power, Efficiency, &amp; Maximum Power Transfer Theory">1401</a></sup> The series resistance is conventionally labelled RS for source resistance.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1401" title="EEVblog 1401 - DC Fundamentals Part 6: DC Power, Efficiency, &amp; Maximum Power Transfer Theory">1401</a></sup>

This internal resistance is what makes the maximum power transfer theorem non-trivial: with a source of fixed EMF and fixed RS driving a load resistance, there is a specific load value that extracts the greatest power from the source.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1401" title="EEVblog 1401 - DC Fundamentals Part 6: DC Power, Efficiency, &amp; Maximum Power Transfer Theory">1401</a></sup> The same quantity appears in simulation as an explicit parameter — a DC voltage source in LTspice exposes a series resistance field among its parametric entries, which is how a real cell is represented.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-516" title="EEVblog #516 - LTSPICE Tutorial - DC Operating Point Analysis">516</a></sup> A CR2032 coin cell, for instance, may carry something on the order of 10 ohms of series resistance within the cell itself.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-516" title="EEVblog #516 - LTSPICE Tutorial - DC Operating Point Analysis">516</a></sup>

## Behaviour in circuit analysis

In superposition and equivalent-circuit work, a voltage source is idealised to zero internal resistance and replaced by a [[short-circuit]] while another source is considered; a current source in the same position is replaced by an open circuit instead.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-820" title="EEVblog #820 - DC Fundamentals Part 5: Mesh &amp; Nodal Circuit Analysis Tutorial">820</a></sup> Where a network contains more than two sources, every source other than the one under consideration must be replaced by its internal resistance in this way.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-820" title="EEVblog #820 - DC Fundamentals Part 5: Mesh &amp; Nodal Circuit Analysis Tutorial">820</a></sup>

Sign convention follows the assumed current direction rather than the physical identity of the part. Traversing a source in which the current flows from the negative terminal to the positive, the source is generating voltage in the circuit and enters the loop equation as positive.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-820" title="EEVblog #820 - DC Fundamentals Part 5: Mesh &amp; Nodal Circuit Analysis Tutorial">820</a></sup> The same battery traversed with current flowing from positive to negative acts as a [[voltage-drop]], purely as a consequence of the arbitrary current direction chosen at the start.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-820" title="EEVblog #820 - DC Fundamentals Part 5: Mesh &amp; Nodal Circuit Analysis Tutorial">820</a></sup> Kirchhoff's voltage law can therefore be stated either as the sum of applied voltages equalling the sum of the voltage drops, or as a loop sum equal to zero — a source is an applied voltage or a drop depending on which side of the equation it lands.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-819" title="EEVBlog #819 - DC Fundamentals Part 4: Kirchhoff&#x27;s Laws Tutorial">819</a></sup>

A canonical worked network is two voltage sources labelled E1 and E2, at 10 V and 1 V, with three resistors of differing values, solved for the current through R2.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-820" title="EEVblog #820 - DC Fundamentals Part 5: Mesh &amp; Nodal Circuit Analysis Tutorial">820</a></sup>

## Converting to a constant current source

A voltage source is the starting point for a [[constant-current-source]] rather than its opposite: adding external regulating circuitry around a voltage source converts it into one.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1688" title="EEVblog 1688 - Constant Current Sources EXPLAINED + DEMO">1688</a></sup> The voltage source behind the regulating circuitry sets the [[compliance-voltage]], the headroom within which the constant current circuit can maintain its programmed current.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1688" title="EEVblog 1688 - Constant Current Sources EXPLAINED + DEMO">1688</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1397" title="EEVblog 1397 - DC Fundamentals Part 2: DC Voltage &amp; Current Sources (Thevenin &amp; Norton Theorems)">1397</a></sup>

## Driving loads and bench practice

A low-[[impedance]] voltage source must not be connected directly across a non-linear element such as an [[led]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1427" title="EEVblog 1427 - An INFURIATING Electronics Exam Question!">1427</a></sup> Impedance can be inferred from the operating point — a part driving 20 milliamps must be at least reasonably low impedance — and the prohibition follows from the load's non-linearity, not from any current rating.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1427" title="EEVblog 1427 - An INFURIATING Electronics Exam Question!">1427</a></sup>

Voltage sources are routinely embedded in test fixtures and instruments as stimulus. An automated [[pcb]] panel test jig can carry a selectable 3 V and 2.6 V source purely to exercise a low-battery detector, alongside a [[current-shunt|current sense shunt]] to read the test current of each board as it is switched on.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-552" title="EEVblog #552 - DFM Automated PCB Panel Testing">552</a></sup> Electrometer-class instruments include a built-in voltage generator for powering the device under test and for extending [[resistance-measurement]], in one case up to 103 volts maximum.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1017" title="EEVblog #1017 - Enter The World Of Atto Amps">1017</a></sup> A femtoammeter similarly provides a voltage source able to drive its ADC input, which is what enables resistance measurement on the instrument.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1755" title="EEVblog 1755 - Tolicore Femtoammeter TEARDOWN + REVERSE ENGINEER">1755</a></sup>

In simulation the same technique isolates faults: substituting a voltage source directly on a control node such as a regulator's set pin, in place of the surrounding loop, reproduces what the real supply circuit does while removing the loop as a suspect.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-260" title="EEVblog #260 - Tracking Pre-Regulator Simulation in LTspice - PSU Part 13">260</a></sup>

## Precision references

A [[voltage-reference]] standard is a voltage source built for accuracy rather than power delivery, and the market divides sharply. Inexpensive imported units offer only fixed taps — typically 10 volts, 5 volts and 1 volt — with no adjustment and no communications port, whereas adjustable designs allow every digit to be set down to the sixth.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-a4Xpsenpd6E" title="The Amp Hour #643 - Calibration &amp; Repair with Ian Johnston">•</a></sup>

Such references are also vulnerable to what is connected to them. A [[multimeter]] left in ohms mode sources its own voltage and current into whatever it is plugged into, and connecting one in that state to a metrology-grade 10 V standard shifted the standard's output to 10.470 volts.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-ZYC763Vx9O8" title="eevBLAB #6 - Don&#x27;t Assume It&#x27;s Faulty">•</a></sup>
