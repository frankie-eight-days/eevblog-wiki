The fast Fourier transform is the algorithm that converts a captured time-domain record into a [[frequency]]-domain display, and on an [[oscilloscope]] it appears as a math function that turns the instrument into an approximate spectrum analyser.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-662" title="EEVblog #662- How &amp; Why to use Integration on an Oscilloscope">662</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-845" title="EEVblog #845 - Oscilloscope FFT Comparison">845</a></sup> It rests on Fourier's theorem, which holds that any wave shape whatsoever is composed of sine waves, so a square wave is in fact a sum of sine waves at many different frequencies.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1417" title="EEVblog 1417 - AC Basics Tutorial Part 1: Alternating Current">1417</a></sup> Practically every digital storage oscilloscope on the market carries an FFT capability, alongside the other math operators — add, subtract, multiply, divide, differentiate, integrate, square, square root, absolute value, logarithms and low-pass filtering.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-199" title="Tektronix Mixed Domain Oscilloscope MDO4000 Review - EEVblog #199">199</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-662" title="EEVblog #662- How &amp; Why to use Integration on an Oscilloscope">662</a></sup>

## How it works in an instrument

A scope with software FFT captures the entire [[waveform]] record, which may extend well off the screen depending on the [[memory-depth]], processes it, and displays the resulting frequency domain.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-199" title="Tektronix Mixed Domain Oscilloscope MDO4000 Review - EEVblog #199">199</a></sup> The same architecture underlies dedicated dynamic signal analysers: front-end gain, a wide dynamic range analogue-to-digital converter, a signal source, and then digitising and FFT processing on the result — which is why so many of them are now simply a small box on a [[usb]] port driving a PC.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-523" title="EEVblog #523 - REPAIR: HP 35660A Dynamic Signal Analyser">523</a></sup> Inside a spectrum analyser the transform typically happens in the same [[fpga]] that performs the Hilbert transform, filtering, video [[bandwidth]], log and detector stages.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-892" title="EEVblog #892 - Siglent SSA3021X Spectrum Analyser Teardown">892</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1109" title="EEVblog #1109 - Spectrum Analyser Design Walk-through">1109</a></sup>

The processing load is real enough that instrument designers throw dedicated silicon at it. The Rigol DS1052E uses an Analog Devices Blackfin DSP because a DSP is far quicker at processing FFTs than a general-purpose processor.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-37" title="EEVblog #37 - Rigol DS1052E Oscilloscope Teardown">37</a></sup> An ultrasound machine's sample space processor handles FFTs along with Doppler colour imaging and sampling.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1314" title="EEVblog #1314 - Ultrasound Machine Teardown!">1314</a></sup> Modern mid-range scopes lean on FPGAs: the Xilinx Zynq in the Siglent SDS1104X-E and SDS1202X-E is what makes a one-megapoint FFT possible at a $399–$499 price point.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1042" title="EEVblog #1042 - Siglent&#x27;s $499 SDS1104X-E 4CH Oscilloscope Teardown">1042</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-985" title="EEVblog #985 - Siglent SDS1202X-E Oscilloscope Teardown">985</a></sup> Keysight instead does it in the MegaZoom [[asic]], where the 64K-point FFT runs entirely in real time in hardware alongside hardware protocol decoding.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-976" title="EEVblog #976 - Keysight 1000 X-Series Oscilloscope Teardown">976</a></sup>

## Point count and frequency resolution

The number of points, equivalently the number of frequency bins, sets how finely the spectrum can be discriminated.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-845" title="EEVblog #845 - Oscilloscope FFT Comparison">845</a></sup> The spread across instruments is wide: 8K points on the Teledyne LeCroy WaveJet Touch 354, 64K on the Keysight InfiniiVision series, 128K on the Siglent SDS1104X-U, one megapoint on the GW Instek GDS-1104B, the Siglent SDS1104X-E, the Rigol MSO5000 and the Rigol DHO800, and two megapoints on the Siglent SDS5000X and the Tektronix MDO3000.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-845" title="EEVblog #845 - Oscilloscope FFT Comparison">845</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1188" title="EEVblog #1188 - $10 DIY EMC Probe using Scope FFT">1188</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1355" title="EEVblog #1355 - NEW Siglent SDS1104X-U $399 4CH Oscilloscope Teardown">1355</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-824" title="EEVblog #824 - GW Instek GDS-1000B Oscilloscope Teardown">824</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1042" title="EEVblog #1042 - Siglent&#x27;s $499 SDS1104X-E 4CH Oscilloscope Teardown">1042</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1146" title="EEVblog #1146 - New Rigol MSO5000 Oscilloscope">1146</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1566" title="EEVblog 1566 - Rigol DHO800 12 Bit Oscilloscope FULL REVIEW">1566</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1220" title="EEVblog #1220 - Siglent SDS5000X 1GHz Oscilloscope Review">1220</a></sup> An 8K-point result is effectively unusable — no amount of tuning the settings recovers a legible spectrum from it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-845" title="EEVblog #845 - Oscilloscope FFT Comparison">845</a></sup> Some instruments do not publish the figure at all, in which case the only clue is how slowly the display updates.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-845" title="EEVblog #845 - Oscilloscope FFT Comparison">845</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1218" title="EEVblog #1218 - 1GHz Oscilloscope Spec Shootout">1218</a></sup>

The same trade-off appears on dedicated analysers as a line count. On the HP 35660A dynamic signal analyser, a 102.4 kHz span divided across 400 lines of resolution means measurements can only be taken at those discrete frequency points, which is visible as sparse data at the low end of a log-scaled X axis.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-528" title="EEVblog #528 - Opamp Input Noise Voltage Tutorial">528</a></sup>

## Speed, and why it depends on the hardware

Point count and update speed pull against each other. The Tektronix MDO3000 computing two million points is markedly slow, while the GW Instek doing one million was more than twice as fast.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-845" title="EEVblog #845 - Oscilloscope FFT Comparison">845</a></sup> Keysight's 64K-point FFTs are dwarfed on paper by the two-megapoint Siglent and one-megapoint Rigol, but because they are hardware-based they are far quicker, which makes the Keysight the likely choice where fastest updating matters more than pure resolution.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1218" title="EEVblog #1218 - 1GHz Oscilloscope Spec Shootout">1218</a></sup>

Where the FFT runs decides what happens to the [[waveform-update-rate]] when it is switched on. On a scope that does the work in an ASIC, it does not matter what is enabled — FFTs, cursors, measurements, other processing — because the main processor never handles it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1478" title="EEVblog 1478 - Waveform Update Rate Shootout - Tek 2 Series vs Others">1478</a></sup> A Keysight instrument with statistics, histograms and FFT all on at once still holds a million waveform updates per second.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1638" title="EEVblog 1638 - NEW Keysight Megazoom V ASIC 1GHz HD3 Oscilloscope!">1638</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-383" title="EEVBlog #383 - Agilent 4000X Oscilloscope Review">383</a></sup> The Tektronix MDO3000, lacking that hardware, is fast with nothing turned on but its update rate collapses once FFT or serial decoding is enabled.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-701" title="EEVblog #701 - Keysight 3000T Oscilloscope Review">701</a></sup> On the MDO4000, turning on the math FFT causes the display to freeze repeatedly.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-199" title="Tektronix Mixed Domain Oscilloscope MDO4000 Review - EEVblog #199">199</a></sup> Even a low-end Rigol DS1054Z, though, stays reasonably responsive with measurements, statistics and FFT math running.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-703" title="EEVblog #703 - Rigol DS1054Z Oscilloscope Review Summary">703</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-704" title="EEVBlog #704 - Rigol DS1054Z Oscilloscope Features Review">704</a></sup>

The FFT's own refresh rate is a separate figure. The Rohde & Schwarz MXO4 computes on the order of 50,000 FFTs per second, and the practical consequence is that glitches invisible on the time-domain trace show up on the spectrum — a runt pulse can be seen disturbing the FFT display, where a Keysight at the same centre frequency and span captures nothing at all.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1529" title="EEVblog 1529 - R&amp;S MXO4 12bit Oscilloscope Unboxing and Experiments">1529</a></sup> Enabling peak detection on that display slows the update markedly and the glitches stop showing up.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1529" title="EEVblog 1529 - R&amp;S MXO4 12bit Oscilloscope Unboxing and Experiments">1529</a></sup>

## Setting it up

An FFT result is not independent of the acquisition: it depends on the [[sample-memory]] depth, the [[sample-rate]] and the [[time-base]] setting.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-845" title="EEVblog #845 - Oscilloscope FFT Comparison">845</a></sup> Getting a usable spectrum therefore means working backwards from the desired span. At 5 ms per division with 65K points the required span is unavailable; dropping to 2 ms per division brings it back.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-845" title="EEVblog #845 - Oscilloscope FFT Comparison">845</a></sup> The procedure that yields the best obtainable result is to set the horizontal time base first, then set the centre frequency and span — at 5 MHz per division there is no resolution to be had at all.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-845" title="EEVblog #845 - Oscilloscope FFT Comparison">845</a></sup> On some instruments the horizontal controls continue to act on the time-domain signal while the FFT is displayed, forcing the operator onto separate multi-purpose knobs or out of math mode entirely to change the time base.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-845" title="EEVblog #845 - Oscilloscope FFT Comparison">845</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-792" title="EEVBlog #792 - Lecroy Wavejet Touch 354 Oscilloscope Review">792</a></sup>

One [[firmware]]-level distinction matters more than it appears: whether the transform is computed on the trace data — that is, the displayed data — or on the entire acquisition memory. Switching a Keysight from trace to memory transforms a mediocre result into a good one.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1188" title="EEVblog #1188 - $10 DIY EMC Probe using Scope FFT">1188</a></sup>

Window selection is a standard offering, typically rectangular, Blackman and Hann.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1723" title="EEVblog 1723 - AlienTek DM40 Multimeter/Oscilloscope Review">1723</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-797" title="EEVblog #797 - Siglent SDS1000X Oscilloscope Review">797</a></sup> Gated FFT, where a particular part of the waveform on screen is selected and the transform taken on only that portion, is a Keysight-specific feature.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1218" title="EEVblog #1218 - 1GHz Oscilloscope Spec Shootout">1218</a></sup>

## Interface details that matter in use

A dedicated FFT button that goes straight in is a real convenience, and the ability to run an FFT and another math function at the same time — rather than choosing between them — was a notable improvement in the Keysight 3000T over its predecessor.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-845" title="EEVblog #845 - Oscilloscope FFT Comparison">845</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-701" title="EEVblog #701 - Keysight 3000T Oscilloscope Review">701</a></sup> The FFT can be taken from a different channel than the math function.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-701" title="EEVblog #701 - Keysight 3000T Oscilloscope Review">701</a></sup> Being able to visually turn off the source channel is valuable because the time-domain trace obscures the spectrum, and not every scope allows it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-845" title="EEVblog #845 - Oscilloscope FFT Comparison">845</a></sup> On the MXO4 the number of FFT points per display pixel is set by a ratio, default 1000 for one point per pixel across the capture window, adjustable up to 5933 or down to 100 for roughly ten pixels per frequency point and correspondingly greater discrimination.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1529" title="EEVblog 1529 - R&amp;S MXO4 12bit Oscilloscope Unboxing and Experiments">1529</a></sup>

Rough edges are common. The LeCroy WaveJet has a bug in which the trace vanishes while the waveform continues updating.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-792" title="EEVBlog #792 - Lecroy Wavejet Touch 354 Oscilloscope Review">792</a></sup> On the Rigol DHO800 the FFT keeps updating live while the display is moved, which is unusual and welcome, but the cursors have no FFT-specific behaviour and do not snap.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1566" title="EEVblog 1566 - Rigol DHO800 12 Bit Oscilloscope FULL REVIEW">1566</a></sup> On the Keysight MegaZoom V instrument, moving a fourth channel stops the FFT running on channel one.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1638" title="EEVblog 1638 - NEW Keysight Megazoom V ASIC 1GHz HD3 Oscilloscope!">1638</a></sup> The standing engineering judgment across this class of instrument is blunt: "I generally hate FFT modes on scopes".<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-792" title="EEVBlog #792 - Lecroy Wavejet Touch 354 Oscilloscope Review">792</a></sup>

## What a scope FFT is not

There is no substitute for a proper spectrum analyser.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-143" title="EEVblog #143 - Agilent 2000 X Series Infiniivision Oscilloscope Review">143</a></sup> The mixed-domain oscilloscope makes the distinction concrete: the MDO4000 is not merely an FFT function but carries a true RF spectrum analyser front end, capturing a genuine RF frequency spectrum at each point in time.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-199" title="Tektronix Mixed Domain Oscilloscope MDO4000 Review - EEVblog #199">199</a></sup> A regular DSO with FFT could not reach a 2.4 GHz carrier even with sufficient sample rate, because the sampling time required would be enormous.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-199" title="Tektronix Mixed Domain Oscilloscope MDO4000 Review - EEVblog #199">199</a></sup> On the MDO3000 the 100 MHz model includes at least a 100 MHz spectrum analyser front end, with better performance than the FFT built into a regular scope; an 8-bit converter yields only around 50 dB of dynamic range at best.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-587" title="EEVblog #587 - Tektronix MDO3000 Mixed Domain Oscilloscope Teardown">587</a></sup> The MDO3000's RF path is still digital-sampling and still performs an FFT, but with specific hardware and software behind it, which is why it updates far faster than a sweep-based system despite the scope itself being slow.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-845" title="EEVblog #845 - Oscilloscope FFT Comparison">845</a></sup>

Some analysers offer both approaches: the Siglent SSA3021X can be placed in FFT mode or sweep mode or left in auto, where the Rigol DSA815 has no such choice.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-891" title="EEVblog #891 - Siglent SSA3021X vs Rigol DSA815 Spectrum Analyser">891</a></sup> In a [[calibration]]-grade phase noise system, using a different FFT gives better dynamic range than a normal D-to-A converter and hence a lower close-in [[noise-floor]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1041" title="EEVblog #1041 - Keysight Calibration Lab Tour">1041</a></sup>

FFT is also the wrong tool for some jobs it superficially fits. Obtaining a [[frequency-response]] plot is not one of its applications — the conventional method needs only a [[multimeter]] and a [[function-generator]], sweeping the input and recording values at spot frequencies.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-396" title="EEVblog #396 - Bode Plotting on Your Osciloscope">396</a></sup>

## Applications

An FFT display turns a scope into a workable EMC troubleshooting setup when paired with a home-made near-field probe costing about $10, removing the need for a $1,000 to $1,500 spectrum analyser for live probing.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1188" title="EEVblog #1188 - $10 DIY EMC Probe using Scope FFT">1188</a></sup> Doing this well requires a fast FFT mode with a high point count and real-time updating; a slow, low-resolution implementation can be pressed into service but is far less pleasant.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1188" title="EEVblog #1188 - $10 DIY EMC Probe using Scope FFT">1188</a></sup>

Other uses run from noise measurement — reading an opamp's input noise [[voltage]] spectrum down to DC on a log X axis<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-528" title="EEVblog #528 - Opamp Input Noise Voltage Tutorial">528</a></sup> — to acoustic work, where the spectrum of ambient lab noise reveals low-frequency content filtering in from outside and a ventilation fan adds a broad peak with content in the 500–600 Hz and 1400 Hz regions rather than any single tone.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1745" title="EEVblog 1745 - Continuity Tester Spectral Probing">1745</a></sup> In a Bulova Accutron teardown the transform confirmed a 300 Hz tuning-fork component present beneath a dominant 100 Hz signal that digital filtering alone could not separate out.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-920" title="EEVblog #920 - World&#x27;s First Transistor Watch! - Bulova Accutron Spaceview">920</a></sup> At the other extreme of scale, SETI@home distributed the enormous FFT and frequency-bin processing load across tens of thousands of idle home computers.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-871" title="EEVblog #871 - Find Aliens With Your Raspberry Pi!">871</a></sup>

FFT results are also a diagnostic on the acquisition system itself. In an interleaved-clock sampling architecture, any jitter in the stagger clocks manifests in the sampling and produces errors in the FFT of the signal, which is why such systems demand very low jitter and accurately spaced clocks.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-19" title="EEVblog #19 - Rigol caught with their pants down!">19</a></sup>

## The low end

Below a certain price the function is nominal. The FNIRSI DPOX180H technically shows an FFT but nothing useful can be done with it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1582" title="EEVblog 1582 - FNIRSI DPOX180H 180MHz DPO for $123?">1582</a></sup> The Digilent OpenScope MZ's is rudimentary.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1056" title="EEVblog #1056 - Digilent Open Scope MZ Review">1056</a></sup> The AlienTek DM40 gives a basic display with selectable windows and visible harmonics but no cursor functionality.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1723" title="EEVblog 1723 - AlienTek DM40 Multimeter/Oscilloscope Review">1723</a></sup> The Uni-T UPO3000 offers windows and a split or full-screen view and little else, with no indication of point count.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1231" title="EEVblog #1231- New Uni-T UPO3000 Oscilloscope Teardown">1231</a></sup> The Owon SDS is not worth the trouble.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-480" title="EEVblog #480 - 300MHz Owon SDS Oscilloscope">480</a></sup> Even a scope with 14 megabytes of sample memory can have an FFT that behaves almost like a toy.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-845" title="EEVblog #845 - Oscilloscope FFT Comparison">845</a></sup> The general lesson from comparing implementations across instruments is that entry-level FFT modes are largely toys — a carrier can be detected, but basic FM sidebands cannot be resolved.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-845" title="EEVblog #845 - Oscilloscope FFT Comparison">845</a></sup>

At the other end, the Rohde & Schwarz MXO4's FFT is streets ahead of most scopes and feels like a real spectrum analyser rather than a mode that kind of sort of works, which is unsurprising from a company whose traditional market is RF and spectrum analysis.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1529" title="EEVblog 1529 - R&amp;S MXO4 12bit Oscilloscope Unboxing and Experiments">1529</a></sup> The Digilent Analog Discovery, with its 14-bit ADC at 100 MS/s, does real-time FFTs and can take a transform of just the contents of a zoom window.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-692" title="EEVblog #692 - Digilent Analog Discovery Review">692</a></sup> Reasonable FFT capability sits comfortably within the expected feature set of a general-purpose scope, alongside logic analyser channels and a couple of hundred megahertz of bandwidth.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-ln_XJDPKJlc" title="eevBLAB 103 - Is an $800 Oscilloscope OVERKILL?">•</a></sup>
