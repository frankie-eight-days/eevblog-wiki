Surface mount technology (SMT, or SMD when referring to the components themselves) mounts parts directly onto pads on the face of a [[pcb|printed circuit board]] rather than passing leads through holes in it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1097" title="EEVblog #1097 - Mailbag">1097</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-304" title="EEVblog #304 - Mailbag">304</a></sup> Parts are set down by a pick-and-place machine working from reels and tubes and then soldered en masse, either by reflowing pre-applied [[solder-paste|solder paste]] or, in older practice, by gluing the parts down and passing the board through a wave.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1097" title="EEVblog #1097 - Mailbag">1097</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-127" title="EEVblog #127 - PCB Design For Manufacture Tutorial - Part 1">127</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-907" title="EEVblog #907 - RETRO Teardown: Sony Video Walkman">907</a></sup> It displaced through-hole construction because it is cheaper to assemble, allows far denser boards, and behaves better electrically — and because at volume, through-hole assembly simply cannot compete.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-127" title="EEVblog #127 - PCB Design For Manufacture Tutorial - Part 1">127</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-375" title="EEVblog #375 - Sony Video 8 Camcorder Teardown">375</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-304" title="EEVblog #304 - Mailbag">304</a></sup>

## Manufacturing economics

A through-hole board does not cut it for volume manufacture: it is too expensive to assemble, and cheap labour does not close the gap with surface mount.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-127" title="EEVblog #127 - PCB Design For Manufacture Tutorial - Part 1">127</a></sup> On a through-hole line, workers sit and install components by hand, which costs time, effort and labour; a pick-and-place machine feeding from reels and tubes churns out boards far faster with less effort.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-127" title="EEVblog #127 - PCB Design For Manufacture Tutorial - Part 1">127</a></sup> The design goal is therefore to go entirely surface mount, because that saves the most cost and cuts assembly time.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-127" title="EEVblog #127 - PCB Design For Manufacture Tutorial - Part 1">127</a></sup> Converting an existing through-hole design can amount to a near-total redesign, but for production runs above roughly fifty boards the up-front effort pays for itself.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-127" title="EEVblog #127 - PCB Design For Manufacture Tutorial - Part 1">127</a></sup>

The saving is undermined by any part that still has to be handled by a human. A design that is otherwise surface mount but retains through-hole switches, binding posts and 4 mm banana plugs still needs those parts hand soldered; selective [[wave-soldering|wave soldering]] the switches is possible but unlikely to be worth the trouble when other parts must be hand soldered anyway.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-239" title="EEVblog #239 - PCB Design For Manufacture Part 2">239</a></sup> Reels also require overrun — order around 10% more parts, or fifty extra passives, rather than an exact count.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-239" title="EEVblog #239 - PCB Design For Manufacture Part 2">239</a></sup>

Reflow assembly is highly repeatable once the thermal profile is right, approaching 100% yield on the [[soldering]] itself; the failures that remain tend to come from the human steps still present in the process, such as a fastener not tightened properly.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-588" title="EEVblog #588 - How To Do PCB Production Testing">588</a></sup> The equipment is the barrier: keeping up with the latest surface mount board loading technology is very expensive and constantly changing, which is a standing argument for using a contract manufacturer instead of an in-house line.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1032" title="EEVblog #1032 Part 5 - John Kenny Keysight Interview">1032</a></sup> Where in-house SMT lines were retained, the labour economics are stark — two optimised lines can be run by an average of about three staff, and SMT is credited with keeping local assembly competitive against outsourcing: "SMT is the thing that's kept Australia still in the game".<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-684" title="EEVblog #684 - Ness SMT Manufacturing &amp; Assembly Factory Tour">684</a></sup>

## Placement, reflow and wave soldering

Even connectors are drawn into the process. Pin headers carrying small pads on top are surface mount parts placed by the machine like any other component and then reflow soldered, rather than being inserted separately.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1097" title="EEVblog #1097 - Mailbag">1097</a></sup>

Early surface mount work was predominantly wave soldered rather than reflowed. The components were held to the underside of the board with dabs of red glue, and the board then passed through the wave.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-907" title="EEVblog #907 - RETRO Teardown: Sony Video Walkman">907</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1033" title="EEVblog #1033 - Sony WatchCam Pocket Flat CRT Monitor Teardown">1033</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-619" title="EEVblog #619 - Dumpster Dive PABX Teardown">619</a></sup> Wave-soldered joints are visually distinct from reflowed ones, carrying noticeably more [[solder]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-619" title="EEVblog #619 - Dumpster Dive PABX Teardown">619</a></sup> Boards built this way carry large solder thieving pads at the ends of component rows, and the pad geometry itself is designed for wave soldering — an SO8 and 1206 resistors on the underside of a wave-soldered board have deliberately enlarged outer pads.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1033" title="EEVblog #1033 - Sony WatchCam Pocket Flat CRT Monitor Teardown">1033</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-651" title="EEVblog #651 - Mailbag">651</a></sup> The presence of solder thieving on the larger pads is diagnostic of the process used.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1452" title="EEVblog 1452 - Fluke PM3370B Combiscope REPAIR">1452</a></sup>

Reflow and [[hand-soldering|hand soldering]] leave different joints. Paste reflowed on a pick-and-place line produces a fillet around the side of a tab, but not the solder carried right over the top that hand soldering gives, which matters for the mechanical strength of parts such as [[connector]] tabs.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1137" title="EEVblog #1137 - Mailbag Monday">1137</a></sup> Flex-to-board attachment uses a different variant again: a hot bar comes down and reflows the solder directly, pressing a chip-on-flex assembly onto pads on the PCB.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-651" title="EEVblog #651 - Mailbag">651</a></sup>

Reflow also stresses the parts. Through-hole components in a precision reference assembly avoid the potential damage that [[reflow-soldering|reflow soldering]] can inflict on surface mount parts.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1496" title="EEVblog 1496 - Winning Mailbag">1496</a></sup> The oven can produce electrical effects of its own — solid polymer capacitors appear capable of generating small currents during reflow, enough to make LEDs across them emit faintly.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1455" title="EEVblog 1455 - Capacitors Produce Current During Reflow Soldering! WTF!">1455</a></sup>

## Packages, footprints and modules

Castellated half-moon pads along the edge of a small PCB turn it into a surface mountable module that can be soldered down onto pads on a motherboard, or alternatively dropped into a spring socket or wired with through-hole links.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-722" title="EEVblog #722 - Mailbag">722</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-592" title="EEVblog #592 - Mailbag">592</a></sup> The castellations are produced by placing pads on the board outline so the router cuts through them, which is easy to design but leaves burrs and ragged remnants on cheap board runs.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1259" title="EEVblog #1259 - PCB Manufacturing Options EXPLAINED">1259</a></sup> The same trick lets a designer turn any small module into a surface mountable one, since a [[soldering-iron|soldering iron]] can still reach the half-moon pads.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1259" title="EEVblog #1259 - PCB Manufacturing Options EXPLAINED">1259</a></sup> It works badly if the module also carries pins on its underside: the board then sits proud, the joints crack, and mounting it demands a much larger blob of solder on each castellation.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1241" title="EEVblog #1241 - Power Up Display Counter Project - Part 1">1241</a></sup>

Package selection has practical consequences beyond footprint area. An SO package is far easier to use and to hand-assemble than a fine-pitch TSOP or a QFN, and is the natural step for anyone transitioning from DIP.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-96" title="EEVblog #96 - The TI LaunchPad MSP430 Development Board">96</a></sup> The modern jellybean small-signal [[mosfet|MOSFET]] is specified in SOT-23 precisely because that is what suits pick-and-place assembly.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1736" title="EEVblog 1736 - TOP 5 Jellybean MOSFET&#x27;s">1736</a></sup> RF transistors and amplifiers in ceramic packages are surface mounted directly to the board, typically with a cutout under the package body and the four leads running straight into controlled-[[impedance]] traces.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-936" title="EEVblog #936 - Mailbag">936</a></sup>

Almost every through-hole part has a surface mount equivalent, and mixing the two in one design is generally an error. A board that is otherwise entirely surface mount but carries a through-hole crystal makes no sense; a surface mount crystal should be used.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1306" title="EEVblog #1306 (2 of 5): PCB SMD Hand Soldering &amp; Assembly">1306</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1323" title="EEVblog #1323 - PCB Layout Review &amp; Analysis">1323</a></sup> Where through-hole crystals persist they are often bodges — a through-hole package with its leads bent over at right angles onto surface mount pads, an arrangement that shears off readily under shock, or watch cans hand soldered and folded down where a surface mount option existed.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-868" title="EEVblog #868 - Keysight U1282A Multimeter Torture Test">868</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-399" title="EEVblog #399 - GoPro Hero 2 Teardown">399</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-860" title="EEVblog #860 - Mailbag">860</a></sup> Other surface mount parts in common use include [[shielding]] cans, board-to-board interconnects on probe PCBs, fuses, coin cell holders, right-angle tact and rocker switches, and DC barrel jacks — the last of which can be mechanically poor, moving substantially under load because nothing anchors the far side.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-521" title="EEVblog #521 - Picoscope 5000 USB Oscilloscope Teardown">521</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1545" title="EEVblog 1545 - R&amp;S MXO4 TEARDOWN. The World&#x27;s Fastest Oscilloscope!">1545</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1635" title="EEVblog 1635 - Mailbag: RF book, Probe Bonanza, Beelink Ryzen 9 EQR6 PC, CueCat">1635</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-72" title="EEVblog #72 - Let&#x27;s Design a Product">72</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-644" title="EEVblog #644 - How To Design Front Panels On Extruded Enclosures - µSupply Part 14">644</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-243" title="EEVblog #243 - Vintage Brick Mobile Phone Teardown">243</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1263" title="EEVblog #1263 - Mailbag">1263</a></sup> Surface mount connector footprints are also awkward to service, since a right-angle surface-mount footprint is not something a repairer is likely to have in stock.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1281" title="EEVblog #1281 - Garmin GPS Repair">1281</a></sup>

## Electrical consequences

Eliminating the lead eliminates its [[inductance]]. A BNC connector intended for the best [[signal-integrity|signal integrity]] uses a surface mount pad rather than a through-hole lead for exactly this reason.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-304" title="EEVblog #304 - Mailbag">304</a></sup> The same logic applies to modifications: replacing an RF bead with a length of mod wire adds inductance, so a proper surface mount zero-ohm [[resistor]] is the better substitute.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-987" title="EEVblog #987 - Keysight U1272A Multimeter EMC Followup">987</a></sup>

The limitation of small parts is [[voltage]] rating. Surface mount resistors run to only a couple of hundred volts each, so high-voltage dividers and grid-connected sensing networks are built as series strings of four or more surface mount resistors where a single through-hole part would once have done the job.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1620" title="EEVblog 1620 - Deye Solar Hybrid Inverter EXTREME TEARDOWN">1620</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1580" title="EEVblog 1580 - MacGruber Mailbag">1580</a></sup>

Density complicates measurement. Fine surface mount circuitry is difficult to pick up with an E-field probe, which needs close direct contact over an individual pin or trace.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-694" title="EEVblog #694 - Mailbag">694</a></sup> Probing two signals at once on modern surface mount boards requires holding both probes in one hand, with a real risk of slipping and shorting something out — an argument for a four-channel [[oscilloscope]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1324" title="EEVblog #1324 - Oscilloscope Reference Waveforms are USEFUL">1324</a></sup> Very fine needle-point probes are markedly better than standard ones on dense surface mount circuitry.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-15" title="EEVblog #15 Part 2 of 2 - Fluke 189/289 multimeter review">15</a></sup>

## Mixed and double-sided construction

Most real boards are not purely one technology. Double-sided load — through-hole parts on top, surface mount on the bottom — was standard consumer practice from the mid-1980s onward and remains common.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-907" title="EEVblog #907 - RETRO Teardown: Sony Video Walkman">907</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-GbADsyp9wM8" title="Failed Lucci Constant Current LED Driver Teardown">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1033" title="EEVblog #1033 - Sony WatchCam Pocket Flat CRT Monitor Teardown">1033</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-504" title="EEVblog #504 - UPS Tutorial &amp; Teardown">504</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-863" title="EEVblog #863 - Sony D50 Discman Teardown - World&#x27;s First Portable CD Player">863</a></sup> Interconnect boards are frequently single-sided with surface mount on the underside only.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1151" title="EEVblog #1151 - Dumpster Dive Yamaha Receiver">1151</a></sup> The reverse mix also occurs: through-hole resistors and capacitors combined with surface mount chips, sometimes because the through-hole passive was believed to be the lower-[[noise]] part.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-738" title="EEVblog #738 - Yamaha DME32 Digital Mixer Teardown">738</a></sup>

Where a design is otherwise through-hole, the handful of surface mount parts are usually those with no through-hole option — an amplifier available only in SO8, a [[voltage-reference|voltage reference]] in SOT-23.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-232" title="EEVblog #232 - Lab Power Supply Design Part 5">232</a></sup> Conversely, on an otherwise all-surface-mount instrument the through-hole survivors are the connectors and relays, and occasionally a DIP that was simply the right device for the job and available in nothing else at the time.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1060" title="EEVblog #1060 - $35,000 DataIO Unisite Universal Programmer Teardown!">1060</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-718" title="EEVblog #718 - Keithley 2400 SMU Teardown">718</a></sup>

## Hand soldering and bench work

Surface mount is widely believed to be hard to hand solder and is not.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-186" title="EEVblog #186 - Soldering Tutorial Part 3 - Surface Mount">186</a></sup> The fundamental principles are identical to through-hole soldering, with a slight variation in technique; a few extra tricks and basic tools are enough to produce first-class joints without much experience.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-186" title="EEVblog #186 - Soldering Tutorial Part 3 - Surface Mount">186</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-183" title="EEVblog #183 - Soldering Tutorial Part 2">183</a></sup>

What changes is the supporting equipment. Vision is the first requirement: a jeweller's loupe is adequate for inspecting individual joints but not for working under, and 0402 parts or 0.5 mm [[pin-pitch|pin pitch]] call for a stereoscopic or [[usb|USB]] microscope.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-180" title="EEVblog #180 - Soldering Tutorial Part 1 - Tools">180</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-590" title="EEVblog #590 - DIYINHK 1080P HD USB Soldering Microscope Review + Tagarno FHD ZIP">590</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-817" title="EEVblog #817 - Mantis Elite Cam Inspection Microscope">817</a></sup> A [[desoldering]] station is of little use on surface mount work, because sucking solder from one side will not lift a chip resistor — heat must be applied to both terminations at once, making a second iron or a hot air tool more valuable than a desoldering station.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-111" title="EEVblog #111 - Live Show #5">111</a></sup> Tip choice matters in the other direction too: the very fine tips supplied with compact irons handle small surface mount parts but have no chance against a TO-220 or a [[ground-plane|ground plane]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1645" title="EEVblog 1645 - $80 Quick TS11 Soldering Station REVIEW">1645</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-QbeTv41l2EQ" title="JBC Nano Soldering Iron">•</a></sup> LCR tweezers, aimed squarely at surface mount sorting and [[production-testing|production testing]], are effectively essential bench equipment.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1471" title="EEVblog 1471 - Mailbag">1471</a></sup> Surface-mount-to-DIP adapter boards remain useful for prototyping.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-573" title="EEVblog #573 - Mailbag">573</a></sup>

Two traps recur in rework. On surface mount electrolytics the black stripe marks the negative terminal, the opposite convention to through-hole parts.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1527" title="EEVblog 1527 - Toshiba T1000LE DOS Vintage Laptop Repair HELL">1527</a></sup> And surface mount tantalums that have leaked should be replaced as a group rather than individually.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1527" title="EEVblog 1527 - Toshiba T1000LE DOS Vintage Laptop Repair HELL">1527</a></sup> Beginners are better started on through-hole; surface mount is too much to learn to solder on.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1069" title="EEVblog #1069 - Mailbag">1069</a></sup>

## Design practice

Laying out a surface mount board is a different mindset from laying out a through-hole one. Through-hole resistors are physically long — 0.4 inches across — and can be used to jump traces underneath, a technique unavailable in SMD.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-244" title="EEVblog #244 - How To Lay Out A PCB - PSU Design Part 9">244</a></sup> Grid handling also changes: an all-SMD metric board carrying one off-grid through-hole part with 0.1 inch pin pitch is a standard reason for mixing imperial and metric grids and for traces ending up off grid, which is why CAD magnetic-pad snapping matters.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-254" title="EEVblog #254 - KiCAD PCB First Impressions">254</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-Ep4r-wD7PPs" title="KiCAD PCB Design">•</a></sup>

A move to surface mount tends to cascade. Deciding to eliminate a separate [[battery]] PCB and put the charger on the main board was enough to justify converting an entire design from a through-hole kit to SMD circuitry.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-259" title="EEVblog #259 - PSU Rev C Schematic - Part 12">259</a></sup> Modernising an old through-hole instrument design with surface mount parts is worthwhile chiefly to tidy it and bring production cost down, while leaving the circuit itself untouched.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-932" title="EEVblog #932 - How Does A HV Differential Probe Work?">932</a></sup>

## Density, dating and repairability

Surface mount is what made miniaturisation possible. Shoulder camcorders of the early 1980s had to go predominantly surface mount to reach their size, and the 1980s Sony portables achieved almost no wasted internal volume through mixed through-hole and surface mount double-sided load construction.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-375" title="EEVblog #375 - Sony Video 8 Camcorder Teardown">375</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-907" title="EEVblog #907 - RETRO Teardown: Sony Video Walkman">907</a></sup> Small details compound: a right-angle surface mount tact switch under a rubber button contributed directly to the shrinking of mobile phone packaging.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-243" title="EEVblog #243 - Vintage Brick Mobile Phone Teardown">243</a></sup> Board count also falls — a design that needed a secondary board linked by a [[ribbon-cable|ribbon cable]] because everything would not fit was consolidated onto a single board once redone in SMD.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-695" title="EEVblog #695 - Mailbag Tektronix 2465 Analog Oscilloscopes">695</a></sup> The parts themselves are so tiny they almost disappear into the board.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-280" title="EEVblog #280-5 - Talking Electronics - Old Hardware - Part 5 of 5">280</a></sup> Where size did not matter, as in older bench instruments, surface mount was available but simply not needed, and designs stayed through-hole and compartmentalised.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1237" title="EEVblog #1237 - Old School Teardown: HP3785A Jitter Analyser">1237</a></sup>

Because of this history, the presence of surface mount dates a board. Machines from 1987 were expected to be predominantly through-hole with surface mount appearing only around the [[lcd-driver|LCD driver]], while a mid-1990s [[processor]] board would be predominantly surface mount throughout.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-382" title="EEVblog #382 - Cambridge Z88 Teardown">382</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-598" title="EEVblog #598 - Sony DME7000 Video Multi Effects Processor Teardown">598</a></sup> A wholly surface mount module inside an otherwise through-hole instrument stands out as a decade or more ahead of the rest of the design.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1410" title="EEVblog 1410 - Mailbag">1410</a></sup>

The transition also ended component-level servicing for most people, which is a principal reason manufacturers stopped supplying schematics in manuals — the move from through-hole to surface mount is identified as the first driver of that change.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1032" title="EEVblog #1032 Part 5 - John Kenny Keysight Interview">1032</a></sup> Repair practice adapted by running the substitution backwards. Through-hole footprints are routinely repopulated with surface mount parts and vice versa: TO-92 transistors and DIP packages converted to surface mount, a through-hole tact switch bodged onto surface mount pads, a [[potentiometer]]'s leads chopped and converted, a surface mount electrolytic fitted where the correct through-hole part was unavailable.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-345" title="EEVblog #345 - Electronics Dumpster Diving">345</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-860" title="EEVblog #860 - Mailbag">860</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-633" title="EEVblog #633 - Mailbag">633</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1360" title="EEVblog #1360 - REPAIR - Aircon Control Panel">1360</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1527" title="EEVblog 1527 - Toshiba T1000LE DOS Vintage Laptop Repair HELL">1527</a></sup> Because a 1N4001-class [[diode]]'s reverse characteristics are essentially identical between packages, through-hole data can be used for the surface mount variety.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1449" title="EEVblog 1449 - What Causes Excess Battery Drain? (BM235)">1449</a></sup>

The technology also attracts a standing objection from repairers and restorers, for whom it is "surface mount rubbish" — denser, less serviceable and less pleasant to look at than the through-hole construction it replaced.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1334" title="EEVblog #1334 - Mystery Dumpster Teardown">1334</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1069" title="EEVblog #1069 - Mailbag">1069</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-913" title="EEVblog #913 - Mailbag">913</a></sup> The objection is aesthetic and practical rather than technical; the same judgement holds that a modern design should be surface mount anyway.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-127" title="EEVblog #127 - PCB Design For Manufacture Tutorial - Part 1">127</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-932" title="EEVblog #932 - How Does A HV Differential Probe Work?">932</a></sup>
