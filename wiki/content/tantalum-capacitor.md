A tantalum capacitor is a polarised solid-[[electrolyte]] [[capacitor]] built around a porous pressed-tantalum slug, offering high [[capacitance]] in a small volume, low leakage and low [[inductance]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-33" title="EEVblog #33 1of2 - Capacitor Tutorial (Electrolytic, Tantalum, &amp; Plastic Film)">33</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-9V99J22aiLE" title="Full Discussion - Film Capacitor Failures with Ron Demcko from AVX">•</a></sup> It occupies the middle [[ground]] between the aluminium electrolytic and the multilayer ceramic: it reaches bulk values a ceramic of the same size cannot, and it is smaller and more reliable than an electrolytic of equivalent value.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-33" title="EEVblog #33 1of2 - Capacitor Tutorial (Electrolytic, Tantalum, &amp; Plastic Film)">33</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-148" title="EEVblog #148 - Agilent 3000 X Series Infiniivision Oscilloscope Teardown">148</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-9V99J22aiLE" title="Full Discussion - Film Capacitor Failures with Ron Demcko from AVX">•</a></sup> It is also the component most likely to be blamed first when an old board develops a shorted rail or a fire, a reputation that has driven many companies to blanket rules against using it at all.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-148" title="EEVblog #148 - Agilent 3000 X Series Infiniivision Oscilloscope Teardown">148</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-230" title="EEVblog #230 - ArduCopter ArduPilot Troubleshooting">230</a></sup>

## Construction and materials

The part is built from a length of wire around which tantalum powder is pressed into a pill, giving a porous tantalum plug with the wire forming one terminal.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-9V99J22aiLE" title="Full Discussion - Film Capacitor Failures with Ron Demcko from AVX">•</a></sup> The tantalum itself is a comparatively rare metal requiring extensive mining, which makes the parts expensive and the supply chain volatile; a severe tantalum shortage once threw the market into chaos, and the conditions for another were recognised as building.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-33" title="EEVblog #33 1of2 - Capacitor Tutorial (Electrolytic, Tantalum, &amp; Plastic Film)">33</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-148" title="EEVblog #148 - Agilent 3000 X Series Infiniivision Oscilloscope Teardown">148</a></sup> Australia is a significant source of the ore.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-756" title="EEVblog #756 - Mailbag">756</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1348" title="EEVblog #1348 - World&#x27;s First IBM Compatible PC - The Compaq Portable">1348</a></sup>

Packaging has changed over the decades. Resin-dipped and axial leaded types gave way to the surface-mount case-size family designated A, B, C and D, which conductive-polymer electrolytic capacitors have since borrowed, producing parts that look exactly like tantalums but are not.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-33" title="EEVblog #33 1of2 - Capacitor Tutorial (Electrolytic, Tantalum, &amp; Plastic Film)">33</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1043" title="EEVblog #1043 - Mailbag">1043</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1245" title="EEVblog #1245 - A Most Excellent REPAIR: IBM PCjr Keyboard">1245</a></sup> Look-alikes are a genuine hazard of visual identification: short, stubby yellow-cased components on a Dale delay-line assembly read as tantalums for all the world despite Dale not making tantalum capacitors at all.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-756" title="EEVblog #756 - Mailbag">756</a></sup>

## Electrical behaviour

Leakage is low, and this is one of the type's genuine strengths.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-33" title="EEVblog #33 1of2 - Capacitor Tutorial (Electrolytic, Tantalum, &amp; Plastic Film)">33</a></sup> [[equivalent-series-resistance|Equivalent series resistance]] is nonetheless significant compared with a ceramic: an in-circuit measurement of a 47 µF part returned essentially its marked value with 7 Ω of series [[resistance]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-81" title="EEVblog #81 - Smart Tweezers LCR Meter Review And Teardown">81</a></sup> [[impedance|Impedance]]-versus-[[frequency]] behaviour shows the same V-shaped curve as any real capacitor, with a 10 µF tantalum resonating around 1.5 MHz and climbing back to roughly 1.75 Ω by 60 MHz — a higher floor than the 10 µF 1206 ceramic it was compared against, but with a broadly similar shape.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-859" title="EEVblog #859 - Bypass / Decoupling Capacitor Tutorial">859</a></sup> Inductance is inherently lower than an aluminium electrolytic's, which is why high-end processors and [[fpga|FPGAs]] favour the technology, and the range of case sizes makes it easier to place the part close to the load.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-9V99J22aiLE" title="Full Discussion - Film Capacitor Failures with Ron Demcko from AVX">•</a></sup>

That finite ESR is sometimes the reason a tantalum is specified rather than a defect to be engineered away. Low-dropout regulator data sheets routinely devote a section to [[output-capacitor|output capacitor]] selection, one such device being specified as stable with tantalum and aluminium electrolytic capacitors of ESR between 0.2 and 10 Ω — a requirement a low-ESR ceramic fails.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1438" title="EEVblog 1438 - The TOP 5 Jellybean Regulators &amp; References">1438</a></sup> A design that avoided tantalums across an entire [[oscilloscope]] board still carried exactly one, a 470 µF part likely mandated by the chosen regulator's stability requirement and by the impossibility of reaching that value in ceramic.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-148" title="EEVblog #148 - Agilent 3000 X Series Infiniivision Oscilloscope Teardown">148</a></sup>

Unlike class-2 and higher ceramics, tantalums do not suffer the [[voltage]] dependency that collapses effective capacitance under DC bias.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-626" title="EEVblog #626 - Ceramic Capacitor Voltage Dependency">626</a></sup> This, together with the volumetric capacitance, makes them one of the standard escapes when multilayer ceramic cracking or microphonics rules the ceramic out.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1037" title="EEVblog #1037 - Solving Ceramic Capacitor Cracking">1037</a></sup>

## Failure modes

Tantalums are famously flammable. The dominant failure is a dead short that develops into combustion, and pulse and [[ripple]] [[current]] are the usual trigger — the parts hate high pulse and ripple current just as electrolytics do, and the data-sheet limits are not to be approached.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-33" title="EEVblog #33 1of2 - Capacitor Tutorial (Electrolytic, Tantalum, &amp; Plastic Film)">33</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1061" title="EEVblog #1061 - Data IO Programmer REPAIR - Part 1">1061</a></sup> Reverse voltage is equally unforgiving: an old dipped 10 µF 10 V part connected backwards to 15 V detonated, and the overload required was markedly less than an aluminium electrolytic of similar rating demanded.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-42" title="EEVblog #42 - Exploding Capacitors in High Speed">42</a></sup>

Thermal environment matters. A 2.2 µF 25 V tantalum in an oven-controlled [[oscillator]] was mounted with its lead physically tied to a power [[resistor]], conducting heat straight into the body as well as absorbing radiated heat; it burned and went short, and the surrounding burn marks made the mechanism obvious.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1139" title="EEVblog #1139 - OCXO Oven Oscillator Repair">1139</a></sup>

The quieter failure is leakage, which does not announce itself. A leaky axial tantalum in an IBM PCjr keyboard drew 150 µA at 6 V; in series with a 1 MΩ resistor that was enough to park a CMOS gate input in the undefined region between logic levels, producing a mid-rail voltage and erratic behaviour.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1245" title="EEVblog #1245 - A Most Excellent REPAIR: IBM PCjr Keyboard">1245</a></sup> The part still measured acceptably for capacitance, and its voltage-dependent leakage wandered under test.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1245" title="EEVblog #1245 - A Most Excellent REPAIR: IBM PCjr Keyboard">1245</a></sup> Electrolyte contamination from adjacent failed capacitors will also take out surface-mount tantalums and the vias around them, which is why all of them get checked once corrosion is found on a board.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1527" title="EEVblog 1527 - Toshiba T1000LE DOS Vintage Laptop Repair HELL">1527</a></sup>

## Tag tantalums

The old through-hole tag type — the coloured blob-and-tag parts of 1970s and 1980s equipment — carries the worst of the reputation, being notorious for shorting, exploding and catching fire.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-jmb9ICnI8xI" title="Dumpster Tektronix 475 Oscilloscope Repair - Part 1">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-9V99J22aiLE" title="Full Discussion - Film Capacitor Failures with Ron Demcko from AVX">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1237" title="EEVblog #1237 - Old School Teardown: HP3785A Jitter Analyser">1237</a></sup> Vintage boards are covered in them: dozens across a Tektronix 475's horizontal and other boards, banks of them in Compaq Portable power supplies where one did in fact blow up on power-up, and further examples in Fluke, IBM and HP equipment of the period.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-jmb9ICnI8xI" title="Dumpster Tektronix 475 Oscilloscope Repair - ## Fault-finding practice

Because a failed tantalum usually presents as a short across a supply rail, the standard technique is low-resistance tracking: null the leads on a 4½-digit meter, then measure across each capacitor and each candidate load in turn, following the falling resistance toward the fault.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-230" title="EEVblog #230 - ArduCopter ArduPilot Troubleshooting">230</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1061" title="EEVblog #1061 - Data IO Programmer REPAIR - Part 1">1061</a></sup> Readings of 0.14 Ω across a tantalum against 0.53 Ω at a nearby FTDI chip exonerate the chip; 55 mΩ across one tantalum against 300 mΩ further along the rail localises the short to that capacitor.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-230" title="EEVblog #230 - ArduCopter ArduPilot Troubleshooting">230</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1061" title="EEVblog #1061 - Data IO Programmer REPAIR - Part 1">1061</a></sup> The gradient is what matters, not the absolute value.

The method is fallible in an instructive way. On one shorted [[5-volt-rail|5 V rail]] every tantalum was lifted and the short remained, the actual culprit turning out to be elsewhere entirely — a textbook case of being [[led]] up the garden path by a component's reputation.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-230" title="EEVblog #230 - ArduCopter ArduPilot Troubleshooting">230</a></sup> The prior is nonetheless sound: where tantalums are present on a suspect rail, they are the first thing to suspect and often the first thing to desolder, since removing four of them takes a minute and settles the question.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-398" title="EEVblog #398 - Lecroy 9384C Oscilloscope Repair">398</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-9s9LXOBknck" title="Unusual Brymen BM235 Fault">•</a></sup>

Tantalums are also useful navigational aids on an unfamiliar board. Their printed voltage ratings — 16 V, 25 V — indicate which rail they sit on, which helps locate a supply point when only a [[service-manual|service manual]] and no board overlay is available.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1515" title="EEVblog 1515 - Dumpster Tektronix TDS540D 500MHz Oscilloscope LCD Upgrade">1515</a></sup>

## Applications

The characteristic role is bulk decoupling and energy reserve rather than high-frequency bypass.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-33" title="EEVblog #33 1of2 - Capacitor Tutorial (Electrolytic, Tantalum, &amp; Plastic Film)">33</a></sup> In older designs, where one microfarad counted as a large value and 10 µF ceramics did not exist, tantalums supplied all the bulk capacitance around a [[processor]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1512" title="EEVblog 1512 - Why Bypass Your PCB Like THIS?">1512</a></sup> The pattern persists in modern hardware: dense arrays of tantalum bypassing around FPGAs and video encoder channels, 470 µF bulk parts ranged beside a processor socket feeding a power plane, and bulk tantalum decoupling on oscilloscope acquisition memory boards.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1289" title="EEVblog #1289 - Mystery Huawei Teardown">1289</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-720" title="EEVblog #720 - Mailbag">720</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-217" title="EEVblog #217 - Lecroy 9384C Oscilloscope Teardown">217</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-969" title="EEVblog #969 - Vintage $80k NLE Video Editor Teardown">969</a></sup> Large tantalums also serve as bulky coupling capacitors in RF equipment power sections.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-571" title="EEVblog #571 - Sennheiser EW100 G3 Wireless Microphone Teardown">571</a></sup>

High-reliability use is more common than the reputation suggests. Tantalums appear in an implantable neurostimulator with gold-plated end caps, as fully qualified parts rather than commodity stock.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1027" title="EEVblog #1027 -  Implantable NeuroStimulator Teardown">1027</a></sup> In space hardware, where a hermetically sealed capacitor costs 50 to 200 euros apiece, tantalum and ceramic are the practical alternatives, and tantalums or tantalum polymers are preferred on negative gate bias rails and similar lines where an electrolytic would be needlessly large.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-896" title="EEVblog #896 - Space Electronics">896</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-9V99J22aiLE" title="Full Discussion - Film Capacitor Failures with Ron Demcko from AVX">•</a></sup> They are also the standard part for the dying-gasp hold-up capacitor in solid-state drives.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-9V99J22aiLE" title="Full Discussion - Film Capacitor Failures with Ron Demcko from AVX">•</a></sup> A 33 µF tantalum is the single component that converts an averaging-responding Fluke 77 IV into a true-RMS meter, matching the value in the Fluke 87V parts list and the converter [[datasheet|data sheet]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1448" title="EEVblog 1448 - Convert a Fluke 77 IV to True RMS for 10 CENTS!*">1448</a></sup>

## Reliability, derating and polymer types

Used within specification, tantalums are very reliable — in some respects more reliable than aluminium electrolytics.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-33" title="EEVblog #33 1of2 - Capacitor Tutorial (Electrolytic, Tantalum, &amp; Plastic Film)">33</a></sup> The bathtub curve applies, but the wear-out limb sits perhaps a thousand years out.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-9V99J22aiLE" title="Full Discussion - Film Capacitor Failures with Ron Demcko from AVX">•</a></sup> Derating is the mechanism by which that reliability is bought: leakage falls as the applied voltage is reduced below the rating, and the dielectric retains some capacity to self-heal, to the point that a 50 V part installed backwards on a low-[[voltage-rail|voltage rail]] has survived in flight hardware.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-9V99J22aiLE" title="Full Discussion - Film Capacitor Failures with Ron Demcko from AVX">•</a></sup> The terminations, by contrast, take considerable mechanical stress and are a real concern under shock and vibration.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-9V99J22aiLE" title="Full Discussion - Film Capacitor Failures with Ron Demcko from AVX">•</a></sup>

Polymer tantalums change the risk calculus outright. They are not the old parts that blow up; they cannot burn, failing benignly instead, and they combine high capacitance with low inductance and good stability.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-9V99J22aiLE" title="Full Discussion - Film Capacitor Failures with Ron Demcko from AVX">•</a></sup> Standard tantalum technology has also been redirected toward ultra-low-leakage parts suited to wake-up capacitors and low-level energy-harvesting storage in small packages, an application area growing with the internet of things.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-9V99J22aiLE" title="Full Discussion - Film Capacitor Failures with Ron Demcko from AVX">•</a></sup> Meanwhile the pressure on conventional tantalums from ceramic technology continues, and the cost and rarity of the raw material keep the blanket-avoidance rule attractive to procurement.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-148" title="EEVblog #148 - Agilent 3000 X Series Infiniivision Oscilloscope Teardown">148</a></sup>
