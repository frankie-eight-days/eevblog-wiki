Constant current mode is the operating state in which a [[power-supply]] or [[electronic-load]] holds [[current]] at a set value and lets [[voltage]] go wherever the load dictates, rather than holding voltage fixed and letting current fall where it may.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1688" title="EEVblog 1688 - Constant Current Sources EXPLAINED + DEMO">1688</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-200" title="EEVblog #200 - Retrospective">200</a></sup> A bench supply with a [[current-limit]] is really a dual-mode instrument: it runs as a [[voltage-source]] until the load demands more than the set current, at which point it crosses over and becomes a current source, dropping its output voltage as far as necessary to keep the current at the programmed figure.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1688" title="EEVblog 1688 - Constant Current Sources EXPLAINED + DEMO">1688</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1293" title="EEVblog #1293 - New Rohde and Schwarz NGP800 800W PSU">1293</a></sup> The maximum voltage it is willing to reach in that state is the [[compliance-voltage]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1087" title="EEVblog #1087 - UNBELIEVABLE Alesis Studio Monitor Fault!">1087</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1676" title="EEVblog 1676 - Lab Timer HACK">1676</a></sup>

The crossover is what makes the mode useful. A supply set to 0.5 A stays in constant voltage while the load draws 100 mA, transitions once the load asks for a little over 0.5 A, and will hold 0.5 A even into a dead short.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1688" title="EEVblog 1688 - Constant Current Sources EXPLAINED + DEMO">1688</a></sup> Applying a load to a supply set for 1.8 A pulls the terminals from 6 V down to 1.1 V, and the discrepancy between the 1.1 V at the load and the 1 V read back at the instrument is simply drop in the leads and connections when nothing is being remote sensed.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1293" title="EEVblog #1293 - New Rohde and Schwarz NGP800 800W PSU">1293</a></sup>

## What determines the operating point

Which mode a supply sits in is set by the load, not by the operator. With a current source feeding a string of nine LEDs whose combined forward drop is around 46 V, the current setting governs brightness and the 46 V is merely whatever the diodes happen to drop; the LEDs are current-driven devices and are not fed from a 46 V voltage source.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1688" title="EEVblog 1688 - Constant Current Sources EXPLAINED + DEMO">1688</a></sup> Push the load [[resistance]] up until the required voltage exceeds what the supply can deliver — at 1 mA, a change from 8 kΩ to 9 kΩ against a 9 V setting — and the instrument falls out of constant current into constant voltage, after which raising the resistance further simply reduces the current.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1688" title="EEVblog 1688 - Constant Current Sources EXPLAINED + DEMO">1688</a></sup>

The same handover happens on its own during switch-on transients. An incandescent lamp pulls maximum current from a cold filament, so the supply enters constant current at the instant of connection and then drops back into constant voltage once the filament has warmed.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-gqzZHbEfWDU" title="KoradFollowup Charles">•</a></sup> A supply set to 2 A driving a 3 A load sits at 0.12 V out while delivering its 2 A.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1030" title="EEVblog #1030 - $20 DIY Bench Power Supply!">1030</a></sup>

## Output capacitance

The dominant design constraint on any supply with a current limit is output [[capacitance]]. Any bulk capacitance on the output terminals sits downstream of the current-sensing loop, so at the moment the supply transitions into constant current that stored energy dumps straight into the load, over and above the set current, before the loop has any chance to react.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-511" title="EEVblog #511 - Rigol DP832 Power Supply Teardown">511</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-224" title="EEVblog #224 - Lab Power Supply Design - Part 3">224</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1691" title="EEVblog 1691 - Uni-T UDP6731 360W Bench PSU REVIEW">1691</a></sup> The design goal for a lab supply with constant current capability is therefore to minimise output capacitance while still keeping ripple and loop stability acceptable — not to fit the largest [[capacitor]] that improves transient response.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-511" title="EEVblog #511 - Rigol DP832 Power Supply Teardown">511</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1691" title="EEVblog 1691 - Uni-T UDP6731 360W Bench PSU REVIEW">1691</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1293" title="EEVblog #1293 - New Rohde and Schwarz NGP800 800W PSU">1293</a></sup> Fitting a 2200 µF capacitor for transient performance defeats the current limit exactly when the current limit is what is protecting the circuit under test.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-222" title="EEVblog #222 - Lab Power Supply Design - Part 2">222</a></sup>

Instruments frequently violate this. A 1000 µF output capacitor is high for any supply with a constant current capability.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-511" title="EEVblog #511 - Rigol DP832 Power Supply Teardown">511</a></sup> Large output capacitance can also be identified indirectly by how long the output terminals take to discharge after switch-off.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1265" title="EEVblog #1265 - $53 360W Lab Bench PSU!">1265</a></sup> The consequence is visible in interaction with an electronic load: a supply with large, low-ESR output capacitors slams current into the load at the changeover, the load's own overcurrent protection trips, the supply briefly re-enters constant current, and the two instruments oscillate against each other.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1298" title="EEVblog #1298 - $70 2000W Power Supply">1298</a></sup> A load in constant resistance mode can drive a supply into sustained oscillation between constant voltage and constant current.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-828" title="EEVblog #828 - Siglent SPD3303X Precision Lab PSU Teardown">828</a></sup> Conversely, a well-behaved pairing shows the load's loop response holding 1 A without upsetting the supply's constant current mode at all, with no oscillation even at an 80 V setting.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1691" title="EEVblog 1691 - Uni-T UDP6731 360W Bench PSU REVIEW">1691</a></sup>

## Implementation and indication

A linear supply implements the mode with a series pass [[transistor]] and a shunt sense [[resistor]]: the voltage developed across the sense resistor sets the current, and the same pass element reverts to acting as a [[linear-regulator|linear voltage regulator]] when the supply falls back into constant voltage.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-200" title="EEVblog #200 - Retrospective">200</a></sup> The approach scales — a 10 kW argon ion laser supply kicks into constant current after the high-voltage strike pulse and regulates it with a water-cooled linear pass stage.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1381" title="EEVblog 1381 - Argon Ion Laser 10kW PSU Teardown">1381</a></sup> Digitally controlled electronic loads instead synthesise constant current, constant resistance and constant power in [[firmware]] around a fast DAC and ADC, which is also how they generate programmable transients and slew rates of up to 5 A/µs.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-862" title="EEVBlog #862 - BK Precision 8601 DC Electronic Load">862</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1550" title="EEVblog 1550 - Keysight E36731A Battery Emulator Teardown">1550</a></sup> Determining which mode the hardware is actually in can require a dedicated circuit; one design uses an LM321 as a [[comparator]] whose output tells the [[microcontroller]] whether the supply is in constant voltage or constant current.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1561" title="EEVblog 1561 - µSupply USB Power Supply - Part 21">1561</a></sup>

Front-panel indication matters because the mode changes silently. Good practice is an unambiguous annunciator — an indicator that turns red in constant current to match the [[silkscreen]], or a display that changes colour on crossover.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1691" title="EEVblog 1691 - Uni-T UDP6731 360W Bench PSU REVIEW">1691</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1293" title="EEVblog #1293 - New Rohde and Schwarz NGP800 800W PSU">1293</a></sup> Changing only a small bullet dot is not obvious enough to read at a glance.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1298" title="EEVblog #1298 - $70 2000W Power Supply">1298</a></sup> Segments too dim to see and instruments that boot without showing which mode they are in are both faults of the same kind.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-TQcV3ftPLgA" title="Matrix PSU Unboxings &amp; Teardown">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-862" title="EEVBlog #862 - BK Precision 8601 DC Electronic Load">862</a></sup>

## Bench applications

Constant current mode is the standard way to bias a device whose voltage is not the quantity of interest. Setting 5 V compliance at 10 mA drives a back-to-back red/blue [[led]] assembly that a [[multimeter]]'s [[diode]] range cannot light, since that range supplies only a couple of milliamps.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1087" title="EEVblog #1087 - UNBELIEVABLE Alesis Studio Monitor Fault!">1087</a></sup> Programming 1 A through a [[pcb]] [[via]] and measuring the resulting voltage across it with a 6½-digit meter turns the supply into the excitation half of a four-terminal [[resistance-measurement]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-543" title="EEVBlog #543 - PCB VIA Current Investigation">543</a></sup> Testing a [[fuse]] works the same way — 400 mA through a fuse producing 0.3 V of [[burden-voltage]], including cable drop in a two-wire connection — with the supply's compliance voltage appearing across the gap once the fuse blows.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1676" title="EEVblog 1676 - Lab Timer HACK">1676</a></sup> Constant current at half an amp into a flat [[battery]] pack is a way to see whether dead cells or their protection circuitry will come back.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-Cn3DVQGmF9A" title="Dyson Battery Failure">•</a></sup>

## Range, resolution and behaviour under test

Useful low-end range is a real specification. A supply whose minimum constant current setting is 1 mA covers sleeping microcontrollers and idle RF products; one whose current limit will not go below 0.6 A cannot do the job at all.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1402" title="EEVblog 1402 - Rohde &amp; Schwarz NGA100 PSU Teardown + GIVEAWAY">1402</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1558" title="EEVblog 1558 - Mailbag">1558</a></sup> Setting resolution can be finer than [[display-resolution]] — increments of 0.5 mA on a display showing whole milliamps.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1293" title="EEVblog #1293 - New Rohde and Schwarz NGP800 800W PSU">1293</a></sup> Accuracy is checked by shorting the output and comparing readback to a reference: 2.995 to 2.996 A against a 3 A setting, and 10 mA settings holding well within a 0.1% plus 5 mA specification.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-439" title="EEVblog #439 - Atten PPS3205T-3S Triple Output Power Supply Review">439</a></sup>

Transition behaviour is checked with a single-shot capture triggered on the falling output voltage.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-224" title="EEVblog #224 - Lab Power Supply Design - Part 3">224</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1265" title="EEVblog #1265 - $53 360W Lab Bench PSU!">1265</a></sup> Clean behaviour means entering constant current without overshoot or oscillation; a small undershoot on entry, recovering in around 50 ms, is normal.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1691" title="EEVblog 1691 - Uni-T UDP6731 360W Bench PSU REVIEW">1691</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1265" title="EEVblog #1265 - $53 360W Lab Bench PSU!">1265</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1030" title="EEVblog #1030 - $20 DIY Bench Power Supply!">1030</a></sup> Defects show up here. A supply may take 7 to 8 ms to recognise the overcurrent, over-volting the load during that window.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1402" title="EEVblog 1402 - Rohde &amp; Schwarz NGA100 PSU Teardown + GIVEAWAY">1402</a></sup> Adjusting the voltage setting while in constant current should not knock the supply out of the mode, but one instrument jumped out for roughly 12 ms at inconsistent intervals whenever the voltage knob was moved.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1402" title="EEVblog 1402 - Rohde &amp; Schwarz NGA100 PSU Teardown + GIVEAWAY">1402</a></sup> Another dropped its output voltage unpredictably at a 4 A setting into a 50 W load where about 3.3 A at 15 V was expected, and a third refused to hold a 5 A constant current setting, sagging to 4 A.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-315" title="EEVblog #315 - Korad KA3005P Review/FAIL">315</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-439" title="EEVblog #439 - Atten PPS3205T-3S Triple Output Power Supply Review">439</a></sup> Shorting the sense terminals ought to put a supply into constant current; disabling the output instead is a different behaviour entirely.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1691" title="EEVblog 1691 - Uni-T UDP6731 360W Bench PSU REVIEW">1691</a></sup>

## Electronic loads

On the sinking side, constant current is one of four standard modes alongside constant voltage, constant resistance and constant power.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-281" title="EEVblog #281 - BK Precision 8500 Electronic Load Teardown">281</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1023" title="EEVblog #1023 - Rigol DL3021 Electronic Load Teardown">1023</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-Y2rcx4vKxlc" title="Unboxing the Keysight E36731A DC Power Supply, Electronic Load, and Battery Emulator">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-393" title="EEVblog #393 - LiPo Battery Discharge Testing">393</a></sup> A load in constant current is the standard instrument for exercising a supply: set 1 A, or step 1 A for one second and 2 A for one second in a repeating list, and observe what the supply does.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-320" title="EEVblog #320 - Mailbag Monday">320</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1265" title="EEVblog #1265 - $53 360W Lab Bench PSU!">1265</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1023" title="EEVblog #1023 - Rigol DL3021 Electronic Load Teardown">1023</a></sup> Configuration typically includes range, high and low voltage thresholds, and slew rate.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-862" title="EEVBlog #862 - BK Precision 8601 DC Electronic Load">862</a></sup> Because a load in constant current forces the supply into its current limit, the pairing is also the most direct way to find the supply's weaknesses — which is why an electronic load has a reputation as a power supply killer.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-320" title="EEVblog #320 - Mailbag Monday">320</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-828" title="EEVblog #828 - Siglent SPD3303X Precision Lab PSU Teardown">828</a></sup>
