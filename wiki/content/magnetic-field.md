A magnetic field is produced whenever charge moves: pass [[current]] through any wire, any component, any [[pcb-trace|PCB trace]], and a field forms around it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1406" title="EEVblog 1406 - DC Fundamentals Part 7: DC Circuit Transients Fundamentals">1406</a></sup> It is one half of the pair of fields that electromagnetics is built on, the other being the [[electric-field|electric field]], and the two are inseparable in anything that radiates.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-694" title="EEVblog #694 - Mailbag">694</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1176" title="EEVblog #1176 - 2 Layer vs 4 Layer PCB EMC TESTED!">1176</a></sup> For practical electronics the magnetic field matters in three distinct ways — it is the mechanism by which inductors, transformers, motors and clamp meters work; it is the storage medium in which an [[inductor]] holds energy; and it is one of the two things that escapes a badly laid-out board and shows up as radiated emissions at a compliance test.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1406" title="EEVblog 1406 - DC Fundamentals Part 7: DC Circuit Transients Fundamentals">1406</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1176" title="EEVblog #1176 - 2 Layer vs 4 Layer PCB EMC TESTED!">1176</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1193" title="EEVblog #1193 - KiCAD PCB 4 Layer Swapping &amp; Stackup">1193</a></sup> It is also a reliable source of confusion, being described within the corpus as "the magnetic fields is the magic black art of electricity".<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-UStV3zyhgnQ" title="Dave &amp; Electroboom - The Amp Hour">•</a></sup>

## Fields around conductors

Current through a straight wire generates a field around it, and its direction follows the right-hand rule.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1406" title="EEVblog 1406 - DC Fundamentals Part 7: DC Circuit Transients Fundamentals">1406</a></sup> The field surrounding a trace on a [[pcb|PCB]] falls off roughly with the square of the distance, which is why a magnetic sensor head intended to read the current in that trace has to sit directly against the board rather than an inch above it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-296" title="EEVblog #296 - AIM-TTi I-Prober 520 Current Probe Review">296</a></sup>

Winding a conductor into a coil makes the fields of the individual turns add and pass through the multiple turns together, which is why inductors are wound as coils rather than left as straight wire, and why multiple turns is the usual route to a more effective inductor.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1406" title="EEVblog 1406 - DC Fundamentals Part 7: DC Circuit Transients Fundamentals">1406</a></sup> A [[ferrite-bead|ferrite bead]] threaded over a wire is essentially a one-turn inductor whose core helps contain the field and makes it more effective.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1406" title="EEVblog 1406 - DC Fundamentals Part 7: DC Circuit Transients Fundamentals">1406</a></sup> Ferrite cores through a coil extend the same idea into transformers.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1406" title="EEVblog 1406 - DC Fundamentals Part 7: DC Circuit Transients Fundamentals">1406</a></sup>

## Energy storage and inductive kickback

Capacitors store energy in an electric field across a dielectric; inductors store energy in a magnetic field.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1406" title="EEVblog 1406 - DC Fundamentals Part 7: DC Circuit Transients Fundamentals">1406</a></sup> Because that energy takes time to build up and time to remove, inductors and capacitors are what make circuit transients possible in the first place.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1406" title="EEVblog 1406 - DC Fundamentals Part 7: DC Circuit Transients Fundamentals">1406</a></sup> At the instant a switch closes, an inductor has no field in it and passes no current at all — the complete opposite of a [[capacitor]], which behaves as a [[short-circuit|short circuit]] at time zero — and the [[inductance]] resists current flow until the field builds up.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1406" title="EEVblog 1406 - DC Fundamentals Part 7: DC Circuit Transients Fundamentals">1406</a></sup>

Collapsing that field is a way of generating large voltages. The magnetic ballast of an older fluorescent fixture uses the collapsing field to produce the [[voltage]] that strikes the arc and starts the lamp, and an automotive ignition coil works on the same principle.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1406" title="EEVblog 1406 - DC Fundamentals Part 7: DC Circuit Transients Fundamentals">1406</a></sup> The same effect is a hazard when a chip drives a [[relay]] coil: removing the current collapses the relay's field and generates a large negative voltage on the inductor.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1406" title="EEVblog 1406 - DC Fundamentals Part 7: DC Circuit Transients Fundamentals">1406</a></sup> This is a property capacitors do not share.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1406" title="EEVblog 1406 - DC Fundamentals Part 7: DC Circuit Transients Fundamentals">1406</a></sup>

In AC power terms the field is the reactive part of a component. An ideal inductor or capacitor in an AC circuit dissipates no power whatsoever; in a real inductor or [[transformer]] the magnetic field is the reactive component, and it is only the [[resistance]] of the windings that actually dissipates power.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1730" title="EEVblog 1730 - AC Basics Tutorial Part 8: Apparent, Reactive &amp; Real Power">1730</a></sup>

## Relationship to the electric field

Changing electric and magnetic fields create one another, and this is what allows current to appear to flow through a capacitor. No electrons cross the dielectric; the changing electric field between the plates creates a changing magnetic field, and that is the displacement-current mechanism that gives the equivalent of current through the insulator.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-486" title="EEVblog #486 - Does Current Flow Through A Capacitor?">486</a></sup> On the conductor the description can stay in terms of [[electrical-current|electric current]]; once inside the dielectric it has to be recast in terms of changing fields and the energy stored and transferred in them.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-486" title="EEVblog #486 - Does Current Flow Through A Capacitor?">486</a></sup> The relationship runs inversely between the two component types: for a capacitor, the faster the electric field changes the more current flows, while for an inductor the greater the change in magnetic field the less current flows.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-486" title="EEVblog #486 - Does Current Flow Through A Capacitor?">486</a></sup>

Light is made of oscillating electric and magnetic fields, perpendicular to each other and in phase, so that when one is at maximum so is the other.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1439" title="EEVblog 1439 - Analysing Veritasium&#x27;s Electricity Misconceptions Video">1439</a></sup> Wherever electric and magnetic fields coincide there is a flow of energy, calculable with the Poynting vector.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1439" title="EEVblog 1439 - Analysing Veritasium&#x27;s Electricity Misconceptions Video">1439</a></sup> A [[battery]] on its own has an electric field but, with no charges moving, no magnetic field.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1439" title="EEVblog 1439 - Analysing Veritasium&#x27;s Electricity Misconceptions Video">1439</a></sup> Once connected, the charge on the conductor surfaces creates an electric field outside the wires and the current inside them creates a magnetic field outside them, and the combination in the surrounding space carries the energy.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1439" title="EEVblog 1439 - Analysing Veritasium&#x27;s Electricity Misconceptions Video">1439</a></sup> For steady-state DC in a current-carrying wire the electric field runs along the wire in the direction of current flow and the magnetic field points out of it, and the Poynting vector still points into the wire.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1439" title="EEVblog 1439 - Analysing Veritasium&#x27;s Electricity Misconceptions Video">1439</a></sup> With an AC source both fields reverse together each half cycle, so the Poynting vector at any instant still points from source to load and the DC analysis carries over unchanged.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1439" title="EEVblog 1439 - Analysing Veritasium&#x27;s Electricity Misconceptions Video">1439</a></sup> Whether magnetic fields are physically real or merely a relativistic shadow of electric fields is a question the corpus raises without settling.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-UStV3zyhgnQ" title="Dave &amp; Electroboom - The Amp Hour">•</a></sup> Kirchhoff's voltage law failing in the presence of a magnetic field has been a related point of contention.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-UStV3zyhgnQ" title="Dave &amp; Electroboom - The Amp Hour">•</a></sup>

## Near field, far field and EMC

Electromagnetic radiation has electric and magnetic components, conventionally drawn with the electric field along one axis and the H field at 90 degrees to it, propagating in different orientations.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1176" title="EEVblog #1176 - 2 Layer vs 4 Layer PCB EMC TESTED!">1176</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1273" title="EEVblog #1273 - EMC Near Field vs Far Field Explained">1273</a></sup> In the near field the two are separable and behave very differently; at a certain wavelength distance they combine to produce the electromagnetic radiation that is measured in the far field, which is what a compliance test house actually measures.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1193" title="EEVblog #1193 - KiCAD PCB 4 Layer Swapping &amp; Stackup">1193</a></sup>

The distinction that decides which field dominates is source [[impedance]]. The magnetic or H field has a very low source impedance in the near field, while the electric field has a very high one.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1176" title="EEVblog #1176 - 2 Layer vs 4 Layer PCB EMC TESTED!">1176</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1273" title="EEVblog #1273 - EMC Near Field vs Far Field Explained">1273</a></sup> High-current, low-impedance sources therefore generate magnetic fields: heavy current switching, or very fast switching dumping energy into bypass capacitors and the interplane [[capacitance]], produces a magnetic field because of the low impedance and high current.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1273" title="EEVblog #1273 - EMC Near Field vs Far Field Explained">1273</a></sup> High-impedance sources that draw little current — a static 5 V supply, for example — generate electric fields.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1273" title="EEVblog #1273 - EMC Near Field vs Far Field Explained">1273</a></sup> The practical shorthand is that high-voltage, low-current situations are E-field dominated and call for an E-field probe, while high-current situations with large loop areas are H-field dominated at a given distance.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-694" title="EEVblog #694 - Mailbag">694</a></sup>

There is a terminology trap here: H is the magnetic field, while B is magnetic [[flux]] density, and the two are often used loosely as if interchangeable.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1176" title="EEVblog #1176 - 2 Layer vs 4 Layer PCB EMC TESTED!">1176</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1273" title="EEVblog #1273 - EMC Near Field vs Far Field Explained">1273</a></sup>

On a board the governing quantity is loop area — the whole loop, from the supply out through the [[ground]] system, the power wires and any signal path.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-694" title="EEVblog #694 - Mailbag">694</a></sup> A chip drawing large gulps of current around a big loop generates a correspondingly big magnetic field.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-694" title="EEVblog #694 - Mailbag">694</a></sup> On a large board with a couple of dozen chips it is not possible to keep every trace's loop area short, so a majority of them will have large loop areas and radiate, mostly magnetically with some electric contribution, which is the origin of the increase in radiated emissions on a two-layer board relative to a four-layer one.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1176" title="EEVblog #1176 - 2 Layer vs 4 Layer PCB EMC TESTED!">1176</a></sup> Whether a given structure radiates predominantly electric, magnetic or combined field depends on source impedance, load impedance, [[frequency]], the bypassing and the capacitive loading, and the interaction gets genuinely complex.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1176" title="EEVblog #1176 - 2 Layer vs 4 Layer PCB EMC TESTED!">1176</a></sup> None of which makes two-layer boards inherently unusable — a careful layout can approach four-layer performance.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1176" title="EEVblog #1176 - 2 Layer vs 4 Layer PCB EMC TESTED!">1176</a></sup>

An H-field probe is identifiable by the coil at its tip, as distinct from an E-field probe.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-889" title="EEVblog #889 - Credit Card RFID/NFC Theft Protection Tested">889</a></sup> Because it responds to the whole current loop, it picks up not just the component being pointed at but everything looping around the board back to the [[power-supply|power supply]] — a broader and dirtier picture than an E-field probe gives.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-694" title="EEVblog #694 - Mailbag">694</a></sup>

## Measuring magnetic fields

Several instrument classes exist purely to convert a field back into a number.

A fluxgate sensor drives a split core into saturation alternately in each direction, and a secondary sense winding around the outside detects minute changes in an external field superimposed on that drive.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-296" title="EEVblog #296 - AIM-TTi I-Prober 520 Current Probe Review">296</a></sup> Many physical constructions exist, including multi-core types, to maximise sensitivity.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-296" title="EEVblog #296 - AIM-TTi I-Prober 520 Current Probe Review">296</a></sup> Because the field around a trace collapses so fast with distance, such a probe must be placed hard against the track.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-296" title="EEVblog #296 - AIM-TTi I-Prober 520 Current Probe Review">296</a></sup> Instruments of this type offer selectable bandwidths, down to a roughly 2 Hz setting for very low frequency or DC field measurement.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-296" title="EEVblog #296 - AIM-TTi I-Prober 520 Current Probe Review">296</a></sup>

A clamp meter closes a laminated core around the conductor and reads the field in the resulting loop with a Hall effect sensor, which is why the jaws must be closed to complete the loop before a reading is possible.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-UJ6JG4eV0nY" title="BM036 Clamp Meter Magnetisation Test">•</a></sup> Magnetoresistive bridges are used for the same purpose: one such part uses a push-pull Wheatstone bridge of four unshielded TMR elements, giving a differential output linearly proportional to the field applied perpendicular to the sensor package.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1413" title="EEVblog 1413 - Oscilloscope Current Probe TEARDOWN + DEMO">1413</a></sup>

DC clamp measurement is vulnerable to the Earth's field, which is large enough relative to a low current range to dominate it — rotating a meter after zeroing changes the reading, and on a 2 amp range the variability can consume something like a tenth of a 2000-count span.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-dv0B_WqL7w4" title="UniT 210E Current Clamp Teardown + Brymen Comparison">•</a></sup> On that basis a low current range on a cheap DC clamp meter is of questionable value, since the range that is supposed to be its advantage is largely eaten by field-induced offset.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-dv0B_WqL7w4" title="UniT 210E Current Clamp Teardown + Brymen Comparison">•</a></sup> Prolonged exposure to a strong magnetic field can also leave a clamp meter with a permanent DC-only offset by magnetising the core, correctable in principle by degaussing.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-UJ6JG4eV0nY" title="BM036 Clamp Meter Magnetisation Test">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-dv0B_WqL7w4" title="UniT 210E Current Clamp Teardown + Brymen Comparison">•</a></sup>

An entirely optical approach exploits the Faraday effect: a polarised laser beam crossing an air gap has its polarisation rotated by an applied magnetic field, with the rotation set by the Verde constant of air, the field strength and the interaction length.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-263" title="EEVBlog #263 - Extech LP100 Laser Test Probes">263</a></sup> Field strength, and from it the current or voltage on the wire, can be recovered from the rotation.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-263" title="EEVBlog #263 - Extech LP100 Laser Test Probes">263</a></sup> The very rapid falloff of field strength with distance and the short interaction length limit such an instrument to a maximum [[working-distance|working distance]] of a couple of feet, with an optimum distance rather than simply closer being better.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-263" title="EEVBlog #263 - Extech LP100 Laser Test Probes">263</a></sup>

## Shielding and stray fields

Fields escaping equipment interfere with sensitive measurements. A [[analog-oscilloscope|CRT oscilloscope]] is an electromagnetic device with large coils generating large fields, with a horizontal scan rate commonly around 25 kHz that appears directly in nearby measurements.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1329" title="EEVblog #1329 - Magnetic Field Shielding DEMONSTRATED">1329</a></sup> Removing the lid of such an instrument makes the spikes easy to see.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1329" title="EEVblog #1329 - Magnetic Field Shielding DEMONSTRATED">1329</a></sup>

Transformer geometry determines how much escapes. A toroidal transformer has effectively no air gap, the winding running around a single large core, and no air gap means far less stray magnetic field — the reason toroidals are favoured in high-end audio equipment.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-511" title="EEVblog #511 - Rigol DP832 Power Supply Teardown">511</a></sup> Conversely a poorly designed [[oscillator]] coil can radiate a massively wideband field that swamps a small wanted signal from a nearby transmitter, so two such units placed close together can upset one another.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-767" title="EEVblog #767 - Super Regenerative Receiver Problems">767</a></sup>

Magnetic coupling can also be an attack surface. Some safes can be opened by swiping a magnet across the front to disturb the field of the door [[solenoid]], making it behave as if it had been activated by the circuitry; a sufficiently thick door, on the order of 50 mm, defeats the attack.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-8MC93KxPTHA" title="Dumpster Diving Fire Resistant Safe">•</a></sup>

## Data storage

Magnetic core memory stores one bit per ferrite ring in the field within that ring.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-668" title="EEVblog #668 - Mailbag + FLIR TG165">668</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-y3WlxxgZwcs" title="Space Shuttle Magnetic Core Memory">•</a></sup> Writing flips the field inside an individual toroid; because the flux is confined to the ring rather than passing through the neighbouring holes, the field does not bleed appreciably into adjacent cores, which is what permits the very high packing densities achieved.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1713" title="EEVblog 1713 - CRAZY Density Magnetic Core Memory from Space Shuttle!">1713</a></sup> Stored data is non-volatile enough that decades-old cores may still hold their contents if a way could be found to read them out.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-668" title="EEVblog #668 - Mailbag + FLIR TG165">668</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-y3WlxxgZwcs" title="Space Shuttle Magnetic Core Memory">•</a></sup>

A crude inductive ferrite hard-disk head is a ferrite structure with a gap and a couple of turns of coil, generating a three-dimensional field at the gap.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-395" title="EEVblog #395 - World&#x27;s Most Expensive Hard Drive Teardown">395</a></sup> The three-dimensionality matters: the design aims to put as much field as possible out in the y direction onto the platter and as little as possible in x, because the x component produces fringing effects across the track width.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-395" title="EEVblog #395 - World&#x27;s Most Expensive Hard Drive Teardown">395</a></sup>

## Electromechanical devices

A brushless fan motor uses four coils with permanent magnets around them and a ring of permanent magnets inside, the drive alternating the field in a circular fashion to make the rotor turn.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1172" title="EEVblog #1172 - TRIAC Testing (WEP Meltdown Part 2)">1172</a></sup> A moving-coil loudspeaker or tweeter has its coil moving within the field of a permanent magnet assembly.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1076" title="EEVblog #1076 - KRK Textile Dome Tweeter Teardown">1076</a></sup> An electromagnetic buzzer is effectively a small electromagnetic speaker, and an external magnet can attenuate its output by acting on the internal coil.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-rZqfp2XKvjE" title="BM786 Magnetic Quirk">•</a></sup> A rotating coil inside a magnetic field is the classic voltage generator and the origin of the AC [[sine-wave|sine wave]], with the [[phase-angle|phase angle]] of the rotor sweeping out the [[waveform]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1469" title="EEVblog 1469 - AC Basics Tutorial Part 2 - Phasors">1469</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1009" title="EEVblog #1009 - DC Fundamentals Part 1: Voltage vs Power vs Energy">1009</a></sup>

An analog watt-hour meter multiplies mechanically: a field produced by a voltage coil and a field produced by the current coil combine in the disc to make it rotate at a rate proportional to their product.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1446" title="EEVblog 1446 - Analog Watt Hour Meters are AMAZING!">1446</a></sup> A braking magnet supplies a controlled magnetic friction to stop the disc accelerating indefinitely, adjusted either by moving the magnet in and out relative to the disc or, in fixed-magnet designs, by a shunt plate.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1446" title="EEVblog 1446 - Analog Watt Hour Meters are AMAZING!">1446</a></sup> Because the magnet's field varies with temperature, and an outdoor meter box can see something like a 60 degree swing between summer and winter extremes, a strap of magnetic material with an inverse temperature profile is fitted to cancel the drift — otherwise the calibrated braking torque changes and the meter runs fast or slow.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1446" title="EEVblog 1446 - Analog Watt Hour Meters are AMAZING!">1446</a></sup>

A moving iron or iron vane ammeter passes the current through a single coil, and two iron vanes inside develop opposing fields that work against a spring.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1463" title="EEVblog 1463 - Mailbag">1463</a></sup> Such meters respond to the square of the current, so without compensation the scale is markedly non-linear at the low end.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1463" title="EEVblog 1463 - Mailbag">1463</a></sup>

An earth leakage circuit breaker passes both active and neutral through the same core, so that equal and opposite currents cancel and produce nothing; any imbalance generates a field which is sensed on a further winding and trips the device at a defined current.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1164" title="EEVblog #1164 - Xbox Engineering Baptism Of Fire">1164</a></sup>

Buried services can be traced magnetically. With no physical access to the pipe, an inductive-mode transmitter set on the ground generates a field that penetrates the soil — a claimed 20 feet of depth in practice — and couples the signal into the conductive pipe, which is then followed with a receiver, though the indirect coupling costs a lot of attenuation.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1338" title="EEVblog #1338 - Rubber Coated Mailbag">1338</a></sup>

## Near-field coupling in RFID

Contactless credit cards are not really radio in the near field sense — the coupling at 13.56 MHz is a magnetic field acting as a conventional transformer, with the coil in the card as the secondary.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-889" title="EEVblog #889 - Credit Card RFID/NFC Theft Protection Tested">889</a></sup> The card's chip draws its power from that coil and signals back by modulating the load on the secondary.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-889" title="EEVblog #889 - Credit Card RFID/NFC Theft Protection Tested">889</a></sup> Passive cards work entirely from the field picked up from the reader, whereas active jamming cards carry their own battery.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-890" title="EEVblog #890 - ArmourCard Active RFID Jamming Teardown">890</a></sup>

Read range is therefore a function of field strength. The field can be captured on a scope with an H-field probe and observed growing as the reader approaches; a phone-sized reader must get to within about an inch before it can synchronise and complete the protocol.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-889" title="EEVblog #889 - Credit Card RFID/NFC Theft Protection Tested">889</a></sup> A signal remains detectable at greater distances even where it is too weak to establish a connection, so a more powerful reader generating a larger field extends the practical skimming distance.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-889" title="EEVblog #889 - Credit Card RFID/NFC Theft Protection Tested">889</a></sup> [[shielding|Shielding]] wallets accordingly work only for cards placed in the shielded compartment; a card in an unshielded pocket reads straight through multiple layers of the same bag.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-889" title="EEVblog #889 - Credit Card RFID/NFC Theft Protection Tested">889</a></sup>

## Large fields and physics instruments

In quantum computing based on electron spin, the spin is the fundamental microscopic magnetic dipole of elementary particles such as electrons, protons and neutrons, and placing an electron in a magnetic field splits it into two states, spin up and spin down, usable as a qubit.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1316" title="EEVblog #1316 - Quantum Computing for Electrical Engineers">1316</a></sup> The field of a spin can be pictured as the familiar dipole field lines of the Earth, emerging from one pole, winding around and re-entering at the other.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1316" title="EEVblog #1316 - Quantum Computing for Electrical Engineers">1316</a></sup> Making that splitting useful means the energy difference between the two states must exceed the thermal energy of the surroundings, which is achieved at around 0.01 kelvin in a field of about 1 tesla.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1594" title="EEVblog 1594 - Inside a Quantum Computer! with Andrea Morello">1594</a></sup> One tesla is a large field by everyday standards: the Earth's field is around 50 microtesla, roughly 60 microtesla in Sydney.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1594" title="EEVblog 1594 - Inside a Quantum Computer! with Andrea Morello">1594</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1316" title="EEVblog #1316 - Quantum Computing for Electrical Engineers">1316</a></sup> Such fields come from a superconducting magnet — a niobium-titanium solenoid of around 10 henry inductance running at about 100 amps produces roughly 6 tesla — or, increasingly, from small arrays of permanent magnets.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1594" title="EEVblog 1594 - Inside a Quantum Computer! with Andrea Morello">1594</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1316" title="EEVblog #1316 - Quantum Computing for Electrical Engineers">1316</a></sup> Field also determines the charge-transition behaviour of the readout structure: at zero field, at a particular bias point, an electron has equal preference for being on or off the donor atom, giving a random telegraph signal.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1594" title="EEVblog 1594 - Inside a Quantum Computer! with Andrea Morello">1594</a></sup>

In a synchrotron the field is ramped synchronously with the particle energy so the beam stays on a fixed radius, which is what removes the need for a long linear accelerator and allows the energy to be topped up on each revolution.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-836" title="EEVblog #836 - Tour Of The Australian Synchrotron">836</a></sup> An insertion device such as a superconducting wiggler undulates the beam back and forth to produce waves of electromagnetic radiation at a chosen frequency, and a higher field produces higher energy because it bends the beam on a shorter radius.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-836" title="EEVblog #836 - Tour Of The Australian Synchrotron">836</a></sup> Pickups inside the beamline detect the electric and magnetic fields produced by the electron beam itself.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-836" title="EEVblog #836 - Tour Of The Australian Synchrotron">836</a></sup>

A caesium beam frequency standard uses a vacuum tube with a magnetic field associated with it, containing roughly 7.2 to 7.5 grams of caesium coated on the [[cathode]] and boiled off over the life of the tube.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-423" title="EEVblog #423 - HP5061A Atomic Clock Cesium Beam Frequency Standard">423</a></sup> A travelling-wave tube uses a quadrature magnet arrangement in the casing, alternating north and south, to keep the electron beam focused as it races down the tube alongside the RF signal on the helix.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-XqakD0dXdjM" title="Guest Video: Kerry Wong - HP493A 8GHz Microwave RF Amplifier Teardown">•</a></sup>

Because neutrinos carry no electric charge they are not bent by magnetic fields, so their direction of arrival preserves information about where they came from.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1gfM_9EdLSs" title="Antarctic Neutrino Detector!">•</a></sup> Conversely, the absence of a planetary field elsewhere makes magnetometers useless on an off-world rover, forcing reliance on an inertial measurement unit instead.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-896" title="EEVblog #896 - Space Electronics">896</a></sup>

Wireless EV charging systems operating around 80 kHz raise field exposure questions, with flux density in microtesla compared against general public and occupational exposure limits across frequency, alongside the ordinary EMC concern of what other electronics in the vehicle are being exposed to.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1584" title="EEVblog 1584 - Wireless Freakin&#x27; EV Charging Roads! ## Magnetic fields in pseudoscience

The magnetic field is a favourite of technically vacuous product claims, largely because generating one is trivially easy and the claim that a field is being produced is therefore literally true while explaining nothing. A wearable device claiming to deliver unique magnetic fields to the body to induce calm, alertness or sleep does generate a field — at frequencies from 0 to 22 kHz — and the accompanying narrative about capturing molecular signatures with Helmholtz coils, gradiometers and SQUID outputs and then playing them back at ultra-low field strength has no mechanism behind it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1290" title="EEVblog #1290 - Hapbee: Anatomy of a Snake Oil Product">1290</a></sup> Nothing about the field being non-ionizing and non-thermal supplies the missing mechanism.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1290" title="EEVblog #1290 - Hapbee: Anatomy of a Snake Oil Product">1290</a></sup>

A heater marketed on the claim that oxidation of a conventional element creates a galvanic effect which in turn creates magnetic fields of various frequencies, that these fields attract and burn dust, and that the absence of a magnetic field is why the product does not smell, is not a description of any real physics.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1186" title="EEVblog #1186 - Solus Graphene Heater Kickstarter BUSTED!">1186</a></sup> Free-energy crowdfunding pitches follow a similar pattern, with over-unity magnet motors sometimes hedged by the claim that the magnetic field degrades over time and so no conservation law is violated.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-HfqpuhYiR3o" title="eevBLAB #60 - Kickstarter Free Energy SCAMS!">•</a></sup>
