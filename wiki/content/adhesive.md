Adhesive is a structural and process material in electronics, not merely a convenience. It holds components to a board during [[wave-soldering|wave soldering]], replaces screws as the primary fastener in sealed consumer products, bonds the layers of flexible laminates and transducers, and secures connectors against vibration.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-431" title="EEVblog #431 - PCB Wave Soldering">431</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-moa3YBdLepg" title="eevBLAB 88 - Right To Repair Questions &amp; Answers">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1262" title="EEVblog #1262 - Designing a Flex PCB + uSupply Update">1262</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-LrLiQYHJ9gs" title="Creality K1 Max Blocked Nozzle Replacement FAIL">•</a></sup> Because adhesive bonds are largely one-way — designed for assembly rather than disassembly — its use dominates how a device is opened, and how likely it is to survive being opened.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1255" title="EEVblog #1255 - Apple iPad 5th Gen Screen REPAIR">1255</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-672" title="EEVblog #672 - GoPro Hero 4 Silver Teardown">672</a></sup>

## Adhesive as a wave-soldering fixture

Wave soldering a double-sided board requires every surface-mount component on the underside to be glued down before the board passes over the [[solder]] bath; otherwise the parts simply float off into the molten solder.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-811" title="EEVblog #811 - How The Varta 15 Minute Battery Charger Works">811</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-431" title="EEVblog #431 - PCB Wave Soldering">431</a></sup> The glue is applied as dots beneath each package and is visible after assembly as material oozing out from under the component body, often as a distinctly coloured deposit around the part footprint.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-811" title="EEVblog #811 - How The Varta 15 Minute Battery Charger Works">811</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-431" title="EEVblog #431 - PCB Wave Soldering">431</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1091" title="EEVblog #1091 - LED Studio Light Repair">1091</a></sup> The presence of that glue, combined with solder plated the full way up component legs rather than confined to the pads, is a reliable signature that a board was wave soldered rather than reflowed.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-431" title="EEVblog #431 - PCB Wave Soldering">431</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1091" title="EEVblog #1091 - LED Studio Light Repair">1091</a></sup>

Adhesive placement is a designed feature of the board, not an assembly afterthought: [[pcb|PCB]] designers may put explicit glue markers into the [[silkscreen]] to show the assembler exactly where the dots belong.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1216" title="EEVblog #1216 - PCB Layout + FPGA Deep Dive">1216</a></sup> The glue mask profile is set up per board position, so an unpopulated site can still show glue where a part was originally intended.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1091" title="EEVblog #1091 - LED Studio Light Repair">1091</a></sup> The requirement to individually glue down every component is a significant part of why wave soldering of surface-mount boards has been almost entirely displaced by reflow, which is more controlled.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-431" title="EEVblog #431 - PCB Wave Soldering">431</a></sup>

## Adhesive in place of screws

Modern portable products are thin, light and almost entirely filled with [[battery]] and edge-to-edge screen, which leaves no room for the screw bosses that traditional consumer goods and white goods use.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-moa3YBdLepg" title="eevBLAB 88 - Right To Repair Questions &amp; Answers">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-Y0SNOsQOBYk" title="Right to Repair - Random Answers">•</a></sup> Glues and other adhesives are therefore used instead of screws. This is a natural consequence of the product's physical design rather than a deliberate repair obstacle, and it is not a barrier to competent repair, because the tools and techniques for opening adhesive-bonded devices are well established.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-moa3YBdLepg" title="eevBLAB 88 - Right To Repair Questions &amp; Answers">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-Y0SNOsQOBYk" title="Right to Repair - Random Answers">•</a></sup>

Adhesive is rarely the only fastener. Enclosures commonly combine adhesive with plastic clips, so prying alone may release a bezel that appeared to be glued.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-672" title="EEVblog #672 - GoPro Hero 4 Silver Teardown">672</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1263" title="EEVblog #1263 - Mailbag">1263</a></sup> Conversely, adhesive is often used alongside a substantial screw count: an e-reader front bonded with adhesive still sits over roughly a dozen screws into an alloy frame, which adds real production cost since each one must be driven by someone.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1370" title="EEVblog #1370 - Kindle Paperwhite REPAIR">1370</a></sup> Multi-stage disassembly with screws hidden under plugs, covers and adhesive-mounted feet means that opening the product effectively destroys the feet's adhesive, which must be renewed on reassembly if the unit is to go back together cleanly.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1672" title="EEVblog 1672 - JBL REPAIR: You&#x27;ll never guess the fault!">1672</a></sup>

## Opening an adhesive-bonded assembly

Heat is the standard release method. A hot air gun set to minimum temperature — around 100 °C — is sufficient to melt or at least loosen the adhesive bead running around a tablet screen, after which the panel can be pried up.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1255" title="EEVblog #1255 - Apple iPad 5th Gen Screen REPAIR">1255</a></sup> The same approach applies to glued-on displays generally: heating first, then working a suction cup, knife or plastic pry bar into the softened joint, is preferable to attacking a cold bond.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-670" title="EEVblog #670 - FLIR TG165 Lepton Sensor Followup">670</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-sqB-PnvQfO4" title="Samsung Galaxy Tab A 10.1&quot; - REPAIR (Part 2 of 2)">•</a></sup> Devices with waterproofing carry additional sealing glue and are correspondingly harder to separate, requiring longer or repeated heating.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-j5XmxupjTdE" title="Sony Experia Z3 Compact LCD Repair">•</a></sup>

Angle and force distribution matter more than force magnitude. Lifting a bonded edge by grabbing it and pulling straight up will bend the part rather than release the bond; the adhesive has to be worked out progressively from underneath.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-672" title="EEVblog #672 - GoPro Hero 4 Silver Teardown">672</a></sup> A shallow entry angle with a slippery, non-adhering material such as a static [[shielding]] bag can release a security sticker's glue and peel it away intact, where a steep approach would tear it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-799" title="EEVblog #799 - How To Remove Warranty Void Security Stickers">799</a></sup> Adhesive is frequently applied not as a continuous bead but as small dabs distributed along a seam, which must be found and released one at a time.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-370" title="EEVblog #370 - Kindle Paperwhite Teardown Review">370</a></sup> The peel direction is also a mechanical design concern in its own right: peeling a bonded stack applies force that lifts the top layers away, distinct from the shear stress the same joint sees in normal use.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1204" title="EEVblog #1204 - Samsung Galaxy Fold Failure - Analysis">1204</a></sup>

Once the part is off, the residual adhesive must be removed before reassembly. It can be peeled, or heated and scraped away with a razor blade or similar tool, along with any embedded debris such as glass shards from a shattered panel.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1255" title="EEVblog #1255 - Apple iPad 5th Gen Screen REPAIR">1255</a></sup>

## Failure and ageing of adhesive bonds

Adhesive bonds degrade. The perimeter glue holding a tablet's front screen can lose adhesion over years of service until the screen falls off on its own — a common enough outcome that heat is unnecessary to open such a unit.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-sqB-PnvQfO4" title="Samsung Galaxy Tab A 10.1&quot; - REPAIR (Part 2 of 2)">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-o4S-TkTuHZs" title="Samsung Galaxy Tab A Troubleshooting">•</a></sup> Prolonged high [[ambient-temperature|ambient temperature]], such as a device left in a hot car, accelerates this.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-sqB-PnvQfO4" title="Samsung Galaxy Tab A 10.1&quot; - REPAIR (Part 2 of 2)">•</a></sup> Replacement is complicated by the fact that a full custom die-cut adhesive surround for a specific model is generally not available as a spare part.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-sqB-PnvQfO4" title="Samsung Galaxy Tab A 10.1&quot; - REPAIR (Part 2 of 2)">•</a></sup>

Adhesive-mounted flexible conductors are a distinct failure mode: the traces on a flex tape are held to their substrate by adhesive alone, and a pin can peel cleanly off the back of the tape under handling, producing an open circuit that looks like nothing at all until the tape is inspected.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1602" title="EEVblog 1602 - Sony Amplifier REPAIR ADVENTURE">1602</a></sup> Where one contact has lifted, adjacent contacts are typically already starting to come up.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1602" title="EEVblog 1602 - Sony Amplifier REPAIR ADVENTURE">1602</a></sup>

In old laptop displays, the polarizer film is glued down over its whole area and will not delaminate at any convenient point, since a film that was not bonded would have fallen out already.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-afwqRu7W8V0" title="Apple Powerbook 165C Vinegar Syndrome Repair Attempt">•</a></sup> Removing it demands very high, evenly distributed force, and [[isopropyl-alcohol|isopropyl alcohol]] will attack the glue layer and bring it off as a gooey residue.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-afwqRu7W8V0" title="Apple Powerbook 165C Vinegar Syndrome Repair Attempt">•</a></sup> Salvaged polarizer film from other teardowns is generally not a usable replacement because it lacks the proper adhesive backing.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-afwqRu7W8V0" title="Apple Powerbook 165C Vinegar Syndrome Repair Attempt">•</a></sup>

Adhesive also obstructs diagnosis. A glue-like coating over solder joints prevents probing until sharp probe tips pierce it, and unexplained deposits of black compound inside an instrument may serve no obvious insulating purpose given there is nothing on the opposite side of the barrier.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-afErPINq8qc" title="Mini Arcade Game Console Repair OOPSIE!">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1336" title="EEVblog #1336 - DT71 LCR Tweezer Destructive Teardown + Lab Update">1336</a></sup>

## Adhesive inside components and laminates

In rigid-flex construction, adhesive is an explicit stack-up layer: the polyimide insulation over the flex copper is bonded with adhesive, with further adhesive layers between the flex core and the FR4 prepreg of the rigid sections. The flex portion is manufactured separately and the rigid fibreglass sections are then bonded onto it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1262" title="EEVblog #1262 - Designing a Flex PCB + uSupply Update">1262</a></sup>

Transducers rely on adhesive for their working geometry. In a textile dome tweeter, glue around the rim of the moving-coil assembly bonds it to the structure above, and the coil itself is glued to the textile dome near its outer rim — that bonded joint is what couples the coil's motion into the radiating surface.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1076" title="EEVblog #1076 - KRK Textile Dome Tweeter Teardown">1076</a></sup> Fine coils elsewhere are glued in place to the point that extracting one would break the bond wires rather than free the coil.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-0nAtroH_6eg" title="Bulova Accutron II Watch Repair - PART 1">•</a></sup>

Adhesive is a specified parameter in bonded assemblies. Membrane keypad overlays are ordered against particular 3M product numbers whose datasheets differentiate temperature range, transparency and backing adhesive, and an IP66 ingress rating is achieved partly through the choice of that backing glue.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1282" title="EEVblog #1282 - Design Your Own Membrane Keypad! (µSupply Part 20)">1282</a></sup> Thermal adhesive on a standard quad flat pack is strong but will still pop the package lid off, whereas some die attachments are clearly engineered for a more secure bond than glue strength alone provides.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-807" title="EEVblog #807 - Mailbag">807</a></sup> Adhesive is used in implantable devices for battery retention where material biocompatibility restricts what may be present inside the sealed, ultrasonically welded enclosure.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1027" title="EEVblog #1027 -  Implantable NeuroStimulator Teardown">1027</a></sup>

Adhesive is also used as a vibration lock. On a 3D printer toolhead, connectors are glued so they cannot shake loose in service, a precaution driven by the machine's inherent vibration.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-LrLiQYHJ9gs" title="Creality K1 Max Blocked Nozzle Replacement FAIL">•</a></sup> The same principle applies to modification work, where added components are bonded down so they do not move about inside the enclosure.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1519" title="EEVblog 1519 - FREE Your Sonos Speaker! (HACK)">1519</a></sup>

## Adhesive and pad adhesion

The strength of a surface-mount pad is the strength of its adhesion to the laminate, and that adhesion scales with pad area. A 180-micron pad offers virtually no bonded area to hold it to a fibreglass board, so pads of that size on an ordinary board will shear straight off if components are pushed sideways, even on high-quality high-temperature laminate.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1341" title="EEVblog #1341 - AMAZING $250,000 IBM Processor TEARDOWN!">1341</a></sup> The practical consequence when removing a fine-pitch part by cutting its leads is that the blade must be kept at a shallow angle: a high angle applies lateral force to the pins, and there is not enough adhesion under each tiny pad to resist it, so the pads tear from the board.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1322" title="EEVblog #1322 - JBL LSR308 Monitor Speaker Repair (FULL)">1322</a></sup> A shallow angle also nearly guarantees the underlying traces are not cut.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1322" title="EEVblog #1322 - JBL LSR308 Monitor Speaker Repair (FULL)">1322</a></sup>

An adhesive layer can also become the mechanical anchor for a component that was never meant to be removed. In RFID proximity cards, the [[antenna]] coil bonds to the card's internal adhesive layer rather than to the card face, so opening the card transfers the coil to the adhesive and it may not come away in one piece.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-539" title="EEVblog #539 - RFID Tag Card Repair">539</a></sup>

## Structural and outdoor adhesive

Adhesive bonding is a genuine installation advantage for thin surface-mounted hardware. A [[solar-roadways|solar road]] element only a couple of millimetres thick can simply be adhered to an existing roadway, avoiding the excavation, concrete slabs and crane lifts required by embedded installations.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1591" title="EEVblog 1591 - MORE Colas Wattway Solar Cycleways in the Netherlands!">1591</a></sup> The open question for such installations is bond durability under traffic and weather.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1591" title="EEVblog 1591 - MORE Colas Wattway Solar Cycleways in the Netherlands!">1591</a></sup> In practice, failure of the adhesive holding a road-embedded panel down has been implicated in delamination and water ingress, with damage in the middle of an element being unreachable from the edge and therefore unrepairable.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1234" title="EEVblog #1234 - MORE Epic Solar Roadways FAIL!">1234</a></sup>
