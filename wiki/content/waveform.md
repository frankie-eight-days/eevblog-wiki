A waveform is the graphical representation of an electrical signal's amplitude as it varies with time, conventionally plotted with [[voltage]] on the vertical axis and time on the horizontal axis.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-UJjMt2-k99c" title="What Is An Oscilloscope?">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-926" title="EEVblog #926 - Introduction To The Oscilloscope">926</a></sup> Displaying waveforms is the primary purpose of an [[oscilloscope]], which is a time-domain instrument; a [[multimeter]] can only report a single numeric value such as a true RMS reading and cannot show what a signal is actually doing.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1661" title="EEVblog 1661 - AC Basics Tutorial Part 5: Time Domain vs Frequency Domain">1661</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-926" title="EEVblog #926 - Introduction To The Oscilloscope">926</a></sup> Because modern digital electronics operates on fast-changing signals invisible to a multimeter, the waveform display is the window into the behaviour of a circuit.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-UJjMt2-k99c" title="What Is An Oscilloscope?">•</a></sup>

## Waveform parameters

A waveform can be characterised by peak-to-peak voltage, average value, RMS value, [[frequency]], and rise and fall times, all of which a modern digital oscilloscope measures automatically, with cursors available for precise manual time and amplitude measurements between arbitrary points.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-926" title="EEVblog #926 - Introduction To The Oscilloscope">926</a></sup> Several distinct conventions exist for quoting the voltage of a single waveform — peak, peak-to-peak, average, and RMS among them — which is why the measurement basis must be stated when communicating a value.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1417" title="EEVblog 1417 - AC Basics Tutorial Part 1: Alternating Current">1417</a></sup> "The base of the waveform is the lower part of the waveform," a definition relevant to automatic amplitude measurements.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-704" title="EEVBlog #704 - Rigol DS1054Z Oscilloscope Features Review">704</a></sup>

The crest factor of a waveform is the ratio of peak voltage to RMS voltage; narrow, spiky waveforms can reach crest factors of five or more, which defeats the sampling hardware of true-RMS converter circuits and produces erroneous readings unless faster RMS conversion hardware is used.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1417" title="EEVblog 1417 - AC Basics Tutorial Part 1: Alternating Current">1417</a></sup> For a full-wave rectified [[sine-wave]], the average value is 2/π of the peak, approximately 0.637.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-Ju19Rz8FcBg" title="Sagan CORRECTING Dave!">•</a></sup> Component datasheets also characterise signals graphically: op-amp input noise, for example, is typically specified as a peak-to-peak figure with a typical waveform captured over a [[bandwidth]]-limited 0.1 Hz to 10 Hz range.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-528" title="EEVblog #528 - Opamp Input Noise Voltage Tutorial">528</a></sup>

## Phase

The phase difference between two waveforms is "the difference in degrees between the two waveforms at the same reference location," where one full cycle corresponds to 360° and the reference location may be a zero crossing, a peak, or an edge.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1751" title="EEVblog 1751 - Oscilloscope Phase Measurement Masterclass">1751</a></sup> A waveform whose reference feature occurs earlier in time is said to be leading the other.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1751" title="EEVblog 1751 - Oscilloscope Phase Measurement Masterclass">1751</a></sup> Oscilloscope techniques for measuring phase include cursor delta-time measurement, automated phase measurements, Lissajous figures in X-Y mode, and computing the arcsine of the ratio of a channel's amplitude at a reference instant to its maximum amplitude.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1751" title="EEVblog 1751 - Oscilloscope Phase Measurement Masterclass">1751</a></sup>

## Common waveform shapes

Function and arbitrary waveform generators provide a standard set of shapes — sine, square, ramp, pulse, noise, and DC — with many instruments adding specialised shapes such as exponential rise and ECG, plus fully arbitrary user-defined waveforms.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1701" title="EEVblog 1701 - $599 Australian Made Moku:Go Oscilloscope REVIEW">1701</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-CMoBGGqojqs" title="Rigol MSO7000 Unboxing &amp; First Impressions">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-323" title="EEVblog #323 - Rigol Scratch&#x27;n&#x27;Sniff">323</a></sup>

In [[switch-mode-power-supply|switching converter]] design, the switching waveform is defined by its on-time and off-time, which sum to the period; for a 10 µs period with a calculated 2.7 µs off-time, the on-time is 7.3 µs.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-110" title="EEVblog #110 - Let&#x27;s Design a DC to DC Switchmode Converter">110</a></sup> In digital logic, timing diagrams depict the relationship between input and output waveforms, and physically feeding a gate with inputs of the drawn timing produces the drawn output waveform at real logic levels such as 5 V or 3.3 V and 0 V.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-7bVnsXHO6Uw" title="EEVacademy | Digital Design Series Part 1 - Introduction To Digital Logic">•</a></sup>

Complementary drive schemes produce paired inverse waveforms: the two coil drives of an electromagnetic toothbrush mechanism are inverse waveforms of just over 13 V peak-to-peak, with ringing visible on the rising and falling edges.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-284" title="EEVblog #284 - Braun Toothbrush Teardown">284</a></sup> [[lcd]] segment and common pins are driven with mutually inverted waveforms that remain at standard 0–5 V logic levels relative to ground, the AC component across the segment arising purely from the phase opposition.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1045" title="EEVblog #1045 - How To Drive an LCD">1045</a></sup> In a Cockcroft-Walton multiplier, each stage carries a waveform of identical amplitude but with its reference point shifted upward, which is why the diodes and capacitors need not be rated for the full output voltage.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-469" title="EEVblog #469 - Cockcroft-Walton Multiplier">469</a></sup>

## Waveform fidelity and measurement traps

A displayed waveform is not necessarily a faithful record of the signal. Digital oscilloscopes apply interpolation by default, so a smooth-looking zoomed-in trace can appear to contain detail that was never actually sampled; the displayed points must be checked against [[memory-depth]] and [[sample-rate]] before drawing conclusions.[1211] Insufficient acquisition memory produces gross artifacts: a complex amplitude-modulated pulse waveform rendered as garbage at 1,000 samples of memory depth only began to resemble the true signal at 100K–1M samples, and required 5–10 Mpts for equivalence with a scope that manages memory automatically.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-617" title="EEVblog #617 - Tektronix Oscilloscope Anomaly">617</a></sup> Aliasing can similarly fold out-of-band frequencies back into range, a hazard of all sampling systems, not only oscilloscopes.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-j49T1E4UEv4" title="Oscilloscope Alias Followup">•</a></sup>

[[signal-integrity]] problems also distort waveforms at the source. With a mismatched coaxial load, individual harmonics reflect back with phase and group delay, adding to or subtracting from the forward signal; this produces pedestal artifacts on square waves and amplitude that varies with frequency at the generator output.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-652" title="EEVblog #652 - Oscilloscope &amp; Function Generator Termination Demo">652</a></sup> Probe [[grounding]] matters at high speed: a longer ground lead introduces visible extra undershoot and slower recovery compared with a short ground connection at the same point.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1718" title="EEVblog 1718 - Cheap 1GHz Oscilloscopes are Useless? ($5 DIY 1GHz Resistive Probe)">1718</a></sup> Amplifiers driven near their gain-bandwidth limit begin to distort a clean input waveform, which is why gain-bandwidth margin is required.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-572" title="EEVblog #572 - Cascading Opamps For Increased Bandwidth">572</a></sup> In LCR measurement, an over-ranged drive signal clips the test waveform and produces wildly wrong readings, such as 470 pF reading far off value.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1474" title="EEVblog 1474 - Can You Measure Capacitors IN Circuit?">1474</a></sup>

Deep acquisition memory is the enabling feature for fidelity over long captures: a 4 Mpts scope can record a long waveform and then be zoomed in to reveal detail such as a single anomalous spike or an odd decaying pulse that would otherwise be missed.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-307" title="EEVblog #307 - Lab Lighting &amp; Measurement">307</a></sup> Display processing can also be used deliberately; an inverted intensity-grading mode makes infrequent glitch energy appear brighter than the main waveform, isolating intermittent anomalies.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-601" title="EEVblog #601 - Why Digital Oscilloscopes Appear Noisy">601</a></sup>

## Generating waveforms

On microcontrollers, the practical way to generate an arbitrary waveform such as a triangle wave at the maximum sample rate is to store the shape in a lookup table and have a DMA controller feed it to a DAC, optionally paced by a timer; this runs almost entirely in the background, leaving the CPU free, whereas an interrupt-per-sample approach would saturate the processor.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-eUA2TRX1NTc" title="STM32 ARM Development - DMA &amp; ADC Discussion">•</a></sup> At the simplest level, bit banging generates a waveform by setting and clearing individual pins in sequence.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-9hMsNOwY5AQ" title="EEVacademy #3 - Bit Banging &amp; SPI Tutorial">•</a></sup> Multiple coordinated digital waveforms — for example the clock, latch, and blanking signals needed by a CD4026-based [[frequency-counter]] — can be generated on spare pins of a small [[microcontroller]] board when a multi-channel signal generator is unavailable.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-NvIv-0-R6qQ" title="Guest Video: TannerTech Designing a Frequency Counter">•</a></sup> An audio file played through a sound card's left and right channels into an oscilloscope's X-Y mode can draw text, scrolling images, and rotating shapes, the two audio waveforms serving as deflection signals.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-153" title="EEVblog #153 - YouScope Demo on a Digital Scope">153</a></sup>

## Waveforms in service documentation

Vintage service manuals annotated schematics and fold-out diagrams with expected oscilloscope waveforms, DC bias test-point voltages, and timing diagrams, supporting a structured troubleshooting sequence: verify [[power-supply]] rails first, then DC bias levels at the marked points, then compare live scope captures against the printed waveforms.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-863" title="EEVblog #863 - Sony D50 Discman Teardown - World&#x27;s First Portable CD Player">863</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-612" title="EEVblog #612 - Mailbag">612</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-407" title="EEVblog #407 - Mailbag">407</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-381" title="EEVblog #381 - How Do Ultrasonic Delay Lines Work?">381</a></sup> The same compare-against-known-good principle underlies component-level signature tools, where probe-point waveforms from a known-good board are captured and published so that suspect boards can be checked point by point for a rough match.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src--ON1AdmAMOY" title="Vintage Tek Museum: Curve Bug Signal Debugger">•</a></sup>

Modern digital oscilloscopes support this workflow directly with reference waveforms: a captured trace can be stored to a reference channel and overlaid in a different colour for comparison against later acquisitions.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1324" title="EEVblog #1324 - Oscilloscope Reference Waveforms are USEFUL">1324</a></sup> This technique has been used to distinguish correct from incorrect key-press sequences in a power-line analysis of an electronic safe lock, where stored reference captures revealed repeatable waveform differences between entry sequences,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-771" title="EEVblog #771 - Electronic Safe Lock Powerline Attack Part 2">771</a></sup> and to compare probing techniques, where a stored reference trace exposed the extra overshoot and undershoot introduced by a longer probe ground lead.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1718" title="EEVblog 1718 - Cheap 1GHz Oscilloscopes are Useless? ($5 DIY 1GHz Resistive Probe)">1718</a></sup> Fine vertical adjustment, decoupled from the calibrated Volts/division steps, is useful precisely for such waveform-to-waveform shape comparison.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1226" title="EEVblog #1226 - Get Better Accuracy On Your Oscilloscope">1226</a></sup> Some instruments can also transfer a captured waveform directly into an arbitrary generator's memory, automatically inheriting its measured amplitude and frequency, so a real-world signal can be replayed.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-209" title="New Agilent 3000X Oscilloscope Firmware - EEVblog #209">209</a></sup>
