The N-channel [[mosfet|MOSFET]] is the default switching element of modern electronics: a three-terminal gate-drain-source device that conducts between drain and source when the gate is driven positive with respect to the source.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-242" title="EEVblog #242 - Hakko FX-888 Soldering Iron Hack">242</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-748" title="EEVblog #748 - How Do Transistors Work?">748</a></sup> MOSFETs come in N-channel and P-channel varieties, and most designs use N-channel because most switching is done on the low side — driving a [[relay]], an [[led|LED]] string, or the switching element of a DC-to-DC converter — with N-channel occupying the same circuit role that the NPN occupies among bipolar transistors.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1736" title="EEVblog 1736 - TOP 5 Jellybean MOSFET&#x27;s">1736</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-748" title="EEVblog #748 - How Do Transistors Work?">748</a></sup> The preference is visible in the supply chain as much as in the [[schematic]]: a [[parametric-search|parametric search]] at a major distributor turns up roughly 8,800 N-channel parts against about 1,580 P-channel ones, an imbalance summarised as "N-channel snobbery".<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1387" title="EEVblog 1387 - MOSFET Repair Replacement Search">1387</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1461" title="EEVblog 1461 - The MOSFET Search CHALLENGE">1461</a></sup>

## Behaviour and terminals

At circuit level a BJT and a MOSFET can be dropped into the same configuration and behave much the same way, and the N-channel device maps onto the NPN.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-748" title="EEVblog #748 - How Do Transistors Work?">748</a></sup> The practical difference is at the control terminal. A bipolar switch driven from a logic pin needs a base [[resistor]] and suffers a base-emitter drop; substituting an N-channel MOSFET removes both, allowing the gate to be tied directly to the driving junction and cutting the component count.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-242" title="EEVblog #242 - Hakko FX-888 Soldering Iron Hack">242</a></sup> Where a MOSFET is driven from a low supply rail, a logic level part is required — one whose threshold falls inside the available gate drive.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-102" title="EEVblog #102 - DIY Constant Current Dummy Load for Power Supply and Battery Testing">102</a></sup>

P-channel devices can be treated loosely as the mirror image of N-channel ones, but the two are not simply inverted: they differ physically, operationally and parametrically, and P-channel parts typically carry somewhat less continuous [[current]] and a higher on-[[resistance]] than an otherwise comparable N-channel part.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-748" title="EEVblog #748 - How Do Transistors Work?">748</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1736" title="EEVblog 1736 - TOP 5 Jellybean MOSFET&#x27;s">1736</a></sup>

### On-resistance

R<sub>DS(on)</sub> is the drain-to-source resistance when the device is switched hard on, and it is the parameter that usually decides part selection in a conduction-limited application.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-931" title="EEVblog #931 - Designing A Better Multimeter PART 2">931</a></sup> In a [[multimeter]] current path, where a wafer [[range-switch|range switch]] cannot carry the current and a big TO-220 or D²PAK part rated for tens of amps must switch the 10 mA shunt instead, a low R<sub>DS(on)</sub> is what keeps the added [[burden-voltage|burden voltage]] acceptable.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-931" title="EEVblog #931 - Designing A Better Multimeter PART 2">931</a></sup> Typical jellybean power parts land in the low tens of milliohms: an FDS6680 is rated 11.5 A at 30 V with about 10 mΩ nominal on-resistance, and a 500 V 18 A part is specified at 0.27 Ω nominal — on-resistance rises steeply with [[voltage]] rating.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-397" title="EEVblog #397 - Turnigy Accucell 6 Charger Teardown">397</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1387" title="EEVblog 1387 - MOSFET Repair Replacement Search">1387</a></sup>

### Body diode

Every such MOSFET carries a parasitic body [[diode]] between source and drain, inherent in the physical construction rather than added deliberately.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-811" title="EEVblog #811 - How The Varta 15 Minute Battery Charger Works">811</a></sup> It sets a hard constraint on where the device can block current, and it can also be exploited. In a NiMH cell-bypass arrangement, a lower N-channel MOSFET turned on shorts across a cell; if a cell is then inserted, the body diode of the upper device sits reverse biased — its [[cathode]] positive with respect to its anode — and with the gate held at zero no current flows out of the [[battery]] at all.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-812" title="EEVblog #812 - Varta 15min NiMH Charger Part 2">812</a></sup> Some integrated drain outputs add Zener clamping alongside the intrinsic body diode, which removes the need for external clamp devices.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-952" title="EEVblog #952 - Nixie Tube Display Project - Part 3">952</a></sup> The body diode is prominent enough to be part of a MOSFET's identity: a low-cost component tester correctly reports an IRF610 as an N-channel MOSFET and draws the internal diode in its symbol, though the same instrument misidentifies a VN10K as an NPN [[transistor]], recognising transistor-like behaviour and enormous gain but not the device type.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1020" title="EEVblog #1020 - Is A $7 LCR / Component Tester Any Good?">1020</a></sup>

## Part numbering and sourcing

MOSFET part numbering is de facto standardised around current, channel type and voltage, which makes cross-manufacturer substitution unusually straightforward.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1387" title="EEVblog 1387 - MOSFET Repair Replacement Search">1387</a></sup> In the common formula, an 18N50 is an 18 A, N-channel, 500 V part — the trailing 50 denotes 500 V, not 50 V, and a P-channel equivalent would carry a P in place of the N.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1387" title="EEVblog 1387 - MOSFET Repair Replacement Search">1387</a></sup> An 18N50 from essentially any manufacturer will do the job in most positions, so a repair search can begin from the naming convention alone.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1387" title="EEVblog 1387 - MOSFET Repair Replacement Search">1387</a></sup> The remaining parameters may still matter a great deal, particularly in a design context such as a mains [[power-factor-correction|power factor correction]] stage.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1387" title="EEVblog 1387 - MOSFET Repair Replacement Search">1387</a></sup>

Substitution is not always so easy. Parts from second-tier Asian manufacturers — Sino Power, Alpha & Omega, China Resources Microelectronics — are extremely common inside consumer equipment yet may not be stocked by the mainstream distributors at all, and a device combining an unusual package with an uncommon feature such as built-in ESD protection may have no true equivalent.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1460" title="EEVblog1460 - REPAIRING a LED Studio Light with a DUMPSTER LAPTOP!">1460</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1461" title="EEVblog 1461 - The MOSFET Search CHALLENGE">1461</a></sup> Sponsored "equivalent" listings returned by distributor search are paid advertising, not verified substitutes; a 300 V part in the wrong package is not an equivalent regardless of where it appears in the results.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1461" title="EEVblog 1461 - The MOSFET Search CHALLENGE">1461</a></sup> Package matters as much as the die: a 2N7000 in SOT-23 rated at 300 mA is no substitute for a power part, whatever the channel type.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1461" title="EEVblog 1461 - The MOSFET Search CHALLENGE">1461</a></sup>

Common jellybean N-channel parts span the range from signal to power. The 2N7002 is ubiquitous and adequate for switching LEDs at tens of milliamps up to about 100 mA.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1736" title="EEVblog 1736 - TOP 5 Jellybean MOSFET&#x27;s">1736</a></sup> The AO3400 costs roughly twice as much for around ten times the capability at 30 V, with the AO3401 or 3401A as its P-channel counterpart at the same 30 V and about 4 A continuous.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1736" title="EEVblog 1736 - TOP 5 Jellybean MOSFET&#x27;s">1736</a></sup> Larger surface-mount parts include the FDT459N in SOT-223 and the IRLML2803 in SOT-23 — the latter sharing its footprint and marking style with ordinary bipolar switching parts, so that [[reverse-engineering|reverse engineering]] a board may not distinguish a 2N2222 from a small power MOSFET until the circuit around it is drawn out and analysed.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1708" title="EEVblog 1708 - What&#x27;s This SMD Part?">1708</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-675" title="EEVblog #675 - How To Reverse Engineer A Rigol DS1054Z">675</a></sup>

## Switching and power applications

**Low-side load switching.** The canonical use is a low-side switch driven directly from a [[microcontroller]] pin: a motor connected across the battery with an N-channel MOSFET returning it to [[ground]] under IC control,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-284" title="EEVblog #284 - Braun Toothbrush Teardown">284</a></sup> a [[pwm|PWM]] LED driver taken straight from a micro pin,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-661" title="EEVblog #661 - Mailbag">661</a></sup> or a motor drive output with low-pass filtering on the gate and a back-EMF diode across the load.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-759" title="EEVblog #759 - Mailbag">759</a></sup> Reverse-polarity protection can also be built this way, with the N-channel device switching the ground leg and its gate fed from a divider off the positive input, although a P-channel part on the high side is the simpler arrangement.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-397" title="EEVblog #397 - Turnigy Accucell 6 Charger Teardown">397</a></sup>

**High-side switching.** An N-channel device can be used on the high side, with drain and source swapped relative to the low-side case, but the gate must then be driven above the load: V<sub>GS</sub> must reach at least the switched [[output-voltage|output voltage]] plus the device's gate-source threshold.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-995" title="EEVblog #995 - Power Supply Sequencing">995</a></sup> This is why P-channel parts are usually chosen for high-side applications despite their worse parameters — [[grounding]] the gate pin is a far simpler control operation.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-995" title="EEVblog #995 - Power Supply Sequencing">995</a></sup> Where an N-channel and a P-channel device are combined, the N-channel part often serves as the low-voltage control element that latches and pulls the P-channel high-side switch on.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1520" title="EEVblog 1520 - Troubleshooting a Faulty BM786 Multimeter">1520</a></sup>

**Linear and pass applications.** An N-channel MOSFET used as a series pass element with an op-amp closing the loop is the standard constant-current dummy load: a logic-level MTP30 with an LM324 in the original form,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-102" title="EEVblog #102 - DIY Constant Current Dummy Load for Power Supply and Battery Testing">102</a></sup> and the same block reappearing as an N-channel device over a bank of ten parallel 1 Ω resistors to make a cheap 100 mΩ sense element,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-661" title="EEVblog #661 - Mailbag">661</a></sup> or in commercial hobby chargers where the source returns through a sense resistor tapped off to an ADC pin.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-397" title="EEVblog #397 - Turnigy Accucell 6 Charger Teardown">397</a></sup> The same topology serves a precision [[current-source|current source]], with an RFP3005 sourcing 1 A through a four-terminal 1.25 Ω [[precision-resistor|precision resistor]] dissipating up to 1.25 W in free air.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-577" title="EEVblog #577 - Precision 1A Current Source Part 2">577</a></sup> Bench-supply pass elements are the same device class: a CEP60N15 at 150 V and 30 to 60 A depending on package,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-861" title="EEVblog #861 - Rigol DP832 PSU FAIL &amp; REPAIR">861</a></sup> four high-voltage, high-current IRFP460s on a fan-cooled, temperature-sensed [[heat-sink|heat sink]] in a laboratory supply,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-268" title="EEVblog #268 - Xantrex 300V 4A Power Supply Teardown">268</a></sup> and a large N-channel power device per channel in a source measure unit, replicated four times with identical values so each section dissipates a quarter of the total.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-607" title="EEVblog #607 - Agilent B2912A Source Measure Unit SMU Teardown">607</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-268" title="EEVblog #268 - Xantrex 300V 4A Power Supply Teardown">268</a></sup>

**Switch-mode conversion.** N-channel MOSFETs are the switching element in boost converters, sitting between the [[inductor]] and ground ahead of the output diode and filter [[capacitor]],<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-397" title="EEVblog #397 - Turnigy Accucell 6 Charger Teardown">397</a></sup> and integrated as on-chip switches in DC-DC controllers — for example a 2 A part specified at 140 mΩ and 75 mΩ.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-139" title="EEVblog #139 - Let&#x27;s Select a DC-DC Boost Converter">139</a></sup> In synchronous buck stages two N-channel devices are used, sometimes paralleled with all their pins shorted together to lower on-resistance.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1606" title="EEVblog 1606 - $45 AlienTek DP100 100W USB-C PSU REVIEW">1606</a></sup> In [[inverter]] service, alternately switched N-channel MOSFETs drive the primary of a step-up [[transformer]], producing a [[square-wave|square wave]] unless sinusoidal drive control is added.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-449" title="EEVblog #449 - Absopulse VFC500 Variable Frequency Converter Teardown">449</a></sup> In a hybrid solar inverter the split is by voltage: high-voltage IGBTs on the DC bus side and ordinary N-channel MOSFETs on the 48 V battery side, there 100 V devices rated 240 A behind a 150 A [[fuse]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1620" title="EEVblog 1620 - Deye Solar Hybrid Inverter EXTREME TEARDOWN">1620</a></sup>

**Series-connected charging.** A more unusual arrangement uses pairs of N-channel MOSFETs across each cell holder in a totem-pole configuration, with a constant-current DC-DC output fed into the centre point and each cell's negative terminal tied to the centre point of the pair below it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-811" title="EEVblog #811 - How The Varta 15 Minute Battery Charger Works">811</a></sup> The result charges the cells in series at around 8 A while allowing any individual cell to be bypassed or switched out by alternating the two FETs of its pair.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-811" title="EEVblog #811 - How The Varta 15 Minute Battery Charger Works">811</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-812" title="EEVblog #812 - Varta 15min NiMH Charger Part 2">812</a></sup>

## Complementary pairs

Where both polarities are needed, N-channel and P-channel parts appear as matched sets. A CMOS inverter is one P-channel and one N-channel device with gates tied together and drains joined at the output, one on whenever the other is off.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-941" title="EEVblog #941 - Schmitt Trigger Tutorial">941</a></sup> A CMOS Schmitt trigger input extends this to four stacked devices — two N-channel below, two P-channel above — with two further transistors acting as source followers that feed the output back into the middle of the upper or lower totem segment.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-941" title="EEVblog #941 - Schmitt Trigger Tutorial">941</a></sup> The same symmetry governs push-pull power stages: an audio output stage uses matched N-channel and P-channel MOSFETs, or NPN and PNP bipolars, to drive hard to each rail about the centre ground point;<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-970" title="EEVblog #970 - Mailbag">970</a></sup> a UPS output uses two P-channel and two matching N-channel devices per heat sink;<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-504" title="EEVblog #504 - UPS Tutorial &amp; Teardown">504</a></sup> an electronic speed controller uses three N-channel devices on the bottom and three P-channel on the top;<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1457" title="EEVblog1457 - Old School Mailbag - ESC Burnout">1457</a></sup> and an AC voltage standard uses a 2SK1058 N-channel part matched with a 2SJ162 P-channel part, each individually fused.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-709" title="EEVblog #709 - EDC 4601 AC Voltage Standard Teardown">709</a></sup> Dual devices are also packaged together — a dual N-channel power MOSFET, or an N-channel and a P-channel in a single six-pin SOT-23 suited to totem-pole drive.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1415" title="EEVblog 1415 - Reverse Engineering the DP10007 Differential Probe">1415</a></sup>

## Robustness and failure

MOSFETs are not as robust as bipolar junction transistors, and this is their principal disadvantage as pass elements.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-861" title="EEVblog #861 - Rigol DP832 PSU FAIL &amp; REPAIR">861</a></sup> A series pass MOSFET in a bench supply that fails destructively — taking the fuse with it — points at a design that does not adequately protect the device, since a correctly designed product should not permit the pass transistor to be blown up in normal use.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-861" title="EEVblog #861 - Rigol DP832 PSU FAIL &amp; REPAIR">861</a></sup> Failure is often unambiguous once measured: a short from gate to drain, or drain to source, on a device that should read open.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-861" title="EEVblog #861 - Rigol DP832 PSU FAIL &amp; REPAIR">861</a></sup> Under severe fault the package itself can be destroyed, as with 60 V 50 A FP50N06 power MOSFETs from a line-interactive UPS whose cases were blown apart.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-SoeVNaz7KGc" title="EEVsmoke #2 - Magic Smoke Captured!">•</a></sup> In a multi-device stage, damage is often confined to part of the array: in a burnt-out speed controller the three P-channel devices along the top rail were destroyed while the three N-channel devices below them survived.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1457" title="EEVblog1457 - Old School Mailbag - ESC Burnout">1457</a></sup>
