A solar cell is a semiconductor junction device that converts incident light directly into electrical energy through the photoelectric effect: photons striking a PN junction generate a [[current]], termed a photocurrent.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-716" title="EEVblog #716 - Raspberry Pi 2 Xenon Flash Problem Explained">716</a></sup> Electrically, a silicon solar cell behaves as a [[diode]] that produces [[voltage]] when photons hit it, yielding about 0.6 V per cell.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1426" title="EEVblog 1426 - WOW! This Problem DROPS Solar Output by 20% !">1426</a></sup> Cells are wired together into [[solar-power-system|photovoltaic]] panels and power everything from calculators and watches to multimeters and satellites.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1093" title="EEVblog #1093 - Casio FX260 Solar II Calculator Review">1093</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-JWr5E2ej5cg" title="Part 2: Citizen EcoDrive Watch Battery Replacement">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1608" title="EEVblog 1608 - Mailbag">1608</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-519" title="EEVblog #519 - Ardusat Arduino Based CubeSat Satellite">519</a></sup>

## Operating principle

The photoelectric effect requires photons of a specific energy and [[frequency]] to generate current flow through a PN junction; any semiconductor junction is light-sensitive, which is why the same phenomenon underlies camera sensors, infrared remote-control receivers, and solar cells.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-716" title="EEVblog #716 - Raspberry Pi 2 Xenon Flash Problem Explained">716</a></sup> As a circuit element, a solar cell is a DC [[voltage-source|voltage source]] in the same category as a [[battery]], [[thermocouple]], or Peltier device, and like any practical source it must be modeled with an internal series [[resistance]] rather than as an ideal source.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1009" title="EEVblog #1009 - DC Fundamentals Part 1: Voltage vs Power vs Energy">1009</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1397" title="EEVblog 1397 - DC Fundamentals Part 2: DC Voltage &amp; Current Sources (Thevenin &amp; Norton Theorems)">1397</a></sup> Solar cells have a characteristic power-versus-voltage (PV) curve, and extracting maximum power requires operating at the maximum power point rather than at open circuit or [[short-circuit|short circuit]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1383" title="EEVblog 1383 - Human Battery Energy Harvesting BUSTED">1383</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1276" title="EEVblog #1276 - Solar Roadways Surface TESTED!">1276</a></sup>

## Panel construction and shading

A typical residential panel contains 60 individual cells in a 6 by 10 arrangement, all wired in series, so that 60 cells at roughly 0.6 V each produce about 36 to 40 V at the panel terminals.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1426" title="EEVblog 1426 - WOW! This Problem DROPS Solar Output by 20% !">1426</a></sup> Because the full string current must flow through every cell, shading even one cell is disproportionately destructive: the shaded cell is still forced to pass the string current, and panels incorporate bypass arrangements to route around disabled sections.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1426" title="EEVblog 1426 - WOW! This Problem DROPS Solar Output by 20% !">1426</a></sup> A small, fuzzy shadow covering only part of one cell — for example from a guy wire or [[antenna]] mast — is sufficient to cut the output of an entire multi-panel string by 10 to 20%.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1426" title="EEVblog 1426 - WOW! This Problem DROPS Solar Output by 20% !">1426</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-NoIjTK249D0" title="How a SMALL SHADOW can drop Solar Power Output by 20%!">•</a></sup> Manufacturing trends over successive panel generations include physically larger cells, smaller gaps between cells, and wider silver bus strips.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1385" title="EEVblog 1385 - 8kW Home Solar Power System EXPANSION!">1385</a></sup>

## Irradiance, efficiency, and losses

[[solar-irradiance|Solar irradiance]] at the Earth's surface is about 1,000 W per square metre in the best case, while outside the atmosphere it is about 1,357 W per square metre.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-896" title="EEVblog #896 - Space Electronics">896</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1591" title="EEVblog 1591 - MORE Colas Wattway Solar Cycleways in the Netherlands!">1591</a></sup> A photovoltaic cell converting 25% of incident energy is doing very well.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1591" title="EEVblog 1591 - MORE Colas Wattway Solar Cycleways in the Netherlands!">1591</a></sup> Reflection is a controllable loss: bare silicon reflects over 30% of incident light, modern processed cells without an anti-reflective coating reflect only 4 to 8%, and with the coating reflection falls to one or two percent; anti-reflective coatings are worth on the order of a couple of percent up to perhaps 5% [[efficiency]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1575" title="EEVblog 1575 - TIPA: The World&#x27;s Most Efficient Solar Panel - BUSTED">1575</a></sup> Surface texturing and oxide coatings also alter the reflection figure across the spectrum.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1575" title="EEVblog 1575 - TIPA: The World&#x27;s Most Efficient Solar Panel - BUSTED">1575</a></sup> The dominant loss mechanism, however, is thermalization: sunlight that is absorbed but not converted to electricity simply heats the cell, and most of the losses in a panel appear as heat rather than reflection.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1575" title="EEVblog 1575 - TIPA: The World&#x27;s Most Efficient Solar Panel - BUSTED">1575</a></sup> Output also follows a cosine law with angle of incidence, so a cell can be many degrees off-axis before output falls materially — only a seriously off-angle orientation drops collection to 50% — and precise aiming matters only when chasing fractions of a percent.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-362" title="EEVblog #362 - LED Tube Lighting Install &amp; Theory">362</a></sup>

A practical figure for real-world energy yield, derived from measured data and [[datasheet|data sheet]] values for a top-end cell and assuming 40% losses including glass, is 380 watt-hours per square metre per day as a best case.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-681" title="EEVblog #681 - More Solar Roadways BULLSHIT!">681</a></sup>

## Low-light and indoor operation

Cell output scales roughly with illuminance, and indoor light is about three orders of magnitude weaker than full sun: a cell delivering 10 to 12 mA in full sun delivers only about 10 to 30 µA under typical bench lighting.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-48" title="EEVblog #48 - Solar Power Hope">48</a></sup> This is why solar-powered calculators are possible at all — they pair the cell with extremely low-power custom ASICs operating at only tens of kilohertz, and the cells themselves generate virtually no current.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-48" title="EEVblog #48 - Solar Power Hope">48</a></sup> Measured behaviour bears this out: the Casio FX-260 Solar II begins operating at about 20 lux (and not at 10 lux), and a PowerFilm flexible indoor cell delivers about 2.3 V but only 60–70 µA at around 70 lux, which is adequate only for ultra-low-power energy harvesting.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1093" title="EEVblog #1093 - Casio FX260 Solar II Calculator Review">1093</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1094" title="EEVblog #1094 - Casio FX260 Solar II Calculator Tritium Nuclear Battery Experiments">1094</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-S-UqkM8iDKM" title="#1179 - PowerFilm Flexible Indoor Solar Cell">•</a></sup> Amorphous cells are specifically designed for indoor use at illuminances of a couple of hundred lux.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1276" title="EEVblog #1276 - Solar Roadways Surface TESTED!">1276</a></sup> Short-circuit current is a convenient relative measurement for comparing cell output across lighting conditions without locating the maximum power point.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-48" title="EEVblog #48 - Solar Power Hope">48</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1276" title="EEVblog #1276 - Solar Roadways Surface TESTED!">1276</a></sup>

Supporting circuitry in solar-only devices is minimal but deliberate. A reservoir [[capacitor]] on the [[power-rail|power rail]] determines how long the device runs after light is removed — in one calculator, enlarging it to 220 µF extended operation to about two minutes but added a one- to two-second power-on delay — and a diode may be used to clamp the cell's [[output-voltage|output voltage]] to a safe level for the load IC.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1094" title="EEVblog #1094 - Casio FX260 Solar II Calculator Tritium Nuclear Battery Experiments">1094</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-962" title="EEVblog #962 - Hacking A Calculator Into A Counter">962</a></sup>

## Cell types and manufacture

Cells encountered in practice include monocrystalline parts (such as surface-mount IXYS devices rated 1.5 V at 12 mA in full sun), amorphous cells for indoor use, extremely thin fragile cells that shatter into sharp shards if mishandled, and printed flexible cells.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-48" title="EEVblog #48 - Solar Power Hope">48</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1276" title="EEVblog #1276 - Solar Roadways Surface TESTED!">1276</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-673" title="EEVblog #673 - Mailbag">673</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-991" title="EEVblog #991 - Mailbag">991</a></sup> Conventional wafer production saws thin slices off a thick silicon boule, turning much of the material to dust; alternative processes using particle accelerators to produce very thin wafers have been proposed to avoid that waste.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-IbYtn420QBM" title="Rayton Solar + Bill Nye = Questionable Solar Technology?">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1025" title="EEVblog #1025 - Minute Physics + Bill Nye Promoting Rayton Solar">1025</a></sup> For space applications, triple-junction cells are used, and a modest array can produce about 93 W in the space environment.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1711" title="EEVblog 1711 - International Astronautical Congress IAC 2025 Walkaround">1711</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-896" title="EEVblog #896 - Space Electronics">896</a></sup>

## Applications

Solar cells power credit-card and scientific calculators, watches in which the dial face itself is the cell, and pocket multimeters combining an amorphous cell with a lithium manganese dioxide rechargeable battery.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-48" title="EEVblog #48 - Solar Power Hope">48</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1093" title="EEVblog #1093 - Casio FX260 Solar II Calculator Review">1093</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-JWr5E2ej5cg" title="Part 2: Citizen EcoDrive Watch Battery Replacement">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1608" title="EEVblog 1608 - Mailbag">1608</a></sup> In satellites, cells feed a [[power-supply|power supply]] module with charge management, current-consumption measurement, and battery-state monitoring.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-519" title="EEVblog #519 - Ardusat Arduino Based CubeSat Satellite">519</a></sup> Multi-source energy-harvesting evaluation boards combine a solar cell input with thermoelectric and piezoelectric generators.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-534" title="EEVblog #534 - Mailbag">534</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-945" title="EEVblog #945 - Thermal Powered Smartwatches Are GIMMICKS!">945</a></sup> Betavoltaic devices use a solar cell to convert the phosphor glow of a tritium source into a few microwatts of electricity.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1595" title="EEVblog 1595 - 50 Year Chinese Nuclear Diamond Battery!">1595</a></sup> Cells can also be optimized as receivers for near-infrared laser wireless power, where the cell must be designed for the specific laser [[bandwidth]] rather than the full solar spectrum.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1092" title="EEVblog #1092 - Wi-Charge IR Wireless Charging - Fact or Fiction?">1092</a></sup>

## Limits and engineering judgment

A number of measurements establish hard practical limits. Covering a cell with a textured glass road surface cuts short-circuit current by roughly half.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1276" title="EEVblog #1276 - Solar Roadways Surface TESTED!">1276</a></sup> Vehicle-integrated photovoltaics are supplemental only: a car offers at most about five square metres of area, and even a two- or three-fold improvement in cell efficiency cannot close the gap to self-sufficiency — solar-powered cars are not viable as primary-energy vehicles.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1480" title="EEVblog 1480 - Lightyear Zero Solar Powered Electric Car">1480</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-QkFioBbH5aM" title="Aptera Solar Powered Electric Car - Is It Practical?">•</a></sup> Night-time generation by coupling a thermoelectric generator to a solar cell's radiative cooling yields about 50 mW per square metre, roughly 0.04% of the cell's daytime output — energy-harvesting territory, not useful power.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1467" title="EEVblog 1467 - Stanford Solar Power at Nightime!  BUSTED">1467</a></sup> More broadly, attempts to power ordinary [[microcontroller]]-class loads directly from small cells fail on current budget: even covering half a product's surface with high-efficiency cells cannot supply a dot-matrix [[lcd|LCD]] drawing 100–200 µA indoors, which is why such designs fall back to charging a [[supercapacitor]] or battery rather than direct operation.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-97" title="EEVblog #97 - Live Show #2">97</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1741" title="EEVblog 1741 - Mailbag!">1741</a></sup> Deployments that place cells in inherently poor locations — flat, under thick glass, exposed to grime and abrasion — remain inferior to conventional rooftop mounting regardless of future improvements in cell cost or efficiency, because the unfavourable geometry and losses do not change.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-681" title="EEVblog #681 - More Solar Roadways BULLSHIT!">681</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1276" title="EEVblog #1276 - Solar Roadways Surface TESTED!">1276</a></sup>
