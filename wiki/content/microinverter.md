A microinverter is a small grid-tie [[inverter]] installed one-per-panel, typically mounted on the racking directly beneath each [[solar-panel]], that converts that panel's low-[[voltage]] DC (nominally around 40 V) straight into mains AC (240 V) rather than feeding DC into a shared string inverter.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1385" title="EEVblog 1385 - 8kW Home Solar Power System EXPANSION!">1385</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-BtQUuD6QRMw" title="Solar Panel Temperature Dependency">•</a></sup> Functionally, microinverters and string inverters are the same kind of device — "Inverters and microinverters are identical, effectively identical" — the distinction being one inverter per panel instead of one per entire series string.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-BtQUuD6QRMw" title="Solar Panel Temperature Dependency">•</a></sup> The architecture trades higher hardware cost for per-panel maximum power point tracking, per-panel monitoring, fault isolation, and the elimination of high-voltage DC cabling.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1386" title="EEVblog 1386 - 295W Inverter vs 370W Solar Panel - WTF?">1386</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1426" title="EEVblog 1426 - WOW! This Problem DROPS Solar Output by 20% !">1426</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1385" title="EEVblog 1385 - 8kW Home Solar Power System EXPANSION!">1385</a></sup>

## Electrical characteristics

A representative unit, the Enphase IQ7+, accepts a maximum input voltage of 60 V DC, suiting panels that produce around 40 V in normal operation and up to about 41 V open circuit, leaving comfortable headroom against the 60 V absolute maximum.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1385" title="EEVblog 1385 - 8kW Home Solar Power System EXPANSION!">1385</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-BtQUuD6QRMw" title="Solar Panel Temperature Dependency">•</a></sup> Continuous AC output of the IQ7+ is 295 W, which is why the rating matters when matching to higher-wattage panels.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1386" title="EEVblog 1386 - 295W Inverter vs 370W Solar Panel - WTF?">1386</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-WtGlolw-BxA" title="Reclaim Heat Pump Hot Water Solar Energy Usage UPDATE">•</a></sup> Multi-channel variants condense several microinverters into one enclosure: the Hoymiles HMS-800T is a two-channel 800 W unit, and four-channel units exist with a 16–60 V MPPT window, 22 V startup, and 400 VA per channel.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-BXVgk-uoxn8" title="Hoymiles Solar Microinverter Install + Deye Gen Connection">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1516" title="EEVblog 1516 - Mailbag Bonanza!">1516</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-puGooNKVE74" title="Hoymiles Solar Microinverter FAIL">•</a></sup> Typical conversion loss is around 5% before cabling; measured Enphase IQ7+ [[efficiency]] data shows about 93% at the 10% power point.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-632" title="EEVblog #632 - Solar Roadways Are RUBBISH!">632</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1386" title="EEVblog 1386 - 295W Inverter vs 370W Solar Panel - WTF?">1386</a></sup>

A defining grid-tie behaviour is anti-islanding: a microinverter that is fully powered on the solar side will not energise its output unless it detects the grid, a compliance and safety requirement.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1626" title="EEVblog1626 - YOU HAD ONE JOB! (Solar Power Install FAIL)">1626</a></sup>

## Advantages over string inverters

- **Shading and fault isolation.** In a series string, shading or failure of one panel degrades the whole string; a thin shadow covering only a couple of cells has been observed to cut a 12-panel string's output by 10–20%.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1426" title="EEVblog 1426 - WOW! This Problem DROPS Solar Output by 20% !">1426</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-NoIjTK249D0" title="How a SMALL SHADOW can drop Solar Power Output by 20%!">•</a></sup> With microinverters, a dirty, shaded, faulty, or bird-soiled panel trips its bypass diodes and drops out alone — 13 of 14 panels could fail and the remaining one still produces full power.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1385" title="EEVblog 1385 - 8kW Home Solar Power System EXPANSION!">1385</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1426" title="EEVblog 1426 - WOW! This Problem DROPS Solar Output by 20% !">1426</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1386" title="EEVblog 1386 - 295W Inverter vs 370W Solar Panel - WTF?">1386</a></sup>
- **Per-panel MPPT.** Maximum power point tracking operates on each panel individually rather than across an entire string, extracting more energy under mismatched conditions.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1426" title="EEVblog 1426 - WOW! This Problem DROPS Solar Output by 20% !">1426</a></sup>
- **Safety.** Output is 240 V AC per panel instead of a 450–500 V (or higher) DC string. High-voltage DC sustains a plasma arc that does not self-extinguish the way AC does at zero crossings, and DC isolator switches are a known rooftop fire source — a hazard the microinverter architecture removes.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1385" title="EEVblog 1385 - 8kW Home Solar Power System EXPANSION!">1385</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-WzpXyNNZg38" title="eevBLAB #64 - Tesla Solar City Panels Are CATCHING ON FIRE!">•</a></sup>
- **Monitoring.** Because each unit is individually serial-numbered and addressed, systems can be mapped to a photograph of the roof, yielding per-panel power, energy, AC voltage, DC panel voltage, [[frequency]], and microinverter temperature, and making a failed or underperforming unit immediately identifiable by location.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1628" title="EEVblog 1628 - Home Solar Power Re-Install + Upgrade + FAIL">1628</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-M4IiR4vW0aY" title="I don&#x27;t have a problem. I only have 4 Solar Monitoring Systems">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1390" title="EEVblog 1390 - NEGATIVE Household Solar Consumption? WHY?">1390</a></sup>

## Cost and trade-offs

Microinverter systems cost more than an equivalent string-inverter system simply because there is one inverter per panel; individual units run around US$150, which can exceed the value of an older 250 W panel and makes retrofitting them onto an existing small string system uneconomic.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1386" title="EEVblog 1386 - 295W Inverter vs 370W Solar Panel - WTF?">1386</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1426" title="EEVblog 1426 - WOW! This Problem DROPS Solar Output by 20% !">1426</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-M4IiR4vW0aY" title="I don&#x27;t have a problem. I only have 4 Solar Monitoring Systems">•</a></sup> String arrays remain the cheaper solution where shading is not a concern.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1426" title="EEVblog 1426 - WOW! ## Panel oversizing and output clipping

It is common and legitimate practice to pair a panel with a microinverter rated below the panel's nameplate power — for example, 370 W or 415 W panels on 295 W IQ7+ units.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1386" title="EEVblog 1386 - 295W Inverter vs 370W Solar Panel - WTF?">1386</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1628" title="EEVblog 1628 - Home Solar Power Re-Install + Upgrade + FAIL">1628</a></sup> The consequence is clipping: the panel can never deliver more than the inverter's limit, so a 14-unit system built from 5.1 kW of panels peaks at about 4.13 kW even on the best day of summer.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1386" title="EEVblog 1386 - 295W Inverter vs 370W Solar Panel - WTF?">1386</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-WtGlolw-BxA" title="Reclaim Heat Pump Hot Water Solar Energy Usage UPDATE">•</a></sup> For a 295 W / 370 W pairing in a high-insolation location, clipping occurs on roughly 38% of days nominally, but the energy lost is only a single-digit percentage of the annual total.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1386" title="EEVblog 1386 - 295W Inverter vs 370W Solar Panel - WTF?">1386</a></sup> Counterarguments favour deliberate oversizing: per-panel MPPT and shading immunity recover energy across the year, and an underrated inverter may run more efficiently at the power extremes.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1386" title="EEVblog 1386 - 295W Inverter vs 370W Solar Panel - WTF?">1386</a></sup> Manufacturer availability constrains the choice — Enphase did not offer a 370 W-output unit, and higher-rated microinverters from any vendor carry a significant price premium, which feeds directly into the payback calculation.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1386" title="EEVblog 1386 - 295W Inverter vs 370W Solar Panel - WTF?">1386</a></sup>

## System architecture and communications

Microinverters are AC-coupled in parallel onto a shared mains bus, with each unit individually addressed by serial number; in the Enphase system, Q relays each support up to 11 microinverters per branch.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1682" title="EEVblog 1682 - Enphase Microinverter FAIL &amp; Warranty Install">1682</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1626" title="EEVblog1626 - YOU HAD ONE JOB! (Solar Power Install FAIL)">1626</a></sup> A separate mains-powered gateway (the Envoy) aggregates data and is supposed to report at roughly 15-minute intervals around the clock, even with no solar production.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-uE0x8YR7nMg" title="Enphase Envoy S FAILING?">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1385" title="EEVblog 1385 - 8kW Home Solar Power System EXPANSION!">1385</a></sup> Communication travels over the mains cabling itself and is slow — a backlog of around 100 days of buffered per-unit data can take days to upload after an installation is first connected.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1626" title="EEVblog1626 - YOU HAD ONE JOB! (Solar Power Install FAIL)">1626</a></sup>

## Reliability

Because they live outdoors under panels, microinverters are hermetically sealed, and their thermal design must account for the full rooftop environment; reliability expectations are high enough that Enphase backs its units with a 10-year warranty.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1385" title="EEVblog 1385 - 8kW Home Solar Power System EXPANSION!">1385</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-isnXYy9vCag" title="Enphase Microinverter FAIL?">•</a></sup> Failures do occur and are observable precisely because of the per-unit monitoring: a single unit among 14 stopped reporting after roughly four years, triggering an email alert, with the faulty unit identified by its status [[led]] and replaced under warranty.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1682" title="EEVblog 1682 - Enphase Microinverter FAIL &amp; Warranty Install">1682</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-isnXYy9vCag" title="Enphase Microinverter FAIL?">•</a></sup> A Hoymiles two-channel unit has also been observed failing with mains present at its input but no output.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-puGooNKVE74" title="Hoymiles Solar Microinverter FAIL">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-GFlckdPzYQQ" title="Deye Solar Inverter Screen FAIL + Hoymiles Microinverter Input">•</a></sup>

## Integration with hybrid inverters

Some hybrid inverters — the Deye 5 kW unit being a noted example — provide a generator port that accepts not only a diesel generator but also microinverter input, selected [[via]] a software checkbox; this AC-couples the microinverter array through the hybrid and keeps the microinverters producing when the grid fails.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1610" title="EEVblog 1610 - Deye 5kW Hybrid Solar Inverter">1610</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1620" title="EEVblog 1620 - Deye Solar Hybrid Inverter EXTREME TEARDOWN">1620</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-BXVgk-uoxn8" title="Hoymiles Solar Microinverter Install + Deye Gen Connection">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-T2yNMQM_TEA" title="Deye Hybrid Solar Inverter Mains Dropout Test">•</a></sup> A quirk observed on the Deye is a persistent, physically unaccounted-for 84 W reading on the generator port whenever microinverter mode is enabled, which measurement against [[battery]] [[current]] shows is not real power flow.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-kCtGoymiShU" title="Deye Hybrid Inverter Gen Port Troubleshooting">•</a></sup>

## Suitability limits

Commodity microinverters are designed for efficient rooftop panels generating substantial power; they are a poor match for very low-output sources. [[solar-roadways|Solar roadway]] and pavement tile proposals suffer specifically because off-the-shelf microinverters assume far more power per panel than a tile produces, because microinverter-plus-cabling losses of around 10% must be budgeted into already marginal output, and because dedicating a microinverter to each small tile multiplies embodied energy far beyond that of a conventional panel per watt delivered.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1363" title="EEVblog #1363 - Solar FREAKIN&#x27; Roadways Gets NEW FUNDING!">1363</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-632" title="EEVblog #632 - Solar Roadways Are RUBBISH!">632</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1389" title="EEVblog 1389 - Spanish Solar Pavement STUPIDITY">1389</a></sup>
