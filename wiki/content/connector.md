A connector is the demountable electrical and mechanical interface between two parts of a product — board to board, board to cable, or product to outside world. It is simultaneously a signal path, a [[current]] path, a creepage barrier, a mechanical retention feature and a serviceability decision, and because it is one of the few components in a design that a human physically operates, it is one of the most common sources of intermittent and outright failure.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-896" title="EEVblog #896 - Space Electronics">896</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-631" title="EEVblog #631 - How To: Soniq LCD TV Repair Part 2">631</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1185" title="EEVblog #1185 - Dumpster Tek 500MHz TDS540D Scope">1185</a></sup> Connectors also dominate the physical constraints of a design in a way their [[schematic]] footprint does not suggest: they are among the first parts placed on a board, they set the case cutouts, and in volume they are heavy, expensive and slow to assemble.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-291" title="EEVblog #291 - USB Power Supply Part 2 - The Housing">291</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-244" title="EEVblog #244 - How To Lay Out A PCB - PSU Design Part 9">244</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-264" title="EEVblog #264 - SMD PCB Pick &amp; Place Machine Assembly">264</a></sup>

## Contact design and sequencing

Contacts inside a connector are frequently staggered in length rather than flush, so that mating happens in a deliberate order — [[ground]] and one other pin make contact first, and the remaining pins connect afterwards.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-135" title="EEVblog #135 - Kindle Case Mythbusting">135</a></sup> The purpose is to guarantee a reference before signals arrive, and staggering of this kind is a general practice inside connectors rather than a quirk of any one part.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-135" title="EEVblog #135 - Kindle Case Mythbusting">135</a></sup>

Contact geometry also determines what a connector is metrologically capable of. Precision four-wire Kelvin connections are brought out on separate terminals so that lead and [[contact-resistance|contact resistance]] fall outside the measurement loop.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1550" title="EEVblog 1550 - Keysight E36731A Battery Emulator Teardown">1550</a></sup> In low-thermal-EMF applications the contact metallurgy itself matters, with tellurium copper used to suppress [[thermocouple]] voltages at the junction.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-544" title="EEVblog #544 - Fluke 5450A Resistance Calibrator Teardown">544</a></sup> At microwave frequencies the 7 mm connector used in mechanical [[calibration]] kits is built with a sleeve arrangement and a centre contact that is neither male nor female — it is "sexless", so two identical connectors mate against each other and the reference plane is defined by the sleeve rather than by a pin-and-socket fit.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1039" title="EEVblog #1039 - Keysight Metrology Standards Lab">1039</a></sup>

## Voltage rating, creepage and contamination

The [[voltage]] rating of a small connector follows directly from conductor spacing. On bare copper traces, 51 to 100 V requires 0.6 mm of creepage distance, which is a small enough number that a fine-pitch connector can legitimately carry a 60 V rating on visually tiny pins.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1222" title="EEVblog #1222 - Apple&#x27;s MacBook Design FAIL - Who&#x27;s To Blame?">1222</a></sup> Creepage requirements also derate with altitude, so a rating valid at sea level is not automatically valid on a 3,000 m mountain.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1222" title="EEVblog #1222 - Apple&#x27;s MacBook Design FAIL - Who&#x27;s To Blame?">1222</a></sup>

Ratings assume a clean, dry interface, and connectors are precisely where that assumption breaks. Liquid spilled on a product can migrate under a covered connector, and if the assembly is later stood on end the liquid runs down the board and into the connector body, bridging pins that the creepage calculation assumed were separated by air.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1222" title="EEVblog #1222 - Apple&#x27;s MacBook Design FAIL - Who&#x27;s To Blame?">1222</a></sup> Failure of this kind need not be reproducible: it may occur in a small fraction of ingress events and pass a water-ingress test entirely.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1222" title="EEVblog #1222 - Apple&#x27;s MacBook Design FAIL - Who&#x27;s To Blame?">1222</a></sup> Leaked [[electrolyte]] behaves the same way, wicking into a connector and remaining wet there long after the failure that released it, with gravity determining which connector it accumulates in.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1511" title="EEVblog 1511 - Solar Analytics System FAIL! (+ Dave gets ZAPPED!)">1511</a></sup> A design defence is to remove copper rather than add coating — routing a slot through the [[pcb|PCB]] between the pins, leaving one or two end pins unconnected and letting the corresponding contact stand over open air, which produces a very large creepage path at no material cost.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1222" title="EEVblog #1222 - Apple&#x27;s MacBook Design FAIL - Who&#x27;s To Blame?">1222</a></sup>

## Retention, vibration and mechanical stress

Most board-to-cable connectors retain by friction alone, sometimes with a small interlocking tab, and friction retention is unreliable under vibration.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-440" title="EEVblog #440 - Atten PPS3205T-3S Triple Output Power Supply Teardown">440</a></sup> Manufacturers therefore glue or silastic connectors down as a secondary retention step, which is a deliberate extra operation rather than a bodge, and shake-proof washers serve the same purpose on screw-terminated connectors.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-440" title="EEVblog #440 - Atten PPS3205T-3S Triple Output Power Supply Teardown">440</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-WFhk1aRqLD8" title="AllPowers R1500 1500W 1150Wh LiFeP04 Battery Teardown + Review">•</a></sup> Where screws hold connectors into a chassis, thread-locking compound is the corresponding measure and its absence is a legitimate criticism of an otherwise well-built instrument.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1592" title="EEVblog 1592 - Fluke 287 Multimeter Teardown">1592</a></sup>

Retention force scales with the number of ways: a low-way-count connector has little total friction on its pins, so reseating one can leave a marginal connection that is invisible on inspection.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1185" title="EEVblog #1185 - Dumpster Tek 500MHz TDS540D Scope">1185</a></sup> Some ribbon-cable headers permit insertion at an offset angle, so a cable can be seated on the wrong pins or at a skew, either open-circuiting or shorting adjacent lines.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-905" title="EEVblog #905 - REPAIR: HP85 Vintage Computer">905</a></sup> Connectors that do not positively lock are worth distrusting outright, and contact cleaner is only a partial remedy; a connector that only works reliably when free-hanging is telling the repairer that the retention, not the contacts, is the fault.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-631" title="EEVblog #631 - How To: Soniq LCD TV Repair Part 2">631</a></sup>

Plugging a connector also stresses the board. Inserting a plug into a board-mounted header flexes the PCB between its mounting posts, and that flexion is a known mechanism for cracking ceramic capacitors sited near the connector — the same mechanism that cracks parts near mechanical standoffs.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1037" title="EEVblog #1037 - Solving Ceramic Capacitor Cracking">1037</a></sup> The mitigation is placement and orientation: rotating a [[ceramic-capacitor|ceramic capacitor]] so its terminations lie across rather than along the axis of flex greatly reduces the stress and can eliminate the problem.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1037" title="EEVblog #1037 - Solving Ceramic Capacitor Cracking">1037</a></sup>

## Current capacity and impedance

Flat flex cables and their connectors carry very little current per pin and per conductor, so power rails on a flex are routinely run over several pins wired in parallel — four in parallel is a common arrangement for a display [[backlight]] feed.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-631" title="EEVblog #631 - How To: Soniq LCD TV Repair Part 2">631</a></sup> Conversely, a well-designed power entry connector is chosen for low [[impedance]] and lands directly on the board with a field of vias stitching the current down through inner layers; a single such connector can carry the whole 130 W input of a system, roughly 8 A at 16 V.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1216" title="EEVblog #1216 - PCB Layout + FPGA Deep Dive">1216</a></sup>

Degraded contacts show up as a voltage loss that appears only under load, and a suspect connector can often be confirmed by wiggling it while current is flowing.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-IXVTMUQGN5U" title="Goal Zero Yeti 400 Lithium Battery Capacity Test #2">•</a></sup> Contact resistance also produces localised heating, which is why clamp connections carrying 11 A become noticeably warm even when nothing is wrong.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-401" title="EEVblog #401 - Lecroy 9384C Oscilloscope Repair - Part 2">401</a></sup> A genuine fault escalates that quickly: a shorted supply will heat both the connector and the cable feeding it before any upstream protection intervenes.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1281" title="EEVblog #1281 - Garmin GPS Repair">1281</a></sup>

## Connector or no connector

Fitting a connector where wires could simply be soldered to the board costs money, and the saving is real enough that products are shipped with speakers, batteries and motors soldered directly to pads to avoid it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1278" title="EEVblog #1278 - Mailbag">1278</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-295" title="EEVblog #295 - $30 eBook Reader Teardown">295</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-671" title="EEVblog #671 - White Van Speaker Scam Teardown">671</a></sup> A daughterboard soldered in through a dual-inline pin header rather than plugged into a socket cannot be removed without [[desoldering]] the entire row.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-641" title="EEVblog #641 - Dumpster Cash Register Teardown">641</a></sup> The same logic removes a microSD socket from a tablet to save the cost of the connector.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-220" title="Amazon Kindle Fire Tablet Review &amp; Unboxing - EEVblog #220">220</a></sup>

The counter-argument is service and safety. A connector allows a subassembly to be separated for repair or for staged manufacture, and directly soldered flat flex, however neatly terminated, forecloses that.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-973" title="EEVblog #973 - Hioki Multimeter Review &amp; Clamp Meter Teardown">973</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-472" title="EEVblog #472 - JBC CD-2BB Soldering Iron Teardown">472</a></sup> On exposed high-voltage terminations the argument is stronger still: a screw-terminal block leaves live metal accessible, whereas a housed plug can be disconnected without tools and presents no exposed metal.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1263" title="EEVblog #1263 - Mailbag">1263</a></sup> Connectors also let a manufacturer stock alternative footprints — an unpopulated second flat-flex footprint paralleled with the first lets the same board accept whichever display panel is available that month, with only the cable moved across.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-295" title="EEVblog #295 - $30 eBook Reader Teardown">295</a></sup>

## Layout and schematic practice

Connectors are the fixed points of a [[pcb-layout|PCB layout]]. They are placed first, at the board edge where they must line up with front-panel cutouts, and locked in position before any other component is moved; the rest of the layout is then fitted around them.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-244" title="EEVblog #244 - How To Lay Out A PCB - PSU Design Part 9">244</a></sup> That constraint propagates: once the connectors are committed to one side of a board, the routing from the front end is dictated and the freedom to shuffle blocks of circuitry is largely gone.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1477" title="EEVblog 1477 - TEARDOWN! - NEW Tektronix 2 Series Oscilloscope">1477</a></sup> Case and connector count are part of the earliest product definition, since enclosure size limits how many input and output connectors can be fitted at all — alongside how much heat can be dissipated.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-291" title="EEVblog #291 - USB Power Supply Part 2 - The Housing">291</a></sup> Through-hole connectors on an otherwise all-surface-mount board are normal, since the mechanical loads demand it, though a through-hole crystal alongside them is not.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1323" title="EEVblog #1323 - PCB Layout Review &amp; Analysis">1323</a></sup>

On the schematic, a connector symbol should be drawn so that its physical arrangement is legible — pins grouped as the real part groups them and rows separated as the real part separates them, rather than a single numbered column of one to fifty with no physical representation.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1222" title="EEVblog #1222 - Apple&#x27;s MacBook Design FAIL - Who&#x27;s To Blame?">1222</a></sup> A library symbol that hides the physical layout contributes to layout errors downstream, because the person routing the board works from the netlist and does not necessarily know how the circuit functions.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1222" title="EEVblog #1222 - Apple&#x27;s MacBook Design FAIL - Who&#x27;s To Blame?">1222</a></sup> Ordinary readability rules apply as much to connectors as to anything else: orient them the same way up as the rest of the sheet, keep them horizontal where there is room, do not route nets straight through the symbol body, and group related connectors physically together on the page.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1129" title="EEVblog #1129 - Creating a Nice Readable Schematic">1129</a></sup>

## Assembly and cost

Connectors are high-mass parts, and a pick-and-place machine must be programmed to pick and move them more slowly than the 0603 passives it can fly across the board.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-264" title="EEVblog #264 - SMD PCB Pick &amp; Place Machine Assembly">264</a></sup> Even in a modern SMT plant some connectors are too large for the line and are hand- or auto-inserted after reflow.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-684" title="EEVblog #684 - Ness SMT Manufacturing &amp; Assembly Factory Tour">684</a></sup> Placing connectors as a separate later operation is a practical scheduling advantage: boards can be assembled and the connectors fitted afterwards, so a late connector delivery does not stall the SMT run.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-m9tza_c4sxc" title="uCurrent GOLD Assembler Parts Shipping">•</a></sup> It also lets test happen through pads and a bed-of-nails jig rather than through the connector, which can be faster than mating a physical plug even when the connector is already fitted.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-937" title="EEVblog #937 - Retro Canon Still Camera Teardown!">937</a></sup>

The logistics are non-trivial at volume. A thousand of a modest board-mount connector can weigh around 15 kg, so an order of 8,000 — four per board across 2,000 boards — is heavy enough that freighting them to the assembler is a material cost, and shipping direct from supplier to assembler is the better arrangement.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-m9tza_c4sxc" title="uCurrent GOLD Assembler Parts Shipping">•</a></sup>

## Modularity and backplanes

Splitting a system across a backplane with plug-in modules is the reflexive engineering answer, and it does have genuine merits: each module can be tested on its own, and it is not difficult to produce.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-896" title="EEVblog #896 - Space Electronics">896</a></sup> The cost is that a large PCB populated with connectors inherits vibration sensitivity and contact problems across every one of those interfaces, which in a launch-and-orbit environment is enough to push the design away from a backplane toward a different card format.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-896" title="EEVblog #896 - Space Electronics">896</a></sup> The same trade-off appears in bench instruments, where multi-board construction forces wiring looms, good-quality connectors, ferrite beads and manual cable-tying steps that a single-board design avoids entirely.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-187" title="EEVblog #187 - Tektronix TDS2024C Oscilloscope Teardown">187</a></sup>

## Failure modes and troubleshooting

The most common connector fault is that it is not plugged in. A connector left disconnected during reassembly produces a completely dead product with no other symptom, and this is a mistake made by professional repairers as readily as anyone: a serviced instrument returned as unrepairable was found to have had a clipped connector simply pulled apart rather than pressed firmly back into place, which is not something vibration can do on its own.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-a4Xpsenpd6E" title="The Amp Hour #643 - Calibration &amp; Repair with Ian Johnston">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-FMluev0WGTc" title="World&#x27;s Quickest Repair">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-762" title="EEVblog #762 - How Secure Are Electronic Safe Locks?">762</a></sup>

Connectors that are not used are not therefore innocent. An unpopulated, unmated connector can short its own pins and take down a working board, which is precisely the part a troubleshooter will exclude from suspicion first.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-230" title="EEVblog #230 - ArduCopter ArduPilot Troubleshooting">230</a></sup> More generally, a low-[[resistance]] point traced to a daughterboard may lie either on that board or in the connector joining it, and separating the two requires disconnecting rather than probing.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-230" title="EEVblog #230 - ArduCopter ArduPilot Troubleshooting">230</a></sup>

Wiring at the connector cannot be assumed correct. A supply has shipped with the wire colours at a connector transposed, so the nominally positive conductor was negative relative to the regulator and return current was flowing through a [[ribbon-cable|ribbon cable]] instead.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-512" title="EEVblog #512 - Rigol DP832 Bad Design Investigation">512</a></sup> Cold or unwetted [[solder]] joints on connector pins are a further class: high-thermal-mass connector terminals may show no solder flow-through at all while every smaller part on the same board is fine, a defect that can sit latent for decades.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-804" title="EEVblog #804 - HP1740A Oscilloscope Repair - Part 2">804</a></sup>

When a suspect connector must be examined, the sequence is to reseat both ends of the cable first, inspect the contacts for tarnish, and only then consider desoldering the connector, which is treated as a last resort because of the damage risk.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1376" title="EEVblog 1376 - Tandy 102 Vintage Computer REPAIR">1376</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-401" title="EEVblog #401 - Lecroy 9384C Oscilloscope Repair - Part 2">401</a></sup> Where the fault is genuinely in a flat-flex termination, the cable can often be salvaged rather than replaced: trimming the flex square and carefully scraping the stiffener off the new end produces a working termination that goes straight back into the connector.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-736" title="EEVBlog #736 - World&#x27;s First IR Thermal Camera Watch">736</a></sup>

## Selection and sourcing

Connector selection is one of the mechanical constraints an engineer hands to a layout contractor at the start of a job, alongside footprint and placement requirements.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-795" title="EEVblog #795 - Electronex 2015">795</a></sup> Communicating a required connector geometry to a vendor is a case where a rough 3D model conveys the requirement immeasurably better than a hand sketch.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-ql7J_tFBVfY" title="The Amp Hour #51 - Vafrous Video Vaniloquence">•</a></sup> Parametric distributor search is poorly suited to the task, since a partial part-number search that fails to find a [[microcontroller]] will happily return pages of connectors instead.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1387" title="EEVblog 1387 - MOSFET Repair Replacement Search">1387</a></sup> Dedicated reference works exist covering connectors, terminals, device sockets, adapters and terminal blocks, and the selection problem — choosing the optimal connection solution for an application — is the substance of them.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1725" title="EEVblog 1725 - Mailbag: with special guest Sagan!">1725</a></sup>

Build quality is assessable by inspection. A cheap clone [[soldering]] station betrays itself partly through a connector that looks like the lowest-cost part available.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-596" title="EEVblog #596 - World&#x27;s Cheapest Soldering Station - Yihua 936">596</a></sup> Conversely, a good instrument uses a proper connector even for a single low-value wire run to a piezo buzzer rather than soldering it to the board, and uses one for the programming interface as well.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-171" title="EEVblog #171 - Agilent U1272A Multimeter Teardown">171</a></sup> Gold-plated contacts are a deliberate upgrade rather than decoration.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-500" title="EEVblog #500 - Live Show">500</a></sup> Small ergonomic touches — pull tabs on flat-flex connectors in a laptop — indicate thought in the mechanical design.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1345" title="EEVblog #1345 - HP DV7 Laptop Repair">1345</a></sup>
