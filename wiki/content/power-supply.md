A power supply is the circuit or instrument that converts available energy — mains, a [[battery]], a [[usb]] port, another rail — into the regulated voltages a piece of electronics actually runs on. In a product it is the block every other block depends on, which is why it is the first thing measured when anything fails to work.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-507" title="EEVblog #507 - The First ARM Processor Computer - Acorn Archimedes A3000">507</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-400" title="EEVblog #400 - 400th Live Show">400</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-863" title="EEVblog #863 - Sony D50 Discman Teardown - World&#x27;s First Portable CD Player">863</a></sup> On the bench it is, alongside a [[multimeter]], the essential item of lab gear, and it is the piece of equipment most often built rather than bought.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1298" title="EEVblog #1298 - $70 2000W Power Supply">1298</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-111" title="EEVblog #111 - Live Show #5">111</a></sup>

## The bench supply as an instrument

A laboratory supply is defined by two knobs, not one: a set [[voltage]] and a set [[current-limit]], with readback of what the load is actually taking.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-876" title="EEVblog #876 - NI VirtualBench Review">876</a></sup> The two-knob arrangement is deliberate, since operators want both values directly settable rather than reached through a mode toggle.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1032" title="EEVblog #1032 Part 1 - John Kenny Keysight Interview">1032</a></sup> Resolution of 1 mV and 1 mA on both set and readback is typical of a mid-range unit, as is an indicator showing whether the supply is presently in constant-voltage or constant-[[current]] mode.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-876" title="EEVblog #876 - NI VirtualBench Review">876</a></sup>

Capability requirements for general electronics work are modest. Zero to 12 V at 1 A covers most benches, and 100 mA is ample for [[microcontroller]] work; a 40 A supply exists mainly for the rare occasions that demand it and otherwise sits idle.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-104" title="EEVblog #104 - Live Show #3">104</a></sup> Multiple supplies are more useful than one large one, and channels can be put in series when a job needs more voltage than one will give — 32 V and 20 V in series to make a 52 V rail, or three 30 V 3 A channels stacked to reach 48 V.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-107" title="EEVblog #107 - Live Show #4">107</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1222" title="EEVblog #1222 - Apple&#x27;s MacBook Design FAIL - Who&#x27;s To Blame?">1222</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-895" title="EEVblog #895 - BEC Pro Model Airplane Regulator Testing">895</a></sup>

Typical instrument-grade offerings span a wide range. A triple-output linear unit of roughly 220 W gives 30 V and 3 A per channel plus a fixed output;<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-828" title="EEVblog #828 - Siglent SPD3303X Precision Lab PSU Teardown">828</a></sup> a low-cost programmable model offers 30 V at 5 A;<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-314" title="EEVblog #314 - Korad KA3005P PSU Teardown">314</a></sup> a used rack supply covers 0 to 35 V at 6 A and works in two quadrants, with the model number and its voltage and current limits set by a jumper link on the board — the 6641, 6642, 6643 and 4445 differ only in those limits.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-667" title="EEVblog #667 - Agilent 6643A Power Supply Binding Post Hack">667</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-655" title="EEVblog #655 - Auction Score">655</a></sup> At the other extreme sit 2000 W benchtop units and a 600 V, 5100 W supply built for an instrument maker by a specialist manufacturer.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1298" title="EEVblog #1298 - $70 2000W Power Supply">1298</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-814" title="EEVblog #814 - Keysight N8762A 600V 5100W PSU Teardown">814</a></sup>

Reading a supply's specification requires care. An output tolerance printed as ±2 with a small circle after it is not ±2 % but ±2 per mil — parts per thousand — which is 0.2 %.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1300" title="EEVblog #1300 - &quot;Parts Per&quot; Notation EXPLAINED">1300</a></sup> Regulation is also not free: a 300 to 400 mV drop across the pass element between the negotiated input and the maximum settable output is ordinary for a small USB-C-fed supply.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1606" title="EEVblog 1606 - $45 AlienTek DP100 100W USB-C PSU REVIEW">1606</a></sup>

## Constant current, compliance and limiting

A supply's current limit is not merely protection; it makes the instrument a current source. With the limit set, the output can be shorted outright and the supply will simply hold the set current — 0.5 A into a dead short — which is exactly the behaviour of a constant-current generator.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1688" title="EEVblog 1688 - Constant Current Sources EXPLAINED + DEMO">1688</a></sup> Dedicated current sources differ only in range and in labelling the voltage knob as compliance: 10 V to 100 V of compliance with a maximum of 10 mA, adjustable down to 1 nA.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1688" title="EEVblog 1688 - Constant Current Sources EXPLAINED + DEMO">1688</a></sup>

[[compliance-voltage]] is the limiting concept when driving current-driven devices. A 1 V 100 A supply will not light an [[led]] string, because without the minimum forward voltage no current flows at all regardless of the supply's current capability.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1617" title="EEVblog 1617 - Architectural LED Lighting Build + Test">1617</a></sup> Conversely, a string of high-forward-voltage LEDs may need 48 V of compliance to pass 20 mA.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1700" title="EEVblog 1700 - Do Dumpster LCD TV&#x27;s Make Good LED Light Panels?">1700</a></sup>

Set conservatively, the limit turns an unknown board into a survivable experiment: 5 V with the limit at 100 mA for a piece of unknown consumer gear, 24 V at 0.1 A for an intercom rated at 70 mA, 20 V at 5 A for a laptop expected to draw 4.5 A, 50 mA when applying 4 V to an LED string.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-573" title="EEVblog #573 - Mailbag">573</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-710" title="EEVblog #710 - Intercom System Repair">710</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1279" title="EEVblog #1279 - Best Dumpster PC Find Yet!">1279</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1427" title="EEVblog 1427 - An INFURIATING Electronics Exam Question!">1427</a></sup> Charging work uses the same discipline in reverse — 4.2 V, the maximum cell voltage for a lithium-ion cell, with the current set to 400 mA, so the supply enters constant current immediately and revives a cell sitting at 2.5 V in undervoltage lockout.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-KTr-44n0bbU" title="Garmin GPS Battery Kickstarting">•</a></sup> A 1 mA constant-current limit is enough to [[coaxial-cable|coax]] a charge into a watch cell.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-0018phUFjuQ" title="Citizen EcoDrive Watch Battery Replacement">•</a></sup>

## Diagnostic uses

Substituting a bench supply for a product's own supply is a standard technique, and much of a supply's value lies in tests that have nothing to do with simply powering a board.

Battery cutoff voltage is measured by driving the product from an adjustable supply and winding the voltage down until it stops working. Terminal voltage under load is the only quantity that matters, so a supply — which sources current while holding its output constant, unlike a cell whose terminal voltage sags with its internal [[resistance]] — reproduces exactly the condition of interest at the product's terminals.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-779" title="EEVblog #779 - Batteriser: How To Measure Battery Cutoff Voltage">779</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-789" title="EEVblog #789 - Batteriser Monkey BUSTED!">789</a></sup> Measuring a used cell's open-circuit voltage after removing it from the product instead measures nothing useful, since it excludes the ESR present when the product is actually running.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-779" title="EEVblog #779 - Batteriser: How To Measure Battery Cutoff Voltage">779</a></sup> It is the industry-standard method and effectively the only practical one short of watching a cell discharge over months.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-779" title="EEVblog #779 - Batteriser: How To Measure Battery Cutoff Voltage">779</a></sup>

Other bench applications recur. [[burden-voltage]] in a [[current-measurement]] can be cancelled by winding the supply up to compensate for the drop across the meter, though this fails as soon as the load current varies.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-929" title="EEVblog #929 - Designing A Better Multimeter">929</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-324" title="EEVblog #324 - DC-DC Converter Testing - USB Supply Part 4">324</a></sup> Small motors will usually turn on a volt or two, making a supply the quickest way to confirm a mechanism moves.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-937" title="EEVblog #937 - Retro Canon Still Camera Teardown!">937</a></sup> A high-voltage rectifier that reads open on a multimeter's [[diode]] range can be lifted and passed real current from a supply to prove it out.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1452" title="EEVblog 1452 - Fluke PM3370B Combiscope REPAIR">1452</a></sup> A hard short on a rail can be hunted by applying a very high-current supply — 40 A set to 3.3 V — directly across the rail to make the offending part reveal itself.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-398" title="EEVblog #398 - Lecroy 9384C Oscilloscope Repair">398</a></sup> Characterising a converter properly needs the supply plus four multimeters, one pair for input voltage and current and one pair for output.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-110" title="EEVblog #110 - Let&#x27;s Design a DC to DC Switchmode Converter">110</a></sup> Power supply sequencing — bringing several rails up in a controlled order — can be studied by powering a four-rail instrument from bench supplies when its own supply is blowing.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-995" title="EEVblog #995 - Power Supply Sequencing">995</a></sup> Some integrated supplies can ramp their outputs, though a 2.5 Hz ceiling and a 10-second minimum ramp fall far short of simulating a 50 µs turn-on.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1552" title="EEVblog 1552 - Digilent Analog Discovery 3">1552</a></sup>

## Supplies inside equipment

Equipment supplies are rarely single-rail. A studio monitor runs ±27 V straight to the low-[[frequency]] driver and regulates that down through [[transistor]] regulators to ±15 V for the front-end op-amps.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1128" title="EEVblog #1128 - Studio Monitor Speaker Noise - Part 2">1128</a></sup> An ultrasound machine's master supply board produces ±12 V, −5.2 V and a separate 24 V for fans.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1314" title="EEVblog #1314 - Ultrasound Machine Teardown!">1314</a></sup> A 1970s calculator needed +5, −7.5, +12, +9, −12, −6 and −4 V from a Darlington pass configuration with a zener reference.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1153" title="EEVblog #1153 - 1970&#x27;s Programmable Calculator Teardown">1153</a></sup> A compact computer of 1988 vintage listed 5 V at 6 A, +12 V at 1.25 A for the sweep, +12 V at 2.1 A for the disk and −12 V at 0.5 A.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-414" title="EEVblog #414 - Apple Macintosh SE Teardown">414</a></sup> Instruments add local regulation close to the sensitive circuitry — ±20 V rails tapped for analog sections, with the shielded precision circuits fed separately.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-607" title="EEVblog #607 - Agilent B2912A Source Measure Unit SMU Teardown">607</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1154" title="EEVblog #1154 - Surprising 4K Dumpster TV Fault">1154</a></sup>

Supply sections are identifiable on sight: large inductors, clusters of bulk and bypass capacitors, wide traces, and an input and output [[capacitor]] either side of a three-terminal package.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-517" title="EEVblog #517 - Car Airbag Controller Teardown">517</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-918" title="EEVblog #918 - REPAIR: Sony Pyxis GPS">918</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-914" title="EEVblog #914 - Sony VAIO UX Micro PC Teardown">914</a></sup> A modern switching front end reads as a chip with no external MOSFETs, meaning the switches are internal.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1530" title="EEVblog 1530 - Siglent SDS2000X HD 12 Bit Oscilloscope TEARDOWN">1530</a></sup> An offline supply board carries mains input, a PTC, a [[bridge-rectifier]], high-voltage bulk capacitors, a common-mode choke, primary-side switching, a main [[transformer]] and a bank of secondary-side electrolytics.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1452" title="EEVblog 1452 - Fluke PM3370B Combiscope REPAIR">1452</a></sup>

Power capability is worth checking against the load. A base rated 15 V at 2 A shipped with a 15 V 1 A plug pack cannot work.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-924" title="EEVblog #924 - Hovering Delorean!">924</a></sup> Ten single-core boards drawing 0.75 A each require a 7.5 A supply, not whatever is to hand.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-934" title="EEVblog #934 - Raspberry Pi Supercomputer Cluster PART 1">934</a></sup> A 180 W notebook supply passing 19 V at 9.5 A does so through an ordinary barrel jack, which is a great deal of current for that connector.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-pKV_JiauAE4" title="Dumpster Dive - HP Envy Touch PC Repair">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1071" title="EEVblog #1071 - (UPDATED) Dumpster Dive HP Envy Touch PC REPAIR">1071</a></sup> Some plug packs shut down below a minimum load, so a light load can look like a fault.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1291" title="EEVblog #1291 - Mailbag">1291</a></sup>

## Failure modes and troubleshooting

The first rule of troubleshooting is "thou shalt check voltages", and the supply is the first port of call — particularly for mains hum, which arrives through the supply.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-507" title="EEVblog #507 - The First ARM Processor Computer - Acorn Archimedes A3000">507</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-400" title="EEVblog #400 - 400th Live Show">400</a></sup> Where a fault is intermittent or a display is dead, electrolytic capacitors in the supply are the leading suspect at something like 90 to 95 % probability, since their liquid dielectric dries out with use and heat while the surrounding active and passive parts remain reliable.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-347" title="EEVblog #347 - Bad Cap LCD Monitor Repair">347</a></sup>

Partial failure is more common than total failure and is more misleading. A unit can appear to work while one rail is low: 13.8 V where a 24 V [[relay]] needs drive is plenty for the microcontroller rails but will not pull in the relay, so the symptom presents as a logic fault.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1481" title="EEVblog 1481 - Dodgy Dangerous Heater REPAIR">1481</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-621" title="EEVblog #621- Stanford Research SR650 Repair - Part 2">621</a></sup> A pass regulator needs headroom — assuming a 2 V maximum dropout, a 5 V output requires at least 7 V at its input, and finding well under the expected 9 V there points at the primary side failing to deliver on that winding and probably all the others.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-804" title="EEVblog #804 - HP1740A Oscilloscope Repair - Part 2">804</a></sup> A rail specified for 6 A that draws 11 A when fed externally is dissipating that extra power somewhere, and it must be heating something.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-401" title="EEVblog #401 - Lecroy 9384C Oscilloscope Repair - Part 2">401</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-398" title="EEVblog #398 - Lecroy 9384C Oscilloscope Repair">398</a></sup>

A missing rail can stop a machine dead through supervision rather than starvation: a power-good signal derived from the rails feeds the processor's reset, so a lost negative rail holds the processor in reset even though the 5 V and 12 V rails measure fine.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1354" title="EEVblog #1354 - Compaq Portable Repair - Part 2">1354</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1348" title="EEVblog #1348 - World&#x27;s First IBM Compatible PC - The Compaq Portable">1348</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1364" title="EEVblog #1364 - Compaq Portable PSU REPAIR">1364</a></sup> Repeated restart with a fan flickering is hiccup mode, the supply retrying into an overload.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1077" title="EEVblog #1077 - Dumpster PC Repair">1077</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-OomIkXYHmTY" title="MORE Asymmetrical Dumpster PC Stacking">•</a></sup>

The supply is not always the culprit, and the distinction matters. A short on the board shutting the supply down looks identical from the front panel to a failed supply, and tantalum capacitors are a common cause.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1061" title="EEVblog #1061 - Data IO Programmer REPAIR - Part 1">1061</a></sup> If a product reaches standby, clicks its relays or paints a boot image, the supply is fundamentally working and the fault lies downstream.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1394" title="EEVblog 1394 - Onkyo Receiver Repair - Part 1">1394</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-630" title="EEVblog #630 - How To: Soniq LCD TV Troubleshooting Repair - Part 1">630</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-631" title="EEVblog #631 - How To: Soniq LCD TV Repair Part 2">631</a></sup> Where a chip has its own core supply and its on-screen display still renders, its local regulators are demonstrably fine.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1154" title="EEVblog #1154 - Surprising 4K Dumpster TV Fault">1154</a></sup>

When a supply does fail it commonly takes other things with it. A fire on a −18 V rail destroyed at least three voltage regulators on the boards it fed, and a supply fault elsewhere took out a −12 V regulator and stopped an oscillator.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-538" title="EEVblog #538 - HP35670A DSA Repair - Part 2">538</a></sup> A dodgy capacitor in a supply can plausibly have taken out a downstream reset supervisor.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-710" title="EEVblog #710 - Intercom System Repair">710</a></sup> Ageing is a slower version of the same story: drying capacitors raise ripple until it exceeds the acceptable limit at peak current, producing reboots that masquerade as thermal problems.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-eSx8MeY6V48" title="CPU Thermal Ghost - Part 2">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-yKflC-fmSX8" title="CPU Thermal Ghost">•</a></sup>

## Judging construction

Construction quality is legible from the input filtering and the capacitor brands. Correct practice on an offline supply includes a fused input, common-mode chokes, X and Y class capacitors, MOV protection, heat-shrunk mains wiring and a proper earth point.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-976" title="EEVblog #976 - Keysight 1000 X-Series Oscilloscope Teardown">976</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-462" title="EEVblog #462 - Video Editing PC Assembly">462</a></sup> Japanese electrolytics — Nippon Chemi-Con, Rubycon at 105 °C, Nichicon — indicate a supply built to last, and their presence in a cheap unit is a genuine surprise.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-587" title="EEVblog #587 - Tektronix MDO3000 Mixed Domain Oscilloscope Teardown">587</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1011" title="EEVblog #1011 - Retro Teardown - World&#x27;s First HD DVD Player">1011</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1098" title="EEVblog #1098 - 4K Video Editing PC Build">1098</a></sup> Unknown brands on the primary side are the norm at low price points.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1004" title="EEVblog #1004 - Owon XDS3202A 14bit Oscilloscope Teardown">1004</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1261" title="EEVblog #1261 - Dumpster Diving 55&quot; LCD TV">1261</a></sup> A single-sided board is standard fare in almost any consumer supply.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-462" title="EEVblog #462 - Video Editing PC Assembly">462</a></sup>

Cost optimisation shows up plainly across product generations: two instruments with identical power requirements can carry visibly different supplies, the later one optimised harder.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1355" title="EEVblog #1355 - NEW Siglent SDS1104X-U $399 4CH Oscilloscope Teardown">1355</a></sup> Smaller SMD inductors in place of large ones mean less current and less power.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1146" title="EEVblog #1146 - New Rigol MSO5000 Oscilloscope">1146</a></sup> Thermal design counts too — an open-frame supply mounted under a fan is cooled by that fan drawing air over it, and shielding the supply is a mark of a more considered layout.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1530" title="EEVblog 1530 - Siglent SDS2000X HD 12 Bit Oscilloscope TEARDOWN">1530</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1042" title="EEVblog #1042 - Siglent&#x27;s $499 SDS1104X-E 4CH Oscilloscope Teardown">1042</a></sup> Discarded supplies designed for high MTBF are worth salvaging for their chokes, protection components, optocouplers and relays even when nothing else in the machine is.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1302" title="EEVblog #1302 - Scrapping a Dumpster Laser Printer For Parts">1302</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1587" title="EEVblog 1587 - Dumpster FAX Teardown">1587</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1168" title="EEVblog #1168 - Dumpster Diving UPS">1168</a></sup>

## Powering a design

Supply routing on a [[pcb]] is not work to hand to an autorouter; power and ground traces are placed by hand and excluded from automatic routing.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-975" title="EEVblog #975 - Human vs Autorouter">975</a></sup> The supply must actually be able to deliver transient demand: twelve inputs drawing 10 to 15 mA each amount to 150 mA that has to come from somewhere, and two 100 nF capacitors will not provide it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1365" title="EEVblog #1365 - Viewer PCB &amp; Circuit Design Review">1365</a></sup> Conversely, a discharged capacitor is a [[short-circuit]] at the instant voltage is applied, which is why large bulk [[capacitance]] in a supply produces inrush and a visible spark when mains is connected.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1406" title="EEVblog 1406 - DC Fundamentals Part 7: DC Circuit Transients Fundamentals">1406</a></sup> Sensitive analog circuits impose their own requirements — a precision transimpedance front end can have its noise performance ruined by a poor supply, with even temperature hysteresis of the supply a factor, and a clock oscillator cannot simply be hung off the nearest rail.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1755" title="EEVblog 1755 - Tolicore Femtoammeter TEARDOWN + REVERSE ENGINEER">1755</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-497" title="EEVBlog #497 - Siglent SDG5000 Function Generator Teardown">497</a></sup>

A precision DC [[electronic-load]] is the natural counterpart to a supply on a design bench, and anyone designing supplies of any kind — switch mode or linear, large or small — should have one.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-281" title="EEVblog #281 - BK Precision 8500 Electronic Load Teardown">281</a></sup> Loads also measure supply rise and fall times directly, without an [[oscilloscope]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-862" title="EEVBlog #862 - BK Precision 8601 DC Electronic Load">862</a></sup> A four-channel oscilloscope earns its keep by showing four rails simultaneously and catching transients.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-804" title="EEVblog #804 - HP1740A Oscilloscope Repair - Part 2">804</a></sup>

## Safety

Power supplies are the one class of test equipment capable of killing the operator or destroying the device under test, and offline switching supplies in particular carry substantial safety considerations.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1032" title="EEVblog #1032 Part 1 - John Kenny Keysight Interview">1032</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-8JwYlFtA1HM" title="Keysight 34460/70 Multimeter Specification Issue Explained">•</a></sup> Exposed mains and a 310 W, 12 V 25 A supply sitting open inside a consumer machine make working on it live a serious hazard.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-esJN76rykfQ" title="Dumpster Diving 27&quot; iMac Upgrade">•</a></sup> A subtler trap: clipping two oscilloscope ground leads to different potentials — the positive and negative outputs of a supply — shorts the output through the probe grounds.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-279" title="EEVblog #279 - How NOT To Blow Up Your Oscilloscope!">279</a></sup>

## Building and acquiring

A power supply is the classic beginner project and among the most rewarding pieces of test gear to build, because the builder gets exactly the specification wanted and can never really have too many supplies.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-111" title="EEVblog #111 - Live Show #5">111</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-89" title="EEVblog #89 - EEVblog Live Screwup">89</a></sup> A practical route to a capable bench unit is a commodity enclosure plus a salvaged or off-the-shelf module — a 320 W industrial supply for A$83 feeding a bench-supply front end, with almost any scrapped supply serving in principle.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1265" title="EEVblog #1265 - $53 360W Lab Bench PSU!">1265</a></sup> Design work on such a project centres on behaviour that a specification sheet does not show: an output that ramps up cleanly to the set voltage and stays rock steady, rather than overshooting and settling.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-224" title="EEVblog #224 - Lab Power Supply Design - Part 3">224</a></sup> Transition behaviour between constant voltage and constant current is likewise a real differentiator between commercial units.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1402" title="EEVblog 1402 - Rohde &amp; Schwarz NGA100 PSU Teardown + GIVEAWAY">1402</a></sup>

Individual practice varies with need. One long-standing home bench pairs a battery charger good for 6 A with a supply giving up to 20 V at 2 A, protected by a push-button breaker fitted because replacing fuses became tiresome.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-874" title="Extended Cut: EEVblog #874 - Ted Wale">874</a></sup> A 3D printer builder runs a nominally 24 V supply tweaked up to 29 V to bring two heated beds, wired in series, up to temperature in acceptable time.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-520" title="EEVblog #520 - Michael&#x27;s 3D Printer">520</a></sup> For teaching laboratories the requirement is simply enough supplies to power things reliably and affordably, since [[breadboard]] work needs neither high frequency nor high accuracy.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1565" title="EEVblog 1565 - UNSW Electronics Lab &amp; Makerspace Tour">1565</a></sup>
