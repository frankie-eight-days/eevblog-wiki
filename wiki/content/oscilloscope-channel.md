An oscilloscope channel is one complete vertical acquisition path: an input BNC, its coupling selection, an attenuator and input amplifier, and — on a digital instrument — the digitiser behind it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-685" title="EEVblog #685 - What Is Oscilloscope AC Trigger Coupling?">685</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-217" title="EEVblog #217 - Lecroy 9384C Oscilloscope Teardown">217</a></sup> Each channel is a physically duplicated copy of the same circuitry, so a four-channel scope carries four nominally identical front ends laid out on the board, differing only in placement.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-565" title="EEVblog #565 - Tektronix TDS3054 Oscilloscope Repair - Part 2">565</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-217" title="EEVblog #217 - Lecroy 9384C Oscilloscope Teardown">217</a></sup> Channel count is the headline number on almost every scope [[datasheet]], but it is also the axis along which manufacturers ration price, front-panel space, [[sample-rate]], [[memory-depth]] and update rate, which makes it the single specification most likely to mislead.

## What a channel physically is

In the classic analogue arrangement each input feeds an amplifier with AC or DC coupling selection, the outputs of the channels are multiplexed to choose what gets displayed, and the selected signal drives the vertical deflection plates; that whole chain is duplicated per channel.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-685" title="EEVblog #685 - What Is Oscilloscope AC Trigger Coupling?">685</a></sup> The same structure survives in modern instruments as shielded front-end assemblies — a Tektronix 475 exposes its two-channel vertical amplifier section with per-channel adjustment trimmer caps and substantial heatsinking,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-jmb9ICnI8xI" title="Dumpster Tektronix 475 Oscilloscope Repair - Part 1">•</a></sup> a LeCroy 9384C carries four 1 GHz vertical channels with trimmers reachable through the shielding,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-217" title="EEVblog #217 - Lecroy 9384C Oscilloscope Teardown">217</a></sup> and an R&S HMO1202 puts metal cans over its two analogue channels on both sides of the board.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-879" title="EEVblog #879 - R&amp;S HMO1202 Hacking Extended Version">879</a></sup> Attenuation switching relays and JFET-style front-end devices sit immediately behind the BNC.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-879" title="EEVblog #879 - R&amp;S HMO1202 Hacking Extended Version">879</a></sup>

Channels are conventionally numbered, but two-channel instruments are often labelled A and B instead — the HP1740A and the Moku:Go both use that convention.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-803" title="EEVblog #803 - HP1740A Analog Oscilloscope">803</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1701" title="EEVblog 1701 - $599 Australian Made Moku:Go Oscilloscope REVIEW">1701</a></sup> Colour coding of channels, matched between the on-screen trace, the front-panel button and its indicator [[led]], is near universal on digital scopes.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-199" title="Tektronix Mixed Domain Oscilloscope MDO4000 Review - EEVblog #199">199</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1638" title="EEVblog 1638 - NEW Keysight Megazoom V ASIC 1GHz HD3 Oscilloscope!">1638</a></sup> Some instruments also allow a channel to be renamed from an on-screen keyboard.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1501" title="EEVblog 1501 - Rigol HDO4000 Low Noise 12bit Oscilloscope Unboxing &amp; First Impression">1501</a></sup>

## Channel count as a pricing lever

Physical channel count and enabled channel count are frequently different things. The Agilent 2000 X series ships with four channels fitted; buying the two-channel model leaves only two functional even though all four are present.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-143" title="EEVblog #143 - Agilent 2000 X Series Infiniivision Oscilloscope Review">143</a></sup> The Rigol MSO5000 goes further — all four channels are physically installed on every unit and the fourth and third are unlocked in software, at 90 dollars if bought with the scope and 399 dollars if bought later, against a base price of 909 dollars for the two-channel 70 MHz version and 999 for the four-channel.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1146" title="EEVblog #1146 - New Rigol MSO5000 Oscilloscope">1146</a></sup> By contrast the Siglent SDS2000X Plus at 999 dollars is a genuinely two-channel unit with only two channels inside it, the four-channel base model being 1,399.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1309" title="EEVblog #1309 - Siglent SDS2000X Plus Scope Teardown+Hack">1309</a></sup>

At the other end, some instruments offer no choice at all: the Tektronix MDO4000 was sold only as a four-analogue-channel machine with 16 digital channels and an RF spectrum analyser bundled in, starting at 19,900 US dollars for the 500 MHz model.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-199" title="Tektronix Mixed Domain Oscilloscope MDO4000 Review - EEVblog #199">199</a></sup> [[bandwidth]], unlike channel count, is now commonly a pure licence key with no hardware difference, to the point that some scopes carry no bandwidth marking on the front panel at all.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-879" title="EEVblog #879 - R&amp;S HMO1202 Hacking Extended Version">879</a></sup>

## Shared silicon: what turning on a channel costs

Enabling a channel almost always takes something away, and the pattern of what it takes away reveals how the acquisition hardware is partitioned. On a GW Instek GDS-2000A the record length halves when channel two is switched on but does not halve again when channel three is added, implying one device serving channels one and two and a duplicate serving three and four.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-474" title="EEVblog #474 - GW Instek GDS-2000A Series Oscilloscope Unboxing &amp; First Impression">474</a></sup> The Tektronix MDO4000 shows the mirror image in the time domain: [[waveform-update-rate]] is identical with one or two channels on, then halves to about 1.5 k [[waveform|waveforms]] per second the moment a third channel is enabled.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-199" title="Tektronix Mixed Domain Oscilloscope MDO4000 Review - EEVblog #199">199</a></sup> A Rigol DS2000 at 50 ns/div captures 700 points with two channels on and 1.4 k points with channel two off.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-369" title="EEVblog #369 - Rigol DS2000 Oscilloscope Playing Around">369</a></sup>

Sample rate is rationed the same way. An R&S MXO4 rated at 5 GSa/s runs at 2.5 GSa/s with all four channels on and returns to 5 GSa/s when channel one is turned off — and because the halving follows the interleaving groups rather than a simple count, two-channel work should be done on channels one and three to keep the full rate.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1529" title="EEVblog 1529 - R&amp;S MXO4 12bit Oscilloscope Unboxing and Experiments">1529</a></sup> The Haasoscope Pro likewise halves its sample rate with two channels enabled, which is enough to visibly change the shape of a 200 MHz [[sine-wave]] on screen.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1709" title="EEVblog 1709 - Haasoscope Pro USB Oscilloscope: An Oscilloscope Oddity">1709</a></sup> On a Tektronix 2 Series, adding channels beyond the first lengthens the dead time between acquisition bursts rather than reducing the peak update figure.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1478" title="EEVblog 1478 - Waveform Update Rate Shootout - Tek 2 Series vs Others">1478</a></sup>

Software can impose its own channel constraints. An HMO1202 will only run its quick-view measurement set on one channel at a time once a second channel is displayed.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-842" title="EEVblog #842 - Rohde &amp; Schwarz HMO1202 Oscilloscope Teardown">842</a></sup> A Uni-T UPO2104CS in independent-time-base mode forces all four channels on and disables channel switch-off entirely.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1038" title="EEVblog #1038 - Uni-T UPO2104CS Oscilloscope Teardown">1038</a></sup> On a Rigol, turning channel one off destroys the math function's data, so the workaround is to leave the channel enabled and move its trace off the visible window using the full-display mode.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1188" title="EEVblog #1188 - $10 DIY EMC Probe using Scope FFT">1188</a></sup>

## Front-panel controls per channel

Whether each channel gets its own volts-per-division and position knobs, or whether one vertical control block is shared and switched between channels, is a recurring design trade-off, and dedicated per-channel controls are the better engineering choice where the form factor permits.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-143" title="EEVblog #143 - Agilent 2000 X Series Infiniivision Oscilloscope Review">143</a></sup> Four separate vertical control groups lined up directly above their own BNCs, with nothing else cluttering the column, represent the ideal layout.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-655" title="EEVblog #655 - Auction Score">655</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-797" title="EEVblog #797 - Siglent SDS1000X Oscilloscope Review">797</a></sup> Fitting four such groups onto a compact scope is possible — the Rigol MSO5000 manages it while remaining only slightly larger than a 1054Z despite a bigger touchscreen.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1146" title="EEVblog #1146 - New Rigol MSO5000 Oscilloscope">1146</a></sup>

The compromise is usually forced by screen size. The Siglent SDS2000X Plus moved from four independent vertical channels to a single combined vertical control specifically to fit a 10.1-inch screen in a sane form factor,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1309" title="EEVblog #1309 - Siglent SDS2000X Plus Scope Teardown+Hack">1309</a></sup> as did the SDS1000X HD, on the grounds that the alternative would make the instrument absurdly wide.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1612" title="EEVblog 1612 - Siglent SDS1000X HD 12 bit Oscilloscope Teardown">1612</a></sup> The Rigol DHO800 does the same for the same reason, with much of the interaction moved to the touchscreen.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1566" title="EEVblog 1566 - Rigol DHO800 12 Bit Oscilloscope FULL REVIEW">1566</a></sup> A separate casualty of high channel counts is the external trigger input, which many four-channel scopes sacrifice; a LeCroy Wavejet Touch 354 keeps four channels plus a front-panel external trigger,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-792" title="EEVBlog #792 - Lecroy Wavejet Touch 354 Oscilloscope Review">792</a></sup> whereas the MDO4000 dropped it in favour of the RF input, meaning an external analogue trigger costs one of the four channels.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-199" title="Tektronix Mixed Domain Oscilloscope MDO4000 Review - EEVblog #199">199</a></sup>

## Multiplexing, extra traces and channel arithmetic

Analogue scopes with a single beam must share it between channels, and the sharing mode is tied to sweep speed. A Kikusui storage scope runs in chop mode at 1 ms/div and slower, literally alternating between drawing channel one and channel two within a sweep, and switches to alternate mode — a whole sweep per channel in turn — at 500 µs/div and faster, because at slow sweeps alternate mode would make waveform comparison unworkable.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1716" title="EEVblog 1716 - University Dumpster Diving: Kikusui Oscilloscope">1716</a></sup>

Beyond the nominal channels, older instruments extracted extra traces from the same hardware. A Kikusui COS 6100M put twelve traces on screen: four channels of which two were full-specification vertical and two limited, each expandable [[via]] a [[oscilloscope-timebase|dual time base]] to double the trace count, plus a trigger view.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1194" title="EEVblog #1194 - 1985 Electronics">1194</a></sup> Trigger view as a de facto extra channel is not purely historical — some scopes can display the trigger system's signal as a third channel, digital only and without a full analogue front end, a capability rarely advertised.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1418" title="EEVblog 1418 - The Most EMBARRASSING Repair!">1418</a></sup> Channel arithmetic is similarly asymmetric on analogue designs: invert is typically provided on channel two only, since inverting channel two and using add mode is how subtraction is performed.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-502" title="EEVblog #502 - $19 Hameg Analog Oscilloscope">502</a></sup>

Channel assignment also carries semantic weight in measurements. In XY mode channel one is the X channel and channel two the Y channel, plotting one [[voltage]] against the other to form a Lissajous figure.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1751" title="EEVblog 1751 - Oscilloscope Phase Measurement Masterclass">1751</a></sup> Automatic phase measurement requires source A and source B to be set explicitly, and swapping which channel occupies which slot flips the sign of the result — a −33° reading becomes +33° purely by exchanging the sources, so the operator must know which signal is the reference before trusting the number.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1751" title="EEVblog 1751 - Oscilloscope Phase Measurement Masterclass">1751</a></sup> Viewing a signal on channel two often also requires changing the trigger source to that channel.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1235" title="EEVblog #1235 - How To Align Signals On A Digital Oscilloscope">1235</a></sup> Multiple channels are what make comparative probing possible at all: driving a [[mosfet]] gate on channel one and the supply output on channel two shows the switching relationship directly,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-512" title="EEVblog #512 - Rigol DP832 Bad Design Investigation">512</a></sup> and two fully isolated channels allow two different points to be probed simultaneously without common-ground constraints.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-804" title="EEVblog #804 - HP1740A Oscilloscope Repair - Part 2">804</a></sup>

## Channel faults and diagnosis

Because channels are duplicated, a fault confined to one of them is both common and comparatively tractable. When repairing a two-channel instrument it is worth hoping only one channel has failed, since the surviving channel provides a physical probe-by-probe comparison reference.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1418" title="EEVblog 1418 - The Most EMBARRASSING Repair!">1418</a></sup> The same logic applies to layout: on a four-channel scope the channels use the same components even where their board placement differs, so a suspect part can be [[resistance]]-checked against its counterpart on a working channel — a shorted SOT-23 on one channel that measures normally on the others localises the fault immediately.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-565" title="EEVblog #565 - Tektronix TDS3054 Oscilloscope Repair - Part 2">565</a></sup>

The first thing to check on a channel-specific fault is the BNC itself, which is mechanically exposed and vulnerable to force on second-hand and ex-military instruments.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-564" title="EEVblog #564 - Tektronix TDS3054 Oscilloscope Teardown Repair">564</a></sup> Beyond that, symptoms vary. A Tektronix DPO can have a dead channel three that nonetheless passes all internal self-tests, indicating a front-end failure the diagnostics do not cover.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-559" title="EEVblog #559 - Auction Score">559</a></sup> Feeding one identical signal into four 50-ohm-terminated inputs and getting a different amplitude on channel four is unambiguous evidence of a broken channel.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1127" title="EEVblog #1127 - Mailbag Monday">1127</a></sup> A TDS540D showed significant vertical offset across several channels at once.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1515" title="EEVblog 1515 - Dumpster Tektronix TDS540D 500MHz Oscilloscope LCD Upgrade">1515</a></sup>

Symmetry across channels is itself diagnostic. When a Keysight 1000X developed a [[dc-offset]] confined to the 200 mV and 500 mV ranges, the fact that both channels behaved identically ruled out a physical hardware failure in any one channel and pointed elsewhere.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-OBOgiMA89Ks" title="My Keysight 1000X Oscilloscope FAILED :-(">•</a></sup> Conversely a defect that appears on one channel but not the other — an artefact visible on channel one but not reproducible on channel two — supports a channel-one front-end fault.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-2FlQnPsZCdE" title="Scope WTF!">•</a></sup> [[firmware]] and acquisition bugs can also present as channel behaviour: switching off channel three on an R&S RTB2004 produces garbage on the display associated with channel four, traceable to an acquisition bug rather than the channel hardware.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-Ott9syzNLuE" title="Rohde &amp; Schwarz RTB2004 Acquisition Bug Part 2">•</a></sup>

A scope with a dead channel is not scrap. Three functional channels out of four leaves an instrument still worth a substantial sum,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-559" title="EEVblog #559 - Auction Score">559</a></sup> and even a single working channel is a useful scope.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-g2kCBfUa9H0" title="Will this $5 Oscilloscope Work?">•</a></sup> The uninteresting failures are the ones that take everything with them — a Fluke 196 Scopemeter with nothing at all on either channel points to multiple independent faults rather than a channel-level problem.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-808" title="EEVblog #808 - Fluke 196 Scopemeter Repair">808</a></sup>
