Silkscreen, also called the component overlay or legend overlay, is the printed layer of text and graphics on a [[pcb|printed circuit board]] that carries component designators, values, polarity marks, logos, and assembly or service information. The term survives from the era when the markings were literally applied through a silk screen; modern boards use other processes, but the name persists: "It's not the process, but it's still called silkscreen."<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-zHpsA1RtQhw" title="How To Get 5 Different &quot;Colours&quot; On A PCB">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-990" title="EEVblog #990 - Getting The PCB Manufactured (Nixie PART 5)">990</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1471" title="EEVblog 1471 - Mailbag">1471</a></sup> Its practical value is large relative to its cost: it makes boards buildable without a [[schematic]], probe-able without a [[service-manual|service manual]], and cheaper to assemble correctly.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-555" title="EEVblog #555 - 555 Timer Kit">555</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1394" title="EEVblog 1394 - Onkyo Receiver Repair - Part 1">1394</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1185" title="EEVblog #1185 - Dumpster Tek 500MHz TDS540D Scope">1185</a></sup>

## Fabrication processes

Two application processes dominate. Photoimageable overlay is a photo-lithographic process applied and exposed like [[solder-mask|solder mask]], and it produces the sharpest result.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-817" title="EEVblog #817 - Mantis Elite Cam Inspection Microscope">817</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-939" title="EEVblog #939 - How Is A PCB Manufactured?">939</a></sup> The cheaper alternative is dot-matrix (inkjet-style) printing, in which individual dots and line artefacts are visible under magnification; low-cost prototype services predominantly use it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-817" title="EEVblog #817 - Mantis Elite Cam Inspection Microscope">817</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-558" title="EEVblog #558 - Beta Layout DIY SMD Thermal Reflow Oven">558</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-997" title="EEVblog #997 - How To Solder Surface Mount Components">997</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-475" title="EEVblog #475 - GW Instek GDS-2000A Oscilloscope Teardown">475</a></sup> The difference between the two is plainly visible side by side, and photoimageable output is preferred when the silkscreen must look professional, for example on a board used as a front panel.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-241" title="EEVblog #241 - Circuit Labs PCBs from New Zealand">241</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-817" title="EEVblog #817 - Mantis Elite Cam Inspection Microscope">817</a></sup>

A board received with blurred or smeared silkscreen is a manufacturing defect that a fabricator would ordinarily replace.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-562" title="EEVblog #562 - More SMD Oven Reflow">562</a></sup> Silkscreen alignment and registration can be checked under a microscope along with the other layers.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-Ep4r-wD7PPs" title="KiCAD PCB Design">•</a></sup>

## Colours and artwork

White is the default legend colour, but manufacturers typically offer a selection — half a dozen colours or more — and multi-colour legends are applied as multiple passes, one process per extra colour.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-zHpsA1RtQhw" title="How To Get 5 Different &quot;Colours&quot; On A PCB">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1471" title="EEVblog 1471 - Mailbag">1471</a></sup> Some low-cost services restrict the choice to white or black, and at least one fabricator sensibly refuses a black legend on matte black solder mask.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1259" title="EEVblog #1259 - PCB Manufacturing Options EXPLAINED">1259</a></sup> Matte black solder mask with white silkscreen gives notably better contrast than gloss black.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-555" title="EEVblog #555 - 555 Timer Kit">555</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-460" title="EEVblog #460 - Mailbag - Plasma Speaker">460</a></sup>

Arbitrary artwork on the silkscreen layer costs nothing extra, because the pattern makes no difference to the mask process; elaborate graphics, logos, and decorative imagery are effectively free as long as no additional production step is introduced.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1229" title="EEVblog #1229 - Mailbag">1229</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1254" title="EEVblog #1254 - Mailbag">1254</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-266" title="EEVblog #266 - Mailbag">266</a></sup> Additional apparent "colours" can be produced by combining solder mask, silkscreen, and exposed copper or plating in the artwork.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-zHpsA1RtQhw" title="How To Get 5 Different &quot;Colours&quot; On A PCB">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1254" title="EEVblog #1254 - Mailbag">1254</a></sup>

## Layout practice

Silkscreen designators should be placed outside component outlines rather than under parts, so they remain readable after assembly — particularly important on single-sided kits.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-244" title="EEVblog #244 - How To Lay Out A PCB - PSU Design Part 9">244</a></sup> Where density makes adjacent placement impossible, the accepted technique is to place the designator in open space with a silkscreen line pointing to the part.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-720" title="EEVblog #720 - Mailbag">720</a></sup> Placing designators and markings on the bottom side of a board is a recognised serviceability measure: it lets every component be identified and measured from the accessible face when a board mounts component-side-down.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-208" title="Tektronix 2225 Oscilloscope Teardown and Calibration - EEVblog #208">208</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-452" title="EEVblog #452 - Stanford Research SR430 Teardown">452</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-628" title="EEVblog #628 - Tektronix 213 Vintage Portable Oscilloscope Teardown">628</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1322" title="EEVblog #1322 - JBL LSR308 Monitor Speaker Repair (FULL)">1322</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-8blgmbXfDEc" title="New Dumpster Diving Monitor Score">•</a></sup>

Silkscreen over pads is a genuine defect, not a cosmetic one: a pad covered by legend ink cannot be wetted by [[solder]], leaving the pin unconnected, and this has been observed as the actual fault in failed products.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1181" title="EEVblog #1181 - Car ECO OBD2 OptiFuel Fuel Saver SCAM!">1181</a></sup> The standard final checks before release therefore include inspecting the board in the CAD 3D view — which renders the silkscreen exactly as it will appear — specifically to catch legend over pads or vias, supplemented by design-rule checks.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-244" title="EEVblog #244 - How To Lay Out A PCB - PSU Design Part 9">244</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-111" title="EEVblog #111 - Live Show #5">111</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1193" title="EEVblog #1193 - KiCAD PCB 4 Layer Swapping &amp; Stackup">1193</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-990" title="EEVblog #990 - Getting The PCB Manufactured (Nixie PART 5)">990</a></sup> On one-off or hobby boards, silk-to-silk clearance and minor overlaps are routinely waived as not worth the effort.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-974" title="EEVblog #974 - PCB Layout Walkthrough - PART 4">974</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-245" title="EEVblog #245 - PSU Design Part 10 - PCB Layout Editing">245</a></sup>

Other established layout conventions:

- Silkscreen that extends past the board edge is simply chopped off by the fabricator; keeping a true-size outline of a [[heat-sink|heatsink]] or similar part on the overlay aids mechanical fit checking, though a mechanical layer is the more professional place for it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-244" title="EEVblog #244 - How To Lay Out A PCB - PSU Design Part 9">244</a></sup>
- The silkscreen component outline can serve as the component's size definition for spacing checks.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-244" title="EEVblog #244 - How To Lay Out A PCB - PSU Design Part 9">244</a></sup>
- Fiducials must not be placed on the silkscreen layer, because the legend can be offset relative to the copper layers; fiducials belong in copper.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1512" title="EEVblog 1512 - Why Bypass Your PCB Like THIS?">1512</a></sup>
- Polarised and keyed parts depend on the legend: [[transistor]] flats are drawn on the silkscreen so the package can be oriented correctly, and a wrong silkscreen flat will cause backwards insertion — though a wrong footprint is worse.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-aQ2AVLs8_7k" title="CB2 Microcomputer Kit Assembly &amp; Soldering">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-555" title="EEVblog #555 - 555 Timer Kit">555</a></sup>
- Pin-1 identifiers should be marked on connectors and board interconnects.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-661" title="EEVblog #661 - Mailbag">661</a></sup>
- Component values printed directly on the legend let a kit be assembled with no reference to the schematic.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-555" title="EEVblog #555 - 555 Timer Kit">555</a></sup>
- Decimal points are easily lost in small printed legends, so the convention of replacing the point with the unit letter — 6R2 for 6.2 Ω — is used.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1617" title="EEVblog 1617 - Architectural LED Lighting Build + Test">1617</a></sup>
- Regulatory markings such as the UL stamp should be added to the silkscreen by the designer rather than requested through a fabricator's checkout option.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1259" title="EEVblog #1259 - PCB Manufacturing Options EXPLAINED">1259</a></sup>
- Fabricators that add their own batch codes or file numbers to the legend are a persistent annoyance, especially when the board serves as a front panel; some fabs provide a checkout option to suppress the markings after customers complain.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-241" title="EEVblog #241 - Circuit Labs PCBs from New Zealand">241</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-997" title="EEVblog #997 - How To Solder Surface Mount Components">997</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-155" title="EEVblog #155 - ITead Studio PCB Prototype Goof">155</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1471" title="EEVblog 1471 - Mailbag">1471</a></sup>
- Putting the designer's name or initials on the silkscreen is recommended practice even when an employer does not require it — there is always room somewhere on the board.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1313" title="EEVblog #1313 - Bad Luck Mailbag">1313</a></sup>

## The legend as documentation

A well-used silkscreen substitutes for documentation at the bench. Marked test points with labelled rail voltages allow a board to be diagnosed without a service manual or schematic.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1185" title="EEVblog #1185 - Dumpster Tek 500MHz TDS540D Scope">1185</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1394" title="EEVblog 1394 - Onkyo Receiver Repair - Part 1">1394</a></sup> Full pinouts printed beside connectors serve the same purpose, and their absence on boards that clearly had room for them is a recurring criticism.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1394" title="EEVblog 1394 - Onkyo Receiver Repair - Part 1">1394</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1420" title="EEVblog 1420 - Mailbag">1420</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1069" title="EEVblog #1069 - Mailbag">1069</a></sup> On vintage equipment, legends identifying functional blocks, chip identities, and high-[[voltage]] hazards ("danger 1,200 V") were standard practice and greatly ease repair.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-788" title="EEVblog #788 - Apple IIC Teardown">788</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-863" title="EEVblog #863 - Sony D50 Discman Teardown - World&#x27;s First Portable CD Player">863</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-261" title="EEVblog #261 - Marconi 2023 RF Signal Generator Teardown">261</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-628" title="EEVblog #628 - Tektronix 213 Vintage Portable Oscilloscope Teardown">628</a></sup>

The legend also carries manufacturing intent. Assembly instructions such as cable colours have been printed directly on the board for production operators.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-925" title="EEVblog #925 - Panasonic CF-U1 ToughBook Teardown">925</a></sup> A large arrow indicating board travel direction through [[wave-soldering|wave soldering]] is a recognised designer's marking, since component orientation relative to the wave affects solder quality.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-431" title="EEVblog #431 - PCB Wave Soldering">431</a></sup> Silkscreen lines marking the primary–secondary barrier and creepage paths on mains equipment delineate the safety isolation boundary.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-388" title="EEVblog #388 - Fake Apple USB Charger Teardown">388</a></sup> When a board is known at design time to need wire links, printing the link path on the legend shows the intent even if production deviates from it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1216" title="EEVblog #1216 - PCB Layout + FPGA Deep Dive">1216</a></sup>

Silkscreen also functions as evidence. The legend recording a designer's intent — for example a footprint marked for an RF bead later found stuffed with a zero-ohm [[resistor]] — reveals design history during [[troubleshooting]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-987" title="EEVblog #987 - Keysight U1272A Multimeter EMC Followup">987</a></sup> Conversely, pin-function markings on the legend can expose that a published schematic does not match the shipped PCB revision.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-XUyjRm1Upjs" title="That moment you realise the Schematic &amp; PCB don&#x27;t match">•</a></sup>

## Cost and ordering considerations

Silkscreen is included essentially for free on standard prototype panels — top and bottom overlays typically come as part of the base specification — so omitting it saves little, and a second side costs only cents.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-155" title="EEVblog #155 - ITead Studio PCB Prototype Goof">155</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1262" title="EEVblog #1262 - Designing a Flex PCB + uSupply Update">1262</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1306" title="EEVblog #1306 (1 of 5): 3 Cent Micro - Open Source Programmer">1306</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-237" title="EEVblog #237 - Makerbot Thing-O-Matic Unboxing">237</a></sup> Deliberately omitting the legend is a recognised cost-saving measure only at the extreme bottom end of manufacturing, measured in fractions of a cent per board; skipping it on an expensive instrument makes no sense.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-813" title="EEVblog #813 - Mailbag">813</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-536" title="EEVblog #536 - HP35670A DSA Repair">536</a></sup> Changes to silkscreen artwork late in a product's life are cheap compared to changes requiring new tooling.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-e9cpKN69Avk" title="121GW Development History">•</a></sup> For boards that will never be seen, such as the underside of a densely loaded assembly, designers routinely decline the bottom overlay.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-974" title="EEVblog #974 - PCB Layout Walkthrough - PART 4">974</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1193" title="EEVblog #1193 - KiCAD PCB 4 Layer Swapping &amp; Stackup">1193</a></sup>

Because the legend is included anyway, a PCB ordered as an enclosure front panel gains fully labelled controls for the price of the board — a widely used alternative to machined and printed metal panels.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-237" title="EEVblog #237 - Makerbot Thing-O-Matic Unboxing">237</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-130" title="EEVblog #130 - The µCalc Credit Card Scientific Calculator / Computer">130</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-11" title="EEVblog #11 Part 2 of 2 - More on DIY product design">11</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-644" title="EEVblog #644 - How To Design Front Panels On Extruded Enclosures - µSupply Part 14">644</a></sup> [[gerber|Gerber]] output for manufacture must explicitly include the top (and, if used, bottom) overlay layers alongside copper and solder mask, and the plotted Gerbers should be inspected before release.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-990" title="EEVblog #990 - Getting The PCB Manufactured (Nixie PART 5)">990</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1193" title="EEVblog #1193 - KiCAD PCB 4 Layer Swapping &amp; Stackup">1193</a></sup>
