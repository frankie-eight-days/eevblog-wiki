A differential pair is two [[pcb|PCB]] traces carrying complementary signals, routed together as a matched unit so that the receiver reads the difference between them rather than either one against [[ground]]. It is the default signalling scheme for essentially every high-speed interface on a modern board — DDR memory, PCI Express, [[usb|USB]], LVDS display links, gigabit Ethernet, and the links between an [[oscilloscope]]'s analog front end and its ADC.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-587" title="EEVblog #587 - Tektronix MDO3000 Mixed Domain Oscilloscope Teardown">587</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-462" title="EEVblog #462 - Video Editing PC Assembly">462</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-340" title="EEVblog #340 - USB 3.0 Eye Diagram Measurement">340</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-631" title="EEVblog #631 - How To: Soniq LCD TV Repair Part 2">631</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1216" title="EEVblog #1216 - PCB Layout + FPGA Deep Dive">1216</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1530" title="EEVblog 1530 - Siglent SDS2000X HD 12 Bit Oscilloscope TEARDOWN">1530</a></sup> Because the pair is only as good as the match between its two halves, the routing rules that govern it — equal length, controlled [[impedance]], an uninterrupted reference plane — dominate the layout of any board that uses them, and their visible signature is the serpentine wiggle found across dense multilayer boards.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1247" title="EEVblog #1247 - DDR Memory PCB Propagation Delay &amp; Layout">1247</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-860" title="EEVblog #860 - Mailbag">860</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-720" title="EEVblog #720 - Mailbag">720</a></sup>

## Length matching and skew

The defining constraint is timing. The two traces of a pair must arrive together, and where several pairs carry one bus, the pairs must arrive together as well. This produces two distinct kinds of serpentine trace: wiggles added *within* a pair to equalise its two halves, and wiggles added *between* pairs, or between single-ended traces such as D0 to D7 and A0 to A7 on a memory bus.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1247" title="EEVblog #1247 - DDR Memory PCB Propagation Delay &amp; Layout">1247</a></sup> On a DDR bus the pair is a signal such as DQS3 positive and negative, and the matching target is on the order of a millimetre or two of length difference across the group.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1247" title="EEVblog #1247 - DDR Memory PCB Propagation Delay &amp; Layout">1247</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-587" title="EEVblog #587 - Tektronix MDO3000 Mixed Domain Oscilloscope Teardown">587</a></sup>

Geometry forces the work. Where a bundle of pairs fans out around a curve, the pair on the inside of the bend is naturally shorter than the pair on the outside, so the inner pairs need progressively more wiggles to catch up; the traces stop at staggered points until the inside pair is exactly the same length as the outside pair and the timing between all the data is identical.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-781" title="EEVblog #781 - Samsung LCD TV Part 2">781</a></sup> The same trick appears at every scale, from a bundle of LVDS lines on a TV [[processor]] board down to a single wiggle beside one trace on a compute module carrier.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-781" title="EEVblog #781 - Samsung LCD TV Part 2">781</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-E1IqcGcZKHE" title="RPi CM4 Compute Module FAILURE Part 2: Thermal Boogaloo">•</a></sup>

The underlying discipline is a timing budget, and it is the reason autorouting is inappropriate on such buses: "don't auto route. Manually route."<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1247" title="EEVblog #1247 - DDR Memory PCB Propagation Delay &amp; Layout">1247</a></sup> Autorouting remains defensible on a dense but low-speed digital board where there are no high-speed design rules, no differential pairs, and [[signal-integrity|signal integrity]] between chips is not critical.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-975" title="EEVblog #975 - Human vs Autorouter">975</a></sup> Length matching is occasionally even done as a rework — added wiggles cut into an existing board, sharp corners and all, to bring a pair back into match.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1079" title="EEVblog #1079 - Mailbag">1079</a></sup> The Saturn [[pcb-design|PCB design]] toolkit is a free calculator covering differential pairs alongside [[via]] [[resistance]], crosstalk, conductor impedance and pad stacks.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1216" title="EEVblog #1216 - PCB Layout + FPGA Deep Dive">1216</a></sup>

## Impedance and reference planes

A differential pair is normally a controlled-impedance structure, and this is visible in the copper. The traces are noticeably fatter than ordinary signal routing, which is a reliable giveaway when reverse-engineering an unfamiliar board — wide traces running as a pair, with a [[ground-plane|ground plane]] beneath them, are a controlled-impedance pair.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-564" title="EEVblog #564 - Tektronix TDS3054 Oscilloscope Teardown Repair">564</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-565" title="EEVblog #565 - Tektronix TDS3054 Oscilloscope Repair - Part 2">565</a></sup> Pairs are also held at a fixed minimum spacing from one another, with a ground plane layer directly underneath.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-462" title="EEVblog #462 - Video Editing PC Assembly">462</a></sup>

The reference planes are a layer-count driver. On a ten-layer board, the high-speed differential gigabit Ethernet routing on an inner signal layer required a further ground layer beneath it, and the need for multiple ground layers is what pushed the stack-up to ten in the first place.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1216" title="EEVblog #1216 - PCB Layout + FPGA Deep Dive">1216</a></sup> Grounds are sometimes also run in the plane of the signal, flanking each side of a pair going into an ADC.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1639" title="EEVblog 1639 - Keysight InfiniiVision HD3 Oscilloscope Teardown">1639</a></sup> That flanking is ambiguous evidence, however: two closely spaced traces separated by ground may equally be two single-ended channels rather than one pair, and the presence of the separating ground is itself a reason to doubt the differential reading.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1639" title="EEVblog 1639 - Keysight InfiniiVision HD3 Oscilloscope Teardown">1639</a></sup>

Controlled impedance is not always required. On a short flex prototype carrying USB, the pair is marked as a differential pair in the [[schematic]] and routed as one, but controlled impedance and length matching can be skipped because the link is not high-speed in any demanding sense.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1262" title="EEVblog #1262 - Designing a Flex PCB + uSupply Update">1262</a></sup>

## Interfaces built on differential pairs

USB 3.0 adds two extra high-speed pairs, one transmit and one receive, on the outside of the [[connector]], carrying 5 Gb/s; these sit alongside the backward-compatible USB 2.0 single pair and its 5 V power.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-340" title="EEVblog #340 - USB 3.0 Eye Diagram Measurement">340</a></sup> USB-C carries five differential pairs, bidirectional, each capable of 400 Mb/s, plus power sufficient to charge a phone.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1753" title="EEVblog 1753 - Designing a 2000V Isolated Oscilloscope (Cleverscope)">1753</a></sup> PCI Express routing fans out from the chipset to the slots as pairs at minimum spacing.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-462" title="EEVblog #462 - Video Editing PC Assembly">462</a></sup> Ethernet pairs run matched-length from the MagJack — which integrates the isolation transformers and termination resistors that were once separate parts — straight into the host [[microcontroller]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-145" title="EEVblog #145 - Agilent LAN/VGA Module Teardown">145</a></sup>

In flat-panel displays the LVDS link between the processor board and the T-con board is a bundle of pairs, on the order of eight or sixteen, carried over a [[ribbon-cable|ribbon cable]]; the T-con takes the incoming LVDS pairs, performs timing and control, and emits further pairs to drive the panel itself.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-781" title="EEVblog #781 - Samsung LCD TV Part 2">781</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-631" title="EEVblog #631 - How To: Soniq LCD TV Repair Part 2">631</a></sup> The same arrangement carries video to the screen in bench instruments and to [[lcd-driver|LCD driver]] boards in office equipment.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-842" title="EEVblog #842 - Rohde &amp; Schwarz HMO1202 Oscilloscope Teardown">842</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1100" title="EEVblog #1100 - Dumpster Photocopier Repair">1100</a></sup> In display-controller datasheets the word *lane* denotes exactly this: a lane is physically a differential pair, a high-speed data path in copper.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1154" title="EEVblog #1154 - Surprising 4K Dumpster TV Fault">1154</a></sup>

Because the pairs are the interconnect, their absence is diagnostic. Where two large chips on a board show no differential routing and no parallel bus between them on any visible layer, the high-speed interface must be on inner layers or must not exist as assumed.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-875" title="EEVblog #875 - NI VirtualBench Teardown">875</a></sup> Conversely, on a display fault where menus render but image data does not, a bundle of pairs between processor and T-con is a prime suspect precisely because a whole-bus failure would produce widespread corruption rather than a selective one.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-781" title="EEVblog #781 - Samsung LCD TV Part 2">781</a></sup>

## Oscilloscope signal paths

Inside a [[digital-oscilloscope|digital oscilloscope]] the analog front end ends in a differential driver: the programmable gain amplifier converts the single-ended, ground-referenced input into a differential output and drives it down a pair into the ADC.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1530" title="EEVblog 1530 - Siglent SDS2000X HD 12 Bit Oscilloscope TEARDOWN">1530</a></sup> This is a deliberate choice of part — a 1 GHz differential driver at the end of the variable gain amp, with a test point on the pair so the path can be characterised in production.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-976" title="EEVblog #976 - Keysight 1000 X-Series Oscilloscope Teardown">976</a></sup> The pair layout is often the clearest map of the instrument's architecture: following the controlled-impedance serpentine pairs from an acquisition [[asic|ASIC]] reveals which channels it actually serves, and which of two similar ASICs is master and which is slave.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-148" title="EEVblog #148 - Agilent 3000 X Series Infiniivision Oscilloscope Teardown">148</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-144" title="EEVblog #144 - Agilent 2000 X Series Infiniivision Oscilloscope Teardown">144</a></sup>

Pair counts read directly as sample-rate architecture. A scope with a single ADC serving four channels shows four length-matched pairs converging on that one converter, and the missing second ADC on a cheaper variant explains a halving of [[sample-rate|sample rate]] from four to two gigasamples per second.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1563" title="EEVblog 1563 - New $389 12bit Rigol DHO800 Scope TEARDOWN">1563</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1510" title="EEVblog 1510 - $699 Rigol 12bit HDO1000 Teardown - WOAH!">1510</a></sup> Where two pairs emerge from one front-end block, the design is switching between converters to double the sample rate, with LVDS pairs on the output.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1503" title="EEVblog 1503 - Rigol HDO4000 12bit Oscilloscope TEARDOWN">1503</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1477" title="EEVblog 1477 - TEARDOWN! - NEW Tektronix 2 Series Oscilloscope">1477</a></sup> A minimalist 500 MHz front end may amount to little more than a single pair going straight into the ADC.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1124" title="EEVblog #1124 - Rigol 7000 Oscilloscope Teardown">1124</a></sup>

The scheme also structures faults. In one Tektronix hybrid amplifier the chip splits the signal into high-[[frequency]] and low-frequency components and drives two separate differential pairs to the ADC, which localises a channel failure: probing showed the same signal present on both pairs of a good channel, isolating the defect on the faulty channel to one of the two paths.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-565" title="EEVblog #565 - Tektronix TDS3054 Oscilloscope Repair - Part 2">565</a></sup> Identifying the differential output on such a hybrid is straightforward — two pins, a pair of coupling capacitors, and two conspicuously fat traces heading for the ADC.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-564" title="EEVblog #564 - Tektronix TDS3054 Oscilloscope Teardown Repair">564</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-565" title="EEVblog #565 - Tektronix TDS3054 Oscilloscope Repair - Part 2">565</a></sup> Elsewhere a pair emerges with 200 Ω series resistors, indicating a clock rather than data.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-824" title="EEVblog #824 - GW Instek GDS-1000B Oscilloscope Teardown">824</a></sup>

## The circuit-level sense of the term

The same phrase denotes the analog topology: two matched transistors sharing a common emitter [[resistor]]. Small [[transistor]] arrays such as the MC3346 package a differential pair together with a couple of discrete transistors on one die, chosen over discrete parts for thermal matching among other reasons.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-208" title="Tektronix 2225 Oscilloscope Teardown and Calibration - EEVblog #208">208</a></sup> In precision instruments a matched pair device is given extraordinary treatment — the SSM2212 in a 7.5-digit [[multimeter]] sits on a completely isolated island of board, with differential traces in and vias carrying the signal away, so that no [[leakage-current|leakage current]] can flow into or out of it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-731" title="EEVblog #731 - Keithley DMM7510 7.5 Digit Multimeter Teardown">731</a></sup>

This dual meaning is a trap when reverse-engineering. A cluster of traces that resembles a differential pair with a common tail resistor may turn out, on tracing the copper further, to be nothing of the kind.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-714" title="EEVblog #714 - Metal Detector Reverse Engineering">714</a></sup> Two traces routed side by side and dragged together in the layout tool are not necessarily a pair either — routing them as a unit can simply be convenience, since they head to the same part of the board and the software maintains the spacing.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-245" title="EEVblog #245 - PSU Design Part 10 - PCB Layout Editing">245</a></sup> And a device drawn in a [[datasheet]] as a pair, such as the 4052 dual four-way multiplexer, is drawn that way because it is convenient for switching signals in pairs; there is nothing differential inside the chip.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1611" title="EEVblog 1611 - Top 5 Jellybean 7400 &amp; 4000 Logic">1611</a></sup>
