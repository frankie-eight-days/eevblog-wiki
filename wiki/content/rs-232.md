RS-232 is an asynchronous [[serial-port|serial interface]] standard defined by its own bipolar signalling levels, carried over a UART and terminated at each end by a dedicated line driver/receiver chip.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1061" title="EEVblog #1061 - Data IO Programmer REPAIR - Part 1">1061</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-587" title="EEVblog #587 - Tektronix MDO3000 Mixed Domain Oscilloscope Teardown">587</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-710" title="EEVblog #710 - Intercom System Repair">710</a></sup> It became the default control and data path for test instruments, industrial equipment, point-of-sale hardware and personal computers, and survives in [[current]] gear long after [[usb|USB]] and Ethernet displaced it for bulk data.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-655" title="EEVblog #655 - Auction Score">655</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1023" title="EEVblog #1023 - Rigol DL3021 Electronic Load Teardown">1023</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1693" title="EEVblog 1693 - Uni-T UTE310 Power Meter Teardown &amp; Practical Demo">1693</a></sup> Its persistence is largely practical: an asynchronous UART link is trivially cheap to isolate, easy to decode, and needs no host-side stack, which are exactly the properties that matter in an instrument or a service port.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1032" title="EEVblog #1032 Part 5 - John Kenny Keysight Interview">1032</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-478" title="EEVblog #478 - Keithley 2015 THD Multimeter Teardown">478</a></sup>

## Signalling levels

RS-232 uses bipolar levels rather than logic levels; measured at a transmitting port the idle and mark states sit at roughly plus and minus five and a half volts.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1061" title="EEVblog #1061 - Data IO Programmer REPAIR - Part 1">1061</a></sup> A logic-level UART pin is therefore not an RS-232 port, and a TTL-only tool such as a bus pirate cannot be connected directly to one — the signal must pass through a transceiver in either direction.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-485" title="EEVblog #485 - Agilent TrueVolt 34461A Multimeter Teardown">485</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-CK5nbC_dBWk" title="Siglent Signal Generator FAIL">•</a></sup> The reverse trap is equally common: a serial header found on a [[pcb|PCB]] is usually at TTL levels and must not be wired to a PC's serial port without conversion.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-977" title="EEVblog #977 - Keysight 1000X Hacking - Part 1">977</a></sup> Adapter boards that handle both cases exist, with a switch selecting RS-232 mode or TTL mode and, in TTL mode, a further choice of 5 V or 3.3 V thresholds.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-680" title="EEVblog #680 - Mailbag">680</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-977" title="EEVblog #977 - Keysight 1000X Hacking - Part 1">977</a></sup>

The requirement for a negative supply is a visible consequence of the standard. On equipment where nothing else needs it, a negative 12 V regulator with only a token [[heat-sink|heatsink]] is usually present solely to feed the RS-232 driver.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1364" title="EEVblog #1364 - Compaq Portable PSU REPAIR">1364</a></sup>

When the object is to read data rather than to conform to the standard, the level converter is a layer to be bypassed. Tapping the serial data inside an instrument ahead of its RS-232 level converter yields the same stream at logic level, avoiding the need to convert it back again.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-6M-xXEn1_iI" title="FAILED PROJECT IDEA - 53131A Mod Board">•</a></sup>

## Clocking and baud rate accuracy

Asynchronous framing has no clock line, so both ends must agree on bit period from their own timebases. As a rule of thumb, RS-232 comms require a [[frequency]] error of 1% or better.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-232" title="EEVblog #232 - Lab Power Supply Design Part 5">232</a></sup> This is the reason a [[microcontroller]]'s internal RC [[oscillator]] is generally unsuitable: although some parts can be software-trimmed to 1% or better, and PIC parts have historically been factory-trimmed to that figure, trimmed accuracy does not hold up over temperature.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-232" title="EEVblog #232 - Lab Power Supply Design Part 5">232</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-238" title="EEVblog #238 - Power Supply Design Part 7">238</a></sup> The usual resolution is to spend two pins on an external crystal or ceramic resonator.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-232" title="EEVblog #232 - Lab Power Supply Design Part 5">232</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-238" title="EEVblog #238 - Power Supply Design Part 7">238</a></sup>

The same arithmetic explains oddball clock frequencies in older designs. 3.6864 MHz is common precisely because it divides evenly into the standard RS-232 baud rates including 9600.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-334" title="EEVblog #334 - History of PDA&#x27;s in Teardowns">334</a></sup>

Rates encountered in the field span the full historical range: 300 baud in early portable machines with built-in modems,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-116" title="EEVblog #116 - Retro Teardown - Tandy Radio Shack TRS-80 Model 100">116</a></sup> 38.4 k on an 8251 UART,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1662" title="EEVblog 1662 - Vintage Tandy 200 Portable Computer Claytons Repair/Teardown">1662</a></sup> 9600 as the default on instruments and the rate at which a bit period is 104 µs,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1691" title="EEVblog 1691 - Uni-T UDP6731 360W Bench PSU REVIEW">1691</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-297" title="EEVblog #297 - Canon LANC Bus Reverse Engineering">297</a></sup> and 115200 on modern embedded debug ports, occasionally printed on the panel next to the [[connector]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-977" title="EEVblog #977 - Keysight 1000X Hacking - Part 1">977</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1289" title="EEVblog #1289 - Mystery Huawei Teardown">1289</a></sup> Instrument software that fails to connect is frequently a baud rate or interface-selection mismatch rather than a fault; on one [[electronic-load|electronic load]] the communications menu defaulted to RS232C and had to be switched to USBTMC before the host would identify the instrument at all.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-862" title="EEVBlog #862 - BK Precision 8601 DC Electronic Load">862</a></sup>

## Drivers, receivers and connectors

The transceiver is the identifying feature of an RS-232 port on a board. Parts encountered include the MAX3232 on a ten-pin header,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-485" title="EEVblog #485 - Agilent TrueVolt 34461A Multimeter Teardown">485</a></sup> the MAX202 alongside an FTDI FT232 on a combined serial/USB board,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-440" title="EEVblog #440 - Atten PPS3205T-3S Triple Output Power Supply Teardown">440</a></sup> the DS14C232,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-710" title="EEVblog #710 - Intercom System Repair">710</a></sup> the ICL3221 as a 232-equivalent,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-587" title="EEVblog #587 - Tektronix MDO3000 Mixed Domain Oscilloscope Teardown">587</a></sup> a Linear Technology LT1137,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-639" title="EEVblog #639 - IBM L40SX Retro Laptop Teardown">639</a></sup> and older Intersil silicon in point-of-sale equipment.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-641" title="EEVblog #641 - Dumpster Cash Register Teardown">641</a></sup> Some processors need no external UART at all — a Hitachi HD6303 with a built-in serial communications interface drives the RS-232 section directly.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-791" title="EEVblog #791 - Ebay Fluke 45 Multimeter Teardown">791</a></sup> Differential line drivers such as dual AM26LS30s appear where the interface hardware is segmented onto its own module in a large systems design.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-598" title="EEVblog #598 - Sony DME7000 Video Multi Effects Processor Teardown">598</a></sup>

Connectors are split between the 25-pin D and the later 9-pin D, with the larger shell a reliable age marker on an instrument.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1554" title="EEVblog 1554 - Optometrist AutoRefractor Keratometer TEARDOWN!">1554</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-955" title="EEVblog #955 - World&#x27;s First Portable Computer Teardown">955</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-cgCFNi8CekE" title="Dave&#x27;s Dodgy Optometry Service - AUCTION SCORE!">•</a></sup> In DTE pinout, pin 2 is transmit and pin 7 is a [[ground]] reference.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1061" title="EEVblog #1061 - Data IO Programmer REPAIR - Part 1">1061</a></sup> A straight cable between two devices that both present as DTE will produce no traffic whatever; a null modem or crossover cable is required, and mislabelled cables are a common cause of an apparently dead port.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1225" title="EEVblog #1225 - Dumpster Credit Card Embosser!">1225</a></sup>

## Isolation and safety

RS-232 over a simple UART can be isolated with an off-the-shelf protocol isolator that carries the link across a barrier into the instrument's processing, which is why floating-front-end architectures worked in the era of GPIB and RS-232 and stopped working with USB and LAN — those require processing right next to the interface because they are grounded.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1032" title="EEVblog #1032 Part 5 - John Kenny Keysight Interview">1032</a></sup> Isolated RS-232 is accordingly common on bench instruments: isolated cables shipped with electronic loads,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-281" title="EEVblog #281 - BK Precision 8500 Electronic Load Teardown">281</a></sup> an isolated RS-232 output on a bench [[multimeter]],<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-791" title="EEVblog #791 - Ebay Fluke 45 Multimeter Teardown">791</a></sup> RS-232 drivers on the digital side of an [[isolation-slot|isolation slot]] away from the ADC input so the instrument can be safely connected to a PC,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-478" title="EEVblog #478 - Keithley 2015 THD Multimeter Teardown">478</a></sup> and optocoupled comms with the entire interface section — Ethernet, USB, logic and RS-232 — referenced to its own supply and isolated from the outputs.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-511" title="EEVblog #511 - Rigol DP832 Power Supply Teardown">511</a></sup>

Handheld multimeters are the case where a galvanic serial port is simply unacceptable. Electrical continuity between a serial connector and the input jacks creates a safety hazard, which is why virtually no multimeter carries a direct RS-232 or USB connection and instead uses an optical or infrared link, with the isolation living in the adapter.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-Xg_niU86bhI" title="Wens 540 Handheld Oscilloscope Multimeter Teardown">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-171" title="EEVblog #171 - Agilent U1272A Multimeter Teardown">171</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1378" title="EEVblog 1378 - NEW Uni-T UT61E+ Multimeter - Still bang-per-buck king?">1378</a></sup> Where the interface is only a single IR [[led|LED]] it is transmit-only; a second LED for receive allows commands and, potentially, [[firmware]] updates back into the meter.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-229" title="EEVblog #229 - IET DE-5000 LCR Meter Teardown">229</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-234" title="EEVblog #234 - Agilent U1733C LCR Meter Teardown">234</a></sup>

The optical approach also produces a hardware distinction between otherwise identical models. Two meters sharing the same case and the same IR window can differ only in whether the serial output is populated and enabled — with the transparent holes present in both, the difference reduced to an unpopulated IR LED pad and firmware that never drives the UART pins.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-5v8qX9PrjnA" title="Brymen BM257 vs BM235 Multimeter IR Serial Protocol">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-912" title="EEVblog #912 - BM235 Multimeter Reverse Engineering">912</a></sup> Not all such protocols are conventional: rather than emitting a measurement as an ASCII string, one 6000-count meter protocol transmits the [[lcd-segment|LCD segment]] digits and leaves the host to map them.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-5v8qX9PrjnA" title="Brymen BM257 vs BM235 Multimeter IR Serial Protocol">•</a></sup>

## Instrument remote control

On test equipment RS-232 typically appears beside GPIB as the cheap alternative to it, on signal generators,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-261" title="EEVblog #261 - Marconi 2023 RF Signal Generator Teardown">261</a></sup> source measure units,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-718" title="EEVblog #718 - Keithley 2400 SMU Teardown">718</a></sup> LCR and combiscope-class instruments,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-559" title="EEVblog #559 - Auction Score">559</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1450" title="EEVblog 1450- $99 Fluke Combiscope Ebay SCORE!">1450</a></sup> THD multimeters,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-478" title="EEVblog #478 - Keithley 2015 THD Multimeter Teardown">478</a></sup> AC power sources,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1698" title="EEVblog 1698 - Awesome Ebay AC Power Source: GW Instek APS-7050">1698</a></sup> power meters,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1693" title="EEVblog 1693 - Uni-T UTE310 Power Meter Teardown &amp; Practical Demo">1693</a></sup> and bench supplies.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-655" title="EEVblog #655 - Auction Score">655</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-8" title="EEVblog #8 Part 2 of 2 - Review of the BK Precision 1697 Programmable Lab Power Supply">8</a></sup> Full remote control over the RS-232 interface is normal even where the instrument's other functions are stripped down.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1450" title="EEVblog 1450- $99 Fluke Combiscope Ebay SCORE!">1450</a></sup> The interface is sometimes hardware-fitted but licence-locked: a supply may ship with the RS-232, LAN and digital IO hardware present and inert until a software key is installed.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-509" title="EEVblog #509 - Rigol DP832 Lab Power Supply">509</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-511" title="EEVblog #511 - Rigol DP832 Power Supply Teardown">511</a></sup>

Its absence on a nominally replacement instrument is conspicuous. When a bench multimeter was positioned as a successor to a model that had offered RS-232, the newer instrument shipped with LAN and USB only, despite an RS-232 header and a MAX3232 being present on its front-panel board.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-485" title="EEVblog #485 - Agilent TrueVolt 34461A Multimeter Teardown">485</a></sup>

Consumer and professional AV equipment carries the interface for automation rather than for data: an RS-232C port for servicing and remote operation on a television, with the full command set — channel changes, power on and off — documented in the consumer manual rather than the [[service-manual|service manual]],<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1154" title="EEVblog #1154 - Surprising 4K Dumpster TV Fault">1154</a></sup> control ports on an HD DVD player intended for studio automation,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1011" title="EEVblog #1011 - Retro Teardown - World&#x27;s First HD DVD Player">1011</a></sup> and RS-232 control alongside 3D sync on a large projector.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1546" title="EEVblog 1546 - INSANE $30k 4K Panoramic DLP Projector TEARDOWN">1546</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-278" title="EEVblog #278 - Elmo Visual Presenter Teardown">278</a></sup>

## Debug and boot ports

Equipment running an embedded operating system very commonly exposes a debug serial interface or RS-232-style terminal that emits boot information, and locating it is the standard entry point for examining or modifying such a device.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-977" title="EEVblog #977 - Keysight 1000X Hacking - Part 1">977</a></sup> The port is sometimes labelled with a serial, debug, TX or RX marking and sometimes not; a four-pin header near the [[processor]] is a likely candidate.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-977" title="EEVblog #977 - Keysight 1000X Hacking - Part 1">977</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-CK5nbC_dBWk" title="Siglent Signal Generator FAIL">•</a></sup> Confirmation is not guaranteed — a port with a nonstandard pinout may not be a serial debug port at all,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-731" title="EEVblog #731 - Keithley DMM7510 7.5 Digit Multimeter Teardown">731</a></sup> and a genuine RS-232 service interface may simply produce nothing useful on boot, with no way to interrupt the sequence into a bootloader.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1154" title="EEVblog #1154 - Surprising 4K Dumpster TV Fault">1154</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1225" title="EEVblog #1225 - Dumpster Credit Card Embosser!">1225</a></sup> Where the digital board's ground is not tied to [[mains-earth|mains earth]], probing with a mains-referenced [[oscilloscope]] is hazardous.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-731" title="EEVblog #731 - Keithley DMM7510 7.5 Digit Multimeter Teardown">731</a></sup>

## Decoding and triggering

An oscilloscope with deep memory has largely absorbed the traditional logic analyser role for serial protocols such as [[spi|SPI]], I²C and RS-232.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-44" title="EEVblog #44 Part 1 - Logic Analyzer Tutorial">44</a></sup> Decoding starts by measuring the period between two transitions to establish the bit rate, then configuring the bus as UART/RS-232 with a threshold, a data width, parity, stop bits and an idle polarity — almost always eight data bits, no parity and one stop bit, idling high.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-977" title="EEVblog #977 - Keysight 1000X Hacking - Part 1">977</a></sup> Because the framing is generic, any signal with matching timing can be decoded by the same engine: the Canon LANC bus, with a 104 µs bit period identical to RS-232 at 9600, can be captured with a scope's UART decoder.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-297" title="EEVblog #297 - Canon LANC Bus Reverse Engineering">297</a></sup>

Serial decode and trigger are near-universally sold as paid options rather than included, on entry-level and mid-range instruments alike,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-704" title="EEVBlog #704 - Rigol DS1054Z Oscilloscope Features Review">704</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-703" title="EEVblog #703 - Rigol DS1054Z Oscilloscope Review Summary">703</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1146" title="EEVblog #1146 - New Rigol MSO5000 Oscilloscope">1146</a></sup> and vendors frequently unbundle UART/RS-232 from I²C, SPI, CAN and LIN so each must be bought separately.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-CMoBGGqojqs" title="Rigol MSO7000 Unboxing &amp; First Impressions">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1501" title="EEVblog 1501 - Rigol HDO4000 Low Noise 12bit Oscilloscope Unboxing &amp; First Impression">1501</a></sup> Option pricing has reached four figures for basic RS-232 and RS-485 trigger and analysis on a mixed-domain scope.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-199" title="Tektronix Mixed Domain Oscilloscope MDO4000 Review - EEVblog #199">199</a></sup> Capability at the top end extends to 20 Mbaud decode.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1146" title="EEVblog #1146 - New Rigol MSO5000 Oscilloscope">1146</a></sup> Software logic analysers handle multiple simultaneous asynchronous serial buses limited only by channel count, since decoding happens after capture.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-436" title="EEVblog #436 - Saleae USB Logic Analyser Review &amp; Teardown">436</a></sup>

## USB adapters and host-side software

Most modern hosts reach RS-232 equipment through a USB-to-serial bridge, generally an FTDI part, cheap enough to be treated as disposable and used as the programming and comms path on countless boards.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-45" title="EEVblog #45 - Arduino, PICAXE, and idiot assembler programmers">45</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-230" title="EEVblog #230 - ArduCopter ArduPilot Troubleshooting">230</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-440" title="EEVblog #440 - Atten PPS3205T-3S Triple Output Power Supply Teardown">440</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1331" title="EEVblog #1331 - BattLab One Review">1331</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-155" title="EEVblog #155 - ITead Studio PCB Prototype Goof">155</a></sup> Any off-the-shelf FTDI or RS-232 lead can often be pressed into service in place of a vendor cable.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-229" title="EEVblog #229 - IET DE-5000 LCR Meter Teardown">229</a></sup> The significant failure mode is driver-level: a Prolific-based isolated adapter can present a Windows code 10 error because the vendor modified its drivers to render counterfeit chipsets unusable.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1225" title="EEVblog #1225 - Dumpster Credit Card Embosser!">1225</a></sup>

Host software is a recurring weak point. Vendor applications may require a VISA layer that conflicts with existing installations,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1691" title="EEVblog 1691 - Uni-T UDP6731 360W Bench PSU REVIEW">1691</a></sup> and may fail to expose modes the instrument itself supports — an electronic load capable of constant-power discharge could not log or export that data through the supplied software despite a working RS-232 port and isolated cable.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-774" title="EEVblog #774 - Low Battery Discharge Testing Part 1">774</a></sup> Where the software does match the hardware, RS-232 or USB control makes automated [[battery]] discharge curves and capacity calculation routine.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-393" title="EEVblog #393 - LiPo Battery Discharge Testing">393</a></sup>

## Related standards and practical limits

RS-232 is single-ended and short-haul. RS-422 uses differential pairs and is preferred for long runs in professional installations; a video effects processor carries mostly RS-422 with some RS-232 alongside it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-598" title="EEVblog #598 - Sony DME7000 Video Multi Effects Processor Teardown">598</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-738" title="EEVblog #738 - Yamaha DME32 Digital Mixer Teardown">738</a></sup> RS-485 covers multi-drop industrial links, and combined boards offering 232, 422 and 485 together are the natural product for industrial automation.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-833" title="EEVblog #833 - Mailbag">833</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-8" title="EEVblog #8 Part 2 of 2 - Review of the BK Precision 1697 Programmable Lab Power Supply">8</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1760" title="EEVblog 1760 - Today in the Dumpster (July 12th, 2026)">1760</a></sup> Half-duplex RS-232 has been used as a servo bus for position feedback on multi-axis robots, though a differential standard would be the better choice.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-614" title="EEVblog #614 - Cebit 2014 Highlights">614</a></sup>

The choice between them can be forced by connector allocation rather than by signalling. A hybrid solar [[inverter]] with a single RS-232 port could not simultaneously accept its Wi-Fi dongle — the only source of a network time sync for a real-time clock losing minutes per day — and feed a Raspberry Pi based monitoring system; moving the monitoring link to RS-485, which shared a physical connector with the CAN bus, freed the port and resolved the clock fault.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-4vY5-obAx6w" title="Deye Hybrid Solar Inverter RTC FAIL!">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-HctGMxWPWRE" title="Deye Hybrid Inverter Time Sync Issue UPDATE + Battery News">•</a></sup>

RS-232-class data rates are low enough to be undemanding of layout. On a flex PCB carrying an RS-232-rate link, the pair should still be routed together as a pair, but controlled [[impedance]], differential length matching and the associated detail work are unnecessary.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1262" title="EEVblog #1262 - Designing a Flex PCB + uSupply Update">1262</a></sup>
