Multiplexing is the practice of sharing one physical resource — a set of drive pins, a converter, a measurement front end, a transmitter — among several loads or signals by servicing them one at a time in rapid rotation. Its purpose is almost always to trade something cheap (switching speed, control complexity) for something expensive (pin count, silicon, connectors, discrete drivers).<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1044" title="EEVblog #1044 - LCD Technology Tutorial">1044</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1491" title="EEVblog 1491 - The MacGyver Project - Part 1">1491</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-948" title="EEVblog #948 - Nixie Tube Display Project - Part 1">948</a></sup> The cost is never zero: a multiplexed resource is only available to each user for a fraction of the time, and that fraction shows up somewhere as reduced brightness, reduced [[sample-rate|sample rate]], or reduced [[bandwidth]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-948" title="EEVblog #948 - Nixie Tube Display Project - Part 1">948</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1717" title="EEVblog 1717 - Rigol&#x27;s INSANE New $999 350MHz Oscilloscope: TEARDOWN">1717</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1090" title="EEVblog #1090 - Sony Mystery Teardown">1090</a></sup>

## Displays

Display driving is the classic case. A small seven-segment [[lcd|LCD]] can be driven statically, with one common pin and one pin per segment, and needs no multiplexing at all — a 3½-digit [[multimeter]] display might get away with forty pins.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1044" title="EEVblog #1044 - LCD Technology Tutorial">1044</a></sup> The pin count becomes untenable as segment count rises: an 8 by 14 segment LCD is given four common pins instead of one, and the segments are then multiplexed against those four commons purely to keep the driver pin count down.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1044" title="EEVblog #1044 - LCD Technology Tutorial">1044</a></sup> A very low pin count on an LCD [[datasheet|data sheet]] is itself the tell that the commons must be multiplexed.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-248" title="EEVblog #248 - LCD Enabled Microcontroller Selection">248</a></sup> The same logic makes dense custom glass possible; the routing pattern on the back of a multiplexed panel is what lets a complex display escape with very few pins.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-851" title="EEVBlog #851 - Mailbag">851</a></sup>

Because the segments are lit sequentially rather than continuously, a multiplexed display is a flickering display observed at a low enough shutter speed. A [[vacuum-fluorescent-display|vacuum fluorescent display]] driven this way shows on-off bursts at around 60 Hz, and brightness is set by adjusting the [[duty-cycle|duty cycle]] and [[frequency]] of that burst.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-717" title="EEVblog #717 - How To Hack Vacuum Fluorescent Displays">717</a></sup> The multiplexing on a bench instrument's display can be visible as flicker when the instrument is filmed or viewed on another screen.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-576" title="EEVblog #576 - Advantest R6142 Current Voltage Generator">576</a></sup> In a working multiplexed digit driver each digit's drive appears staggered in time from the others when probed simultaneously, which is why all digits lighting up on power-on is evidence that the multiplexing chain — [[shift-register|shift register]], data latch, digit drivers — is functioning even before any data has been loaded.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-689" title="EEVblog #689 - Back To The Future Time Circuits Troubleshooting">689</a></sup> A 74HC164 shift register cycling through the digits is a representative implementation, with the multiplex and drive circuitry mounted on the back of the display panel itself.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-689" title="EEVblog #689 - Back To The Future Time Circuits Troubleshooting">689</a></sup> Segment refresh only needs to beat the eye: at least 40 times per second per segment is enough that the sequential switching is invisible.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1493" title="EEVblog 1493 - MacGyver Project - Part 2">1493</a></sup>

Multiplexing also serves as a [[current]]-budget tool rather than a pin-count tool. Where a design cannot supply enough current to light every element at once — forty segments across five displays, for example — multiplexing one display at a time, blanking and shifting between displays, cuts the simultaneous load by a factor of five at the price of constant data shifting.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1491" title="EEVblog 1491 - The MacGyver Project - Part 1">1491</a></sup>

The counter-case is direct drive. For a [[nixie-tube|Nixie tube]] display, multiplexing was rejected in favour of driving every element continuously: multiplexing raises peak currents, produces a dimmer display, and leaves the result uncertain, whereas direct drive gives a known constant brightness at the cost of many more high-[[voltage]] driver channels.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-948" title="EEVblog #948 - Nixie Tube Display Project - Part 1">948</a></sup> The judgement stands that multiplexing earns its place mainly when space or pin count is genuinely constrained.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-948" title="EEVblog #948 - Nixie Tube Display Project - Part 1">948</a></sup>

Very simple segment driving can sidestep multiplexing altogether. A single e-ink or similar segment can be placed between two [[microcontroller]] I/O pins and AC-driven by toggling the two pins against each other, then tri-stated when the segment should not be driven — a different and much simpler proposition than multiplexing.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1241" title="EEVblog #1241 - Power Up Display Counter Project - Part 1">1241</a></sup>

## Switch and sensor matrices

The reciprocal technique reads many inputs through few wires. A keypad is normally built as a switch matrix in the microcontroller: digital lines for the commons, digital lines for the rows, multiplexed and scanned continuously.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1360" title="EEVblog #1360 - REPAIR - Aircon Control Panel">1360</a></sup> A properly scanned matrix is tolerant of degraded contacts — a switch a couple of hundred ohms out of spec still reads correctly and is not confused with other buttons on the array — so a keypad that fails on one button is evidence that a matrix scan was not what the designer implemented.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1360" title="EEVblog #1360 - REPAIR - Aircon Control Panel">1360</a></sup> A 68-key musical keyboard uses the same arrangement with diodes at the matrix, sampling every key and recovering per-key displacement over a small number of cables.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-256" title="EEVblog #256 - Yamaha PSR-80 Keyboard Teardown">256</a></sup>

## Shared converters and measurement front ends

Instruments multiplex their analogue front ends into a single converter to avoid duplicating expensive hardware. An [[lcr-meter|LCR meter]] measures differential voltage across the device and current through a selectable range [[resistor]] from about 1 ohm up to 10 meg, then multiplexes both into one ADC, with the remaining work done in maths and filtering.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-757" title="EEVblog #757 - HP4263A LCR Meter Teardown">757</a></sup> A multimeter chipset multiplexes its input network internally: the commons are shorted to [[ground]] for AC and DC volts, while in ohms and [[capacitance]] modes the ground connection is dropped and the lower end of the resistor network is activated instead.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-853" title="EEVblog #853 - How A Multimeter Works">853</a></sup> A VNA built on spectrum analyser hardware reuses the existing measurement chain by alternating between a spectrum sweep and a reflected-power sweep, obtaining S11 with very little additional hardware.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1101" title="EEVblog #1101 - Siglent SVA1015X VNA Teardown">1101</a></sup> Even an older instrument without an ADC chip multiplexes: a slope-conversion design counts up in a decade counter, then multiplexes the counter output through a character generator [[rom|ROM]] and cycles through the digits [[via]] digit transistors.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-628" title="EEVblog #628 - Tektronix 213 Vintage Portable Oscilloscope Teardown">628</a></sup>

Where converters are cheap enough relative to the specification, designers avoid sharing. A power meter with separate 16-bit SAR ADCs for voltage and current multiplexes nothing.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1693" title="EEVblog 1693 - Uni-T UTE310 Power Meter Teardown &amp; Practical Demo">1693</a></sup> A 16-channel data recorder gave each channel its own 16-bit converter, "none of that multiplexing rubbish", though bandwidth still scaled with channel count — 20 kHz [[analog-bandwidth|analogue bandwidth]] in two-channel mode falling to 5 kHz with all sixteen channels sampling at once.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1090" title="EEVblog #1090 - Sony Mystery Teardown">1090</a></sup>

Front-panel behaviour can be multiplexed too, and not always desirably. Giving ohms and continuity separate rotary switch positions, rather than multiplexing them onto one position with a soft button, is preferable in use.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-60" title="EEVblog #60 - Fluke 117 Multimeter Review and Teardown">60</a></sup>

## Interleaving in oscilloscopes

Digitising instruments multiplex ADCs to reach headline sample rates that a single converter cannot. The ADC08D1520 is a dual 8-bit converter rated 1.5 GSa/s per channel, or 3 GSa/s from a single input when the two halves are interleaved.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-875" title="EEVblog #875 - NI VirtualBench Teardown">875</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1477" title="EEVblog 1477 - TEARDOWN! - NEW Tektronix 2 Series Oscilloscope">1477</a></sup> Two such dual parts give four channels at full rate with no sharing; interleaving is what buys the doubled single-channel figure.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-875" title="EEVblog #875 - NI VirtualBench Teardown">875</a></sup>

The trade is visible directly in published specifications. A four-channel scope using two dual ADCs must multiplex them, so its 2.5 GSa/s single- or dual-channel figure halves to 1.25 GSa/s in four-channel configuration — and that 2.5 GSa/s is itself well under the 3 GSa/s the silicon can deliver, plausibly for [[noise]] reasons.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1477" title="EEVblog 1477 - TEARDOWN! - NEW Tektronix 2 Series Oscilloscope">1477</a></sup> Another design combines both ADC chips such that the individual channels cannot be separated at all, ruling out the channel-one-and-channel-three trick available on some scopes; 4 GSa/s on one channel becomes 2 GSa/s with a second channel enabled and 1 GSa/s with all four on.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1717" title="EEVblog 1717 - Rigol&#x27;s INSANE New $999 350MHz Oscilloscope: TEARDOWN">1717</a></sup> An [[asic|ASIC]] that integrates two converters but exposes four inputs implies the same internal multiplexing, and is the natural basis for a cost-reduced variant once the non-recurring engineering cost of the ASIC has been absorbed.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1639" title="EEVblog 1639 - Keysight InfiniiVision HD3 Oscilloscope Teardown">1639</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1717" title="EEVblog 1717 - Rigol&#x27;s INSANE New $999 350MHz Oscilloscope: TEARDOWN">1717</a></sup> Front-end analogue sections perform their own multiplexing alongside DC offsetting and level shifting before the converters.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-37" title="EEVblog #37 - Rigol DS1052E Oscilloscope Teardown">37</a></sup> Source measure units similarly carry muxing throughout the feedback and sensing paths.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-718" title="EEVblog #718 - Keithley 2400 SMU Teardown">718</a></sup>

## Multiplexing power and beams

The principle extends beyond signals. A [[battery]] charger with four cells alternates them in pairs on a 500 ms period — cells one and three on while two and four are off — because charge and bypass paths cannot both be active on a cell without shorting it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-812" title="EEVblog #812 - Varta 15min NiMH Charger Part 2">812</a></sup> The multiplexed switching also interleaves brief measurement intervals of a few tens of milliseconds.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-812" title="EEVblog #812 - Varta 15min NiMH Charger Part 2">812</a></sup> A single-emitter infrared power beaming system must track multiple receivers and alternate between them, multiplexing the delivered power across up to four devices from a claimed 5 W total.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1092" title="EEVblog #1092 - Wi-Charge IR Wireless Charging - Fact or Fiction?">1092</a></sup> A phased array intended for wireless charging could in principle be multiplexed between transmit and receive to locate the target device.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1369" title="EEVblog #1369 - Xiaomi Mi Air Wireless Charging BUSTED!">1369</a></sup> Frequency division multiplexing and related terminology are also common padding in wireless power marketing material with no supporting hardware behind it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1408" title="EEVblog 1408 - $5M WiGL Wireless Charging BUSTED!">1408</a></sup>

## Related uses

Where a bus cannot address the devices attached to it — several I²C peripherals sharing one address, or too few hardware serial ports — a multiplexer is one of the standard remedies, the alternatives being separate bit-banged lines or paying for an extra chip.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-9hMsNOwY5AQ" title="EEVacademy #3 - Bit Banging &amp; SPI Tutorial">•</a></sup> Existing multiplexing infrastructure is also what makes display retrofits practical: a VFD-to-[[led|LED]] conversion can reuse the instrument's row and column drivers and multiplexing timing unchanged, substituting only the emissive technology.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1669" title="EEVblog 1669 - Agilent 53131A VFD to LED Display Upgrade MAILBAG">1669</a></sup> Dot-matrix LED modules likewise assume multiplexed driving as a matter of course.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-960" title="EEVblog #960 - Mystery Merry Mailbag Teardown">960</a></sup>
