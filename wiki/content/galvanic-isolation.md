Galvanic isolation is the absence of any direct electrical connection between two parts of a circuit, so that no conduction path exists from one side to the other even though power and information still cross between them.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-bg6QsTT0Plw" title="Laser Driver PCB &amp; Circuit Review">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1110" title="EEVblog #1110 - Mailbag">1110</a></sup> The traditional case is a [[transformer]], which couples energy magnetically while leaving the windings electrically separate.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-bg6QsTT0Plw" title="Laser Driver PCB &amp; Circuit Review">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1110" title="EEVblog #1110 - Mailbag">1110</a></sup> It matters wherever one side of a system sits at a hazardous or arbitrary potential — a mains-referenced primary, a high-side switch node, a floating measurement front end — and the other side must be safe to touch, safe to connect to a computer, or free to float at its own reference.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-Xg_niU86bhI" title="Wens 540 Handheld Oscilloscope Multimeter Teardown">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-409" title="EEVblog #409 - EDMI - Smart Meter Teardown">409</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1753" title="EEVblog 1753 - Designing a 2000V Isolated Oscilloscope (Cleverscope)">1753</a></sup>

## The barrier as a physical feature

An isolation barrier is a geometric fact on a [[pcb|printed circuit board]] before it is anything else: a band of bare laminate with no copper, no components, and nothing routed across it. Manufacturers mark it so it cannot be violated by accident. A heavy [[silkscreen]] line drawn across the board separates the high [[voltage]] part from the low voltage part, with the bridging transformer straddling it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-272" title="EEVblog #272 - Manson 9400 40A 3-15V Switchmode PSU Teardown">272</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-446" title="EEVblog #446 - Dumpster Diving Samsung Plasma TV">446</a></sup> A crosshatched region on the silkscreen serves the same purpose, dividing the switching primary from the secondary.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1388" title="EEVblog 1388 -  Dumpster Diving 4K TV Murphy&#x27;s &quot;Repair&quot;">1388</a></sup> On some boards the isolated section is outlined entirely, so every component belonging to it is enclosed by the marking.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-607" title="EEVblog #607 - Agilent B2912A Source Measure Unit SMU Teardown">607</a></sup>

The barrier can also be seen directly. In a multi-channel bench supply built on one large PCB, the isolation between channels is visible as a gap that light shines through, each channel's circuitry completely galvanically isolated from its neighbours.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1174" title="EEVblog #1174 - Rohde &amp; Schwarz PSU Teardowns">1174</a></sup> The isolation gap between primary and secondary sides of a main switching transformer is similarly a physical separation between two areas of the board.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-449" title="EEVblog #449 - Absopulse VFC500 Variable Frequency Converter Teardown">449</a></sup>

Clearance is not decoration. On a [[microinverter|solar microinverter]] the isolation path runs under the transformer and under adjacent resistors, tracing a continuous boundary around the isolated region, and that routed clearance is as important to the design as the isolation inside the transformer itself.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-yOJ7xPugsdc" title="Hoymiles Microinverter Reverse Engineering CORRECTION">•</a></sup> Where creepage distance across the barrier is short, [[solder-mask|solder mask]] over the gap carries the remainder of the requirement.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-Xg_niU86bhI" title="Wens 540 Handheld Oscilloscope Multimeter Teardown">•</a></sup>

## Crossing with power

Power crosses the barrier magnetically. In a [[switch-mode-power-supply|switching power supply]] the main switching transformer is the isolating element, and everything else in the topology is arranged around it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1388" title="EEVblog 1388 -  Dumpster Diving 4K TV Murphy&#x27;s &quot;Repair&quot;">1388</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-449" title="EEVblog #449 - Absopulse VFC500 Variable Frequency Converter Teardown">449</a></sup> An LLC resonant converter takes its isolation from the transformer that couples the resonant tank to the output, with a conventional full-wave bridge on the secondary; the resonant switching itself exists to cut switching losses, not to isolate.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1294" title="EEVblog #1294 - LLC Resonant Mode Converter Design">1294</a></sup> Because the transformer works at the [[switching-frequency|switching frequency]] rather than at mains [[frequency]], high-frequency operation allows a physically tiny transformer to do a job that a 50 Hz transformer of the same size could not.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-yOJ7xPugsdc" title="Hoymiles Microinverter Reverse Engineering CORRECTION">•</a></sup> Instruments frequently use a small auxiliary transformer purely to power their own control circuitry across the barrier, bridging the high voltage part of the circuit to the low voltage part and feeding a rectifier, [[inductor]] and filter capacitors to produce VCC.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-272" title="EEVblog #272 - Manson 9400 40A 3-15V Switchmode PSU Teardown">272</a></sup> In a power analyser the same approach supplies positive and negative 5-volt rails to an entire isolated measurement channel from a dedicated switching transformer.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-589" title="EEVblog #589 - Voltech PM300 Power Analyser Teardown">589</a></sup>

Packaged isolated DC-DC converters make this a purchased part. The common single-in-line format takes 5 volts in and gives 5 volts out, with the [[isolation-slot|isolation slot]] visible in the package body; variants with extra pins produce split supplies.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-324" title="EEVblog #324 - DC-DC Converter Testing - USB Supply Part 4">324</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-810" title="EEVblog #810 - Micsig MS310 Handheld Oscilloscope Teardown">810</a></sup> Ratings are quoted as a withstand voltage between input and output — 1000 volts is typical for a small 2 watt module, with higher grades available.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-324" title="EEVblog #324 - DC-DC Converter Testing - USB Supply Part 4">324</a></sup> The modules are fully potted, and each contains its own [[oscillator]] and magnetics, so the user supplies only the input rail.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-324" title="EEVblog #324 - DC-DC Converter Testing - USB Supply Part 4">324</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-680" title="EEVblog #680 - Mailbag">680</a></sup> Sizes run from roughly 1 watt to 5 watt in the same family.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-680" title="EEVblog #680 - Mailbag">680</a></sup> In a handheld scope-meter, such modules isolate the [[processor]] [[ground]] side from the [[multimeter]] input side, taking 5 volts from the digital side and producing the rails for the analog section.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-810" title="EEVblog #810 - Micsig MS310 Handheld Oscilloscope Teardown">810</a></sup>

## Crossing with signals

Signals cross by optical, magnetic or optical-fibre means. The [[optocoupler]] is the default: a switching supply sends its [[output-voltage|output voltage]] feedback back to the primary-side controller through one,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-449" title="EEVblog #449 - Absopulse VFC500 Variable Frequency Converter Teardown">449</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-446" title="EEVblog #446 - Dumpster Diving Samsung Plasma TV">446</a></sup> and digital interfaces are handed across the same way. A smart meter uses four optocouplers to carry a serial link from the control section down to the communications module,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-409" title="EEVblog #409 - EDMI - Smart Meter Teardown">409</a></sup> a source measure unit returns measurement data to its main processor board through digital opto-isolators,<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-607" title="EEVblog #607 - Agilent B2912A Source Measure Unit SMU Teardown">607</a></sup> and a bench multimeter isolates its entire measurement section, leaving a two-wire [[serial-port|serial interface]] and a reasonably fast optocoupler as the only things that cross.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1382" title="EEVblog 1382 - Keysight EDU34450A 5.5 digit Bench Multimeter TEARDOWN">1382</a></sup>

Not everything needs to be discrete. A complete [[usb|USB]] opto-isolator can be built as one chip with the tracks routed clear around it, which is what makes a USB port and an external metal [[connector]] possible on a certified handheld multimeter.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-Xg_niU86bhI" title="Wens 540 Handheld Oscilloscope Multimeter Teardown">•</a></sup> Isolated USB-to-[[rs-232|RS232]] converters exist as small commodity boards.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-680" title="EEVblog #680 - Mailbag">680</a></sup> Gate drivers for GaN transistors integrate the isolation on-die: the [[pwm|PWM]] input crosses a galvanically isolated interface inside the package and a totem pole output stage on the far side drives the [[transistor]], with one driver required per transistor.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1557" title="EEVblog 1557 - Micsig SigOFIT Optical Fibre Probe + GaN Experiment">1557</a></sup> Whole protocols can be carried across a barrier by a matched pair of transceivers — one part at each end gives a fully [[spi|SPI]]-compliant interface over a single galvanically isolated twisted pair.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1110" title="EEVblog #1110 - Mailbag">1110</a></sup> Measurement front ends can be bought pre-isolated as well: an isolated [[current-shunt|current shunt]] rated at 0.1% nominal accuracy and 300 kHz [[bandwidth]], priced around 69 US dollars, removes the design work of remote [[current-sensing|current sensing]] and presents a differential output ready for any acquisition system.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1410" title="EEVblog 1410 - Mailbag">1410</a></sup>

Isolation can also be exploited for sensing rather than just protection. A design that must read the state of a mains switch from the low-voltage side can do so by detecting the [[impedance]] presented across the barrier: a series resonant circuit presents high impedance at the sensing frequency while a closed switch presents low impedance, letting a low-voltage CPU read the switch position across the barrier and removing the need for a separate power switch.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1170" title="EEVblog #1170 - TRUE Mystery Teardown! (not even Dave knows)">1170</a></sup>

An optical fibre probe removes conductors from the crossing altogether. Power is sent up the fibre to an active FET front end and the signal returns down it in the analog domain with no sampling, which yields complete galvanic isolation rated as high as 60 kilovolts because no metallic conductor exists between probe and [[oscilloscope]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1557" title="EEVblog 1557 - Micsig SigOFIT Optical Fibre Probe + GaN Experiment">1557</a></sup>

## Where it is genuinely required

Isolation is mandatory where a fault would otherwise put a hazardous potential onto an accessible circuit. In a grid-tied solar installation, a failed panel, a bad installation or water ingress can create a leakage path from [[mains-earth|mains earth]] through the panels back into the [[inverter]]'s low-voltage side, and that is the specific hazard the barrier exists to interrupt — the reason a manufacturer spends board area on it at all.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-yOJ7xPugsdc" title="Hoymiles Microinverter Reverse Engineering CORRECTION">•</a></sup>

It is equally required when the two sides must sit at different potentials by design. Measuring VDS on the high side of a half bridge demands an isolated instrument, since the reference point is not ground and swings with the switching node.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1753" title="EEVblog 1753 - Designing a 2000V Isolated Oscilloscope (Cleverscope)">1753</a></sup> A source node switching hundreds of volts at 1 MHz makes any non-isolated connection to that point untenable.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1557" title="EEVblog 1557 - Micsig SigOFIT Optical Fibre Probe + GaN Experiment">1557</a></sup> [[battery|Battery]]-powered instruments get isolation for free by floating: a floating front end can sit at 30 kilovolts with no consequence, which is considerably simpler than engineering an equivalent barrier.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1753" title="EEVblog 1753 - Designing a 2000V Isolated Oscilloscope (Cleverscope)">1753</a></sup>

Uninterruptible supplies split along this line. An online, double-conversion topology has no direct electrical connection from output to input and is therefore galvanically isolated, which is the choice when the powered equipment must be electrically separated from the incoming mains; other topologies place a transformer in the path yet remain common from input to output and provide no isolation at all.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-504" title="EEVblog #504 - UPS Tutorial &amp; Teardown">504</a></sup>

Isolation also enables composition. An isolated bench supply output can be connected in series with another to form a split supply.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-285" title="EEVblog #285 - USB Lab Supply - Part 1">285</a></sup> Isolated channels in a multi-channel instrument can be used independently without their grounds interacting.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1174" title="EEVblog #1174 - Rohde &amp; Schwarz PSU Teardowns">1174</a></sup> For [[production-testing|production test]] work, an isolated instrument interface is often a hard requirement, and an otherwise capable [[lcr-meter|LCR meter]] whose USB interface is not isolated is ruled out for that use unless an external isolator is added.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-137" title="EEVblog #137 - BK Precision 879B Handheld LCR Meter Review">137</a></sup> Where the instrument is isolated, the marketing distinction is worth reading carefully: a supply may have its USB and serial ports optically isolated from the output while the USB shell remains electrically connected directly to mains earth.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-315" title="EEVblog #315 - Korad KA3005P Review/FAIL">315</a></sup>

## The cost of isolation, and when to skip it

Isolation is never free and is frequently added where nothing requires it. Optocouplers and transistor arrays duplicated across a set of boards that already share a common ground add chips, board area and drive [[current]] for no isolation reason; the parts are the right answer when isolation is actually needed in the system, and pure overhead when it is not.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1365" title="EEVblog #1365 - Viewer PCB &amp; Circuit Design Review">1365</a></sup>

The costs are cumulative. Sending power across a barrier is inefficient enough that obtaining 4 watts on the isolated side can require putting 16 watts in, with the difference appearing as heat that must be removed.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1753" title="EEVblog 1753 - Designing a 2000V Isolated Oscilloscope (Cleverscope)">1753</a></sup> An isolated interface needs power supplied on the far side, which in an opto-isolated USB-to-serial adapter means the device under test must feed the remote transceiver.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1656" title="EEVblog 1656 - MAILBAG: ElectroPermanent Magnet, DIN PSU&#x27;s, Sponge, Beelink EQ14 N150 PC">1656</a></sup> Architecturally, isolation forces duplicated intelligence: a fully isolated USB supply needs processing on the secondary side in addition to the primary, on top of the isolated converters themselves.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1561" title="EEVblog 1561 - µSupply USB Power Supply - Part 21">1561</a></sup>

The result is a [[bill-of-materials|bill of materials]] that climbs quickly. Reviewing a fully isolated portable USB supply built with off-the-shelf isolated converters, the retrospective engineering judgment was that isolating it had not been worth it: the isolation eliminated ground problems neatly, but the intended users were powering the unit from a [[battery-pack|battery pack]], a power bank, or a laptop that already floats as long as its mains connection is absent, so the benefit largely evaporated.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1561" title="EEVblog 1561 - µSupply USB Power Supply - Part 21">1561</a></sup> A production version of that design would drop the isolation and take the resulting reduction in bill of materials cost.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1561" title="EEVblog 1561 - µSupply USB Power Supply - Part 21">1561</a></sup> The general form of the judgment is that isolation should be specified where a fault path, a safety requirement, or a floating measurement reference makes it necessary — and that a heavy penalty is paid for it everywhere else.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1561" title="EEVblog 1561 - µSupply USB Power Supply - Part 21">1561</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1365" title="EEVblog #1365 - Viewer PCB &amp; Circuit Design Review">1365</a></sup>
