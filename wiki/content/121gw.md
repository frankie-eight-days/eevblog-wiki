The 121GW is a handheld [[digital-multimeter|digital multimeter]] developed as an EEVblog-branded instrument and delivered in 2018 after roughly two years of development.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-e9cpKN69Avk" title="121GW Development History">•</a></sup> It was designed and financed by the contract manufacturer rather than by the brand it carries, which shaped both its feature set and its compromises.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-121" title="EEVblog 121GW Multimeter Kickstarter Live Chat">121</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-e9cpKN69Avk" title="121GW Development History">•</a></sup> Its distinguishing features are a 15 V [[diode-test|diode test]] mode, a built-in low-burden-[[voltage]] [[current]] amplifier, [[bluetooth|Bluetooth]] and SD-card logging, and simultaneous voltage and current display.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1157" title="EEVblog #1157 - Transistor Zener Clamp Circuit">1157</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1604" title="EEVblog 1604 - BEWARE! Multimeter Burden Voltage">1604</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-96XE5Ye1q6I" title="121GW EEVblog Multmeter Firmware Update">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1095" title="EEVblog #1095 - Is a $38 Multimeter any good? ANENG Q1 Review (4K!)">1095</a></sup>

## Origins and development

The project began as an attempt to rebadge an existing Brymen design; Brymen were not interested in producing a custom [[multimeter]], and the idea was dropped.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-e9cpKN69Avk" title="121GW Development History">•</a></sup> It restarted after a charity mud run in which a Klein Tools MM500 and a Tektronix [[oscilloscope]] were dragged through an obstacle course; conversations with Klein Tools — associated with Kain Test and UEI — [[led]] to an offer to design, develop and build a multimeter at no cost to the brand, on the reasoning that the audience was large enough to sell the volume.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-e9cpKN69Avk" title="121GW Development History">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-xUAKjWMsUfA" title="TWO BOXES of Mystery Multimeters!">•</a></sup> Formal development dates to 2014–2015.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-e9cpKN69Avk" title="121GW Development History">•</a></sup> Early specification work benchmarked the Klein Tools MM6000, the Fluke 87 and the UEI 397, and at that stage the design still assumed AA cells and included power measurement.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-e9cpKN69Avk" title="121GW Development History">•</a></sup>

Because the manufacturer paid for development, the brand side deliberately avoided micromanaging the board layout and circuit design, accepting design compromises in exchange.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-121" title="EEVblog 121GW Multimeter Kickstarter Live Chat">121</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-e9cpKN69Avk" title="121GW Development History">•</a></sup> Development ran about two years — not continuous effort, but a back-and-forth cycle of prototype, shipment, investigation and re-spin — with the whole design re-spun once in the middle.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-e9cpKN69Avk" title="121GW Development History">•</a></sup> By February 2016 the [[schematic]], case, user interface and buttons were finalised and tooling was under way; the HY3131 chipset, the MOV and PTC [[input-protection|input protection]], and the MAX4258-based burden-voltage circuit were all in place by then.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-e9cpKN69Avk" title="121GW Development History">•</a></sup> Through 2017 the outstanding work was [[calibration]] and settling-time issues, followed by a linearity bug and a lockup bug in June.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-e9cpKN69Avk" title="121GW Development History">•</a></sup>

The first case design was rejected for size: it was well made but consumed too much volumetric space and weight for shipping in the thousands, so a smaller case was developed that suited both the 121GW and the [[bm235|BM235]].<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-e9cpKN69Avk" title="121GW Development History">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-xUAKjWMsUfA" title="TWO BOXES of Mystery Multimeters!">•</a></sup> The meter body itself ended up roughly on par with the BM235 in size, having originally been physically larger and differently shaped with AA cells.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-e9cpKN69Avk" title="121GW Development History">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-xUAKjWMsUfA" title="TWO BOXES of Mystery Multimeters!">•</a></sup> The earliest prototype was entirely 3D printed, including the knob and a plastic stand-in for the rubber boot, and predates the 121GW name — it was then called the EEVblog [[true-rms|True RMS]] meter.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-121" title="EEVblog 121GW Multimeter Kickstarter Live Chat">121</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-xUAKjWMsUfA" title="TWO BOXES of Mystery Multimeters!">•</a></sup> Case tooling was custom and expensive, and the manufacturer negotiated the right to reuse the case on other non-competing products.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-e9cpKN69Avk" title="121GW Development History">•</a></sup>

Two years of development for a meter of this complexity is not unusual; the considerably simpler BM235 also took Brymen over two years from original concept to first shipment.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-e9cpKN69Avk" title="121GW Development History">•</a></sup>

## Measurement architecture

The meter is built on the HY3131 measurement chipset, the same part used in the Keysight U1282A.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-uUCQuIp_hzU" title="121GW Ohms Autoranging Speed Update">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-izCDWjK_pV4" title="121GW Autoranging Speed">•</a></sup> The [[microcontroller]] is an STM32L1, chosen for low power, cost and availability at the time, and for its [[lcd-driver|LCD driver]] capability.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1539" title="EEVblog 1539 - NEW PROJECT Part 3 - STM32L vs PIC24F">1539</a></sup>

[[input-impedance|Input impedance]] is the conventional 10 MΩ except in low-[[impedance]] mode, where a PTC sits directly across the input.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-121" title="EEVblog 121GW Multimeter Kickstarter Live Chat">121</a></sup> That PTC starts at roughly 2 kΩ cold and rises as mains current heats it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-121" title="EEVblog 121GW Multimeter Kickstarter Live Chat">121</a></sup> The same range-switch wiper arrangement that creates low-impedance mode also shorts the input through the ohms path, which is what allows the meter to safely discharge a [[capacitor]] by switching briefly to ohms.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1655" title="EEVblog 1655 - How to Discharge Capacitors SAFELY using a Multimeter!">1655</a></sup>

Late in development, thermal chamber testing showed more drift than intended, and a better [[voltage-reference|voltage reference]] was fitted despite the cost target.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-121" title="EEVblog 121GW Multimeter Kickstarter Live Chat">121</a></sup> The design still avoids the laser-trimmed and ceramic hybrid precision divider networks used in higher-end meters, a deliberate cost decision.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-121" title="EEVblog 121GW Multimeter Kickstarter Live Chat">121</a></sup> A later revision, the 1707-dated model, added a further improved voltage reference and lower-tempco divider resistors.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-Wwz_fdU17aQ" title="121GW Multimeter Thermal Test">•</a></sup> In a ramped thermal chamber test against a Gossen MetraHit Ultra and a Keysight U1282A, that revision held a 5 V reference to better than 0.01 % over a 32 °C delta, while the MetraHit dropped one least significant digit and the Keysight drifted substantially more.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-Wwz_fdU17aQ" title="121GW Multimeter Thermal Test">•</a></sup>

## 15 V diode mode

The diode test mode drives up to about 15 V of compliance, well above the two or three volts typical of a multimeter.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1452" title="EEVblog 1452 - Fluke PM3370B Combiscope REPAIR">1452</a></sup> This makes it possible to test parts a conventional meter cannot: series [[diode]] stacks in an EHT section that read open on an ordinary diode range will read around 10.7 V on the 121GW, and an 8.2 V zener can be checked directly in its reverse direction.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1452" title="EEVblog 1452 - Fluke PM3370B Combiscope REPAIR">1452</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1394" title="EEVblog 1394 - Onkyo Receiver Repair - Part 1">1394</a></sup>

The high test voltage propagates into the input protection. The diode mode shares the ohms input circuitry, which is protected by the back-to-back dual-[[transistor]] zener clamp that Fluke popularised and that appears in countless multimeters.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1157" title="EEVblog #1157 - Transistor Zener Clamp Circuit">1157</a></sup> That clamp relies on the reverse emitter-base breakdown of a [[bipolar-transistor|bipolar transistor]], and VEBO is typically only six to twelve volts on general-purpose parts — a 2N2222 or 2N3904 is adequate for ordinary meters but not here.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1157" title="EEVblog #1157 - Transistor Zener Clamp Circuit">1157</a></sup> The 121GW instead needs a part rated around 20 V or more, which is scarce; the Rohm IMX25, a dual general-purpose transistor with VEBO of 25 V, is one of the few that qualifies.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1157" title="EEVblog #1157 - Transistor Zener Clamp Circuit">1157</a></sup> The clamp cannot dissipate much energy, but it clamps fast enough to protect the ohms circuit and survives long enough for the PTC to take over.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1157" title="EEVblog #1157 - Transistor Zener Clamp Circuit">1157</a></sup>

## Input protection and overload behaviour

The voltage input carries a nominal 1.5 kΩ PTC in series with a 1 kΩ high-voltage protection [[resistor]], clamped by two 575 V MOVs.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-YPUJipe8Loo" title="121GW Meter 1210V Test">•</a></sup> The meter beeps and shows a lightning-bolt warning indicator above 600 V.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-YPUJipe8Loo" title="121GW Meter 1210V Test">•</a></sup> Against a high-voltage DC supply it survived 1210 V, the supply's limit.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-YPUJipe8Loo" title="121GW Meter 1210V Test">•</a></sup> On AC — where the peaks are considerably higher than the equivalent DC — it sustained 1100 V against a 600 V AC rating, occasionally tripping its protection but continuing to measure correctly afterwards.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-vwVXl-RQWXs" title="121GW High Volatge AC Test">•</a></sup>

Under crude high-voltage impulse testing, the MOVs clamp the incoming [[waveform]] in the same way as those in the BM235, letting the PTCs, series protection resistors and diode clamps do their work; meters with only a PTC and no MOVs or gas discharge tubes survive by luck rather than engineering.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1016" title="EEVblog #1016 - Crude Multimeter High Voltage Overload Testing">1016</a></sup>

The rotary [[range-switch|range switch]] was retained rather than soft pushbuttons because a multi-ganged mechanical switch gets isolation between ranges more cheaply, simply and reliably; achieving the same integrity with electronic switching requires considerably more circuitry.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1095" title="EEVblog #1095 - Is a $38 Multimeter any good? ANENG Q1 Review (4K!)">1095</a></sup>

## Low burden voltage

The meter incorporates a µCurrent-style ×100 amplifier ahead of the [[current-shunt|current shunt]], giving a [[burden-voltage|burden voltage]] substantially lower than a conventional multimeter, where burden can reach a volt or more on the low current ranges.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1604" title="EEVblog 1604 - BEWARE! Multimeter Burden Voltage">1604</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1533" title="EEVblog 1533 - How To Estimate Product Battery Life">1533</a></sup> The burden voltage is not zero — it is only lower — so the specified figure still has to be checked against the requirement at hand.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1604" title="EEVblog 1604 - BEWARE! Multimeter Burden Voltage">1604</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1533" title="EEVblog 1533 - How To Estimate Product Battery Life">1533</a></sup> The low-burden range has limited headroom for load current: a device drawing 22 mA would not start up through it and had to be measured on the 500 mA range instead.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1238" title="EEVblog #1238 - Vion: The World&#x27;s Worst Multimeter!">1238</a></sup>

## Firmware and auto-ranging

[[firmware|Firmware]] is user-updatable, which is unusual — most manufacturers do not permit end users to reflash their meters.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-XOuYJ1nxVJQ" title="BM787BT Bluetooth CYBLE-212006-01 Fimware OTA Update">•</a></sup> The procedure copies a .bin file to the root directory of the [[sd-card|SD card]], then holds the HOLD and MEM buttons while powering on and presses SETUP to start the download.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-96XE5Ye1q6I" title="121GW EEVblog Multmeter Firmware Update">•</a></sup>

Ohms auto-ranging speed was the meter's weakest characteristic at launch and was addressed across several releases: 1.04 improved it, 1.05 improved it again, and 2.00 substantially increased both the high-[[resistance]] update rate and general ohms auto-ranging.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-uUCQuIp_hzU" title="121GW Ohms Autoranging Speed Update">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-q4gXnpFPFzQ" title="121GW Multimeter 2.00 Firmware">•</a></sup> Version 2.00 also fixed a [[capacitance]]-range bug that had previously required a manual step.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-q4gXnpFPFzQ" title="121GW Multimeter 2.00 Firmware">•</a></sup> By firmware 2.02 a 10 Ω resistor auto-ranged in roughly three seconds — on par with, or a shade better than, the Keysight U1282A running the same HY3131 chipset, and slower than the Brymen BM869, which settles in under a second.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-izCDWjK_pV4" title="121GW Autoranging Speed">•</a></sup> Auto-ranging speed is largely a matter of how each manufacturer drives the chipset rather than a hard limit of the silicon: other implementations of the HY3131 have reportedly reached about two seconds.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-uUCQuIp_hzU" title="121GW Ohms Autoranging Speed Update">•</a></sup>

An EEVblog forum user, tpwrules, decompiled the firmware from the binary and produced a modified build that fixed the slow auto-ranging without owning the meter.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-3MD88EYLdQQ" title="121GW Hacked firmware test">•</a></sup>

## Range switch defect

Some early production meters developed intermittent range-switch contacts, a fault never seen in any prototype.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-e9cpKN69Avk" title="121GW Development History">•</a></sup> The cause was a minor, unannounced change in [[pcb|PCB]] thickness from the supplier: contact force in the rotary switch depends on a controlled board thickness, and once that tolerance stacked with the others, contacts could become marginal — enough to affect perhaps one meter in a thousand rather than the whole run.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-e9cpKN69Avk" title="121GW Development History">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1259" title="EEVblog #1259 - PCB Manufacturing Options EXPLAINED">1259</a></sup> The same tolerance mechanism was later considered as a candidate explanation for switch complaints on other meters.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-9u8TEJdAVdQ" title="Returned Faulty BM235 Switch Investigation">•</a></sup>

The fix was a routed FR4 shim supplied by UEI, with tooling holes locating it against the routed edges.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-u2-ot2vWLxI" title="121GW Range Switch Cycle Testing">•</a></sup> Validation used a purpose-built automated range-switch endurance tester: 3D-printed knob adapters driven by a stepper motor and controller, with a fan fitted because the thermal behaviour of a continuously cycled switch was unknown, running batches of 1000 full end-to-end cycles.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-QSmiMlWEpy0" title="121GW Range Switch Cycling Test Jig">•</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-u2-ot2vWLxI" title="121GW Range Switch Cycle Testing">•</a></sup>

## Companion software

The mobile and desktop applications were written in Xamarin C# as a cross-platform project.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src--_rqzsJy_Qs" title="Demonstrating Creation of a Xamarin C# Multiplatform Calculator App">•</a></sup> The Bluetooth app connects without requiring an account or login.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1238" title="EEVblog #1238 - Vion: The World&#x27;s Worst Multimeter!">1238</a></sup> It does request location permission, which is a quirk of the Bluetooth driver rather than a data-collection measure.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1238" title="EEVblog #1238 - Vion: The World&#x27;s Worst Multimeter!">1238</a></sup> The Windows application was developed with high-DPI scaling disabled during development and the proper scaling applied afterwards, avoiding the blurry-font problem that afflicts applications compiled without DPI awareness.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-qjLXJl0kDjg" title="How to fix Windows blurry font scaling problems">•</a></sup>

## Measurement capabilities in use

The [[frequency-counter|frequency counter]] reads to 100 kHz, useful for switching supplies where a lower-[[bandwidth]] meter reads incorrectly.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1602" title="EEVblog 1602 - Sony Amplifier REPAIR ADVENTURE">1602</a></sup> AC millivolt measurement resolves [[noise]] floors in the hundreds of microvolts — around 250 µV RMS across a 4 Ω tweeter in one measurement.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1128" title="EEVblog #1128 - Studio Monitor Speaker Noise - Part 2">1128</a></sup> Current logging over time is supported directly, allowing the current drawn by a component to be recorded through a reflow profile.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1455" title="EEVblog 1455 - Capacitors Produce Current During Reflow Soldering! WTF!">1455</a></sup> [[thermocouple|Thermocouple]] [[temperature-measurement|temperature measurement]] tracks a reference meter closely, agreeing to within about a degree on a shorted-probe internal reading.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-48J0m3dvpgA" title="Multimeter Fridge Test">•</a></sup> Simultaneous volts and amps display on the two displays is a genuine capability, unlike meters that advertise a VA display without implementing it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1095" title="EEVblog #1095 - Is a $38 Multimeter any good? ANENG Q1 Review (4K!)">1095</a></sup>

Capacitance and [[inductance]] are measured with a triangular test waveform at a little over 100 Hz, not the clean [[sine-wave|sine wave]] at a selected [[frequency]] that a proper [[lcr-meter|LCR meter]] produces, and the test frequency varies with the measured capacitance.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1465" title="EEVblog 1465 - Your Multimeter Can Measure Inductors">1465</a></sup>

## Production and related designs

Production units shipped in cases of 32, manufactured in Korea, with a thermocouple and a statement of calibration in the box, and factory firmware version 1.01.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1051" title="EEVblog #1051 - 121GW Multimeter Mass Turbulence">1051</a></sup> A hand-and-face motif is moulded into the inside of the case rather than printed on the front, a deliberate choice against putting it on the fascia.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-121" title="EEVblog 121GW Multimeter Kickstarter Live Chat">121</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#src-xUAKjWMsUfA" title="TWO BOXES of Mystery Multimeters!">•</a></sup> Component cost crept above the contracted price during development, partly from the added voltage reference and other precision parts, and partly from broker pricing on scarce chips.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-121" title="EEVblog 121GW Multimeter Kickstarter Live Chat">121</a></sup>

Kain Test retained the rights to the design, having financed and executed it, with exclusive distribution rights to the 121GW itself held by EEVblog.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1668" title="EEVblog 1668 - HORRIFIC Alkaline Battery Leakage in Rare Prototype Multimeter">1668</a></sup> A separate prototype meter, the EEVblog DMM555, was later built on the 121GW design and reuses the identical case moulding, including the [[battery]] compartment and range-switch contacts.<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-1668" title="EEVblog 1668 - HORRIFIC Alkaline Battery Leakage in Rare Prototype Multimeter">1668</a></sup>
