# pwm

Pulse width modulation is a fixed-frequency digital waveform whose on-time, or duty cycle, is varied from 0 to 100% to carry an analog value on a single switching output.[225] Because the waveform is just a digital pin toggling, it can be produced by any microcontroller IO pin in software or, better, by a dedicated hardware block, which makes it effectively free compared with the alternatives of a precision potentiometer or a digital-to-analog converter.[225] Filtered into a DC level, it substitutes for a DAC; left unfiltered, it directly meters energy into LEDs, heaters, motors and switching regulators, and in some systems it is used purely as a signalling protocol.

## Duty cycle and averaging

The period is fixed — 10 kHz is a typical microcontroller PWM frequency — and only the fraction of that period spent high changes.[225] The average value of the waveform follows the duty cycle directly: a 0 to 1 V waveform that is high 10% of the time and low the other 90% averages to one tenth of its peak.[1417][225] In simulation, a 1 V pulse source with a 100 µs period and a 10 µs on-time produces exactly 100 mV after an RC filter, and an op-amp buffering that node settles to the same value after a short delay.[225] Pushing the on-time down to 0.1 µs out of 100 µs — one part in a thousand of a 5 V rail — yields 5 mV out, which a precision op-amp with only a couple of hundred microvolts of offset reproduces faithfully.[225]

The same arithmetic governs power rather than voltage when the load is not filtered. Chopping a resistive heating element off for half the time delivers half the energy, and no amount of pulsing changes that; the element's resistance is chosen for full-power operation and the laws of physics are not negotiable.[1186]

## Replacing a DAC

Few microcontrollers have a DAC built in, and an external one costs a couple of dollars against the ten dollars or so of a good 10-turn potentiometer, so PWM plus a resistor and capacitor is the cheap third option.[225] A PWM of a given bit depth gives the same voltage resolution as a DAC of the same bit depth.[225] An 8-bit module divides the range into 256 steps of 0.39% each, which on a 5 V reference is 19.5 mV per step — acceptable for a general-purpose bench supply, less so once the output range is doubled.[225] A design wanting more than the 10-bit resolution typical of microcontroller peripherals may be better served by an external 12-bit DAC and ADC instead.[232]

Absolute accuracy is set by whatever the PWM output swings between, so the microcontroller rail becomes the reference: a part powered from a 7805 inherits that regulator's 5% tolerance, and the modulator output is 5% accurate in absolute terms — not good enough for a precision bench supply without software calibration or a trimmed gain stage.[225] Modern FET output stages swing to within a millivolt or so of the rails, so the rail itself, not the driver, is the limiting term.[225] Any noise present on the PWM waveform that the filter fails to remove passes through the buffer and appears on the regulated output.[222]

The filtered node must be buffered before it drives anything.[221][222] An op-amp voltage follower is the standard answer, since no current flows into its inputs and it therefore does not disturb the filter.[600] The high input impedance of that op-amp is also why an RC filter works here at all: a plain RC, even a two-stage one, is hopeless at anything but very low currents, but when the destination draws essentially no current, values of a few to ten microfarads with hundreds of ohms or a kilohm of series resistance will knock a PWM waveform down to negligible ripple.[1116] The same trick lets a function-generator-grade voltage be produced without a DAC at all, fed to a voltage follower and on to a load.[1701]

## Filtering, ripple and response time

Filter design is a direct trade of ripple against settling time. A single RC with a 3 dB point at 159 Hz, calculated from 1/2πRC, attenuates a 10 kHz PWM poorly and leaves around 10 mV of ripple.[225] Increasing the capacitance tenfold moves the corner to 15.9 Hz and, because the response is first order, drops the ripple by the same order of magnitude.[225] Adding a second RC stage of 10k and 100n is more efficient than simply enlarging the first: it brings ripple down into the hundreds of microvolts, around 0.1 to 0.2 mV, while keeping the response time near 10 ms.[225] Filter amplitude figures quoted for a sine wave do not transfer directly to a PWM waveform, whose harmonic content makes the analysis more involved; in practice the residual is lower than the sine-wave estimate suggests.[225] Where the PWM swings 0 to 5 V but only 0 to 1 V of control range is wanted, a series resistor into the filter attenuates it — adding 10k halves an expected 500 mV to 250 mV.[225] Because such a control loop is only ever slewed by a human turning a knob at a few hertz, the slow filter costs nothing in usability.[225]

## Hardware modules and pin allocation

Dedicated PWM hardware inside a microcontroller generates the waveform independently of the running code, and is strongly preferred over bit-banging it in software.[225] On AVR parts the PWM function is built on the output compare module, so only the handful of pins carrying OC functions can be used — typically five or six on a small part — and the LEDs or loads must be routed to those pins at schematic time.[238] Peripheral pin sharing is a recurring trap in microcontroller selection: LCD segment drivers, ADC channels, I²C and PWM outputs frequently contend for the same physical pins, and a part can be ruled out because the ADC channels needed are mapped onto the LCD pins.[248]

Moving IO onto an I²C port expander frees microcontroller pins but forfeits the fast peripheral functions: PWM cannot usefully be generated through an I²C bus, because the expander has no internal hardware to do it.[238] That trade is sometimes worth taking — RGB LCD backlight brightness is a low-value capability to lose in exchange for cheaper pin count.[259]

Available PWM channels are therefore a real selection criterion, not a footnote. A design needing two PWM channels, a couple of 10-bit ADC channels and 24 IO lines can eliminate candidate parts purely on PWM count, as with an ATtiny offering only one channel where the rest of the family offers at least two; an ATmega8A with three channels in a TQFP32 satisfies it.[87] Some parts advertise three high-frequency PWM outputs alongside differential ADC pairs, which suits high-side current sensing in the same design.[87] Manufacturer parametric search is more useful than distributor search for this, since the vendor's own site exposes the peripheral counts.[87] Even microcontrollers costing a few cents carry the function: one such part offers three hardware 11-bit PWM generators with a 12-bit ADC, and a five-pin SOT23 variant with a comparator and internal reference does PWM without an ADC at all — cheap enough to drop into a product purely to generate an 8 kHz waveform so the main processor does not have to.[1132] Historically, missing a PWM peripheral was enough to lose a design socket outright to a competitor.[d1D3d3YIc9A]

## LED dimming and flicker

PWM is the standard method for setting LED brightness, from individually addressable strips and DMX fixtures switching MOSFETs into 12 V LED strings, to I²C drivers such as the PCA9632 with 8-bit PWM resolution per channel across 16 outputs.[227][928][1453][787] The advantage over a linear dim is efficiency, and pulsing LEDs hard but briefly — 20 mA or more for a short fraction of each cycle — extracts good light output for very little average current.[708] Taken to its limit, pulse width modulating an indicator LED at a very low duty allows eight years of battery life from a sensor light.[684] A small strap light generating its brightness levels from a microcontroller PWM still holds 5 V at the far end of its cable at both extremes of brightness.[1010] Constant-current LED drive is the alternative and is what avoids the problem entirely.[765][361]

Flicker is the characteristic failure mode. It appears when the PWM frequency falls low enough to enter the visual range of 60, 50 or 40 Hz, and it is worst at intermediate brightness.[361] At 0% and 100% there is no flicker content because the waveform is not modulating at all; ceiling panels showing significant strobing at half brightness show none at full, and turning them to maximum removes both the strobe on a camera LCD and the modulation itself.[361][364] Whether it is captured on camera depends on the relationship between the modulation frequency and the frame rate — 25 frames per second in one such test.[361] Some products avoid PWM in favour of an adjustable constant current, with no detectable flicker even at the lowest setting.[370] Long-exposure photography is more forgiving than expected: PWM-dimmed RGB LEDs did not produce artefacts in long exposures despite the concern.[227]

The other cost of dimming is conducted and radiated noise. LED panel lights that were capable of PWM dimming generated substantial noise when dimmed and none when run at full brightness on constant current, and a set of studio lights running pulse width modulated is enough to couple noise into a supply-ripple measurement setup and corrupt it.[765][594] Noise attributed to a switching plug pack can in fact come from the modulation in the luminaires themselves.[441]

PWM cannot rescue a driver from a peak-current limit. Where a shift register such as an HC164 must share current between many multiplexed segments, no duty cycle scheme increases the instantaneous current the chip can source; attempting to drive segments at hundreds of milliamps simply exceeds the output rating.[1491]

## Switching converters

Switch-mode power supplies are the classic high-power application. The TL494 is a generic PWM controller — not a complete switch-mode voltage regulator as such, but usable for several things, with switch-mode supplies among its primary uses; in a 40 A bench supply it drives a pair of MOSFETs into the main transformer.[272] The same part appears controlling the main transformer in an insulation resistance meter, and a Signetics SG3524D fills the role in a handheld scopemeter's DC-DC section.[468][430] A variable-frequency AC source generating a selectable 40 Hz to 440 Hz sine output with typical distortion under 5% does so with PWM technology.[449] A mains-driven LED tube converter adds zero-current and valley switching, cycle-by-cycle control and fast transient response on top of the modulation.[533]

Not all converters run in PWM all the time. Many switch between a normal or pulse-frequency mode at very low load, where PFM gives greater efficiency, and PWM at higher currents, and a discontinuity in an efficiency curve is a signature of that mode change.[139][957] The peak inductor current limit is itself specified per mode — around 2.7 A at 5 V out for one boost converter in PWM mode.[139] Resonant converters are the other branch: an LLC resonant design switches smoothly rather than hard, in contrast to a conventional PWM converter which varies both pulse width and, in some modes, frequency.[1294]

Gate drive at high frequency is its own problem. Isolated GaN driver chips accept the PWM signal on the low-voltage side, cross a galvanically isolated interface inside the package, and drive both gate and source of the transistor from a totem-pole output stage — one driver per transistor.[1557] MOSFETs are the natural switching element for pulse width modulating loads, being voltage driven with no standing base current and faster than BJTs into the megahertz region.[1736]

## Motors, fans and actuators

A BLDC motor drive takes rectified mains down to a 12 V DC rail and then modulates the gate driver to synthesise the AC that turns the motor.[VTHcxTst_RA] A spacecraft-style motor controller runs its pulse modulation at about 64 kHz, and can afford to leave it asynchronous to everything else because the controllers sit physically outside the shielded aluminium enclosure.[HBLKxC4JzTA] Locked anti-phase drive is a variant in which the motor is driven forward and then reverse within every PWM cycle.[551]

Fan control depends on the connector. A three-pin fan has a tachometer output reporting speed but no control input, so the only way to vary it is to change the supply voltage; PWM control requires the four-pin type.[4oHUuXgK_i4] A fan controller board with a temperature sensor ramps the duty cycle on its PWM pin linearly from 10% to 100% between a low and a high temperature threshold.[-9l41zNWlxY] Instrument fans are similarly driven from a temperature sensor through a power transistor.[621]

Modulated drive turns up in unexpected mechanisms — a breast pump's motor drive shows packets of variable pulse width modulation lasting a few milliseconds each, correlated with the pump cycle.[330] A toothbrush charger applies pulsed charging to its cell at around 800 Hz, visible as charge ripple appearing and disappearing on the battery and as a corresponding amplitude change in the transformer waveform as the extra current is drawn.[284] An electropermanent magnet exposes an RC-style PWM interface alongside CAN and other buses, and can be operated from a 50 Hz 5 V PWM signal.[1656][759][964]

## PWM as a signalling protocol

In electric vehicle charging the modulation carries no power at all. An EVSE is not really a charger — it contains a large relay that switches the AC through and a small microcontroller that sends a PWM signal to the car telling it how much current is available, after which the car regulates its own charging.[1507] The duty cycle of this pilot signal is the message: 50% is the maximum on one 7 kW unit, at which the car elected to draw 29 amps.[31gkF0q9VfE] A solar-diverting charger varies the pilot duty cycle continuously, with the car reading it and adjusting its charge rate roughly once per second; a configured 10% floor sets the minimum charge rate, which means a small amount of grid import when the excess solar falls below it.[EYx46kRv2Bw] The whole loop is slow, since the controller must respond to the power measurement, change the PWM, and then wait for the car to change its draw.[EYx46kRv2Bw] Such units report the pilot duty cycle on their own display.[1437]

Elsewhere the waveform is the data channel by convention: an optical rangefinder module works correctly in PWM mode while its I²C mode is unusable because of the busy-wait NAK it sends.[759] A four-channel Cirrus Logic digital audio driver puts out four PWM outputs into external hex inverters and discrete transistor output stages.[1514] A camcorder system-on-chip includes mono PWM audio intended for an external amplifier.[595]

## Generating PWM without a microcontroller

The 555 timer in its standard PWM configuration remains a valid answer for dimming. An adjustment pot sweeps the duty cycle over very nearly the full range — measured from 8.8% up to 99.6% at the output pin, and from 0.3% to 99.2% at the discharge pin — with the frequency changing somewhat as the pot is turned, which for a dimmer does not matter.[392] The circuit works equally well switching a constant-current source on and off as switching a voltage, so an existing current source can be retained.[392] The inversion introduced by a PNP output stage shifts which end of the pot's travel corresponds to full brightness.[392] Discrete 555-based PWM controllers with a pot adjusting the pulse width turn up in commercial products.[1291] The 558 quad 555 was used for a joystick port, with the joystick potentiometers setting the timer's pulse width and a timer counter chip measuring the resulting pulse length to derive position.[1053]

A microcontroller PWM output can also drive charge-pump topologies directly: a single pin producing a 10 kHz waveform into a Dickson doubler yields around 4.62 V DC into a 10k load, enough for a small LCD's bias, and tying the pump's reference to another pin allows the output to be switched off entirely under software control.[473]

## Measurement and diagnosis

Whether a load is PWM driven is usually settled with an oscilloscope. A current probe on an LCD backlight string immediately distinguishes constant-current drive from modulation at around 120 Hz.[915] An open-collector output suspected of being a simple logic level turned out to carry a PWM signal, explaining a flicker whose rate changed with temperature.[242] Spectral analysis of the audible tone from an electronic safe lock identified a 4.077 kHz peak matching a 4.075 kHz measurement, confirming that the tone was the PWM signal driving the piezo transducer.[762] Not every rhythmic waveform is a modulation channel, and identifying what an unexplained pulse train actually drives can remain open.[330] Silkscreen and package markings can also give it away — a part labelled with PWM feeding thick traces is likely a stepper or motor driver.[1011]
