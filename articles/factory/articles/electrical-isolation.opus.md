# electrical isolation

Electrical isolation is the deliberate absence of a conductive path between two parts of a system, so that energy or information crosses between them by some other means -- magnetic coupling, light, or a small deliberate capacitance -- while no current can flow directly.[279][1152] In an isolation transformer the primary and secondary are wound in separate plastic bobbin sections and share only the laminated core, giving physical separation and therefore electrical separation between them.[1152][279] The consequence that matters is that an isolated output floats: it has no fixed relationship to mains earth, so touching one point of it does not complete a circuit through a person or through a grounded instrument.[279][543]

Isolation is what makes a supply behave, from a safety point of view, exactly like a battery.[279] A supply with electrical isolation and no earth connection at all is equivalent to a battery-powered one, whatever the topology behind it -- half-wave bridge rectifier, switching power supply, switching plug pack, or a conventional transformer-based plug pack all make no difference so long as the isolation barrier is intact.[279]

## Isolation as a test-equipment feature

The defining selling point of a handheld oscilloscope is that its channels are isolated from one another: the BNC shells are not electrically connected, and on a fully isolated instrument the multimeter input is isolated from both scope channels as well, giving three independent front ends.[810] That independence is what carries the safety rating -- 300 V CAT III on the scope BNCs and 600 V CAT III on the multimeter inputs on one such instrument.[810] Isolated channels are also what let a scope make high-side measurements: an older isolated mini-lab could measure across a primary, a secondary and the high side simultaneously at up to 1,000 V isolation, 660 V RMS.[1724] Purpose-built isolated scopes push this further, with a kilovolt of isolation between each channel, the other channels and ground on one four-channel design,[1119] and 2 kV per channel at 200 MHz bandwidth on a later one.[1753] The practical payoff is probing the innards of a live motor drive -- gate drive and transistor saturation voltage on a rail swinging 320 volts -- without shorting anything through the scope's earth.[1119]

Where two isolated channels exist, they allow two points of a circuit to be probed at the same time without either measurement disturbing the other.[804] Isolation must extend to the accessories: isolated probes with a full plastic shroud surrounding the BNC prevent accidental contact with the connector when it is plugged in.[810]

Not every probe is isolated, and some are conspicuously not. A high-voltage differential probe with a consistent 4 megohms measured between either input terminal and the output ground is not isolated -- the resistance is the same on both inputs and does not change when the leads are swapped, which rules out active circuitry behind it.[932] An active FET probe with a ±25 V input range is isolated neither from its USB connection nor from its output, and is not intended to be.[1715]

## The USB port as the isolation problem

Adding a USB socket to a handheld meter creates the safety question that dominates its design: whether the port's ground is isolated from the measurement common.[1704][1248] A meter measuring 5.8 kilohms between USB ground and the input common has no isolation and will not pass a safety standard.[1704] The compliance question is unavoidable for any battery-powered process meter or multimeter that exposes an external USB connector -- the port has to be isolated for the product to be certifiable.[1248] A cheap USB-C-equipped meter without that isolation is an exposure risk in normal handheld use, and proper USB electrical isolation is expensive enough that its absence is a reasonable default assumption on very low-cost gear.[D2PANd9Hu3U]

When the isolation is present it is worth having for reasons beyond safety: an isolated USB-C port can be left permanently connected to a computer for data logging while the meter is in use.[1723] Measurement distinguishes the cases -- one instrument shows genuine isolation between the multimeter common and the function generator and oscilloscope grounds, and at least megohms to the USB-C port, while its oscilloscope ground remains connected straight through to USB ground.[1723] An $80 scope-meter with the scope ground electrically isolated from the multimeter common is acceptable from a safety point of view at that price, and the useful test is the breakdown voltage of the common jack relative to the isolated scope input.[1540] A USB-C-powered bench scope is only technically isolated: 1 megohm from earth to the shield through a bleeder rather than a direct connection, which is why such an instrument ships with a low-impedance earth lead for the chassis.[1563]

Isolation is also lost by accident through power connections. Adding 5 V USB charging to an otherwise isolated instrument breaks the isolation the moment it is powered from a non-isolated 5 V source such as a PC.[259] Conversely, isolation designed into an instrument is wasted effort when the source is already floating: a supply built as a completely isolated design so it could be run from anything is crippled for no reason if it is normally run from a battery pack, which is isolated already.[MecbPOPeovs] Isolation costs output power as well -- the fully isolated version of that design managed only five to ten watts.[MecbPOPeovs]

## Interfaces across the barrier

Instruments are routinely split into an isolated analog measurement section and a digital processing section, with the split visible in the layout as a break in the ground planes and an absence of traces across it.[427][1189] A bench multimeter's digital and display section sits electrically isolated from the analog measurement part below, with a shield over the boundary and optocouplers as the only crossing.[427] The same split appears in a programmable power supply, where the Ethernet, USB, RS-232 and logic output grounds are all electrically isolated from the supply outputs, with optocouplers carrying serial data across and a separate small supply for the isolated side derived from extra taps on the mains transformer.[511] Output circuitry in a distortion analyser is likewise kept totally electrically isolated from the generation circuitry, with a serial interface crossing through optical isolators.[478]

The crossing mechanisms are few. Optocouplers, sometimes visible as a discrete infrared LED and infrared phototransistor pair facing each other across the barrier, carry signals;[1489] modern instruments use packaged digital isolators for the same job, with further isolation between the serial and GPIB interfaces and the analog section.[1189] An unusual variant pairs an LED with a CdS photocell rather than a phototransistor, which provides isolation and was also used as the stabilising element in precision oscillators.[709] Power crosses through isolated DC-to-DC converter modules -- the transformer inside is what provides the isolation.[1313][MecbPOPeovs] These are used even where the isolation is not strictly needed: a current probe whose sensing head makes no electrical contact with the circuit still uses an isolated ±5 V DC-to-DC converter for its supply, purely as belt and braces.[1413] Where isolation must be kept, a separate board carrying an isolated USB-to-serial interface, or a wireless link, keeps a controlling computer's earth reference out of the instrument's grounds -- rear-panel connectors referenced to a computer that is referenced to mains earth cause major problems in a power supply.[232]

## Layout, clearance and construction

Isolation is enforced physically as much as electrically. An isolated section shows up on a board as a visible routed path or gap around the region, with the ground plane starting on one side and absent on the other so the mains area is totally separate.[511][164][1691] Slots milled through the board increase the creepage distance; isolation slots between input divider resistors are what let a differential probe meet its voltage rating, alongside custom sealed plastic enclosures giving complete separation between the two input paths.[1744] The same technique appears at very low currents for the opposite reason: generous isolation, including a slot around a free-standing low-leakage reed relay, keeps leakage current out of a sensitive node.[1189] At the extreme, components in an electrometer front end are mounted on Teflon standoffs and must not touch the PCB or other components at all.[1017]

A multimeter input path with no traces at all around the input sockets -- the connection going straight through -- is about as good as isolation gets, when there is board area to spare for it.[1393] Good practice extends to mechanical detail: a rubber insulating mat inside a transformer prevents sharp metalwork from piercing the winding wrap and lowering its electrical isolation,[828] and stick-on insulating feet stop a board shorting to raised pins behind it.[917] Bad practice is equally visible. A mains flyback supply whose outputs are isolated from the mains can still have inadequate clearance around individual pads, which is unlikely to meet any standard;[525] a mains switch-mode section with a DIP switch sitting next to the mains input filter capacitors shows no real thought given to separating low voltage from the mains side.[525] In quality meters the high-voltage input protection -- HRC fuses, spark gaps, high-voltage axial resistors and a PTC -- is itself isolated from the main electronics, with a long path around the barrier before anything reaches the inside.[15][1083]

## Isolating heat from electricity

A large class of isolation problems is thermal rather than electrical in origin: the metal tab of a power device is usually a live pin, so any attempt to use metalwork to cool it must break the electrical connection while keeping the thermal one.[1347][744] Because a device's tab is typically V out, V in or some other electrical node rather than ground, it cannot simply be via-stitched to a ground or power plane -- doing so shorts it out.[1347] This is the reason sil pads exist and why insulation is used on the majority of thermal devices.[1347] A mica washer or sil pad between the tab and the case or heatsink electrically isolates the device while still transferring heat, at the cost of some thermal performance.[744][1196] Plastic spacers holding a heatsink down serve the same purpose, with a sil pad underneath transferring heat while leaving the heatsink electrically isolated.[607] Where multiple devices share a heatsink they generally need isolating from each other as well.[744]

The mounting hardware is a trap of its own: screws are metal, so a board needs a clear isolation ring around each screw hole to stop the screw from touching copper it should not touch, while still allowing enough clamping pressure.[744] The problem can also be designed away. If a free-standing TO220 has enough clearance to the enclosure that they cannot touch, no insulating washer is needed at all;[644] equally, a device whose thermal tab is isolated internally, or genuinely grounded, removes the conflict entirely.[1347] SMD thermal jumpers address the same trade-off in surface mount: they look like ordinary chip resistors in various aspect ratios, contain no resistance and are electrically isolated, but conduct heat, using the standard footprint for their package size.[1347] The alternative -- devoting a large isolated copper area on the top or bottom layer as a dedicated heatsink for one device -- works thermally but consumes routing area.[1347]

## Medical, industrial and mains-side isolation

Isolation requirements scale with what is on the other side of the barrier. Equipment connected to a person is built around it: a medical-grade isolation transformer, complete ground separation, and high-value high-voltage resistors in series with each electrode lead make it safe to connect leads to both wrists.[660] An ultrasound machine's ECG front end has its own isolated analog ground plane, a large routed gap, an isolation amplifier and a DC-to-DC converter across a barrier rated on the order of 2.5 kV.[1314]

At mains potential the barrier is often the whole architecture. A hybrid solar inverter isolates the house side so that when the grid fails the inverter does not feed power back into the grid, automatically isolating the grid side while continuing to run an emergency output.[5JT8M8v_zNg] In such an inverter the battery, the PV input and the boost converter share a bus but sit behind isolation, so the house cannot tell and does not care whether it is being supplied from the battery or from the array.[1620] Modems have always had to provide isolation from the telephone line, and the requirements have become considerably stricter over time.[116]

Not every product needs it, and pretending otherwise is worse than omitting it. A very cheap 2 kW supply with its output shunt directly connected is simply not isolated -- unbeatable for high voltage, high current and high power cheaply, but not a candidate where an isolated supply is required.[1298] Its auxiliary 5 V rail is described as being "isolated in quote marks", sitting on one big flood fill with no specification given, useful only for interface purposes.[1298] Where isolated rails matter, as on a controller board accepting external power, they are worth providing so that arbitrary non-isolated rails cannot be plugged in and break things.[843]

## Verifying isolation

Isolation is a claim that is cheap to test and should be tested. A continuity or resistance measurement between the connectors in question settles it: a panel meter whose common measures open to the supply ground is confirmed floating,[9lIC3ZzIht4] and probing between BNC shells on a handheld scope demonstrates channel-to-channel isolation directly.[810] Resistance also exposes the failures -- 5.8 kilohms across a supposed barrier,[1704] or a constant 4 megohms from input to output ground regardless of which terminal is used.[932] For high-voltage barriers a megohmmeter applies a realistic test voltage, chosen below the device's own maximum rating so the test does not destroy what it is measuring.[932]

Once everything in a setup is genuinely floating, ordinary bench prohibitions relax: it becomes possible to solder on live equipment, provided the whole system including the soldering iron is confirmed floating and the iron is not earthed.[543]
