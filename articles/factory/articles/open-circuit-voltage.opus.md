# open circuit voltage

Open circuit voltage is the potential measured across the terminals of a source when nothing is drawing current from it — the value a high-impedance meter reads on an unloaded battery, a disconnected solar panel, or a switched-off driver output.[779][484][RXk_wr0g8UM] Because no current flows, no voltage is dropped across the source's own internal resistance, so the open circuit figure is always the highest voltage the source will present and is systematically higher than what a load will actually see.[779][pa8j-Lje_2o] That makes it two different things at once: a nearly useless indicator of stored energy, and the number that sets the voltage rating of whatever has to survive being connected.[772][RXk_wr0g8UM]

## Why it says almost nothing about a battery

Every cell has internal resistance that is part of its electrochemistry and cannot be separated from it, so there is no such thing as a characteristic curve taken before that resistance.[779] Loading the cell drops voltage across that resistance, and the difference between the loaded and unloaded discharge curves is exactly that drop.[779] The unloaded curve is what results from pulling the battery out of the product, measuring it with a multimeter — effectively an open load — putting it back, and repeating.[779] The loaded curve is the one manufacturers publish, because it is the only one that exists in a data sheet, and it is the one a real measurement matches closely when taken the same way.[779][772] Using the open circuit value to talk about discharge curves, cutoff voltages, or remaining capacity is therefore meaningless: "it's meaningless to talk about the voltage of a battery when it's not loaded".[779][772]

The failure is not subtle. Alkaline AAA cells discharged at constant power right down to zero volts — and held there, effectively shorted, oscillating around zero — still recovered to around 1.2 V and 1.15 V open circuit once released.[789] Cells discharged to a 0.9 V cutout and left several days recovered to 1.34 V.[pa8j-Lje_2o] Cells at 1.25 V open circuit had a little energy left, perhaps on the order of 1% of the original, thanks to ionic resistance and the chemistry inside.[789] A 9 V alkaline that had leaked of its own accord still measured 1.3 V on a cell with essentially nothing left in it.[2Y1tVvllklc] The recovery is a chemical relaxation, not returning capacity, and a rising terminal voltage on an unloaded cell must not be read as energy that reappeared.[789][pa8j-Lje_2o] The consistent engineering position is that open circuit voltage is an extremely poor indicator of remaining energy and simply is not used that way in industry.[789][772]

Open circuit voltage is not even a fixed number for a fresh cell. A brand new alkaline manganese dioxide cell can sit anywhere from the nominal 1.5 V up to about 1.65 V per cell — 9 V to about 9.9 V for a 9 V battery, which contains six AAAA-size cells internally — with the spread coming from the electrochemistry, the purity of the chemicals, and the materials used.[515]

## Measuring a cutoff voltage correctly

The correct way to find a product's cutoff voltage is to power it from a bench supply and probe right at the product's terminals, sweeping the supply down until the product stops.[779][789] A power supply has no electrochemistry and no meaningful source resistance, so what is set is what the product sees; a battery is not equivalent to a supply when measured open circuit, but is equivalent at the terminals under load, which is the only place the cutoff is defined.[789] The alternative — running a real battery in the product until it dies naturally — takes days, months or a year, which is why nobody does it.[779]

In practice a test is set up as a constant-current discharge with a programmed cutout voltage: half an amp into a two-cell pack with a 1.8 V cutout works out to 0.9 V per cell nominal, and 3.23 V open circuit on a pack drops sharply the instant the half-amp load appears.[XDjyY48u0PU][hSkaZEgrZkY] The open circuit reading is worth noting only as a starting point before the load goes on, and where the drop across the test leads matters, remote sensing may be needed.[XDjyY48u0PU]

## Photovoltaics: V<sub>OC</sub> versus the maximum power point

For a solar panel the open circuit voltage is a data sheet parameter alongside short circuit current, and both bracket the maximum power point that sits between them.[484][1385] A 370 W LG module is rated 35.8 V at maximum power but as high as 41.8 V open circuit, with short circuit current up to 10.9 A against 10.3 A at maximum power.[1385] A 250 W LG module of the earlier generation gives 30.77 V and 8.13 A at peak power against 8.62 A short circuit, with a 1000 V maximum system voltage and a 15 A maximum series fuse.[484] A 3 kW array measured 456 V open circuit and 7 A short circuit at installation while producing just over 2 kW.[484]

The gap between V<sub>OC</sub> and the operating point is what sizes the input stage. A microinverter accepting 16 V to 60 V with 60 V an absolute maximum is safe behind a panel whose open circuit output will not exceed 41 V, and that is an input-voltage limit protecting the inverter rather than any clipping of output power.[BtQUuD6QRMw] Small harvesting front ends are specified the same way: an 8 V to 66 V open circuit string input with a maximum power point tracking range reaching only 42 V.[991] Open circuit voltage on its own describes a condition in which no load is being powered at all, which is why energy-harvesting claims quoted at open circuit say nothing about deliverable power without the maximum power point.[1383] The full characterisation is open circuit voltage, short circuit current, and maximum power transfer swept with a resistance box — the same three measurements whether the source is a commercial panel or a lemon.[0eL_byioeqQ]

## Open circuit voltage as a stress rating

Where a source is disconnected or switched off, the open circuit voltage is the stress the downstream part must survive. On a Nixie tube driven from 170 V through a nominal 22k resistor, the un-selected cathode pins do not sit near ground: measurements across the pins of one tube gave 49 V, 24 V, 43 V, 122 V, 118 V, 69 V, 47 V and around 123 V, with a couple of pins reading 120 V and 223 V.[RXk_wr0g8UM] That spread is large and varies tube to tube, so the driver transistor or driver array must be rated for the open circuit voltage present rather than for the tube's nominal cathode drop — the tube's own voltage drop cannot be relied on to protect the driver.[RXk_wr0g8UM]

A current transformer is the extreme case. It is a current-output device, converted to a voltage output by placing a burden resistor across the secondary; left open circuit with primary current flowing, the secondary voltage goes in theory to infinity, limited in practice only by breakdown.[yOJ7xPugsdc] Clamping a current transformer around a conductor with its output leads unterminated can generate genuinely high voltages.[yOJ7xPugsdc]

Test equipment carries the same specification. The open circuit voltage of a multimeter's diode test mode determines what it can measure and what it can disturb: raising it from a 1 V maximum to 5 V allows modern white LEDs to be tested, but the higher open circuit voltage can potentially damage parts in existing test systems or turn on junctions that were not meant to conduct, and where the range cannot be switched back there is no way to avoid it.[489] Stimulus outputs are specified the same way — a TENS unit delivering 4 mA at 100 Hz in 200 µs pulses is rated at 100 V open circuit.[1471]
