# depth of field

Depth of field is the span of distance along the optical axis within which a subject remains acceptably in focus. In practice it is defined by where focus is visibly lost either side of the focal plane, which makes it a measurable working figure rather than an abstract one — a soldering microscope with roughly 10 mm of usable range can be raised and lowered by that much before the image goes soft.[590] It governs almost every imaging task in electronics work, because circuit boards are not flat subjects: connectors and electrolytics stand well above the substrate, and boards are usually shot or inspected at an angle rather than square-on.[1373][1451]

## Aperture, light and noise

For a given lens and subject distance, aperture is the primary control. A low f-number produces a shallow zone of focus; a high f-number extends it. At f2.8 a board angled to the camera cannot be held in focus front and rear at once — bringing the front in pushes the rear out — while at f4 the entire board falls inside the zone.[364] The same progression is visible across a wider range on interchangeable-lens cameras, where a lens run from f3.5 upward through f5, f5.6, f6.3, f8, f16 and f18 only brings everything into focus near its f22 limit, by which point the image is very dark.[vgHtVKgHfmE] The shallowest possible setting is used deliberately for the opposite effect: a fixed f2.2 is the lowest available on one camcorder and gives the least depth of field it can produce.[vgHtVKgHfmE]

Stopping down costs light, and that cost is the real constraint on a bench. Closing the aperture to gain depth of field must be paid for either with a longer exposure, more camera gain, or more illumination. Gain is the worst of the three: running a fixed f5.2 at a gain of 9 introduces visible noise, and a working range of gain 3 to 6 is preferable, leaving depth of field and image noise as a direct trade-off.[QxMmEAb2JME] Adding light removes the trade-off rather than balancing it. A ceiling lighting installation delivering over 1500 lux across the bench allows deeper depth-of-field shots on angled boards without the noise penalty that comes from working under-lit.[764] The same logic drives fill lighting from the sides, since more light permits a higher fixed aperture,[1460] and it is the deciding argument in photographic light-box construction: a paper-lined box measures brighter than a foil-lined one, more light permits a larger f-stop, and a larger f-stop permits greater depth of field, so the tops of connectors and the components at board level are both sharp.[1373] Left on automatic under poor light, a camera selects a low f-stop and the tops of components go soft while the board surface stays sharp.[1373]

## Fixed-aperture studio practice

Bench video is shot at a fixed aperture rather than on automatic, chosen so that the near and far ends of an angled board are simultaneously in focus. F5.2 is the standing value for the main bench camera.[1451][1460] Other setups have used a constant f3.5 to f4, with the acknowledgement that the camera can open up further but that objects toward the back of the shot then fall out of focus.[943] Working at a lower aperture than intended produces a visibly shallow depth of field on close work, against a normal practice of about f4.8 to f5.[q7VQfhkOLm8]

Whiteboard tutorial shooting is a distinct case. The camera is set to manual focus locked on the whiteboard, with face tracking switched off so the lens does not hunt between the board and the presenter, and a fixed aperture chosen to give enough depth of field to hold both.[4Up3dshwg8Q][2FbwdnMARAw] Around f4 to f4.5 is adequate at that geometry.[2FbwdnMARAw] Too small an f-number risks the background falling out of focus depending on lens quality.[4Up3dshwg8Q] Standing two and a half to three metres from the camera, the presenter is constrained by the focus zone and cannot freely stop and restart, so the take runs continuously.[4Up3dshwg8Q]

Photographing boards for reverse engineering inverts the usual video compromise. With the camera on a tripod, a high f-stop is used to obtain a very deep zone of focus so that tall components are not soft, with focus set on the smallest surface-mount part on the board; the resulting exposure runs to something like half a second, which the tripod makes acceptable.[675]

## Sensor size and cameras

Depth of field also depends on sensor format. A small-sensor webcam cannot produce a strongly blurred background — the sensor is not large enough for a very shallow zone — which means subjects can be held up at varying distances and stay usable, and the operator can lean in and back on manual focus without going soft.[6n4IQ2nvTw0] A larger sensor is what produces the blurred-background bokeh look.[6n4IQ2nvTw0] Differences between comparable webcams are measurable by placing a test chart and objects roughly half a metre behind the subject and comparing how legible the background text remains; on that test one model resolves the background labels more clearly than the other, indicating greater depth of field.[17Y6XEoGuLw]

Distance from camera to subject matters as much as aperture: the further a background is behind the plane of focus, the more defocused it becomes for a given lens.[1362] The relationship is a general one — focus behaviour is a function of distance — which is why raising a microscope head and bringing the object up to it is preferable to working close in when a tilted view at the same magnification is needed.[e98cYNPUxcg]

## Microscopes and inspection

For inspection microscopy, depth of field is the specification that separates a usable instrument from a frustrating one. A microscope with only a couple of millimetres of focus range is poor for surface-mount work; a claimed 10 mm, confirmed by raising and lowering the subject, means the top of a large component and the board beneath it are both sharp at once, which is close to ideal for soldering.[590] Instruments at the other end of the range make the limitation immediate: on one 4K digital microscope there is no depth-of-field adjustment at all, so with a connector viewed on an angle the first pin is sharp while the rear pin is not, and a capacitor cannot quite be brought entirely within the focus zone.[1640] On a $500 inspection microscope, moving one or two pins away from the focal plane is enough to lose sharpness.[1125] Getting the required depth of field when viewing a suspect solder joint at an oblique angle is genuinely difficult, and a high-quality stereo microscope is what makes a hairline crack visible at that angle.[1091]

Where the instrument permits it, depth of field is adjusted as an operating control rather than accepted as fixed — X-ray-style BGA inspection setups are tweaked to bring the inner ball rows into focus,[795] and inspection stations are expected to offer a manual setting for it.[1468] Generous depth of field combined with long working distance is what allows a board to be examined at extreme tilt: an instrument with roughly 200 to 250 mm of working distance holds a capacitor and the tombstoned part behind it in focus together at something near a 70-degree viewing angle.[FLsCLAf-ahg] Depth of field, alongside auto-zoom framing, is likewise what distinguishes the Eakins microscopes.[1143]

Fixed-focus instruments trade the control away entirely. A benchtop thermal camera with a fixed 70 mm ±10 mm focus distance is confined to small subjects; a longer working distance of, say, 30 cm combined with a reasonably large depth of field would have allowed larger products to be imaged with everything in frame sharp.[988] A macro attachment likewise offers so little depth of field that the subject cannot be tilted, which rules out solder-joint inspection.[1608]

## Shallow depth of field as a tool

At high magnification the shallow zone becomes diagnostic rather than a defect. Focusing through a BGA solder ball layer by layer resolves the ball surface and then the die beneath it.[1380] On a metallurgical microscope the separation between physical layers on a die can be as little as about one micron, and the shallow depth of field of the imaging camera is precisely what allows those layers to be brought in and out of focus individually with a stage dial calibrated in microns.[-JDsRZ4nmbo] At 400x on a wafer inspection microscope — a 40x objective through the camera port — focus becomes extremely touchy for the same reason.[992] The stereo microscope's shallow zone is also what reveals the height difference between a USB connector shell and the pins below it as the head is coerced gently up and down.[390]

Software removes the constraint at the top of the market. A motorised microscope with Z-axis control captures a stack of images at different heights and combines them, producing a composite in which an entire massive depth of field is in focus, and stitching across the lateral plane as well to yield a single high-resolution image of a whole die.[796]

## Magnification trade-offs

Increasing magnification narrows the focus zone and the field of view simultaneously. Fitting a higher-power lens to a macro inspection camera — a x10 in place of a x4 on a 52 mm macro lens — gains magnification but degrades both depth and field of view together; the reverse swap widens the angle enough to fit a whole subject in frame at the cost of the zoom range.[2G7Z9IIQoIs]
