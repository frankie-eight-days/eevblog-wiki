# optocoupler

An optocoupler is a component that passes a signal across a galvanic isolation barrier as light rather than as current, typically an LED facing a phototransistor inside one opaque package.[660][709] It is the standard means of getting feedback or data from one ground domain to another where the two grounds must never touch — most commonly from the isolated secondary of a mains switching supply back to the primary side, and from a measurement section to a digital section inside test equipment.[1438][427] Because the coupling is optical, the two halves can sit at wildly different potentials, and the only thing limiting the arrangement is the physical separation designed into the package and the board underneath it.[1277][388]

## Construction and variants

The internal arrangement is visible in the datasheet pinout: on a Vishay CNY64, two pins on one side are the LED and the opposite side is the phototransistor, which also tells you which direction the signal travels.[660] Common jellybean parts of this type come in 8-pin DIP packages, and where a manufacturer has relabelled the device with a house part number it is usually a 4N25 or something very similar underneath.[427]

Not every optically coupled part uses a phototransistor. A vactrol — a photoresistive opto-isolator, a name trademarked by Vactec — pairs an LED with a CdS photocell instead, and was used for isolation in stable oscillator circuits in the same era that an HP 200 used a light bulb as its stability element.[709] Output devices are chosen to suit the load: a phototriac output is used where the far side must switch mains directly,[1292] and gate-drive optocouplers such as the A3140 in an 8-pin DIP are used to drive power MOSFETs from a PWM controller across the barrier.[449]

Slotted and reflective variants appear where the light path is deliberately left open. Some instruments use the kind of optocoupler found in a photocopier, with a gap you can physically interrupt to cut the signal between the two halves,[426] and camera mechanisms use them as rotation sensors for a manual control ring.[1429]

Where no off-the-shelf part offers enough withstanding voltage, designers roll their own: an infrared LED and a phototransistor or phototriac facing each other across an air gap, held apart by heat-shrink tubing in earlier designs and by a custom injection-moulded plastic carrier in later ones.[1292][1277] Electric fence controllers use this approach because it is the only way to get the required high-voltage isolation, and it is a cheap way to do it; it also lets the controller measure the output voltage across the barrier rather than merely switching it on and off.[1292][1277] The same construction — described in one instance as a "do-it-yourself optocoupler" — turns up elsewhere with well over an inch of PCB clearance beneath it.[1292][1489]

## Isolated feedback in switching supplies

In an offline mains supply, the control loop sits on the primary side while the regulated output sits on the secondary, so the error signal has to cross the transformer's isolation barrier. The near-universal solution is a TL431 shunt reference on the secondary driving an optocoupler LED, with the phototransistor loading the primary-side controller.[1438] The TL431 is the dominant part for this in current designs, with output voltage set by a resistor divider, though the compensation capacitance must be chosen carefully or the loop can go unstable.[1438]

Optocoupler feedback is so standard that its presence or absence characterises a supply. It appears in plug packs,[1239] set-top boxes,[612] PC supplies,[462][1313] TV supplies,[446][1388] instrument supplies,[985][800][360][475][497][1309] EV charging equipment,[1507][1726] and standby DC-DC converters running at a tenth of a watt.[378] Its absence marks either extreme cost-cutting — a single-transistor eBay USB charger with a half-wave rectifier, no optocoupler, no interference suppression and no secondary-side regulation[1313] — or a deliberate alternative, such as an LED driver that takes its feedback from an extra transformer winding and does constant-current regulation entirely on the secondary side.[1252] The optocoupler route carries real cost: in LED lighting, the optocoupler plus the LMV431 plus a separate constant-current driver is a significant addition to the bill of materials.[1253]

Feedback optocouplers are also a convenient diagnostic point. Probing the primary side of the feedback optocoupler in a suspect supply is trivial and confirms in one measurement whether the loop is active and the optical path intact — the confusing part being that the "primary side" of the optocoupler physically sits on the secondary side of the transformer.[1726] Where feedback exists it does not always do much; a supply with a downstream linear regulator may use the optocoupler for little more than coarse control.[1364]

## Isolation on the board

An optocoupler is only as good as the clearance around it. Good practice is a routed isolation slot in the PCB directly beneath the package, extending the creepage path between primary and secondary pins,[360][497][800][1309] with silkscreen marking the barrier and the creepage paths that matter.[388][446] Creepage is the distance across the surface of the board, as distinct from clearance through air.[388] Reasonable spacing across the optocoupler itself can be undone by a single defect elsewhere — a fake Apple USB charger placed the optocoupler with adequate primary-to-secondary distance and then ruined it with a gap in the barrier, alongside a total absence of fuse protection or inductive filtering.[388] Equipment where the barrier is taken seriously shows slots between every bridge-rectifier pin as well as under the optocouplers,[1161] and slots even around the mounting nuts.[1309]

The isolation is often legible from the physical layout alone. A package with no traces running from one side to the other, sitting on a split in the ground plane, is an optocoupler regardless of what the marking says,[745][1314] and a hard split in the ground planes with a single serial line crossing it identifies an isolated measurement section.[427][409]

## Digital isolation in instruments

Isolating a digital serial link with a few optocouplers is far easier than isolating an analog signal path, which is why instruments push the ADC and front end onto the isolated side and cross the barrier with serial data.[589] The pattern recurs across bench instruments: HP 3457A and 3478A multimeters separate the digital/display section from the analog measurement section and bridge them with a serial-in, serial-out optocoupler link,[426][427] the Fluke 45 brings serial data out of its custom analog processor ASIC through large optocouplers,[791] the Prema 6047 uses them between the digital processor board and the sampling logic,[613] and the Voltech PM300 shifts an 8-bit parallel ADC output into shift registers and feeds it over an optocoupler.[589] A Rigol DP832 uses a pair of optocouplers to carry serial data so that Ethernet, USB, RS232 and logic outputs are all electrically isolated from the supply outputs, with the isolated section running from its own transformer taps.[511] In a Korad KA3005P the USB connector is mains-earth referenced, so the USB/RS232 interface is optically isolated from the main supply outputs to avoid problems when connecting to a mains-earthed PC.[314]

The same approach isolates digital I/O generally: alarm and metering equipment optocouples control signals through to output stages and comms modules,[409][1049][682] CNC breakout boards use phototransistor optocouplers rather than relays for their I/O,[930] and industrial front ends cross the telecoms barrier with them.[682][287]

## Speed, drive and pull-ups

The main practical limitation is speed, and it is the output stage rather than the LED that causes it. A typical logic-interface optocoupler has an open-collector output — no totem pole, so no active device to drive the load up to the positive rail quickly.[1208] The rising edge is therefore set by the pull-up resistor charging the load capacitance, which with a 4.7 k pull-up can land in the order of tens of microseconds; even the falling edge, driven actively by the transistor, can already sit at about 500 ns.[1208] Where the receiving device specifies a maximum input slew rate, an optocoupler-sourced clock can violate it and drive a counter into a metastable state, producing skipped or multiplied pulses and erratic counting.[1208] Driving logic directly from an open-collector optocoupler output is therefore poor practice; the first remedy is to drop the pull-up value substantially — down toward a hundred ohms — and inspect the resulting edge on a scope, and the better remedy is to reclock the signal or feed the logic from a proper source.[1208]

The input side is not free either. Optocoupler inputs on relay interface boards can demand 15 to 20 mA of drive current per channel, which is significant when several are driven from a microcontroller or single-board computer.[1365] Optocouplers also do not, by themselves, solve grounding problems: if the two boards already share a ground, the optocoupler's output current still returns through that shared ground, so the isolation is nominal and the loop remains.[1365]

Where many isolated lines are needed, a dedicated isolator chipset is preferable to a bank of optocouplers — a galvanically isolated SPI link over a single twisted pair replaces multiple optocouplers on multiple lines with one part at each end.[bg6QsTT0Plw][1110]

## Mains interfacing

Optocouplers with the LED driven from the mains through a high-value dropper serve as mains-presence and zero-crossing detectors. The MID400 is a packaged mains optocoupler interface intended to connect straight to an Arduino or Raspberry Pi, protected by a high-wattage high-voltage axial resistor so the arrangement stays safe even with reversed mains wiring; its output is digital, so it can report presence and frequency but cannot monitor the analog mains waveform.[1069] Oscilloscopes use the same idea for line triggering: a dedicated AC trigger board takes mains in and gives an optocoupler output over a cable to the main board.[1503][842][1530] The synchronisation is exact enough that gate drive can be timed to the AC mains input through the optocoupler.[530]

On the switching side, a gate-control optocoupler is how a low-voltage processor commands a mains triac in soldering and hot-air rework stations and in lighting and heating controllers.[1172][1058][513] The isolation does not protect against every failure: if the triac itself fails short, there is nothing the processor can do through the gate-control optocoupler to switch it off, which is a credible fire mode.[1172] Custom DIY optocouplers pressed into service as high-voltage sensors typically feed pulsed DC rather than continuous DC, which is also why pulse-grade capacitors are required at input and output.[1277]

## Salvage

Optocouplers are among the parts worth recovering from scrap boards, along with mains filter capacitors, common mode chokes, bridge rectifiers, varistors, relays and heatsinks — a junk box of scrap power supply boards is a practical source.[1161][1302][488][1587] Mail-order grab bags of ten optocouplers were sold in the same spirit long before the Shenzhen market existed.[1240]
