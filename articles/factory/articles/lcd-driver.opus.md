# lcd driver

An LCD driver is the circuitry that generates the alternating segment and common waveforms an LCD glass needs in order to display anything. Bare LCD glass is a passive component: it does not drive itself, and connecting it directly to a microcontroller port and expecting an image is not workable.[1044] Whichever of the common glass interconnect methods is used — pins, flat flex, or zebra strips — the glass still requires separate driver circuitry beneath it, supplied either by a microcontroller with a built-in LCD controller, a dedicated driver chip, or a home-grown drive circuit.[1044]

## Why dedicated drive circuitry is required

The essential requirement is AC drive. Over time the net DC level across the liquid crystal must be zero, and a driver that fails to achieve this can potentially damage the display.[1074] Meeting that condition needs a set of distinct bias voltage levels rather than simple logic-level outputs, and generating those levels is precisely what a dedicated driver takes care of.[1074] The same reasoning applies at the small end of the scale: individual-segment displays of the kind used in wristwatches and calculators — as distinct from dot matrix displays — need special LCD driving circuitry to drive all those segments, which is why segment-LCD projects begin with the question of where the drive is coming from.[248] Later clones of the classic Hitachi HD44780 character controller integrate an LCD driver for exactly this reason: to supply the bias and AC signals that drive the segments directly.[334]

## Rows, commons and segments

The complexity of the driver is set by the geometry of the glass. The number of rows and segments dictates not only how complex the LCD is, but how complex its driver must be.[1055] A custom segment LCD laid out as eight commons by thirty-two segments needs a driver chip with at least that many commons and segments, and forty glass pins to reach it, every one of which must be driven when the glass has no on-board driver.[1055] A six-digit display is already a large-ish requirement in segment count and pushes the designer toward a physically bigger part.[1242] Concrete examples recur: a µSupply front panel used eight commons and thirty-one segments driven by a Holtek HT1622;[1561] a microcontroller selection exercise put the requirement at seventy-three segments;[1538] and an ST ARM part chosen for a product offered eight by forty segments against a need of roughly thirty-six.[900]

Large dot matrix panels multiply the problem rather than change it. A 240 by 64 display required a striking number of driver chips on its own display board,[116] a handheld scopemeter carried six Hitachi LCD dot matrix driver chipsets integrated onto the main board,[430] and a 40-column by 8-line panel used two rows of drivers with each row handling four lines plus five column drivers, eight characters apiece.[1376] In a graphing calculator the row of Toshiba parts surrounding the main CPU were all LCD drivers, identifiable by the huge tracks fanning out from them.[715] A driver chip may also hold the display data sent to it by the processor.[1662]

## Where the driver lives

Four integration choices recur across designs.

**Built into the microcontroller.** For a relatively small LCD the existing microcontroller can drive it, or a variant of that microcontroller with a built-in LCD driver can be selected — and many families offer one.[1055] This is the cheapest arrangement because an external LCD controller is an extra BOM item and extra cost.[1538] The presence of an adequate LCD driver was a decisive selection criterion in more than one design: an STM32L1 was specified into the 121GW multimeter partly for its LCD driver signals,[1539] and an STM32L part was chosen for a product because it had an LCD driver with the required rows and columns alongside built-in EEPROM.[900][1055] The constraint is that suitable parts tend to be large: an STM32L0x3 offering 4 by 52 or 8 by 48 drive also brings 20K of SRAM, 6K of embedded EEPROM and a 32-pin minimum package, none of which is cheap.[1242]

**A separate driver chip.** When no suitable integrated part can be found or the integrated option costs too much, a standalone driver is cheaper, which is how a Holtek part ended up in a design after ST candidates were ruled out.[1561] Holtek make both microcontrollers and LCD drivers, and they are inexpensive.[1561] The same trade-off appeared during the 121GW development, where a suggested ARM micro had no built-in LCD driver and would have required a separate one at the cost of extra PCB space and extra bill of materials.[e9cpKN69Avk] A separate driver is also the fallback when the main processor simply lacks pins — an 8051-class multimeter micro with no LCD block and not enough pins implies another driver elsewhere on the board.[468]

**Chip on glass.** A COG display carries the driver chip bonded onto the glass itself, so the host talks to it over I²C, SPI or a Hitachi-style interface instead of driving forty pins.[1055] This is how displays with very high segment counts become practical at all; a triple-digit meter display with many hundreds of circuits would be extremely difficult to drive from a generic micro without a fully custom chip-on-glass part.[173] COG is not free: for one custom LCD the quoted difference was $3.71 each against $1.47, which did not justify it once the external driver chip was priced in.[1055] COG parts appear in a GoPro with a simple serial interface off the glass,[399] and in handheld consoles where the driver die is visible embedded in the glass under the polariser.[afErPINq8qc]

**Chip on flex or chip on board.** The driver die may instead be bonded to a flex tape or directly to a small board, hot-bar attached at both ends.[643][1110] A calculator LCD module with more segments than its main die could drive used a hot tape bond to a chip-on-tape driver,[1094] and a Casio assembly put the LCD driver and its full pinout onto flex tape rather than a PCB.[1229] Chip-on-board driver dies also handled character LCDs and sometimes keypad scanning in early handsets.[492][856]

## In multimeters and instruments

Multimeter architecture splits cleanly along this line. A complete multimeter chipset contains the processor, ADC, measurement switching and the LCD driver in one package, with traces running off to zebra strips and the glass.[912] All-in-one chipsets appear in clamp meters, where the absence of a separate driver plus an insufficient segment count implies the glass itself carries a COG driver.[3kdYGneg9xI] Elsewhere the two-chip arrangement dominates: one LCD driver and one multimeter chipset,[1351][1457] with the driver chip often the only other active part on the board.[884]

Which arrangement a manufacturer picks is not always explicable from outside. The BM235 uses an external LCD chipset despite its Hycon measurement chip appearing to have enough built-in segments to do the job,[912] and a socket tester likewise ignores an available internal LCD driver in favour of an external chip, apparently because the custom segment layout demanded it.[1598] Reverse engineering exploits the split: a separate driver forces a serial interface between the chips, and finding an I²C driver part identifies the SCL and SDA pins on the measurement chipset and confirms the pin mapping, since the segment pins of the internal driver go unused.[912] The same logic tempts investigators to look for a serial output on the driver itself when an instrument emits an unusual protocol.[5v8qX9PrjnA]

Named driver parts recur across teardowns: the Hitachi HD44780 character controller paired with an HD44100 display driver in the original two-chip arrangement, a combination almost nobody uses now;[334] the Hitachi HD44102 in vintage portables;[1376] Holtek's HT1622 and HT6121B;[1561][1105][712] the Philips PCF8577 I²C driver and the Philips 8566;[603][1483] the Hycon HY2613C in Brymen meters;[JI4b-7vpIDc][5v8qX9PrjnA] and a TM1622 in a low-cost meter.[1351] Hitachi's dominance of the field is longstanding enough that a Hitachi part beside an LCD is a reasonable first guess.[1376][1090][1662] Custom ASICs subsume the function entirely in high-volume consumer gear, handling all the LCD driving alongside other mixed-signal work.[1275][715][1229]

At the higher end the driver disappears into the application processor. ARM-class application processors from Freescale and similar vendors integrate the LCD driver along with everything else, which is why some instruments show no separate driver at all;[823][993][1286] a TMS320 with an integrated LCD driver handles a 640 by 480 panel in a handheld scope.[810] Conversely, an instrument with no processor at all can still drive a display, using 74-series and 4000-series CMOS logic to convert the analogue signal and drive the LCD directly.[1258]

## Debugging and failure

Driver chips are a plausible but usually incorrect suspect when a display misbehaves. In a vintage portable showing duplicated lines, the column and line drivers were demonstrably fine because the correct characters were appearing — the fault was in the data being clocked into them, garbage in and garbage out.[1376] The candidate causes for that class of fault are a dry joint, a failed driver chip, or something else loading down the shared bus,[1376] and distinguishing them means probing, or swapping a known-good LCD module to prove the fault is on the module rather than the driving side.[1376] Similarly, a GPS with a dead display could in principle have lost an LCD driver or its bias voltage, but the actual cause was an electrolytic capacitor — a more probable failure from the outset, and a fortunate one, since replacement drivers are generally unobtainable.[918] A display that lights up at all after the boot-up and turn-on delay is evidence that the processor and the driver are both alive,[1425] and an insertion error appearing on screen after reprogramming confirms both processor and LCD driver are working.[1522]

Where the LCD driver is clearly not the culprit, that fact narrows the search: an intermittent fault in a meter whose display is fine points at the custom measurement ASIC instead.[9s9LXOBknck][1522] An LCD showing all segments at once suggests the processor never booted far enough to send the clear command, leaving the driver in its power-on state.[JI4b-7vpIDc] When a custom LCD showed a marginal common in prototype testing, suspicion fell on the glass rather than the driver, on the grounds that an industry-standard driver chip that has been available for a long time and is optimised for the application is unlikely to be the weak link; the diagnosis was confirmed by breaking into the HT1622 supply track and sweeping its voltage in 0.1 volt steps.[1105]

Driver chips also cause trouble on the bus rather than the panel. An I²C LCD driver was traced as the source of intermediate voltage levels on a bus that should have carried only ones and zeros — isolated to the LCD rather than the other I²C devices or the driver code, which had been written from scratch against the LCD manufacturer's datasheet.[240] Parallel-mode graphical LCDs are notoriously awkward to bring up: with sixteen lines to check and only a two-channel scope, separating a wiring fault from a code fault becomes tedious.[8] Even in a modest project, driver code plus string handling accounts for a substantial share of program memory.[45]

## Selection

Choosing an LCD-capable microcontroller is a parametric search problem with awkward edges. Distributor search tools have no dedicated LCD column, so candidates must be picked out by looking for LCD in the peripheral descriptions one family at a time, yielding on the order of 2,306 microcontrollers across manufacturers — and the result depends on the distributor having imported the manufacturer data correctly.[248] Beyond the segment count the comparison quickly becomes intractable, because driving an LCD interacts with timers, ADCs, comparators and other peripherals in ways that a datasheet current figure does not capture; a genuine shootout requires spinning boards and writing test software that exercises each peripheral.[1539] The pragmatic engineering position is that an on-chip driver is preferable when one exists in a part that otherwise fits, and a separate driver chip when it does not, decided on total cost rather than elegance.[1538][1561][1055]
