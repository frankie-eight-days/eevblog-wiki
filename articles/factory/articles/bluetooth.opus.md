# bluetooth

Bluetooth is the short-range wireless link that has become the default way a battery-powered device talks to a phone, and by extension the default way test instruments, locks, speakers, inverters and sensors get a user interface without a cable.[759][1238][1719] Its appeal to designers is cost and power: complete assembled modules sell for a few dollars, low enough that the serial interface chip used to program one can cost more than the entire Bluetooth board it talks to.[759] Its cost to users is a connection layer that is slow to pair, breaks on operating-system updates, and puts an app between the operator and the measurement.[Q_RYG_5cQk8][1238] The name derives from Harold Bluetooth, a Nordic king.[1483]

## Power budget

The low-power variants draw little enough that a coin cell is a realistic supply. A continuous connection at a once-per-second interval averages roughly 80 microwatts, which is about four times the operating current of the host device in a smartwatch-class product — significant in relative terms, negligible in absolute ones.[945] At a combined 100 microwatts, a CR2450 of around 2,000 mAh capacity runs for roughly 20,000 hours, about 2.3 years, with the radio connected 24 hours a day and no deep sleep at all.[945] This is the arithmetic that makes energy-harvesting schemes for such products unnecessary.[945]

The draw is pulsed rather than continuous: the radio takes short bursts of current on top of the host's steady consumption.[XOuYJ1nxVJQ] In a Bluetooth-equipped meter this changes end-of-life behaviour — the instrument detects the low-battery condition under the transmit pulses and shuts itself down or drops into sleep, and on the next power-up gives no low-battery indication at all.[XOuYJ1nxVJQ] Logging over the link costs measurable extra power in the host; with transmit mode switched off the current waveform falls back to peaks of around 3 milliamps.[1693] Where the radio is a design constraint on physical layout, battery capacity wins: a meter built around an integrated Bluetooth module was made about five millimetres thicker to move from AAA to AA cells purely for run time.[RDUn3CoVmuc]

Range is the price paid for that power budget. A Bluetooth link is viable for a short hop to a nearby relay but cannot reach far, which rules it out as the primary link wherever the far end is not close by.[896] Metal enclosures compound the problem — an aluminium chassis forces the antenna outside to get any reception at all.[885]

## Modules and silicon

Bluetooth is almost always bought in as a module or a combi chip rather than designed from scratch.[759][672] Nordic Semiconductor BLE parts turn up repeatedly in commodity products, from proximity-tracing badges to consumer devices whose radio exists only to reach a phone app.[1483][1290] The nRF52 family is cheap enough — a dollar or two — that it is worth selecting as the general-purpose microcontroller even in a design that does not use the radio, on the grounds that any future connectivity requirement is then already covered; the same argument applies to the ESP32 and Wi-Fi.[1694] This is a case for keeping the option open, not for standardising on one part regardless of its trade-offs.[1694]

On the low-power side, ultra-low-power Bluetooth development kits built around Gecko processors have sold at around $99 US with an e-ink display and control board.[899] At the integrated end, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth commonly share one combi chipset — a dual-band 802.11n and Bluetooth 2 part, for instance, in an action camera.[672] Integration has a failure consequence: when the shared silicon is disturbed, both radios go down together, as with the Raspberry Pi 3, where a camera photoflash locks up the Wi-Fi and, because the chipset is integrated, almost certainly the Bluetooth with it.[901] Devices from an era before that integration carried separate chipsets for each of GPS, Bluetooth and the rest, and the chip count reflected it.[334]

## In test instruments

Bluetooth in a meter is a data path, not a feature in itself, and the corpus treats it that way. A top-of-the-line handheld multimeter that advertises PC and tablet automation but omits an internal radio, offering instead the same old external module as a paid optional extra, is a design failure.[832] The same criticism applies to well-built instruments that put the Bluetooth icon on the LCD but ship the radio separately.[986] Where the radio is fitted, its behaviour still needs to be right: on one clamp meter the Bluetooth annunciator appears only after pressing a mode button rather than being present as expected.[UiSlVsvjDR0] Elsewhere a Bluetooth link is quietly wasted — an instrument with internal memory, an app and a radio that still cannot record its trend chart to either.[1723]

A recurring design idea is to use the wireless link to separate the display from the meter: integrating Bluetooth, ZigBee or similar for a second display that a user buys only if they need it, magnet-mounted and separately battery-powered, which also serves as the PC or phone interface.[184] Commercially, the option can simply never arrive — one meter series was sold from 2020 with a promised Bluetooth option, a footprint on the board and a production marker where the module belonged, and the option remained unreleased for about five years.[TkmMitYnHUA]

The counter-example is the instrument that is nothing without the phone. A Bluetooth multimeter that reduces its own front panel to indicator LEDs is borderline useless for anything unless connected, and loses the immediate, obvious information a rotary function switch gives.[1238] Its only redeeming property was a reasonable Bluetooth update rate, everything else being unimplemented or unreliable.[1238] Bluetooth connectivity in such products functions as a funding pitch rather than an engineering decision.[1238]

## Pairing, apps and reliability

BLE instruments typically do not use the operating system's pairing flow at all: the device broadcasts continuously and the app receives it, so attempting to pair through the phone's Bluetooth settings returns a pairing rejection and is unnecessary.[Q_RYG_5cQk8][ruxzp0OMicg] Connecting from inside the app rather than through the Android connection dialog is the better arrangement in practice.[ruxzp0OMicg] Classic-Bluetooth devices still use the older flow, discoverable by scan with a default passkey of 0000 and an RSSI reading — around −57 dB at bench distance.[wSdkpx4BjzY]

Android apps for these devices ask for location permission. This is a requirement imposed by the Bluetooth driver stack rather than anything the application needs, and it applies equally to meter apps and to unrelated Bluetooth accessories.[1238][1567] Requiring an account login merely to operate a multimeter is unnecessary; a Bluetooth meter app should connect and work without one.[1238]

Reliability is the standing complaint. A link that was rock solid on a given Android handset can stop connecting entirely after an app and OS update while continuing to work on iOS, with no change at the device end.[Q_RYG_5cQk8] Setting up one Bluetooth product required disabling other Bluetooth devices in range first.[gsfDx7B9sks] Bluetooth stacks on some accessories never work at all.[7iYcpiY2T2U] The judgement is blunt: "Bloody Bluetooth, it's pain in the ass. Always has been."[Q_RYG_5cQk8] In development terms, Bluetooth and gesture handling are the two components that consume the porting effort when moving a meter app to a second mobile platform, even when the underlying libraries claim support for it.[121]

Firmware in the radio module is itself field-updatable over the air. Updating a Bluetooth multimeter's module from within the app is done wirelessly; the process gracefully shuts down the meter, and on reconnection a waveform capture and data log resume from where they left off.[XOuYJ1nxVJQ] Such updates fix real defects — one release addressed a long-running logging bug that cut off notifications.[XOuYJ1nxVJQ]

## Interference

A transmitting Bluetooth phone is an EMI source at bench distances. Placing a phone that is transmitting over Bluetooth on top of a powered monitor speaker produced audible popping and crackling that disappeared when the phone was moved away and returned when it was brought back.[1714] The general lesson is that the radio's energy has to go somewhere, and nearby analogue circuitry is a candidate path.[1714]

## Applications and hazards

Bluetooth is the standard data-out route for equipment that must be configured or logged in place. Solar inverter output has been extracted over the Bluetooth interface by a PC-side program and uploaded automatically to a public logging service, run continuously and without trouble for years.[724][1086][1385] The limitation is proximity — commissioning an inverter over its Bluetooth link means physically standing next to it.[1719] Other instruments offer Bluetooth to a phone alongside a direct USB connection, which is the more reliable of the two for extended runs.[UE_srZZdPjM]

In audio, Bluetooth is the cheap universal input. It is what a hacked smart speaker is reduced to when its cloud dependency is stripped out, and it is present on essentially every consumer boombox and party speaker.[1519][1513] It is not free of trade-offs: on cheap hardware the Bluetooth path is mono and limited to speech-grade quality.[1590] On serious monitors a physical switch to disable the radio is preferable to a software toggle, and a balanced input remains the better interface where one is available.[1162]

For access control the concern is dependency. A Bluetooth padlock can be well-executed hardware and still a poor product, because habitual phone use erodes memory of the backup combination; when the coin cell dies, the phone fails, or the link does not come up, the fallback is gone.[1014] Whether the radio's own security has been implemented correctly is a separate and generally unverified question.[1014] The inverse argument holds for devices with no radio at all: a camera with no Wi-Fi, no Bluetooth and no cloud upload cannot be attacked remotely, and its files are reachable only by physically connecting to it.[1619]

The proximity-detection application uses the radio as a range proxy rather than a data pipe. Contact-tracing badges built on a Nordic BLE part ping other badges nearby, carry a serial number rather than personal data, and hold no wireless connectivity beyond that.[1483]
