# solenoid

A solenoid is a wound coil that converts current into linear mechanical motion: energising the coil pulls a plunger or armature in, which in turn moves a lever, plate, latch or valve.[191][1494] It is the standard way of getting a small amount of electrically commanded force into an otherwise mechanical assembly, and it appears wherever a circuit board has to physically move something — safe bolts, coin diverters, paper handling, charging-plug locks and mains contactors.[1494][651][312][0JM9enfOHgM][1457] Because the actuation is magnetic, a solenoid is also the single component that determines how a piece of equipment can be attacked, jammed or defeated.[1483][762]

## Construction and behaviour

The basic form is a coil with a movable plunger or actuator arm, usually returned by a spring so that the mechanism has a defined resting state with no power applied.[8MC93KxPTHA][1500] Sensitivity is set by the winding count: a coil with thousands of turns can pull in its actuator arm on very little current.[363] A payphone coin mechanism of that type operates from a line supply of roughly 48 volts and can be made to pull in with a bench supply limited to 10 milliamps of constant current at 12 volts.[363]

Motion is typically fast and violent relative to the load. In an automatic transfer switch the armature is pulled in hard enough to produce an audible thump and mechanical bounce, and the switchover itself takes on the order of tens of milliseconds — a few milliseconds per division on a scope showing around 8 milliseconds to return from the backup source to the priority source.[1500] Force can be substantial for the size: a railway point coil physically throws the mechanism over, but needs only a brief pulse of current to do it.[1351]

At the other extreme of scale, a solenoid is simply a long air-cored electromagnet. A superconducting magnet for a spin qubit experiment is a solenoid of niobium-titanium wire that goes superconducting at about 10 kelvin, has an inductance of about 10 henry, and produces roughly 6 tesla with about 100 amps flowing.[1594] The device under test sits on a finger that reaches into the bore so that it lands exactly at the centre of the field.[1594] A solenoid wound around a sample of olive oil forms a Faraday rotator, and because the applied field can be reversed, the direction of optical rotation can be reversed with it — something a sugar solution cannot do.[1120]

## Driving a solenoid

A solenoid coil is an inductive load and generally cannot be driven directly from small-signal electronics. A 24 volt solenoid used as a mechanical thumper needs a power transistor between it and a function generator rather than being driven from the generator output, with about 16 volts measured as the minimum to make it move.[87E3wqfxHqg] Comparators with open-collector outputs are frequently used for this: the output stage exists precisely so it can drive a relay or solenoid coil rather than only logic, and a typical part will withstand 50 volts on the output, well above the chip's own rated supply.[1464] This is why such comparators turn up in white goods like washing machines and dishwashers, which are full of driven solenoids.[1464]

A dedicated driver plus protection is the usual arrangement in a product: an electronic safe lock uses a PNP drive transistor for the solenoid together with a substantial protection diode and a polyfuse.[762] Duty cycle matters as much as drive capability. A coffee machine flow-control coil is assumed to be normally off, since holding power on it continuously merely to stop coffee flowing would be wasteful.[1161] The same logic applies at mains level, where a solenoid may only need to be energised for a split second to trip a mechanism; in one earth leakage circuit breaker a varistor sits in series and goes high resistance as it heats up, which is acceptable precisely because the solenoid pulse is brief.[1164] Where the coil is left permanently connected across the mains, that is a design decision that demands explanation rather than being assumed as a mistake — in the same breaker the solenoid sits directly across active and neutral in series with the AC side of a bridge rectifier, and tripping works by the thyristor shorting the rectifier output, which creates the current path that activates the solenoid and simultaneously kills the breaker's own supply.[1164] That is the inverse of the chip's own first-page application circuit, in which the solenoid drives the contacts from the DC side.[1164]

Latching arrangements avoid holding current entirely. An automatic transfer switch uses two solenoids and a spring-loaded lever so that a pulse throws the assembly one way or the other and it stays there; when static, none of the solenoids are energised.[1500] A model railway point motor works the same way, with a brief pulse driving a lever that clicks the blades across.[1351]

## Actuators in mechanisms

Solenoids do the small commanded movements in machines whose bulk is mechanical. A cash register drawer is released by a solenoid that trips a catch under software control, after which the tray pops out and re-locks when pushed back.[641] Photocopiers and laser-class printers are dense with them — three solenoids on one side of a single copier paper path, plus clutches, cogs and gears — and a fax machine's fuser assembly has a solenoid engaging a paper mechanism amid a large amount of cog work.[312][1587] A film camera contains a small solenoid whose contact meets an exposed pad on the flex.[816] A 30 000 dollar DLP projector uses solenoids rather than motors to flip protective shutters into and out of the light path.[1546] Coin validators use them for the accept/reject decision: the validator result kicks in a solenoid that pulls in an arm and determines whether the coin is ejected, or drives a small flap that diverts the coin down a refund chute instead of into the machine.[363][651] Discarded equipment is worth stripping for them, along with the motors, transformers, cogs and the unusual connectors and cables that come attached.[345][312]

In an electric vehicle charging inlet a solenoid drives a locking pin. Once the plug is inserted and the EVSE and car have negotiated the charge current, the car activates the solenoid to insert the pin lock, and only then does charging begin; if the solenoid cannot be activated — a jammed pin, for instance — charging will not start at all, which functions as a safety interlock against a badly seated or faulty plug.[0JM9enfOHgM] Release is also under car control via the control pin, and a sticking solenoid will trap the cable in the socket.[0JM9enfOHgM] A 2020 Hyundai Ioniq exhibits exactly this fault; pulling hard on the charge lock manual release handle frees the mechanism with an audible clunk and restores normal operation, and the intermittent nature of the fault makes it awkward to demonstrate to a dealer.[0JM9enfOHgM] Thermal imagers use a solenoid for shutter-based non-uniformity correction, with the microcontroller — not the sensor itself — switching the solenoid so the shutter presents a flat, even-temperature surface for the camera to recalibrate its internal tables against.[670]

Mains-carrying versions are contactors. An off-peak hot water controller contains a large solenoid driving a gigantic contactor with two heavy contact sets rather than one, though the return spring can look underspecified for the job.[1457]

## Solenoids and physical security

Because a solenoid is operated magnetically, it can be operated from outside by a magnet. A sufficiently large neodymium magnet placed in the correct location against a flimsy safe door can influence the solenoid's magnetic field and open it as though the circuitry had commanded it.[1483][8MC93KxPTHA] The attack is location-dependent and defeated by distance and mass — on a safe with a door around 50 mm thick it fails — but plenty of cheap safes remain vulnerable if the attacker knows where to place the magnet.[8MC93KxPTHA] Manufacturers respond with mu-metal shielding over the solenoid to prevent exactly this.[1457] Certified high-security locks are not vulnerable to magnetic or bump attack, and one common defence is to abandon the solenoid entirely in favour of a motor drive, which cannot be pulled by an external field.[1483][7xF1RCVw4Fc][1014] A Masterlock Bluetooth padlock survives the magnetic attack for this reason: a motor rotates a piece that drops down and lets the locking pin move.[1014]

The second family of attacks is electrical. Early digital safe locks were susceptible to spiking — removing the front escutcheon to reach the pins feeding the solenoid and simply applying a battery to fire it.[762] Modern designs keep the solenoid circuit entirely inside the secure boundary so it does not penetrate to the outside, which makes spiking effectively impossible, and quality locks on the market have not been vulnerable to it for a long time.[762] Backing high voltage into the electronics through an external opening to reach the solenoid remains a theoretical route on some designs, alongside drilling to the mechanism directly; applying power to the solenoid retracts or extends the latch immediately.[1484]

Cheap hardware store safes fail on all of these counts. Excessive clearance around a poorly welded plate allows a bent coat hanger to be inserted and used to flip the solenoid arm directly.[1494] Drilling reveals two pairs of wires — one to the solenoid and one to the reset button — either of which is sufficient: pull the solenoid wires out and apply a battery to open the safe, or reach the reset button and simply enter a new code.[1494] Whether that works in practice depends on something as mundane as lead length, since a solenoid pigtailed too short cannot be dragged out through the hole.[1494] Bump attacks exploit the spring return and the deliberately light solenoid: with almost no force holding the plunger, dropping the safe on the ground while turning the handle is enough, and this is realistic on any safe one person can lift.[8MC93KxPTHA] Removing the solenoid altogether leaves a fire-resistant box that opens on the handle every time, which is a reasonable use for such a product.[8MC93KxPTHA] Better design at only modestly higher cost means a motor instead of a solenoid, a different key type, a stiff arm that defeats coat hanger attacks, no externally accessible reset switch, and actuator wiring routed away from the access holes.[8AsaFrsGhuc]

High-security electronic locks show what the solenoid has to coexist with. In a six-lever design, springs bear against the solenoid so the lever plates can swing back, with a key override added to the same universal mounting footprint.[1509] The Kaba Mas X-09 is not externally battery powered — spinning the dial generates and stores enough charge to run the microcontroller and then operate the solenoid motor assembly that releases the mechanism.[1484] In such locks the electronics are deliberately kept on the secure side, with only the rotor encoder and display outside, and the solenoid-driven arm moves a plate that in turn drives the bolt; in a full-size safe that bolt pushes on large armatures carrying several bolts of perhaps 30 mm diameter into the safe body.[1483][1484]

## Related uses

Solenoids are a plausible first answer to any "how do I trigger this mechanically" question — applying voltage to a coil to pull a plate back — though for something as simple as releasing a spring-loaded mouse trap mechanism the solenoid route is more complexity than the job requires, and a resistor suffices.[191] A battery eliminator circuit in a model aircraft powers the solenoids and receiver from the main flight battery instead of a separate pack.[895] Custom magnetics houses that wind toroidal and isolation transformers will also wind solenoids and ferrites as part of the same low-volume business.[1468]
