# ceramic capacitor

A ceramic capacitor stores charge between plates separated by a ceramic dielectric, and it is by a wide margin the most common capacitor type in use, applied to almost every job a small capacitor can do.[33] Ceramic dielectrics reach relative permittivities of several thousand, far above the values available from plastic film, which is what allows large capacitance in a very small package.[9V99J22aiLE][1406] Steady advances in ceramic materials have progressively displaced other technologies, to the point that the migration away from tantalum and electrolytic parts is close to complete for small and mid-range values.[33][148]

## Classes and dielectrics

The EIA divides ceramic capacitors into two basic classes.[33] Class 1 covers the NP0 and C0G types and a few more obscure variants; their defining property is that their capacitance does not change with temperature, and once a part has been measured it stays where it was measured.[33][626] Class 1 parts are confined to low values, typically 1 nF and below.[626]

Class 2 dielectrics — X7R, Y5V and similar — buy their much larger capacitance at the cost of stability.[626] Modern class 2 parts reach hundreds of nanofarads, microfarads, and even hundreds of microfarads in small surface-mount packages.[626] A Y5V part may carry an initial tolerance of −20% / +80% before any operating condition is applied.[626]

## Voltage and temperature dependency

Class 2 ceramics lose capacitance as DC bias is applied across them, and the effect is large enough to invalidate simple design assumptions. A 10 µF 16 V Y5V part measuring 8.65 µF on an LCR meter behaved as 7.8 µF in an RC charging test at 1 V, and its charging characteristic changed shape entirely when the applied voltage was raised to 10 V.[626] X7R parts are markedly better behaved than Y5V, but still not stable, and the dependency varies between different case sizes within the same dielectric family and the same manufacturer's range.[626] The practical consequence is that class 2 ceramics are unsuitable for RC timing, where the capacitor value directly sets the result.[626] Precision integrators use plastic film types rather than ordinary ceramics for the same reason.[777] Where a design does specify ceramics for accuracy-sensitive analog work, the usual discipline is to require C0G or NP0 dielectric and a voltage rating far in excess of the working voltage.[354]

## Impedance and bypassing

A ceramic capacitor's impedance against frequency has a characteristic V shape: falling capacitively, reaching a minimum at self-resonance, then rising again as parasitic inductance dominates.[859] Value and package set where that minimum sits. A 10 µF 1206 part resonates around 1 MHz.[859] A 10 nF part in an 0805 package is above 150 Ω at 100 kHz and resonates up near 40–50 MHz, while a larger value in the same package sits above 15 Ω at 100 kHz with resonance around 5–6 MHz.[859] The broad conclusion is that a large value is good at low frequencies and useless at high ones, and vice versa.

Because no single part covers the whole band, the traditional practice was to parallel several values — a 100 nF with a 1 µF, or a 10 nF with a 1 µF — so that their resonant dips overlap into a broad low-impedance region.[wjMIsM4sDw8] Modern high-capacitance ceramics reduce the need for that stacking.[wjMIsM4sDw8] Package geometry, not just capacitance, determines how wide the useful window is.[wjMIsM4sDw8]

In practice a ceramic bypass placed close to the load is dramatically more effective than the same part placed further away, and the improvement is measurable as reduced high-frequency ripple as the capacitor is physically moved.[1089] A 0.1 µF ceramic and a 0.47 µF film capacitor perform about equally well in the same position.[1089] Density of bypassing tracks how hard a device is working: on one instrument mainboard a secondary ASIC carried a set of heavy-duty ceramic bypass capacitors while the main processing ASIC was served by ordinary 100 nF parts distributed around it.[148]

## Regulator output capacitance

Low-dropout regulators depend on output capacitance for loop stability, and ceramics are the usual choice. A typical LDO specifies a minimum of 2.2 µF ceramic on the output, and is then guaranteed stable across its load range.[222][224] Removing the output capacitance entirely makes such a regulator misbehave immediately.[224] The specified minimum has margin in it — 0.47 µF was still stable at about a quarter of an amp — but designing below the recommended value is poor practice.[224] For an unspecified jellybean LDO, roughly 1 µF of ceramic across the output is the normal starting point.[1147]

Output capacitance also sets load transient response. With 2.2 µF of ceramic, a 200 mA load step produced about 50 mV of output deviation; raising the value to 10 µF reduced the excursion but lengthened the recovery.[222] More output capacitance generally improves transient response, but it is not free: a very large bulk capacitance on a supply output creates its own stability problems.[222][1293]

Ceramics also appear as small local additions in signal circuits, where they are not always benign. Adding 0.1 µF ceramic bypasses from each supply rail to a virtual ground can cure an op-amp oscillation caused by a capacitive load, but it also places a capacitive load on the amplifier output through the series resistor, and is not a guaranteed fix.[l2LBkXxN81Y] Bypassing the divider on the input side instead — a 0.1 µF ceramic across one of the 200 kΩ divider resistors — is the technically better way to solve the same problem.[n7XX75S98e8]

## Failure by short circuit

The dominant failure mode of a multilayer ceramic capacitor is a short, not an open.[1035][1077] A capacitor that fails open rarely ruins the day; one that fails short usually does, because ceramics are typically fitted directly across power rails, and a low-impedance supply can dump enormous current into the fault.[1504] The result is not merely a blown fuse: the part can catch fire and vaporise.[1035][1504] One documented case was a large ceramic across a 0–50 V bench supply output that burned away completely, leaving carbonised board underneath — most likely a part rated at only 25 V, or a defective one, since it failed only when the output was wound up towards full scale.[1035] A mains alarm panel caught fire from a shorted ceramic, and a commercial bench power supply likewise, the latter fixed by relocating the part.[1505][682][1265] Ceramics failing short are also a routine cause of blown fuses in consumer equipment.[1077]

Three mitigations are used. Derating: for a 0–50 V output, the correct search filter is multilayer ceramics rated above 50 V — 63 V, 80 V, 100 V, or another preferred value.[1035] Redundancy: two capacitors in series, so that if one breaks down the other still stands off the voltage and the circuit merely loses half its capacitance.[1035][1710][1293] Construction: parts with soft terminations, or dielectrics and terminations designed to fail open or to resist cracking, are available, are more expensive, and are used routinely in automotive work.[1504][1293] Instrument manufacturers who fit ceramic output capacitors on precision supplies generally use the properly terminated types for exactly this reason.[1174] Series redundancy is rarely done in practice, but it is the available answer where reliability matters most.[1710]

## Mechanical fragility and piezoelectric behaviour

Ceramic capacitors crack very easily — a chip capacitor can be levered off a board with a small flat-bladed screwdriver simply by cracking it.[1089] The same brittleness produces micro-cracks in service, which are the usual precursor to a short.[1293] The mechanism is not always obvious: in a high-current switching converter, the physical vibration of the ceramic capacitors at the switching frequency travelled through the rigid PCB and set up out-of-plane mechanical resonances that cracked nearby MEMS oscillators.[1504] Vibration is also why space-qualified assemblies favour ceramics but pay close attention to how tall components stand off the board.[896]

Class 2 ceramics are piezoelectric, so mechanical shock generates a voltage. Tapping an oscilloscope probe or its compensation network produces a visible response on the screen, traceable to the ceramic compensation capacitors inside.[162] The effect is exploited deliberately in some transducers: hydrophone elements are effectively two-plate ceramic capacitors that bend under sound pressure, and are known in the industry as benders.[1722]

## High-voltage and isolation applications

High-voltage ceramics are common where the alternative is bulky. Old equipment used 3 kV disc ceramics with insulating sleeving over the leads.[1169] A high-voltage differential probe carries 6.8 pF 1 kV ceramics for frequency compensation, straddling the isolation slot with the rest of the high-voltage input network, some of them heat-shrunk — plausibly for explosion containment, as is done with input MOVs in multimeters.[932] Scope-meter front ends likewise bridge isolation slots with ceramic capacitors to couple signals across a barrier.[430] Where a specific capacitance is needed at high voltage and only ceramics offer the required stability, the parts are stacked in series for voltage and in parallel for value; one source-measure unit did this across a ±245 V network.[607]

Voltage rating alone does not make a part safe across a safety barrier. A counterfeit USB charger used an ordinary 1 kV 470 pF ceramic between transformer primary and secondary where a Y-class safety-rated capacitor is required for type approval — inadequate, though still better than the competing unit that had no capacitor there at all.[388]

## Packages, values and displacement of other types

Surface-mount ceramics are made in the standard chip sizes — 1206, 0805, 0603, 0402 and smaller — with 0805 a convenient general-purpose size for hand work.[186][954] Through-hole disc ceramics remain in older designs and in kit-building, where their lack of polarity is one of their conveniences.[116][268][aQ2AVLs8_7k]

The available capacitance has grown enormously. Where 1 µF or 10 µF was once an extremely large part and electrolytics were mandatory above that, a 100 µF ceramic is now unremarkable, though such parts remain expensive.[1209] That progression is what has eroded the case for tantalum, which is costly, uses a scarce material, and in older or cheaper forms is prone to failing violently.[148][33] Some manufacturers apply a blanket rule against tantalums; one 4-channel oscilloscope design contained exactly one, a 470 µF part where ceramic options on that board could not reach the value.[148] Very high value ceramic surface-mount parts are even used as small energy stores in energy-harvesting designs.[534]

Ceramics substitute directly for electrolytics in AC coupling, high-frequency bypassing and general filtering, and there is no reason to prefer an electrolytic in those roles.[827] They are the natural choice in charge-pump voltage doublers and inverters, where the parts already present in an SMD design — 0.47 µF or 1 µF ceramics — do the job that would otherwise call for larger and more expensive electrolytics.[473][483] They also appear across the individual diodes of bridge rectifiers, four 1206 parts in one studio-monitor supply, to suppress high-frequency switching transients from the diodes.[1156] Adding 100 nF of ceramic across a switching supply output brought its high-frequency output excursions down to roughly 200 mV peak to peak.[1298]

## In fault-finding

Because the failure mode is a short, a ceramic capacitor is a standing suspect whenever a board shows a dead rail or a blown fuse — especially on boards carrying no electrolytics at all, where nothing else is a likely candidate.[1145][1714][1322] The difficulty is that a rail may carry many of them: one repair turned up seven further ceramics on a 3.3 V rail after the first suspects were removed, at which point unsoldering candidates one at a time gives way to injecting current from a high-current supply to find the short by other means.[398] Ceramics are not always guilty — a shorted electrolytic near a heatsink is often more likely — and the odds should govern the order of investigation.[398][1714] Where a suspect ceramic has only surface contamination, the dielectric elements are internal to the body, so cleaning the outside and refitting the part is reasonable.[1564]
