# electromagnetic shielding

Electromagnetic shielding is the use of conductive barriers — cans, cases, plates, tapes, sprayed coatings and meshes — to stop electromagnetic energy crossing from one place to another, in either direction. It keeps a circuit's own switching harmonics from radiating out of the box, and it keeps ambient fields out of sensitive front ends.[546][e4wvxWWMla0] Because the barrier only works when it is conductive and connected, shielding is inseparable from grounding: an ungrounded piece of metal over a circuit is decoration.[1090][KsBVBtUDYx4] In practice the amount of shielding in a product is one of the clearest visible indications of how seriously its designers took electromagnetic compatibility.[674][619]

## What the shield is doing

There are two distinct escape routes for noise from a switching product. Radiated emissions come from the circuit itself: fast square waves generate large harmonics, and the traces carrying them act as little antennas that radiate RF energy out of the box.[546] A properly shielded enclosure should in theory suppress that entirely.[546] The second route survives the shield: unless the product runs from batteries entirely inside the enclosure, cables must pass in and out, and the internal noise piggybacks onto them as conducted emissions.[546] A shielded box on its own is therefore only half a solution.

The same barrier works in reverse. An unshielded oscilloscope front end sits open to whatever is nearby, and mains-frequency electric fields couple straight into it — an ESD mat left floating behind the bench makes an effective 50 Hz pickup source, and the coupling to the scope is capacitive.[e4wvxWWMla0] The distinction between electric-field and magnetic-field problems matters when choosing the remedy: relays in the same instrument were covered with magnetic shielding tape, but the 50 Hz ingress was an electric field problem, for which a plain metal can is the fix.[e4wvxWWMla0]

That failure mode is easy to reproduce by omission. Reassembling a scope without refitting the metal can over a channel's front end reintroduces visible 50 Hz on that channel, with no other plausible cause.[wWymcaiaQXM] Refitting a substitute can, tack-soldered at one corner and not even fully formed to the original shape, removes the pickup.[e4wvxWWMla0] Manufacturers solder such cans down rather than clipping them, presumably so vibration cannot shake them loose.[wWymcaiaQXM] A comparable effect shows in an unshielded display module driven on the bench, where the readout picks up surrounding noise simply because it is not yet enclosed.[1669]

## Oscillators, clocks and self-interference

Shielding is often aimed at a specific aggressor rather than the board at large. A shield inside a multimeter, bent over and bonded by a trace to the chassis wiring, exists to isolate the digital circuitry, and its position immediately adjacent to the crystal indicates the clock is the source being contained.[427] An audio-band signal generator built around a 1 kHz oscillator is screened as a complete assembly.[693]

Self-interference is the acute case. A super-regenerative receiver contains an LC tank oscillator with positive feedback that genuinely oscillates, so an unshielded one transmits on very nearly the frequency it is trying to receive.[767] Two such receivers in proximity, neither properly shielded, spew RF at the receive frequency and swamp one another, producing erratic reception that looks like a receiver fault rather than a shielding fault.[767]

## Signal integrity and low-level measurement

At the extremes of measurement, shielding stops being an EMC nicety and becomes the only way the instrument works at all. A precision pulse generator of the kind Jim Williams built requires the utmost signal integrity, and without shielding, 50 Hz swamps the measurement outright — even its battery box is shielded.[306] A Wheatstone bridge intended for megohm, gigaohm and teraohm resistance measurement devotes considerable design effort to shielding and guarding, together with careful wiring layout, to prevent leakage and interference from corrupting extremely low currents.[1476] A precision resistance calibrator shields its wire-wound elements because a wire-wound resistor is a coil, and nothing should be allowed to couple into it; some of the resistors arrive already shielded at one end of the can.[544] Similar reasoning applies to source measure units, whose switch-mode supply is put in its own well-shielded enclosure as a matter of necessity.[607]

The principle extends beyond electromagnetic fields to any unwanted radiation. In a photon-counting setup, wrapping the lead in black electrical tape left the count slightly high, and adding several turns of aluminium foil over it brought the count down — the same wrap-and-fold technique, applied to light.[869]

## Construction and materials

Shielding appears in a wide range of physical forms. Bulk pressed metalwork and die-cast alloy cans account for much of the weight of well-built test equipment.[323][144][674][800][536] Surface-mount cans soldered to the board shield individual sections and are frequently unopenable without desoldering.[521] Machined aluminium covers with built-in braces seal off individual RF sections in a spectrum analyser.[470] Conductive spray applied to the inside of a plastic case shields a product with no metalwork at all,[Yyuk3--FWfs] and nickel plating or screening of a 3D-printed enclosure serves the same purpose in low volumes.[1715] Metallized shielding tape — genuinely metallized, not plain mylar — covers small groups of components such as capacitors and an inductor.[907] Copper shielding tape wrapped around a tape head keeps noise out of the head signal, which then travels to the electronics on shielded coax.[752] Elsewhere the enclosure itself does the work: a mostly metal-shielded computer case is dense enough that antenna placement has to be designed around it,[1211] and an aluminium housing shields motor-controller electronics well enough that a 64 kHz motor PWM frequency need not be synchronised to anything.[HBLKxC4JzTA]

RFI gaskets close the seams. Conductive gaskets are fitted around USB and Ethernet connectors on scopes,[674] and the general product family includes clip-on gaskets, ultra soft shield gaskets and V-shaped gaskets, sitting between metal shields to maintain continuity.[477] Conductive RFI sponge, normally an enclosure-shielding component, is occasionally repurposed for something else entirely — in one appliance it serves as the touch contacts.[1523]

Where a shield must be transparent, fine conductive mesh is used. Such mesh measures around 50 ohms across it yet conducts all the way through, allowing an opening in a case to be shielded while remaining see-through — the same principle as a microwave oven door, which blocks 2.45 GHz while permitting vision, though a very fine mesh covers a considerably wider range.[477]

## Grounding and bonding

A shield's effectiveness depends on where it connects. Battery contacts decoupled directly to chassis ground, together with a separate shield behind the case metalwork, indicate the designers judged the back metal case alone insufficient and wanted a more localized, lower impedance ground.[1090] The same intent appears where metal cans are earthed and bonded over to a mechanism's main chassis,[937] where a shielding board placed between the mains wiring and the audio electronics is explicitly earthed,[KsBVBtUDYx4] and where a shield ties down to the metal frame surrounding a battery.[937] A large grounded shielding plate under a board can be covered with tough insulating film, not mylar, to guarantee that nothing arcs through to the shield.[619] In an oscilloscope repair the fix was reciprocal: grounding the ESD mat removed it as a 50 Hz source, but the unshielded front end remained the underlying problem.[e4wvxWWMla0]

Shielding sometimes also serves mechanically. Deep ridges and high walls in a shielded multimeter case, combined with an O-ring, provide blast containment should the meter fail internally.[372] A structural inner metal plate in a plastic-backed instrument does double duty as heat sink and as shielding where shielding is needed.[PcxEO3fA_Ls]

## Judging a design by its shielding

Comprehensive shielding correlates with instrument weight, and belt-and-braces metalwork is the norm in mid-range and better test gear.[323][674][800][199][144][536] Notable omissions are therefore informative. A 200 MHz oscilloscope whose analog input circuitry has only partial shielding walls rather than a complete metal can is unusual, since even lower-end scopes in that category typically use a full can.[187] Another scope has no shielding at all between its switch-mode supply and the main logic board — less consequential when the adjacent circuitry is digital.[587] An inexpensive handheld multimeter may contain no extra internal shielding whatsoever.[1692]

Older equipment shows both extremes. A 1970s home computer combines long unshielded ribbon cable running to an expansion box with no metallized shielding inside the case and no chokes on the I/O lines — an arrangement that cannot work when wide parallel memory buses are involved.[645] By contrast, a 1980s notepad computer shields the entire keypad backing as well as the main board,[385] a portable PC of the same era contains enough shielding to account for much of its mass,[1348] a benchtop multimeter of 1980 vintage adds a metallized front panel for extra front-end shielding,[951] and camera, camcorder and portable-audio products of the period apply heavy shielding around transport mechanisms, image sensors and video processing.[375][380][860] A workstation-class computer was additionally offered in a Tempest variant — an electromagnetically shielded model for government and military use.[1404]

At the far end of the scale, the copper box inside a dilution refrigerator shields a quantum computer chip both to reduce electromagnetic noise and to prevent high-frequency photons, as carriers of energy, from reaching the chip and heating it.[1594] In spacecraft ordnance handling, safety practice combined dead-shorting and twisting the bridge wire leads so they could not pick up broadcasts, solid grounding, and enclosure in an aluminium cylinder, so that no conceivable path existed to pass current through the wire.[BUW6h88weXU]

## Simple shields versus elaborate ones

Where a shielding problem is real, the simple passive answer is usually the right one. Against RFID reading, an active jamming card is a complex solution to a problem that either carries no real risk or is solved by aluminium foil or an ordinary shielding sleeve.[890] Conversely, invoking shielding where no mechanism exists is a marker of pseudoscience: claims that wiping a fuse box reduces electromagnetic waste appear to descend from an earlier product that was simply a piece of copper or brass presented as a shield.[870]
