# reflow soldering

Reflow soldering is the process in which solder paste is deposited onto a bare board's pads, components are placed into that paste, and the whole assembly is then heated until the solder melts — reflows — and forms every joint at once.[239][167] It is the dominant method for assembling surface mount boards, having displaced wave soldering almost entirely for SMD work because it is far more controlled and far cleaner.[431] The solder is already present on the pads before the part arrives, so the operation consists only of raising the assembly to temperature; the same principle scales from a pick-and-place line with a multi-zone infrared oven down to a soldering iron tinning a single pad by hand.[186][264]

## The production sequence

The paste mask layer in the PCB design data is used to cut a stainless steel stencil, so that solder paste is deposited only on the intended pads and nowhere else on the board.[239] Paste is squeegeed across the stencil, the stencil is lifted to leave an even deposit on each pad, components are pick-and-placed into the wet paste, and the panel travels through the reflow oven where the solder melts.[239][415] Solder paste itself is a controlled consumable: it must be refrigerated, kept airtight and kept at constant temperature or it goes off.[415][163] Paste printing equipment is generally driven from the same Gerber paste file the PCB package outputs, with CAM software that can print individual boards or a whole panel.[163] A complete small-scale line consists of paste dispenser, stencil printer, pick-and-place and a multiple-zone IR reflow oven.[uR69Vmh7qnI]

Once the thermal profile is correct, the process is close to deterministic: yields approaching one hundred percent are achievable on the soldering itself, and the defects that remain tend to come from the human operations bolted on around it.[588]

## The thermal profile

The profile — the programmed ramp, dwell and peak — is the controlled variable of the process, and it is followed from the component manufacturers' instructions to minimise risk to the parts.[415] Published profiles are shapes rather than absolute values, and shift with the component mix, the board and its thermal mass.[558] A reflow controller ramps the temperature gently rather than simply switching elements on and baking the board, which is what an unmodified toaster oven does.[558] Even with control, heat does not spread evenly across a board: parts on one side of a panel can be fully reflowed while parts on the other side have not yet begun to melt.[558]

Getting the profile wrong damages parts rather than merely producing bad joints. A precision surface mount oscillator costing fifty or a hundred dollars can be pushed out of specification by an inappropriate profile, so an assembler should not be left to apply any generic profile to a board that depends on a critical part.[813] The same concern applies to precision SMD capacitors, where improper reflow can damage the part in ways that are not visible afterwards; through-hole equivalents avoid the risk entirely.[1496] The industry treats the whole business as a discipline with its own IPC standards, tack test data and particle size grades for the paste balls.[558]

## Surface tension and self-alignment

Molten solder pulls a part into position. Surface tension acting on the exposed pad under a chip drags it back to centre as the joint forms.[434] The same effect governs BGA placement, which is why a chip must be left mechanically free to float during reflow — glue or potting added for mechanical strength belongs on the board only after the chip has reflowed and the balls have pulled it into alignment.[1477] The effect can also work against the assembly: uneven surface tension can pull a small chip resistor down at an angle, particularly where the pads were never sized for that package.[735] A large thermal pad under a QFN can defeat self-centring and leave the part visibly offset.[346]

Solder mask is a functional part of the process, not just cosmetic. Mask between pins stops solder bridging during reflow and permits tighter conductor spacing; paste that appears to have smeared between pads will be pulled back into the pad wells as it melts, because solder does not wet the mask.[1222][415] Where the mask has been opened as one large square with no webbing between pins, too much solder will simply bridge, and the problem worsens as pitch drops below about 0.5 mm.[1353] Enlarged solder-thieving pads at the ends of a chip's pin rows catch the solder and peel it away from the preceding pin so vortices and shorts do not form.[856] Vias in pads are permitted by the bare board fabricator but matter to the assembler, since paste will be wicked down an unplugged hole during reflow; plugging is a paid option.[1259]

Board finish interacts with reflow. Gold finishes such as ENIG give a flat pad surface, which matters for a high-ball-count BGA that must sit flat to reflow in a controlled way, whereas hot air levelled tin is comparatively lumpy.[1259][127] Immersion silver survives multiple reflows and stores better before assembly than OSP.[1259] ENIG is also the option chosen where multiple reflows or subsequent wire bonding are required.[1259]

## Double-sided assembly

On a double-sided load, the components on the underside are held with a dollop of glue under each part so they do not fall off when that side passes inverted through the oven.[1314][1527] The presence of a glue dot beneath a passive is therefore diagnostic: on a single-sided load there is no reason to glue anything down before reflow.[1374] Glue is also what identifies the older wave-soldered SMD boards, where every part had to be adhered because a molten solder bath passed over the whole underside.[431][907]

## Telling reflow apart from wave soldering

Reflowed joints and wave-soldered joints look entirely different, and the distinction can usually be made from the board alone. Wave soldering plates solder all the way up the legs of a part and leaves noticeably more of it, whereas reflow leaves solder only where paste was printed.[431][619] A DIP marking with a directional arrow indicates the direction the board travelled through the wave machine and implies through-hole work done as a second process after the SMD side was reflowed.[446] Solder thieving pads on outer pins are a wave-solder design feature, though their presence is not conclusive on its own.[430][1452] Hand rework is equally identifiable: excess solder, dragged tails at the end of a pin, or flux residue left uncleaned around connectors all mark joints that were not machine reflowed.[1520][582][511] Reflow quality on a well-made consumer board is generally first class, and the visible flaws cluster around whatever was hand-finished afterwards.[397][1571]

## Component survival

Plastics and packages must be rated for the profile. Surface mount connectors are moulded from higher-temperature thermoplastics specifically so they survive reflow, while their visually near-identical through-hole equivalents are not — put an already-populated board with through-hole connectors through a thermal oven and the connectors melt.[782] The mechanism is that in a reflow oven the entire connector, plastics included, is slowly brought to temperature, unlike a solder wave which heats only the pins passing through it.[782] Even properly rated surface mount connectors can have problems, data sheets do not always give the necessary figures, and the practical rule is to hold parts at temperature for the absolute minimum time required.[782]

Moisture sensitivity is the other major constraint. Non-hermetic packages absorb moisture from the air; during the reflow ramp that moisture expands and can crack the die or the case, which is why such parts ship in heat-sealed moisture barrier bags with a desiccant and should not be opened before they reach the assembler.[500][346][m9tza_c4sxc] At production volumes this is a genuine yield problem rather than a theoretical one.[500] Other parts are simply out of scope: printed conductive ink limited to about 140 °C cannot go through a reflow machine at all, and needs hand soldering or a leaded solder with a lower-temperature, shorter profile.[aLcdm6gB6eE] Breadboards and similar assemblies are not heat rated for reflow, though they sometimes survive.[1608][341] Leaded solder reflows at a lower temperature than lead-free and is preferred where the substrate is marginal — printed circuits on Kapton and even on 200 GSM linen paper have been reflow soldered successfully.[614]

## Parts designed for the process

Because reflow is the default, components are designed to suit it. Surface mount connectors are placed by the pick-and-place machine like any other part and reflowed in the same pass.[1097] Surface mount heat sinks exist for pick-and-place, but must be kept small because the suction heads have limited grip, and they draw heat out of the joint during reflow precisely because they are heat sinks.[1347][744] Right-angle tact switches, metal domes and membrane keypad contacts are all available in reflowable form, though reflow soldering a dome onto a PCB changes its feel by preventing the corners lifting.[761][1282][1551] Castellated modules — small camera modules, or retrofit mod boards carrying an obsolescence replacement — are treated as components: soldered as a unit, either by hand or, with a specialised production tray, picked and placed and reflowed like anything else.[1523][1158] Fine-pitch leadless packages with no side-accessible pads are effectively reflow-only, as an iron cannot reach the terminations at all.[181] Flex assemblies are joined with a hot bar tool that presses down and reflows the solder directly onto the board's pads.[651]

## Hand reflow

The same physics is available on the bench without an oven. In the tack-and-reflow method a pad is tinned, the part is positioned and the pre-existing solder is remelted with an iron, which is reflow soldering by definition.[186] With paste and hot air, paste is printed or dabbed, parts are placed, and a hot air gun at around 250 to 260 °C melts everything into place — with the airflow kept low, since there is little adhesion holding parts down.[167][346][415] A stencil, paste and a hot air gun can reflow a whole board including delicate LEDs, and the result is visually indistinguishable from machine assembly except that the parts were placed by hand.[415] Proper oven work remains preferable where the profile matters, and for a small board it is a genuine toss-up whether stencil and oven beat simply hand soldering the thing.[415]

QFN and MLF packages can be reflowed with an iron alone by fluxing thoroughly, tinning the pad, pressing the chip down and feeding 0.5 mm or finer solder in at the edge so it flows under and wets the hidden pads — no paste or hot air strictly required.[346] Drag soldering relies on the same behaviour, with the correct tip size being critical: too large and it digs into the FR4 rather than riding in the solder groove.[408] Attempts to force reflow by raising iron temperature have limits; at 385 °C on a 0.4 mm pitch part the results were no better than at lower settings and the temperature was simply too high.[408] Fine-pitch hand reflow demands inspection gear and, at least initially, a microscope, since the actual behaviour of the solder at the pin cannot be judged by eye.[408] The extreme end of the hobbyist practice runs to reflow soldering of thousand-pin BGAs on frying pans in their kitchen.[54]

## Reflow as a repair operation

In troubleshooting, reflowing a suspect joint — reheating it, sometimes with added solder — is a standard first move, and the phrase is used for reworking existing joints as much as for original assembly.[620][565] Thermal parts that heat and cool repeatedly are candidates for microcracks in their joints, making a precautionary reflow reasonable before deeper investigation.[1322] Reflowing an entire board is a recognised approach to a suspected BGA failure caused by thermal stress, though it is a last resort and often not worth the effort.[780][781][631] Its diagnostic value is limited: reheating all the joints on a suspect module frequently changes nothing, and a joint that reflows cleanly rules out only that joint.[564][Yk_T-uCbg10][e98cYNPUxcg] When solder must be reworked around fine pins, drag soldering is sometimes chosen over a full reflow.[1522] Where a connection turns out to be conductive adhesive rather than solder, gentle reheating with a bare iron at around 260 °C can restore it.[1100]
