# cooling fan

A cooling fan is the forced-air element of a thermal design: a motor-driven impeller that pulls ambient air through an enclosure and across heat sinks so that dissipated power leaves the case faster than natural convection allows. In benchtop test equipment, power supplies, computers and consumer electronics it is usually the only moving part, and it is simultaneously the component that determines whether the instrument survives full load and the component that determines whether it is tolerable to sit next to. Its two design axes — airflow path and acoustic noise — are frequently in tension, and the way a manufacturer resolves that tension is one of the clearest signals of the care taken over the rest of the product.

## Thermal necessity

Where a fan is fitted, it is generally load-bearing rather than precautionary. A 12-bit oscilloscope of slim form factor, run in still lab air with its fan disconnected, passed just over 70 °C internally, rebooted, hung on its splash screen and began to smell before it could be shut down; with the fan running and an ambient of roughly 24 °C, the same measurement point sat at 39.4 °C overnight.[pwS6HnM9PDo] Another oscilloscope operated with its case off logged an over-temperature event and shut itself down within a couple of minutes, having already warned that internal temperature was approaching a damaging point and that the fans and clearance should be checked.[587] A scope under repair with its fan unpowered produced an input-termination overheat message purely from lack of cooling.[565]

The temperatures involved justify the hardware. An ADC ASIC in a high-end scope was measured at 84 °C and still climbing toward 85 °C, with a companion chipset near 74 °C and analog front ends too hot to keep a hand on.[1124] A linear bench supply's output transistor reached 100 °C at worst case, within margin but only with the fan running at full tilt.[828] A fifteen-minute NiMH charger cannot work without one: an 8 A charge rate to 70% capacity in fifteen minutes makes the cells themselves the heat source, so the fan draws air in from the bottom past the contacts,[811] and its presence forces the design to sense each cell individually rather than relying on a single in-case sensor, since the forced air destroys any correlation between case air and cell temperature.[35]

## Airflow architecture

The canonical arrangement draws cool air in through vents on one face, directly over the finned heat sinks carrying the power devices, and exhausts it from the opposite face.[587][1023][800][828][1402] Placing the inlet vents low, so the incoming stream is drawn across the board and over the heat sinks before leaving through the rear exhaust fan, is better than venting at the top.[864] An electronic load teardown showed the principle duplicated: each MOSFET heat sink had its own fan pulling air from its own vent holes through its own fin stack and out the back independently.[281]

Fin orientation is part of the same system. Fins must run along the flow direction; rotating them so the air meets the flat faces produces turbulent flow and destroys the effectiveness of the arrangement.[1289] In one slim scope the fan pulls through the fin stack directly, and that efficiency is what permits the reduced depth.[1717] Multiple fans may be spread evenly across a large board set rather than concentrated at one end.[1289] Stacking instruments reduces fan efficiency slightly but need not block intake, because the feet maintain clearance for air to be drawn in.[1173]

Failures of that architecture are common enough to be recognisable. One scope drew air from a rear vent and blew it straight into the shield can of the front end, where it could only reflect and exit again.[824] A bench multimeter's inlet path was obstructed by the mains transformer sitting in front of the vents.[829] In a 7.5-digit meter the fan sat hard against the back of the case with no usable path.[731] A logic-analysis instrument with the fan mounted at the very top could only circulate air inside the lid rather than exchange it.[e98cYNPUxcg] Two instruments from the same manufacturer, sharing an identical fan brand and model, had them fitted in opposite directions — one intake, one exhaust — with no evident reason.[1431] A self-built PC ran for some time with its cooler fan installed backwards.[1098]

## Sizing and noise

Small fans must spin fast to move useful air, and speed is what produces the whine. A 45 mm frameless fan of the type once common on graphics cards is audible and high-pitched, and no small fan of that class will be genuinely silent no matter how it is swapped; reaching silence means moving to a 100 mm fan on the rear panel, at the cost of depth and of interference with VESA mounting.[zoyaHOqp9gI][1566][1563] The same logic applies to a 2 kW supply brick whose small fan was already loud at 150 W, an order of magnitude below rating: a larger fan turning slower would have been the better choice.[1298]

A quiet fan is treated as a first-order specification rather than a refinement — the most underrated feature of any benchtop test equipment is a quiet fan.[1545] Large fans, sometimes angled to exhaust out the side, are audible only as a low hum or not at all;[143][1639][1545] two-fan dual-exhaust arrangements and small high-speed units are the ones that intrude in a quiet room.[1503][976][703][480][1][474] Bearing quality is audible independently of size: a rumble suggests a lower-grade bearing,[1220] and a rattle or the noisy ball-bearing character indicates wear or a loose mounting.[315][474][lXvXBdTP7Tg][1709][1279] Mechanical decoupling addresses part of the problem — plasma television chassis used shock-compliant fan mounts and foam surrounds specifically to reduce transmitted noise,[725][52] whereas a basic rigid mounting with no compliance is a visible cost saving.[1679]

## Speed control and start-up behaviour

Temperature-controlled fans are widespread, cycling with load rather than running at a fixed speed.[315][1279][1280] A bench supply increases fan flow rate as the main heat sink warms, with the side effect of cooling adjacent heat sinks that are not themselves the load;[512] the same family of supply audibly speeds up at power-on and then slows once running, rather than being fixed at full speed in firmware.[549] Full speed at boot is a deliberate strategy: with the environment unknown before the system has come up, the fan is run flat out until temperature data is available.[nO09bc5ozng] The cost is a loud turn-on transient that persists until the processor has booted.[1402] Once running, a well-controlled fan spins up briefly and settles to a low-level hum.[587] Not all designs stop the fan when there is nothing to cool: one supply ran its fan continuously with no load at all.[315] Conversely, a low-power standby mode may exist largely to stop the fan,[Fx3YixoQS6E] and mini-PCs stop theirs entirely when idle or powered down.[1739][p287qKMXqxQ]

Post-shutdown run-on is a related control behaviour: a hot-air rework station cuts the heating element but keeps the blower running until the sensed temperature falls to 100 °C.[167]

## Fan presence as a monitored condition

A fan can be part of the boot interlock. A 500 MHz oscilloscope repeatedly cycle-booted and flashed a diagnostic code that the service flowchart attributed to the processor board; the actual cause was the disconnected fan. The connection is only two pins with no tachometer wire, so the detection must be some form of load sensing on the supply, and the fan plugs into the processor board rather than the power supply board.[1515]

## Retrofits, service and dust

Because most fans are standard sizes, replacement with a quieter modern unit is a routine modification, and a free-standing fan not integrated into a casting or heat pipe is trivially swapped.[591][655][620][703] Where the thermal load is small enough — a processor with a TDP of roughly 30 W — a passive heat sink can replace the fan entirely, though it may require custom engineering.[Pq8ncblgqss]

Dust is the dominant service issue. Fans in laptops and desktops accumulate enough debris to lose most of their effectiveness while the fan itself remains mechanically sound, with no broken blades and a bearing that still spins silently; cleaning it and renewing the thermal compound restores normal behaviour, and the resulting change is audible as the fan no longer running at full blast on boot.[1345] The cleaning is done outdoors rather than indoors.[1345] Genuine fan failure also occurs, as motor windings that no longer engage at all.[1280]

## Fan as afterthought

Several designs betray a fan added late. One power analyser had its fan wired with a red and a black lead bodge-soldered directly onto the leads of the axial main output filter capacitors, where a design that had planned for a fan from the start would have carried a two-pin connector.[589] A monitor repair found a small fan hot-glued in place with its wires bodged across the main filter capacitors.[1202] In a 7.5-digit multimeter the fan reads as tacked on as an afterthought to the whole design, with no proper path and no acoustic consideration.[731] The counter-example is a supply in which a second fan, added to move air over the main switching transformer, was held by a soldered metal bracket on the main board — mechanical integration that indicates a deliberate late-stage decision rather than a bodge.[1691] A television power supply board carried a dedicated fan supply rail and labelled fan connectors that were simply left unpopulated for the model in question.[446]

## Designs that omit the fan

A fan is not universally desirable. A precision bench multimeter deliberately has none: the instrument is rated for around 30 W maximum and the airflow itself is the objection, because drawing air through side vents and out again creates thermal gradients across the board and over sensitive parts, the voltage reference in particular.[426] Comparable low-dissipation instruments are fanless outright, which is a point of comparison against otherwise similar equipment that is not.[1][kdjpbWLi7UI] The same threshold appears in power conversion: the 5 kW member of an inverter family is passively cooled, while the 8 kW top model carries two fans.[kKfZgAyFu8Q] Where a small regulator is put into a sealed case, adding a fan and vent holes becomes necessary once dissipation reaches the tens of watts.[1030]

## Beyond enclosure cooling

Fans also serve as process tools rather than enclosure exhaust. A 3D printer's part-cooling blower feeds a duct wider than the print head so that airflow crosses the whole deposited layer, switchable per filament type in software,[jFapDuowXKU] and a pair of 50 mm fans mounted over a heated bed exists to bring the bed temperature back down quickly, the cool-down being the slow half of the cycle.[520] Extruder hot ends carry their own heat sink and fan.[843] Low-noise 12 V fans are used at the bench simply to blow solder fumes away from the work, where a full extractor would be too loud.[555] Forced air is also used to cool components that carry no heat sink at all, such as banks of ceramic power resistors in an electronic load, where the fan alone provides the entire thermal path.[SoeVNaz7KGc]
