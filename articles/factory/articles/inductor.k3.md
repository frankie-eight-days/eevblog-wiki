# inductor

An inductor is a passive component that stores energy in a magnetic field when current flows through it, and whose defining circuit behavior is opposition to any change in that current.[1406][859] Physically it is a coil of wire, often wound on a ferrite or other high-permeability core that concentrates the magnetic field, though an air-core coil or even a single straight wire exhibits inductance.[1406][1049] Along with the capacitor, it is one of the two energy-storage elements that make circuits time-dependent, moving analysis from steady-state DC into transient and AC behavior.[1406]

## Physical principles

Passing current through any conductor — a wire, a PCB trace, a component — generates a magnetic field around it, with direction given by the right-hand rule: the thumb points along conventional current flow and the curled fingers show the field direction.[1406] Winding the conductor into a coil makes the fields of adjacent turns add through the common core, which is why inductors are wound with multiple turns rather than left as straight wire; adding a ferrite core further contains and concentrates the field.[1406] A wire simply passed once through the center of a toroid core constitutes a full turn — no wrapping around the ferrite is required — and such a one-turn arrangement on a stacked set of toroids can yield substantial inductance, on the order of 50 µH.[708] A ferrite bead slipped over a wire is likewise a small inductor, the bead acting to contain the field.[1406]

## DC transient behavior

When voltage is first applied to an un-energized inductor, it behaves as the opposite of a capacitor: the capacitor initially acts as a short circuit, while the inductor acts as an open circuit, passing no current until its magnetic field builds.[1406] Current then rises exponentially, reaching 63.2 % of its final value in one time constant and settling within roughly 1 % after five time constants; the final current is set only by the winding's DC resistance.[1406][1409] Measured on a relay coil, this exponential rise shows a small discontinuity at the point where the armature pulls in and the coil begins doing mechanical work; a free-standing coil follows the predicted curve precisely.[1409]

The governing volt-amp relationship is that the voltage across the inductor equals the inductance multiplied by the rate of change of current, V = L·di/dt.[1660] The practical consequence is that an inductor resists changes in current: when a load suddenly demands current behind a supply-line inductance, the inductance cannot respond quickly and the supply voltage dips, which is why bypass capacitors are placed close to loads.[859]

## Back EMF and inductive kick

Interrupting current through an inductor reverses the voltage across it: the collapsing magnetic field, per Lenz's law, induces a voltage opposite to the one that established the current, and the inductor drives current in whatever path remains in an attempt to maintain flow.[1409][1406] If the coil is simply opened with no discharge path, the field still collapses and in theory generates an infinite negative voltage across the inductor — in practice a large, damaging spike.[1406] The standard protection is a flyback diode (also called a snubber, freewheeling, or back-EMF diode) across the coil; the "freewheeling" name comes from the flywheel analogy, in which the stored energy keeps the wheel spinning after the drive is removed, and the diode gives the current a circular path to decay through.[1409] Relay-driver ICs such as eight-channel devices integrate one such diode per output for this purpose.[1409] Power amplifiers driving heavily inductive loads use the same reasoning, catching the kick to the rails with diodes, since interrupting current to an inductive load forces the voltage to "fly" one way or the other.[354] The effect is also exploited deliberately: passing current through an inductor, open-circuiting it, and capacitively coupling the resulting spike onto a supply rail is a simple way to simulate transient disturbances for immunity testing.[354]

## AC behavior

An inductor presents an AC resistance called inductive reactance, XL, measured in ohms and given in complex form by jωL, with magnitude ωL = 2πfL; Ohm's law then applies as V/I = XL just as V/I = R for a resistor, provided the phase relationship is accounted for.[1660][1470] In a pure inductor the voltage leads the current by 90°, equivalently stated as the current lagging the voltage; the CIVIL mnemonic encodes this (in L, V leads I) alongside the capacitor case.[1660][1446][1469] Real inductors fall short of the full 90° shift.[1446] Ideal inductors and capacitors dissipate no real power; they only shuttle energy back and forth, which appears as reactive power Q due to the voltage-current phase difference.[1730] A sinusoid is the one waveform shape that passes through networks of inductors and capacitors without distortion of its shape.[1417]

## Use in switchmode converters

The inductor is the energy-storage element of a switching converter: while the switch is on, current builds in the inductor and stores energy in its magnetic field; when the switch opens, the inductor's collapsing field dumps that energy through the catch diode into the output capacitor and load.[90][139] Component selection is dominated by peak current, not average output current — a converter delivering 100 mA output may see 0.74 A peak, and both the inductor and the diode must be rated for the peak, not the nominal output.[110] A common rule of thumb is to rate the inductor at roughly three times the output current capability.[259] The lowest possible series (DC) resistance is wanted because winding resistance directly costs efficiency, and physically larger inductors generally offer lower resistance; inductor type, value, and brand all measurably shift a converter's efficiency curve and dropout point.[110][259] Higher switching frequency permits a smaller inductance and a physically smaller part — valuable because inductors are often the largest component in the design — at the cost of increased switching losses.[110][139] Shrinking the magnetics too far, however, both reduces efficiency and limits output current capability, a hard constraint in extreme low-profile designs where the inductor may be an 0201/0402-class part.[751] Multi-phase converters distribute the load across several inductors — a four-phase step-up design requires four — trading board space for efficiency and current capability.[139] An optional series inductor and capacitor after the main output form an LC post-filter that reduces output ripple.[110]

## Filtering and signal applications

An inductor passes DC while blocking or impeding high-frequency AC, which makes it the standard element for feeding DC power to RF circuitry along a signal line: masthead amplifier and probe circuits route supply voltage through an inductor (or RF bead) to the amplifier chip while the RF passes unimpeded, and conducted-EMI measurement taps use an inductor to keep high-frequency noise out of the supply source.[940][966][546][1275] Combinations of series inductors and shunt capacitors form LC filters, and at microwave frequencies the same topology is realised as distributed elements — pads etched on the board acting as capacitors to ground with connecting traces acting as series inductors — in multi-element stub filters on controlled-impedance substrates.[391][823][1101][470] Inductors also serve as magnetic pickup elements: a plain wound coil with enough turns detects the field from an induction loop,[1287] and a small inductor forms an LC proximity sensor responsive to nearby magnetic field changes over a few millimetres.[269] Key fobs use a designated inductor (L1) as the low-frequency antenna coil.[cc_GqtBuP4c] In a defibrillator, a series inductor limits inrush current and shapes the rise time of the energy pulse delivered to the patient; older monophasic designs relied on this inductor to create the waveform itself.[909] In electromechanical watt-hour meters, the voltage coil is deliberately made highly inductive so that its flux lags the applied voltage by 90°, providing the phase shift the metering element requires at unity power factor.[1446] Audio amplifier outputs sometimes place an inductor in parallel with a small series resistor.[276]

## Non-ideal behaviour

No component is purely one element: an inductor carries the inductance of its connecting leads, capacitance between its windings, and the DC resistance of its wire, and conversely every wire, resistor, and capacitor carries parasitic inductance, so a purely inductive circuit does not exist.[1728] Capacitors similarly contain equivalent series inductance (ESL) from leads and construction, making every real capacitor an RLC network with resonances.[859] Layout matters for the same reason: a long trace between a decoupling capacitor and the pin it serves places unwanted inductance in series,[1034] and even a mod wire can add enough inductance to matter compared with a surface-mount zero-ohm jumper.[987]

## Acoustic noise and mechanical fragility

Wire-wound inductors can emit audible high-frequency noise when driven at a frequency that excites a mechanical resonant mode of the winding, core, mounting, and PCB — a common source of the squeal heard from switching power supplies, distinct from but analogous to the piezoelectric "singing capacitor" effect.[1743][855][1607] Ferrite cores are brittle, making inductors a mechanical weak point under shock and flex: inductors have sheared clean off boards in drop tests,[66][417][868] and board flex has cracked inductor solder joints and fractured the parts themselves, a failure that destroys grounding and measurement integrity in affected instruments.[884][1449][9u8TEJdAVdQ] Slotting the board under inductors was one historical contributor to flex-induced cracking.[9u8TEJdAVdQ]

## Measurement

Inductance is measured at a defined test frequency by LCR meters — typically selectable across 100 Hz to 100 kHz — and the phase angle of the result confirms the component type, an inductor showing voltage leading current where a capacitor shows the opposite.[1465][757] LCR tweezers report inductance together with equivalent series resistance, with inductance ranges from the nanohenry region to tens of millihenries at a few percent accuracy.[1335][137] In-circuit measurement is unreliable when other components shunt the part under test.[81] Ordinary multimeters, though lacking a specified test frequency, excite the component at some internal frequency during measurement and can be pressed into service to give a reasonable indication of inductance, a little-known capability that works in many but not all cases.[1465]

## Identification on boards

The presence of an inductor is the reliable visual signature of a switching (rather than linear) regulator — "you can tell by the inductors" — since linear regulators need no magnetics.[1552][1701][805][837][444][454] Conversely, their absence identifies linear regulation even where the controller package resembles a switcher.[1617] Major inductor manufacturers such as Coilcraft mark parts with a recognizable logo, and shielded SMD power inductors are identifiable by their shape and corner profile.[1475]
