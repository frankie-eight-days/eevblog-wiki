# lcd

A liquid crystal display is a passive, field-driven display: the individual segments are filled with liquid crystals that turn on or off according to the electrostatic polarity applied across them, with no light generated and essentially no current drawn.[1297] Because the drive contacts are extremely high impedance and the only real consumption is charging and discharging the segment capacitance as it switches, an LCD watch or meter can run for years on a button cell.[1670][1045] That property, more than resolution or colour, is why the LCD became the default readout of handheld test equipment, calculators, watches and low-power instruments, displacing LED displays that consumed orders of magnitude more current for far less information.[243]

## Why an LCD must be driven with AC

An LCD segment does not care about circuit ground. Its backplane, or common pin, can be tied to circuit common, a 5 V rail, or an arbitrary potential; only the relative difference between the common pin and the segment pin matters.[1045] Applying a steady DC potential is against the recommendations of every LCD manufacturer, so the drive waveform must alternate in polarity between common and segment.[1297]

The usual approach is to drive the common pin with a square wave — around 100 Hz is typical. That frequency is high enough to avoid visible flicker and low enough not to waste power, since the higher the drive frequency the more reactive current flows through the display's capacitance and the higher the consumption.[1045] Driven at 1 Hz the switching is plainly visible, and at 0.1 Hz the segments visibly fade between transitions.[1044]

Turning a segment on or off then reduces to a phase decision. Drive the segment pin with the same waveform as the common and the two pins are effectively tied together, giving no difference and no visible segment; drive it with the inverted waveform and the segment turns on. That is the whole of static LCD drive with ordinary TTL or CMOS logic levels.[1045] In a microcontroller implementation the inversion is a bitwise exclusive-OR against the port value, one bit per segment.[1045] The same arrangement yields a useful bonus: because the segment sees the difference of two out-of-phase logic signals, a 5 V logic swing produces 10 V peak-to-peak at the display, and 3.3 V logic produces 6.6 V peak-to-peak — enough to drive practically any LCD at high contrast without generating a higher rail.[1045]

Static drive is straightforward. Multiplexed drive is not: it requires multiple intermediate bias voltage levels, and the biasing for an eight-common display is complicated enough that it is normally delegated to a dedicated driver chip rather than implemented in discrete logic.[1045][1074]

## The DC bias failure mode

Manufacturers state that a sustained DC bias will eventually ruin a display, but not how long that takes. A long-term bench test set up in December 2017 addressed the question directly: the common pin was tied to ground rather than driven, the segments were driven straight from Arduino outputs counting 0 to 9, and the display was left running continuously.[1297][ZtOXfFkV2jw] With no alternation the drive is no longer frequency-driven at all — the segments are simply switched on and off against a fixed reference.[ZtOXfFkV2jw]

Two side effects appeared immediately. Moving the common connection from ground to 5 V inverts the display, and disconnecting the common altogether leaves the display still working but slowly fading, with the image responding to a hand waved nearby — the charge sitting on the capacitive segments is enough to hold an image, and stray capacitance perturbs it.[ZtOXfFkV2jw] The same effect is visible on any exposed multimeter display: rubbing a finger along the LCD contacts will bring segments up, and they hold, because there is no static consumption at all and the segment is simply holding an electric field.[1670] The long-running experiment eventually produced an e-paper-like persistence effect for which no documentation could be found.[1297]

## Contrast, bias voltage and viewing angle

An LCD's contrast is strongly angle-dependent, and the drive bias voltage is what sets where the optimum lies. Adjusting the bias voltage moves the best viewing angle up or down, so a meter intended to be read lying flat on a bench is biased differently from one read straight on.[898] The specification is often given as a bias angle in degrees: the angle between the perpendicular plane of the display and the centre of the viewing cone.[898] Off-the-shelf displays are commonly ordered as 6 o'clock or 12 o'clock types, referring to the direction of the preferred viewing position.[1699]

Many designs fix the bias with a resistor rather than providing an adjustment, which locks in whatever angle the manufacturer settled on.[898] On character modules the contrast control is the VO pin, pin four of the standard connector.[1202] A typical monochrome module is readable only within roughly 30 degrees of straight on; go too far above and the image washes out and then inverts, and top-to-bottom the segments vanish or invert quickly.[624] For bench instruments this is a real design constraint, because the same meter may be read from above when it sits on the bench and from below when it sits on a shelf, so a wide symmetric angle matters more than an optimised one.[1371]

## Designing a custom LCD

A custom segment LCD is specified to the manufacturer with a drawing much like a set of Gerbers, carrying a display area — the glass area to be manufactured — and a physical area that allows for the pins, plus a note of which dimensions the manufacturer may modify.[1055] Specifying the artwork in that detail is optional; a simple product can be left to the manufacturer, who will then use their own seven-segment and character fonts, with whatever proportions they choose.[1055] Two reasons argue for doing the work: control over the font and segment shapes, and second sourcing, since manufacturers do not release their in-house manufacturing files, so without your own drawing the part cannot be moved to another house if the first one fails or goes out of business.[1055] Custom tooling is not necessarily expensive, but a delivered display can still come back wrong — one custom part exhibited faded digits and contrast problems that only partially improved with a higher drive voltage, and was replaced by the manufacturer at essentially no cost.[3ELZ2eIDk78]

The choice of display drives the mechanical design more than the reverse. Once the LCD is locked in, the physical form factor is locked with it; the display itself is not a huge cost to change, but it must be tooled, and the case, knobs, moulded buttons and rubber boot around it are the expensive custom parts.[e9cpKN69Avk] A display that is too large or too tall constrains where it can sit on a front panel and how the enclosure stacks.[400][258]

## Driving from a microcontroller

Two architectures are common. A microcontroller with an integrated LCD peripheral drives the commons and segments directly — the 121GW meter uses a PIC24F selected specifically because it had the required number of segment pins.[e9cpKN69Avk] Choosing such a part is not trivial: the pin allocation table must be cross-checked segment by segment against the other peripherals, because LCD segment pins and the LCD bias voltage pins compete with the ADC channels, and reconciling just two peripherals against a price target can take days.[248]

The alternative is an external LCD controller. A display with eight commons and 31 segments was driven by a Holtek HT1622 rather than by the microcontroller, because a microcontroller with enough pins cost more than the separate driver chip, and the external part also improved the layout — the driver can sit immediately next to the display pins so the fan-out routes cleanly, potentially allowing a two-layer board instead of four.[1561][1074] Complex displays with many segments and commons make that the obvious choice.[1561] Where the display needs a higher rail than the logic, a charge pump can supply it; a 5 V module running from a lower-voltage design was fed by a Dickson doubler, with a second doubler for the LED backlight.[473] In integrated multimeter chipsets the LCD drive is simply part of the single chip that also does range switching and the ADC.[853]

## Character modules

The Hitachi-compatible character module is the de facto standard low-cost display, most often as a 16 character by two line part.[710][203] Compatibility is good but not perfect: driver code that works with ten brands can still fail on an eleventh nominally compatible module because of a minor difference, though the great majority of parts on the market will work with the same code.[313] These modules are cheap in a compact 40 mm by 35 mm format around 8 mm thick, and can be had for a couple of dollars each in quantity.[313] A 53 by 20 mm 16-by-2 module is small enough to screw-mount on the front panel of a compact extruded enclosure.[644] Getting a hello-world message onto one from an Arduino board takes about a minute and around 2.3 K of a 30 K program space.[45]

Two behaviours of these modules matter when debugging. An uninitialised module powers up showing a solid row of blocks rather than characters, which distinguishes a display that is powered but never talked to from a display that is dead.[710] And the module's own character memory retains what was written to it, so text stays on screen after the host processor has crashed and reset — the display is not evidence that the host is still running.[710] A Bus Pirate provides LCD macros for exactly this kind of bring-up, including reset, init and a selection of display line count.[313]

Larger dot-matrix panels use the same principle at scale. The panel in a Tandy 102 is an electrode matrix with 64 common signals and 480 segment signals, split into common driver, segment driver and waveform generation, with HD44103 common driver chips and multiple driver ICs each responsible for a block of lines and columns.[1376] When such a display shows correct characters that wrap and overwrite after the eighth character, the column and line drivers are working and the fault is in the data being fed to them.[1376]

## Interconnect and mounting

The electrical connection between glass and board is where most of the mechanical risk lies. Elastomeric zebra strips must be held under compression, typically by screws through the board or bezel, which is why many meters cannot have the screen removed from the front.[Q_RYG_5cQk8][597] Other constructions bond a flex tail to the glass by hot bar.[808][1078] Chip-on-glass places the controller die directly on the display glass, driving the commons on one edge and the segments on another, with a flexible printed circuit taking the signals off.[1699] Chip-on-flex mounts the silicon flipped face-down directly on the membrane, which is how high-density panels achieve their driver pitch.[979]

Mounting options on the board side are more flexible than they first appear. A module can be right-angle mounted by soldering a pin header on both sides of a small carrier PCB, which is more rigid than a right-angle header and works well with a 1.6 mm board.[644] A DIP-pinned module can have its pins bent back and trimmed flush so the assembly is only as thick as the glass itself — useful when the display is the thickest component and therefore sets the minimum thickness of the whole product, as with a 2 mm thick 128x32 DOGM graphic module from Electronic Assembly.[130] A module can also be mounted behind the board through a cutout so it protrudes through the front.[203]

## Failure modes and repair

Missing rows or columns are the characteristic LCD fault, and the first suspicion should be mechanical. Where a fault is about equally likely to be mechanical or electrical, Dave Jones applies what he calls Dave's razor and investigates the mechanical cause first — for a display that means the zebra strips or hot bar attachment before the drivers.[808] The standard field repair is to warm the flex attachment and press it back down in the hope the thermal adhesive re-takes; it works, but alignment is extremely touchy, and getting a single missing column back can take ten attempts before the bezel goes back on.[1192][808] If pressure on the connections does not restore the missing rows, the fault is inside the glass and not repairable.[1192] Flat flex interfaces of this kind can also be heat sensitive, losing rows at random as the instrument warms.[1192]

Physical damage is unambiguous. Cracked glass leaves visible stress marks around the liquid crystals at the fracture, and there is no repairing the panel.[1758] Pressing on an unprotected screen visibly disturbs the liquid crystals and produces patterns; a well-protected display shows no segment disturbance when tapped, whereas a poorly protected one will show segments coming on.[1457][1447] A display with no separate protective window relies entirely on its own glass surface for protection, which is less rugged than it could be.[81] In drop testing the LCD glass shatters readily even where the case survives.[868][100]

The optical stack fails on its own timescale. Polarizer degradation — the vinegar syndrome familiar on old Apple laptop panels — attacks the film rather than the electronics, and is generally not worth repairing because the rot is on the LCD itself and not only on the removable film.[afwqRu7W8V0] Removing the reflective backing from a reflective display without replacing the polarizing film leaves only ghostly outlines of the segments, legible in principle but not in practice.[1425] Old panels can also simply bleed, the liquid crystal spreading visibly across the glass.[979]

Replacement is often impractical. On one meter the model designation is printed on the LCD itself, so swapping the module would reveal the wrong series name underneath.[1425] Sourcing a substitute character module is a qualification exercise in its own right — a cheaper part may use pin 3 as the LCD operating voltage and lack the serial interface of the original, and the time spent confirming a match is worth more than paying for the correct part outright.[1664] Displays soldered directly into the board cannot be removed at all without desoldering.[409]

Not every display fault is in the display. A camera screen that scrolls and appears upside down points at the ribbon connection rather than the panel.[1428][1429] A calculator that powers up with random segments lit or extinguished is showing the absence of a proper reset circuit as the supply drains slowly, which is why some designs give the on key a hard-reset function.[1093] And a display can be electrically fine while the bus feeding it is not: an I2C-driven character module was found dragging the SDA line into a third intermediate level with a runt pulse on the clock, a contention that persisted with a stock library and was not caused by the driver code.[240]

## Neighbouring technologies

The LCD displaced earlier approaches outright. Flat CRT modules were the only way to build a pocket monitor into the late 1980s and early 1990s, and were made obsolete once LCDs arrived.[1033] Early LCD televisions were poor enough that the panel had to be viewed via a mirror, with a clip-on electroluminescent backlight for extra brightness.[775] Later panels are backlit transmissive rather than reflective.[979]

Vacuum fluorescent displays remain in use and are close to a drop-in replacement for a standard character LCD, at orders of magnitude more current.[1137] OLED gives a brighter display at the cost of battery life, and an LCD version is preferable where run time matters.[832]

Two variants sit between the LCD and electronic paper. The Sharp memory LCD is an ultra low power dot matrix part that holds its image with power applied but no refreshing, so the host microcontroller does not have to stay awake at 32 kHz to keep the display alive as it must with a conventional LCD.[1242][1241] The Pebble Time uses an LPM012A220A from Japan Display, a low-temperature polysilicon panel with an individual memory per pixel — an LCD rather than e-ink, despite the appearance.[761] A more recent high-resolution segment part achieves a strongly e-paper-like look at roughly an order of magnitude lower power than the conventional module it replaced, while remaining an ordinary LCD.[1703]
