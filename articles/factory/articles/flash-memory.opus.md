# flash memory

Flash memory is non-volatile solid-state storage that can be electrically erased and reprogrammed in blocks, holding its contents with the power removed. It is the technology that made the modern microcontroller what it is, because it put reprogrammable program storage on the same die as the CPU.[1060] Outside the microcontroller it serves as the firmware store for almost every piece of digital equipment — the chip that holds the operating system, the boot image, the calibration constants and the FPGA bitstream — and as bulk mass storage in everything from tablets to spacecraft.[1761][1477][tWW56LAnT1Y]

Flash was developed in 1980 by Toshiba, but it was not until 1988 that Intel released its first NOR flash memory chip, and it took some years beyond that before the technology had displaced what came before.[937]

## What came before

Before cheap non-volatile memory existed, program and user data lived in volatile SRAM kept alive by a backup battery. Pocket organisers, PDAs and portable computers of the 1980s were built this way: everything was held in SRAM because there was no flash to hold programs and data in.[334][1209][1662][643] A 1986-vintage machine might rely on nothing more than a pair of axial electrolytic caps — a 100 µF 40 V part among them — to hold the charge on the battery-backed SRAM, with no lithium cell on the board at all.[334] The Apple Newton used five Mitsubishi 128k × 8 SRAMs for 640k total and stored nothing in flash, which is precisely why it needed battery backup; later versions of the Newton moved to flash.[418] Machines with a memory-protect switch made the dependency explicit — switching the backup off lost the contents outright.[1662]

For fixed program code the alternatives were mask ROM, UV-erasable EPROM and E²PROM. Mask ROM was the cheapest option because the die does not carry the expense of reprogrammable flash, and it remained attractive in high-volume consumer parts long after flash existed.[284][651] EPROM required the familiar clear window in the package and an ultraviolet lamp to erase, which is why in-circuit emulators substituted fast volatile RAM for the ROM rather than waiting on an erase-and-program cycle.[410] E²PROM allowed byte-level modification and enabled the early reprogrammable micros, but it never scaled to large capacities and was supplanted by flash within about five years.[818][d1D3d3YIc9A]

## Flash in microcontrollers

Microchip moved from EPROM to E²PROM — the PIC16C84 being an E² product that could be byte-programmed a single byte at a time — and then introduced flash-based products around 1995, where the part is bulk-erased and bulk-programmed.[d1D3d3YIc9A] The 16F84 was essentially the first flash-programmable microcontroller on the market, and it changed the expectation of what a microcontroller is.[1060] Other families followed with their own on-chip programming, removing the need to apply an external high-voltage pulse as the early 16C-series parts had required.[1060]

The three process technologies did not replace one another in the fab so much as accumulate: EPROM, E² and flash remained distinct technologies running in parallel on the same manufacturing floor, so mask sets for a 16C54, a 16C84 and a flash part could be started on successive days, or all on the same day.[d1D3d3YIc9A]

Flash capacity is now one of the primary selection axes for a microcontroller, alongside I/O count and package.[1180][87] Representative figures across the range: 2K on an MSP430F2013 with 128 bytes of RAM but a 16-bit sigma-delta ADC;[440] 8K on an ATmega8A[87] and on a low-end STM8S103;[359] 16K with 2K of SRAM on a 48 MHz RISC-V CH32V003;[1524] 32K on a Cortex-M0 STM32F070 running at 48 MHz;[ag-MjKAfATw] 64K on the 8051 inside a 5100 W laboratory supply;[814] 128K on an NXP LPC2364 ARM7 and on a TI CC2530F128 ZigBee controller;[571][417] 256K with 64K of SRAM on an ultra-low-power Cortex-M4;[1336] 1 MB on an STM32F103;[669] and 2 MB with 256K of RAM on the STM32F439.[761] Very small flash is not automatically disqualifying: C compiles perfectly well onto 16-bit PICs with only 1 or 2 kB of flash and around 300 bytes of SRAM given a good compiler.[63] At the bottom of the market, quoted headline prices such as 25 cents in volume typically buy a half-kilobyte flash part with no ADC.[96]

Where reprogrammability is not needed, one-time-programmable parts remain cheaper still: three-cent microcontrollers are OTP, with only one or two models in the family carrying flash at all, and development is done with an in-circuit emulator before committing the part.[1132][1306] The same economics applied historically — a one-time-programmable CMOS PIC16C61 was cheaper than the flash F84, so the F84 was used for software development and the C part shipped in production.[C1on-LaIsCA]

## Capacity as a design constraint

Flash size is a hard limit that regularly forces a redesign. On the µSupply, a 20-pin TSOP STM32 with 32K of flash was chosen on the assumption that 32K was ample for USB power delivery configuration, serial comms and HID.[1262] Both the Richtek and ST USB-PD libraries turned out to be enormous — the ST one on the order of 2 megabits — and neither would fit alongside everything else.[1262] Working with the vendor's design engineers eventually pruned the Richtek library down enough to fit, but the fallback was a larger micro, and because the largest part in that TSOP package was the 32K device, moving to 128K meant moving to an entirely different package with prototype boards already built.[1262] Estimating flash and SRAM requirements against candidate micros before committing is normal practice at the design-document stage.[1561]

Where code executes from also matters for power. Datasheet run-mode current is quoted separately for code executing from flash and code executing from RAM, and the flash figure is the one that applies to ordinary operation.[1539] Flash is also a natural DMA source: a bootloader can start a DMA transaction that moves an image from flash or external memory straight into program memory without running interrupts, which keeps the bootloader small, costs no CPU time and runs as fast as the part can manage.[eUA2TRX1NTc]

## Configuration memory for programmable logic

SRAM-based FPGAs are volatile — they have no flash in them the way a microcontroller does, so a JTAG-programmed design vanishes the moment power is removed.[635] An external configuration flash is therefore mandatory for an FPGA to be useful in a product: typically a 4 or 8 megabit serial (or parallel) flash holding the state of every configurable interconnect, logic-block and I/O-block bit.[496] Even a small, cheap FPGA needs a comparatively large configuration flash because of the sheer number of bits to be programmed.[496] On power-up the configuration logic loads the data from that flash and programs the fabric, which is why FPGAs are not instant-on and can take seconds to boot.[496] A 4 megabit flash sitting next to a Spartan-3E is exactly this configuration store.[976] Parts that boot instantly from internal configuration memory and need no external bitstream memory exist and remove the requirement.[800] CPLDs with built-in flash likewise need no external configuration PROM.[499]

## Identifying flash in equipment

In a teardown the flash is usually the chip holding firmware next to the applications processor, distinct from the DRAM or SDRAM working memory and, in an oscilloscope, from the sample memory.[1761][824][1293] Typical finds are an 8 megabit Atmel 25DF081 with a 1.65 V interface,[417] an 8 megabit EN39SL800 beside a TMS320 DSP,[271] a 64 megabit 3 V ST/Micron part,[444] a 64 megabit 3 V CMOS SPI flash rated at 80 MHz,[1071] a 128 megabit Spansion,[761] and Winbond SPI parts in low-cost gear.[511][afErPINq8qc] Bulk storage scales far higher: 8 GB of Micron flash in a tablet,[219] 3.2 GB reported by a network printer,[qDcIgWGpuGw] and a 4 gigabit eMMC carrying the application image and firmware in a modern scope.[1477]

Programmed flash devices are frequently marked by hand in production — a stamped code, an X, or a firmware-version sticker on the package — as a factory indication that the part has been loaded.[278][800][807][1229] Boot messages give the same information from the other side: a first-stage bootloader reporting QSPI boot mode identifies the flash it is booting from.[1563]

## Programming, erasing and updating

JTAG is not only a boundary-scan interface; on a modern microcontroller it is also the path used to program the built-in flash, talking to the on-chip flash controller.[499] Universal programmers handle flash alongside ROM, non-volatile RAM and old-style EEPROMs, and some offer automatic detection of 8- or 16-pin flash devices.[411] Programming time scales with size — a 256K flash part takes a noticeable while — so verification is normally folded into the same operation.[448][1071] The standard confidence test on a recovered or reflashed device is to program the entire part and read it all back.[1071]

Field firmware updates follow a download-verify-erase-program-verify sequence, and a single device may hold several separately updatable firmware images for different processors.[1151][1709] The erase and write phases are the point of maximum risk: interrupting an update in progress is how a working unit is lost.[1709]

Flash also serves as small-scale non-volatile configuration storage. Wi-Fi credentials entered once into an ESP8266 captive-portal setup are saved in flash and reused on every subsequent start-up, so the configuration page reappears only when those details fail.[yabl87SD-3M] The same modules carry 4 megabytes of flash and enough RAM to run the application outright, making an external micro unnecessary.[998]

## Failure modes and reliability

Corrupted flash presents as a device that powers up and gets partway through boot but never runs. Where a bootloader clearly executes and reaches a boot screen but the application program never loads, the fault is localised to the flash holding that application rather than to the supply rails or the processor itself.[CK5nbC_dBWk] The same reasoning applies to a calibrated instrument that passed factory test and later stopped working: the leading explanation is flash inside the internal processor that was corrupted or incorrectly reprogrammed after calibration, since the calibration itself still measures correct.[mkZFHEeQkQI] Marginal flash can be probed by thermal soaking — days at 60 °C is a harsh test, and a part that retains its contents through it is not marginal.[mkZFHEeQkQI]

Long-term reliability is a genuine concern as cell geometries shrink. Multi-level cell flash is reported to distinguish its levels on the order of 20 electrons while operating at around one volt, which places device reliability, not just software quality, among the fundamental limits on how long a product remains functional.[818]

## Consequences of non-removable flash

Because flash is the storage medium in modern portable and all-in-one machines, and because it is soldered down rather than socketed, its wear-out ends the life of the whole product. An all-in-one desktop made thin enough that neither the RAM nor the storage could be made upgradable becomes an aesthetically excellent piece of scrap the moment its flash memory fails, and designing for that outcome is treated as a design failure rather than an inevitability.[k4zrmTsZj_s][1407] The reprogrammability of flash is also not always exposed to the owner: instruments whose manufacturers will not disclose the chipset, or will not say whether the memory is reprogrammable at all, cannot receive user firmware updates regardless of what the silicon supports.[1731]

## Beyond conventional equipment

The capacity and robustness of solid-state storage make it the recording medium where no other option exists. New Horizons collected roughly 5,000 times as much data at Pluto as the first missions to Mars and Venus, buffered all of it in large on-board flash memories, and then spent 16 months trickling it back to Earth over a deliberately low-rate radio link.[tWW56LAnT1Y] The same pattern — FPGAs plus a large local flash — appears in instrumentation destined for places that cannot be serviced, such as optical sensor modules deployed in deep ice.[1gfM_9EdLSs]
