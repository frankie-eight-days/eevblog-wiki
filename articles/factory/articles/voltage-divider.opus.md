# voltage divider

A voltage divider is a series string of resistors across a voltage source, tapped at an intermediate node to produce a known, precise fraction of that source.[1399] It is the single most reused piece of theory in practical electronics: dividers appear in the feedback path of essentially every regulator, in the front end of every multimeter and oscilloscope probe, in comparator threshold networks, in biasing and level-shifting circuits, and embedded inside chips wherever a scaled copy of a signal is needed.[1399] Its formal name is the Kelvin divider, after Lord Kelvin, though almost everyone calls it a voltage divider.[210]

The concept is broader than the resistive case — capacitive and non-linear dividers exist too — but the resistive divider is the canonical form.[1399] Division is also unavoidable rather than optional: any real source with series resistance driving any load is already a divider, and the output is always less than the source voltage.[1399]

## The formula

The output across the lower resistor is R2 divided by (R1 + R2), multiplied by the input voltage.[1399] The labelling convention is not universal, so the identity of R1 and R2 must be checked before the formula is applied.[1399] A 10 V source across two 10 k resistors gives 5 V, which the formula confirms as 0.5 × 10 V.[1399] The relation generalises to an arbitrarily long string: the voltage across any single resistor, not just the bottom one, is that resistor over the total, times the input — useful where a circuit taps a mid-string node or takes a differential pair of taps.[1399]

The divider equation has a dual, the current divider equation, which apportions a total current between parallel branches; both belong alongside Ohm's law in the basic toolkit, and both fall out of nodal and mesh analysis.[820]

## Output impedance and loading

A divider is a poor voltage source. Reduced to its Thevenin equivalent, two 10 k resistors on a 10 V rail become a 5 V source behind 5 k of series resistance — behaviour identical to a 5 V battery with a 5 k resistor in series.[1399] That series resistance means the tap can only feed high-impedance loads. Bipolar op-amp inputs, which draw almost no input current, are untroubled by 5 k of source resistance.[1399]

The design tension is that dividers in regulator feedback paths are deliberately made high in value — hundreds of kilohms — so they do not waste power, and high resistance in turn restricts what they can drive.[1399] The working rule of thumb is that the load must exceed the divider impedance by an order of magnitude or more: against the 5 k example, a 50 k load produces roughly 10 % error, while a 500 k load produces about 1 %.[1399]

Loading error is the mechanism behind the most common bench surprise in precision measurement. A multimeter's nominal 10 M input impedance is itself the lower leg of a divider once it is connected. Placing a 10 M meter across a divider built from two 1 M resistors visibly shifts the reading, because the divider ratio has changed.[584][1399] The error becomes serious with source impedances in the tens of kilohms and upward — hundreds of kilohms, or megohms in a divider designed for very low power consumption.[584] A meter with an 11.1 M input reads about 4.78 V where the unloaded divider gives 5 V.[1399] Precision work therefore calls for a meter with a high-impedance (effectively infinite input) mode; on many instruments that mode is available only on the lower ranges, sometimes only on millivolts.[584] Even a nominally infinite input is finite, and at extreme source impedances that matters too.[584]

Analog moving-coil meters are far worse, and predictably so. At 20 kΩ/V on the 10 V range the meter presents 200 k; across a 100 k/100 k divider that 200 k parallels the lower leg to 66.666 k, and the divider formula predicts 0.4 V against an expected 4 V — an enormous error.[1067][1399] The same arithmetic runs the other way: high-sensitivity analog meters at 50 or 100 kΩ/V can outperform a 10 M digital meter on their highest voltage ranges.[1067]

Unintended dividers appear wherever series resistance is overlooked. A lossy coax cable with roughly 330 Ω of centre-conductor resistance, terminated into a 50 Ω scope input, divides the signal by about 7.5 and explains an inexplicably small measured amplitude.[594] A function generator's 50 Ω output resistance must be counted as part of any external divider hung on it, or the resulting voltages will be wrong.[1560] A degraded input jack that develops a high-resistance path forms a divider with a meter's 10 M input, producing readings that are low rather than absent — a failure mode more confusing than an open circuit.[1576] An inductor's DC winding resistance likewise forms a divider with the rest of the circuit once the magnetic field has saturated, so the settled current is set by that resistance rather than by an ideal short.[1406]

## Regulator and converter feedback

The traditional three-terminal linear regulator is a series pass transistor, an op amp, a voltage reference, and two feedback divider resistors — nothing more.[90] Fixed devices such as the 7805 have the divider integrated to set the output voltage; adjustable parts such as the LM317 bring the divider outside the package and expose a feedback pin, but the mechanism is identical.[90] The classic LM317 application places a divider on the output and returns the tap to the adjust pin, with the output voltage set by R2 in the lower leg; the same topology and the same formula recur on the 7805 and LT1117 families, along with a reverse-protection diode and an optional capacitor across the adjust pin to improve the ripple rejection ratio.[221][1438]

The divider values in that standard circuit are not arbitrary. Data sheet values are chosen low enough that the divider itself satisfies the regulator's minimum load requirement; a design that drives the adjust pin directly instead of through a divider loses that load and must add one deliberately for stability.[221] Discrete regulators follow the same pattern — a typical output divider feeding back into the regulator with an external pass transistor is standard practice.[804] Switching converters use the same arrangement, which is why a wrong part in the divider network, such as a 10 k resistor fitted where a 100 pF bypass cap for the upper divider resistor belonged, breaks a DC-DC converter outright.[366] A tap taken off the output filter capacitors of an unmarked SMD regulator is the identifying mark of an adjustable part rather than a fixed one.[1475] Boost converters with a fixed-output option let the designer delete the two external voltage-set resistors entirely and reduce the component count.[139] The general error-amplifier architecture is the same regardless: the amplifier compares the divider tap on the output against a reference — which may itself come from a DAC or a pot — and drives the pass element to hold them equal.[861]

Feedback dividers are equally fundamental in amplifiers. A non-inverting op-amp stage is precisely a divider in the feedback path, with the feedback resistor always larger than the lower resistor so that a smaller portion of the output is returned to the inverting input.[600] Discrete amplifier stages set their DC operating points the same way, with a 330 k into 6k2 network for AC and 330 k into 6k2 plus 100 k for DC, biased against a 15 V reference derived by a 47 k from the 120 V rail into 6k2.[354]

## Instrument front ends

Every multimeter voltage range is a divider. A 7.5-digit meter's high-voltage input divider is essentially two resistors, 9.9 M and 100 k, giving a large division ratio in one step.[731] Power meters rated to 600 V use a nominal 100:1 divider on the input, often with several taps for different ranges.[1693] Socket testers built on multimeter chipsets follow the same architecture — input divider, multiplexers, ADC.[1598] In practice the divider and the ADC front end together carry a great deal of range- and protection-related complexity.[373][1023][1608]

Because the divider defines the ratio, it is also the prime suspect when ranges misbehave. A meter reading out by a similar amount on both AC and DC volts across every voltage range, while the millivolt range — which bypasses the divider — reads within spec, points squarely at a drifted input divider.[1564] Where calibration allows individual ranges to be trimmed independently, a single out-of-spec range can be corrected without disturbing the others.[1189] A single well-behaved divider is also why calibrating a meter on one range usually leaves the other ranges acceptable.[775]

Oscilloscope front ends face the harder version of the problem. A ×10 probe is a voltage divider.[1399] Designing an attenuator front end whose divider gives good pulse response at both low and high frequencies is genuinely difficult, and the response can differ with the volts-per-division setting depending on where in the divider ladder the tap sits — a tap at the top of the ladder can behave better than one further down, since each tap brings its own resistive-divider compensation problem.[6XpyOGw6RFM]

High-voltage dividers are built as long series strings rather than as single parts. An insulation resistance meter runs its high-voltage divider as a chain of resistors separated by an isolation slot, stepping the voltage down along the board until the final tap is low enough to be carried back to the main board on an ordinary low-voltage connector.[468] An AC voltage standard uses a staggered 100:1 network so that each element sees a voltage within its own rating, which is better engineering practice than trusting a single resistor.[709] A defibrillator front end achieves a 101:1 division ratio with high-value dropper resistors handling ±700 V common mode, with no transformer isolation at all.[909] Precision laboratory Kelvin-Varley dividers extend the idea into decade strings, tapping successive decades; a seven-decade instrument reaches 0.1 ppm resolution.[210]

## Biasing, level shifting and virtual grounds

Single-supply analog circuits need a mid-rail reference, and a divider is the standard way to make it: a resistor to V+ and a resistor to ground, tapped at half rail, maximises headroom for an op-amp stage.[600] A signal riding on ground would clip, so it is shifted up onto a DC offset set by a resistor divider — half or two-thirds of the supply — with filtering on the tap to keep it clean.[713] Where the pseudo-ground must supply current, the divider tap is buffered with a voltage follower to make it low impedance.[72]

In battery-powered low-noise designs the divider approach beats the alternatives on cost and noise: a switching element is a bad idea in a microvolt-level design and adds bill-of-materials parts and capacitors, whereas splitting a 3 V battery with two series resistors of around 100 k draws negligible current and taps 1.5 V directly.[72] The op amp buffering such a splitter is itself selected for lowest possible power consumption.[72] The divider resistors that create a virtual reference can be a stability liability, though: bypassing the divider leg with a 0.1 µF ceramic is one route to killing oscillation in a circuit driving a capacitive load through a series resistor.[n7XX75S98e8]

## Thresholds, comparators and references

Comparator threshold networks are dividers. In a Schmitt trigger the upper threshold is R2 over R1 plus R2 times the maximum output swing — the voltage divider formula unchanged — so 1 k over a 10 k total gives 0.1 × Vout, or +1 V for a ±10 V comparator.[941] Referencing the divider to something other than ground shifts the trip points wholesale; the formula becomes messier but the mechanism is unchanged.[941] The same structure appears inside CMOS Schmitt triggers, where transistors rather than resistors do the dividing: as the input rises, N1 turns on and forms a divider with N3 between VCC and ground, biasing the source of N2 to a threshold level, with the mirror behaviour occurring in the P-channel devices on the way back down.[941]

The LM3914 bar-graph driver exposes its internal divider string directly: the upper and lower threshold pins sit across the divider resistors that set each comparator's threshold and hence each LED.[204] Interfacing it to a battery is a divider problem in both directions — an external divider halves the battery voltage so it falls inside the workable common-mode range of the internal comparators, and a further divider on R1 is needed if the reference span must be smaller than the native 1.25 V range.[204] The same reference-plus-divider pattern sets adjustable outputs on comparator-based charger circuits.[1464]

Divider networks are also the only practical route to non-standard voltage references. A bandgap cannot be built below about 1.2 V, so a 0.3 V reference must be divided down from a higher one and buffered with an op-amp follower.[400] The choice is between 0.1 % precision resistors and ordinary resistors trimmed with a pot; either way the resistor temperature coefficient dominates the result, and there is no way to do it other than a divider.[400] Commercial DMM check sources pair a nominal 0.05 % 10 V reference with a divider for the same reason.[735]

## Precision, trimming and failure

Divider accuracy is resistor accuracy. Standard 1 % resistors in a panel-meter input divider produced a 16 mV offset that had to be trimmed out with an added component.[102] Trim range is itself a design parameter: a divider of 100 k and 47 k with a trim across it can offer an absurdly large adjustment range, which is poor practice and should be limited.[853] Where an ADC feeds from a divider, the ratio is chosen to land the full-scale signal on the reference — a divider scaled so the ADC sees exactly one fifth of the output voltage, matching a 2.04 V reference and a ×5 gain elsewhere in the chain.[232] Scaling a 200 mV panel meter to read 0 to 2 V or 0 to 2 A means a divide-by-ten input divider, and rescaling the instrument to a finer range is done by adjusting the divider resistors.[102][9lIC3ZzIht4]

Dividers also fail. In a resistor-capacitor divider, a 220 nF capacitor that has degraded to 100 nF changes the division ratio and takes the circuit with it — a failure with no bulging or other visible sign.[1481] Divider values that do not reconcile with the measured tap voltage are a diagnostic signal in themselves: 47 k, 330 and 680 that cannot produce the 2.8 V present at a test point indicates something else in the circuit is off.[966]

## Configuration, monitoring and other roles

Dividers are used as passive identity straps. An oscilloscope reads a voltage divider at boot to determine which bandwidth model it is, and the corresponding selection resistors on the main board are the target when the model configuration is investigated — a pot substituted for a divider resistor avoids desoldering resistor values repeatedly during experiments.[1418][977][978] Instruments spanning a wide model range likewise differ in hardware, with different dividers and ADC ranges between an 8 V and a 600 V power supply variant.[814]

Rail monitoring is a divider per rail: a supply board scaling −12, +12, −5.2, 105, 80 and 120 V rails down for measurement uses one divider for each incoming voltage.[1314] Multi-channel measurement follows the same pattern — dividers ahead of a multiplexer feeding a single ADC, with the microcontroller selecting which cell voltage to measure.[397] Dividers scale current-sense and battery-tap voltages into ADC range in chargers and dummy loads,[397][102] set up a thermistor for temperature measurement in a lower-impedance arrangement,[811] and, with a parallel resistor added for the open-circuit case, let an ADC compute switch contact resistance around 0.9 Ω in an automated cycling jig.[u2-ot2vWLxI]

Beyond DC, a divider straight off the rectified mains feeds the multiplier input of a power factor correction controller so the loop can track the rectified waveform.[273] Three-phase energy monitors use MELF resistor pairs as the input divider, one pair per phase.[1511] Overload detection scales an input down before it reaches an op amp so that headroom above the positive supply is always preserved, with a 1 M and 270 k pair and a matching 100 k and 22 k elsewhere in the circuit.[471] Digital systems use dividers too — midpoint termination in fast DDR memory is built from them.[1399] Small dividers also drive discrete stages directly, as in an EPIRB where a divider drives an NPN transistor into a microcontroller pin.[368]

## Standing

Dividers occupy about one page in a typical textbook, which understates them: the formula is used in practically every aspect of electronics, unlike much of the theory an engineer learns and never touches again.[1399] Introductory hardware teaching aids group them with Kirchhoff's laws and series-parallel combinations as core material.[1695] The practical test is simple — using a ×10 oscilloscope probe correctly is not possible without understanding voltage division.[1399]
