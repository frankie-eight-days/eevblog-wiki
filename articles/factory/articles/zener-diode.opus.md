# zener diode

A zener diode is a diode manufactured and doped specifically to have a controlled, usable breakdown voltage in the reverse direction, so that reverse conduction becomes a feature rather than a destruction mechanism.[908] In the forward direction it behaves like any ordinary silicon diode, conducting above a knee of roughly 0.6 volts; in reverse it blocks until it reaches its zener voltage, Vz, and then conducts like a diode pointed the other way.[908] Because the voltage across it stays approximately constant once it is past that knee, the part serves as a cheap voltage reference, a shunt regulator, and a clamp, and it turns up in almost every category of equipment from mains-powered smoke alarms to 6½-digit multimeters.[1285][1189]

## Breakdown behaviour and terminology

An ordinary diode also breaks down eventually — a 1N4007 does so at 1,000 volts — but the breakdown of a regular diode is uncontrolled, drifts with temperature, and occurs at voltages of no practical use.[908] Zener diodes are made to break down cleanly anywhere from about 2 volts up to 30 volts or higher.[908] Assortment kits spanning 2 volts to 39 volts are a standard stocking item for a hobby bench.[954]

Two physically different mechanisms produce the effect. Below roughly 5 volts the true zener effect dominates; above it the mechanism is avalanche breakdown. A 2.5-volt part is working by the zener effect and a 12-volt part is definitely avalanching, with both potentially in play near the 5-volt crossover.[908] The distinction rarely matters in practice, and the whole family is called zener regardless of which mechanism is actually operating.[908] The breakdown voltage appears on data sheets as VR.[908]

The knee is not a right angle. It tapers, and the reverse characteristic slopes rather than sitting perfectly flat, because the device has an internal series resistance known as the dynamic resistance.[908] That resistance is not a fixed value: it varies with current and with temperature, including the part's own junction temperature, which matters because zeners are routinely run as power devices and heat themselves.[908] Leakage is likewise nonzero in both zeners and regular diodes, typically in the order of microamps to tens of microamps against operating currents in milliamps.[908]

## Shunt regulation and dropper sizing

The classic circuit is a series dropper resistor feeding the zener, with the load in parallel across it.[908] Sizing the resistor starts from the data sheet test current IZT, the nominal current at which the nominal voltage is specified.[908] For a 1N4733 at 5.1 volts from a 12-volt input with no load, the required 50 mA of test current through a 6.9-volt drop gives 138 ohms.[908] The dynamic resistance of about 5 ohms is more than an order of magnitude below that, so it hardly influences the result when there is no load.[908]

Adding a load changes the arithmetic, because the resistor must now carry the zener bias current plus the load current. A 50 mA load alongside 50 mA of bias means 100 mA through the resistor by Kirchhoff's current law, giving roughly 69 ohms.[908] This is where the topology gets awkward: the resistor is fixed once the board is built, so if the load current disappears — a microcontroller going to sleep — all 100 mA must flow through the zener instead.[908] The dissipation becomes 5.1 V × 100 mA, or half a watt. That fits inside a 1 watt part, but a half-watt zener might cook and a quarter-watt one would release the magic smoke.[908] With both supply voltage and load current varying, the calculation has to be redone for the worst case and the result is a compromise value.[908]

Regulation quality depends directly on how hard the zener is driven. With a 1K dropper and a 5.1 V zener, sweeping the input from below the knee up to 15 volts produces only about 100 mV of output change; dropping the dropper to 100 ohms produces more than 200 mV over the same sweep, with the output creeping upward as the junction heats.[908] The device does not regulate at all below its own zener voltage — the output simply follows the input down.[908]

There is also a hard lower limit on current. Driven from a current source, a 5.1 V zener holds up fine at 10 mA and 1 mA, begins to sag at 100 µA where it reads 9.45 volts on a 10-volt part, and is unusable at 10 µA and 1 µA.[908] Zeners cannot be operated at arbitrarily low currents, which makes them poor regulators for low-power circuits.[908] Shunt references generally want a known, controlled current through them, which is one standard application for a constant current source.[1688]

## Clamping and protection

The second major application is clamping. A series current-limiting resistor followed by a zener to ground will hold a node at the zener voltage even if the input spikes to tens of volts, protecting the downstream IC.[908] The response is fast and, critically, the clamp never overshoots — it approaches the clamp level asymptotically rather than ringing past it, which makes it well suited to protection duty.[908] A lower dropper resistance sharpens the clamping edge.[908] In the reverse polarity direction the same part conducts as an ordinary diode at about 0.6 volts, giving negative-going protection for free.[908]

Two zeners in series back to back clamp an AC waveform symmetrically: one operates in reverse breakdown while the other operates forward, so the clip level is the zener voltage plus roughly 0.6 volts on each side.[908] With a pair of 5.1 V parts and a 15 V peak-to-peak input, the clipping occurs a little under 6 volts, the extra coming from the forward drop of the partner device.[908]

A well-known variant replaces the zeners with two NPN transistors, collectors joined, bases joined, emitters on either side.[1157] A BJT can be modelled as two zeners: the base-emitter junction is a diode forward, but in reverse it breaks down at VEBO, typically 5 to 10 volts for most parts.[1157][n4NBUruLyoo][1000] The result is a bidirectional clamp of roughly 6 plus 0.6 volts in either direction.[n4NBUruLyoo] This configuration appears in multimeter input protection going back to the Fluke 45 and Fluke 77 Series 3, where a dual-transistor arrangement wired as back-to-back diodes uses the transistor breakdown as the zener.[853] It is still in current use — in the Brymen BM235 the low-impedance path runs 1K plus a roughly 1.5K PTC into such a clamp, so the 2.5K load only appears above the clamp threshold of about 7 volts.[1667][1655] Similar back-to-back zener clamps appear in the Owon XDS3202A and the Uni-T UT61E+, and pairs are sometimes put in parallel to share the impulse dissipation for CAT-rating compliance.[1004][1378][1157]

For high-voltage work the same idea scales. In a Nixie tube driver, a 22K source resistor from a 170-volt supply feeds anode pins that would otherwise present 120 volts to a transistor rated at 50 volts; a 48-volt zener clamps the pin to about 48.6 volts including the forward drop of the series diode, keeping the driver inside its rating.[950][952] A high-voltage zener on the common pin of a ULN2003 array achieves the same protection with a jellybean part.[950]

Other protection uses include a zener across a bench supply output so a 0–10 volt supply cannot deliver dangerous voltages under fault[233], a 12-volt zener with a 10K gate resistor forming the gate clamp of a P-channel MOSFET reverse-polarity circuit[1681], and a zener across a relay coil in place of or alongside a flyback diode, which switches the coil off faster than a plain diode.[1409] A TVS or transorb is functionally a zener applied to transients — an SA12AG is a 12-volt device that clamps overvoltage and provides reverse protection at once.[238]

## Noise and the shape of the knee

Zeners are noise sources, and the noise depends on how sharp the knee is. Hard-knee, low-impedance zeners are markedly noisier than soft-knee parts — a batch substituted inadvertently into a condenser microphone design turned out to be a "noisy, noisy, noisy brute", and about a hundred units were built before the noise testing traced the fault back to the zeners.[609] Because the hard-knee parts present a low impedance, no amount of capacitance across them will quieten them.[609] The engineering conclusions are to pick a fairly soft knee for zener regulation, and not to filter at the zener itself, which is a low-impedance point; instead add series impedance and form an RC low-pass further along.[609] For this reason a zener-derived supply can be quieter than a regulator in a low-noise front end, since regulators may themselves be noisier.[609]

## Precision references

Selected and aged zeners underpin the reference in precision instruments. In an AC voltage standard the reference is a specially aged, hand-selected zener measured at a particular current to 6.1517 volts, chosen for extremely low drift rather than absolute accuracy — the absolute tolerance could be ±5% and it would not matter, because the value is trimmed out and only stability counts.[709] The same reasoning applies across DC standards and multimeters: the Fluke 45 reference is a zener selected on a characterisation jig[791], the HP 3457A carries a reference zener on its own board[426], and the Keithley 2302 uses a buried zener reference.[1005]

What matters is temperature coefficient, and the tempco is itself current dependent. One reference diode is specified at 7.5 mA where its coefficient is best but still not zero at 0.005% per degree C; operating at any other current moves the part elsewhere on that curve.[210] A precision standard therefore drives the reference from a highly temperature-stable current source designed to be at least as good as the diode itself.[210] Temperature-compensated and buried reference zeners exist for exactly this reason.[210] Gas discharge tubes such as the OA2 filled the same role in high-voltage valve equipment, working on the same shunt-reference principle as zeners do at lower voltages.[XqakD0dXdjM]

## Zener regulation versus a regulator

Zener shunt regulation is wasteful by construction, since the dropper must carry the full worst-case current whether the load draws it or not. In a mains smoke alarm, the zener circuit drew 80 mA where a modern low-power alternative drew 80 µA — three orders of magnitude difference in quiescent current.[1285] The judgement is unambiguous: zener regulator circuits are wasteful, they always have been, and once linear regulators arrived the industry moved to them; zeners "went the way of the dodo in terms of doing regulation in the 70s, only for niche applications".[1285] Nor does a zener protect a voltage regulator against overvoltage without a series resistor to limit its current, and that is only tolerable in low-power circuits where the series resistance does not matter.[104]

What keeps the circuit alive is cost and certainty. A zener is cheaper than a linear regulator, particularly a high-voltage one, even at twenty cents in volume[1285], and it is practically guaranteed to work, where a complex chip carries its own traps — a genuine advantage when designing by inspection.[1285] Consequently it persists in cost-driven and mains-derived supplies: the Compaq Portable PSU regulates its 5-volt rail with nothing but a zener while using a proper linear regulator on the 12-volt rail[1364], a Yamaha receiver taps mains through a 2.2K resistor into a zener and half-wave rectifier to power its control chip[379], and a Sony boombox derives 6.9 volts from a 7.5-volt zener followed by an emitter follower.[1243] That emitter-follower topology — zener, resistor, and a pass transistor — is essentially what sits inside a jellybean three-terminal regulator anyway.[1438] The TL431 looks like a zener on a schematic but contains an op-amp and is a different class of device.[1688]

A capacitive dropper feeding a bridge rectifier and a zener is the simplest AC-derived regulated supply there is, giving 24 volts, 5 volts, or whatever is needed, and is dangerous only in that it is not isolated — acceptable inside a sealed product.[1482] The design trap is current budget: with 240 volts across about 14.5K the circuit passes only around 16 mA, while a 24-volt relay coil alone wants 50 mA, leaving nothing for the zener.[1482] A second, lower-voltage zener in the coil's return path can generate the 3.3 or 5 volt logic rail from the same current rather than dropping the 24 volts a second time.[1482]

## Practical notes

Zeners are not precise parts. A nominal 10-volt device used as a threshold element sits a smidge under 10 volts in circuit, depending on the resistor current and the characteristic curve.[1676] Component testers show the same effect: a 20-volt zener reads 18.9 volts and a 3.9-volt part reads slightly low, because the reading depends entirely on the tester's test current.[f_SdM6sXHD4][1020]

Handheld diode-mode testing only reaches so far. A multimeter with a 15-volt diode test range can confirm an 8.2-volt zener in circuit but cannot test a 36-volt part, which must be pulled and driven from an external supply.[1394][1395] A part that reads as a diode in one direction is at least not open.[1395]

Orientation is the common assembly error, since the mind treats the part as an ordinary diode when it must go in backwards to use its breakdown. The black band is the cathode, and in a threshold circuit the anode goes to the transistor base; installed the wrong way round the part simply works as a regular diode and the circuit does not function.[1676] Reversed zeners are a persistent enough bench mistake to be a standing joke.[1676][qRV1a-rnaSU]

Zeners fail short circuit when overloaded. In a flyback supply where the coil was effectively short circuited, the zener was snotted and measured about three ohms.[1423] Short-circuited zeners also appear as the identified fault in repaired radio equipment.[606]
