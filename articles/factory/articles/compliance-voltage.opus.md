# compliance voltage

Compliance voltage is the maximum voltage a current source can develop across its load while still delivering its programmed current.[908] It is the ceiling imposed by the voltage source that sits behind every constant current circuit: a constant current source is a voltage source with constant current circuitry wrapped around it, and it cannot exist without that voltage source.[1688] The term matters because it sets the hard boundary of a current source's usefulness — below the compliance limit the circuit forces its current and the voltage falls out of Ohm's law; at the limit the current source simply stops being a current source.[1688] The concept follows directly from the elementary theory of voltage and current sources and their Thevenin and Norton equivalents.[1397]

## Behaviour at the limit

A constant current circuit adjusts its output voltage to whatever the load demands in order to sustain the set current, following V = IR.[1688] Drive a low-value load and the output voltage winds down to match; short the output entirely and the current source is untroubled, delivering its full current at a few tens of millivolts.[1688] The failure is at the other end. A constant current circuit is not a voltage multiplier: with a 5 V compliance limit it cannot produce more than 5 V across the load no matter what the load asks for.[1688] Past that point the output voltage pins at 5 V and stays there whether the load is 7, 8 or 9 ohms, and the programmed current is no longer delivered.[1688] Into an open circuit the output sits at the compliance voltage with zero current flowing.[1688]

The limit is often discovered rather than designed for. A fuse endurance test drifted off its set current because the rising resistance of the heating fuse element pushed the required voltage past the 6 V compliance of the supply, which topped out at 6.17 V.[1377] A diode ramp test aborted at 5 V compliance and had to be repeated with the limit raised to 10 V before the curve could be traced to 1.5 A.[638]

## Bench supplies and source measure units

Any laboratory supply operating in current-limiting mode has a compliance voltage: it is simply the voltage setting, renamed for the mode it is in.[1688] Set a supply to 10 V and current-limit it, and 10 V is the compliance.[1688] The term is not normally used while the supply is in voltage mode, but it is the correct industry parlance once the supply is regulating current.[1688][1676] Dedicated current sources make the naming explicit — a Keithley 225 has a knob labelled voltage compliance, adjustable from 10 V to 100 V, making it effectively a 100 V supply with the current dialled in instead, restricted to a maximum of 10 mA.[1688] Source measure units go further: on a Keithley 2400 the compliance can be set to 150 V at 1 mA.[729]

Choosing the compliance setting is itself a measurement decision. When an SMU is used to sink a small current from a converter output for an efficiency measurement, the compliance is set just above the expected output voltage — 13 V for a nominally lower output — because too low a setting clamps and loads the circuit under test rather than measuring it.[957] Working the other way, the compliance reading reveals the load's own voltage: a bar graph tube driven at 1.05 mA from a 150 V compliance setting was found to be sustaining 105 V, its own sustaining voltage.[729] Charging a battery pack in constant current mode shows the same thing — with an 8.4 V maximum compliance the terminal voltage read 4.27 V and climbed as the pack recovered.[XpZVIWdXliY] A 500 mA constant current charge reading 28.98 V and rising was taken as evidence the pack was accepting charge.[1760]

Compliance voltage cannot substitute for current. A Zener diode refuses to operate at 1 µA regardless of whether it is fed from a 10 V or a 100 V compliance source; the limitation is the current, not the headroom above it.[908]

## LED strings

Series LED strings are the archetypal constant current load, and the string's total forward voltage sets the compliance a driver must have.[1688] Twenty white LEDs at a nominal 3 V each require around 60 V, and individual parts can run as high as 5 V.[pKV_JiauAE4] Because the string is driven at constant current, the driver's output voltage is not constant — it rises to whatever the string needs, up to the compliance limit.[GbADsyp9wM8]

The threshold behaviour is sharp. A string dialled up from a 9 V compliance setting passes no current at all, then at roughly 43 V current begins to flow and the LEDs light, with the output settling at 45 V, the string's total forward drop.[1688] A 1 V, 100 A supply lights nothing: without the minimum compliance voltage, no current flows through the diode at all.[1617] Recovered television backlight strips illustrate the range — one set of LEDs dropping 5.1 V each at 1 mA and 5.8 V at 100 mA allowed only 18 in series from a 100 V compliance source,[916] while another strip was run at 20 mA from a 48 V compliance supply.[1700] A half-amp string in a photography light box required about 36 V, measuring 35.7 V against a 40 V maximum setting.[1372] LEDs mounted in series around the perimeter of a light box likewise call for a relatively high compliance voltage.[6ZH2KeplSrs]

Commercial constant current LED drivers are specified by current and compliance together. Architectural fittings used 500 mA drivers rated to 47 V and 45 V compliance,[GbADsyp9wM8] a mains LED floodlight ran 4 A at a nominal 37 V, likely extending to around 40 V,[773] and a deck lighting installation of eight series LEDs used a 700 mA driver with roughly 40 V of compliance.[1343] Finding a replacement with the right current, compliance voltage, form factor and dimming capability proved difficult through local trade suppliers and had to be sourced from overseas.[GbADsyp9wM8]

Compliance also bounds dimming. Where a constant voltage strip is dimmed by lowering the rail, the usable window runs from the nominal rail down only as far as the string's compliance requirement — one strip drew current down to a measured 13.7 V minimum and extinguished around 15 to 16 V on the way down from 24 V.[1617] Below roughly 14 V there is nothing left to work with — "No more compliance left for you." — and any dimming scheme has to take that floor into account.[1617] A strip using a simple dropper resistor instead of active electronics behaved the same way, dropping out at about 15 V for six LEDs in series, over 2 V per LED.[1617]

The same relationship makes compliance a diagnostic. On a backlight driver suspected of failure, measuring zero across the output ceramic capacitors showed the boost stage was not generating the compliance voltage needed to drive the LED string.[1071] Non-linear loads make the point more generally: charge extracted from a ceramic capacitor during reflow could push 30 µA, but without the compliance voltage the LEDs would not light regardless of the available current.[1455] Plotting voltage alongside current would show both the compliance reached and the power actually delivered.[1455]

## Compliance in test instruments

The voltage a meter presents to the circuit under test is a compliance voltage, and its magnitude determines what the measurement disturbs. Some 1980s digital multimeters carried a low ohms function that reduced the open circuit voltage of the resistance ranges to under 0.6 V, below the silicon junction turn-on threshold, so that in-circuit resistance could be measured without switching on transistors or diodes in parallel with the part.[1723][1663] Implementations set the maximum compliance of the resistance range to under about half a volt.[1348] The function is rare on modern meters,[1663][p-eLu1z7-cs] and the accompanying habit — reversing the probes on an in-circuit resistance measurement to check whether the reading is genuine resistance or a semiconductor turning on — remains the practical substitute.[1663][1348] One modern meter advertising the feature was found still to exceed 0.6 V, and so not to be a true low ohms mode.[1723]

Diode test ranges run the opposite way: the compliance must exceed the forward drop of the part under test. A meter powered from two AAA cells managed 2.43 V, enough to light a white LED,[1574] while another delivered 2.1 V from three AA cells against 2.3 V from a two-cell competitor.[1731] A meter whose diode range tops out at 1.5 V cannot light a white LED or a red one.[1084] Meters with elevated diode compliance extend what can be characterised — a 15 V diode test compliance read 10.7 V on a part that a lower-voltage tester showed simply as open.[1452] An LCR meter's component test compliance was measured at 0.6 V.[1649]

Compliance is also a repair symptom. A meter fed 30 mA from a programmable source reading zero, with a compliance voltage present at its terminals, indicates a fault in the meter rather than the source,[1424] and a reading of 13.4 V on a current input is the compliance voltage of the driving source appearing because the instrument is on the wrong range.[2QZ8GWwzXXY]

## Other current-driven loads

Piezoelectric accelerometers require a constant current supply, typically a few milliamps to 10 mA, together with a compliance voltage in a specified window — one part calling for anywhere from 18 V to 30 V, set to 20 V in practice with the resulting bias landing mid-range once the sensor is connected.[1443] Industrial 4-20 mA loop sources specify the same parameter under the name loop voltage, commonly 12 V to 24 V.[f_SdM6sXHD4] Nixie tube drive raises the question of whether transistors must withstand the full 170 V compliance, or a nominal 200 V, or something lower.[950] A small auxiliary output rated 5 V at 20 mA for driving an external LED is likely a constant current output with 5 V of compliance, which is the sort of thing that ought to be marked on the product.[1741]

Compliance voltage also explains an otherwise puzzling measurement in fuse testing. A fuse carrying 400 mA drops only around 0.2 V of burden voltage, or 0.3 V measured two-wire including the lead drop.[1676] When the fuse blows, the voltage across it jumps to the compliance voltage of the constant current supply — 12 V in one such arrangement — and that jump is what a detector circuit triggers on.[1676] Even a deliberately low compliance setting is not always low enough: an LED matching exercise at 20 mA with the compliance reduced to 10 V still destroyed a part connected the wrong way round.[1427]
