# calibration

Calibration is the measurement of a device against a reference that is traceable to a primary standard or to a national measurements laboratory.[420] It is a measurement activity, not an adjustment activity: the widespread assumption that sending a multimeter away for calibration means someone opens the case and tweaks the trim pots is wrong.[420] What the calibration buys is a confidence level — a statement, backed by the calibrating laboratory's accumulated history of its own standards, that the instrument was within its published specification at the moment it was measured.[500][420] Adjustment, where it happens at all, is a separate step that follows from the measurement.

## Calibration versus verification versus adjustment

A calibration is the measurement of something against a traceable standard; a verification is that same measurement performed against a set of specifications, so that the device under test is judged pass or fail rather than merely characterised.[420] Adjustment — turning pots, entering new constants — is a third thing again, and an instrument can be calibrated without ever being adjusted. Where the check comes out inside specification, the correct outcome is to leave the instrument alone: a picoammeter found to be one least significant digit out against a calibrated current source was left untouched despite the availability of its adjustment procedure.[406]

The confidence a calibration provides is bounded in time. It is valid at two points only — the calibration just performed and the previous one — and it says nothing about the future until the next calibration is done.[420] This is why an instrument used as a reference must still be calibrated when it is retired from service: without that closing measurement, the period since the last calibration has no reference point and the data taken during it is unsupported.[420] Some organisations specify a deliberately short initial interval, six months or even three, to build historical drift data on a particular instrument before settling on a longer cycle.[420] From a manufacturer's service perspective a six-month recalibration cycle may be the technically defensible choice even though it is not what customers want to pay for.[420]

## Test uncertainty ratio

The reference used must be substantially better than the device being calibrated. The working rule is an order of magnitude — ten times better accuracy than the device under test.[1496][286] A 0.1% handheld meter therefore needs a 0.01% reference; comparing one Fluke 87 against another Fluke 87 achieves nothing.[286] A test uncertainty ratio of four is treated as the minimum figure in the calibration industry, with ten as the target.[286] At the metrology end the ratio collapses: at the top of the chain only a factor of a few may be available.[1496] Where a resistance calibrator's readings and a bench meter's readings differ by roughly 20 ppm, neither instrument can adjudicate the other, and resolving the disagreement requires taking the calibrator to a standards laboratory at least four times better than the unit itself.[544] A working ratio of 3.5 to 1 is short of ideal but can still be usable when the better reference is unaffordable.[210]

## Absolute accuracy does not matter; drift does

The dominant design principle across modern instrumentation is that initial absolute accuracy of a reference is nearly irrelevant, because it can be calibrated out, while drift cannot.[1382][440][709] A 5.5-digit bench meter using a reference with 0.02% initial accuracy and 7 ppm drift is a sound design on that basis.[1382] A power supply DAC reference specified at 0.25% is not an obstacle to 0.05% supply performance for the same reason — the error is removed in software, and the reference could be 5% absolute without changing the argument, provided it is stable with temperature.[440] The plus or minus 10 V rails inside a 7.5-digit multimeter need not be precise, because calibration values held in software compensate for them, and the internal voltage reference can be 5% out in absolute terms.[426] A precision AC voltage standard follows the same logic: the Zener itself may be 5% out of tolerance, the pots can be tweaked to taste, and what remains critical afterwards is drift.[709] A smart meter current transformer with a specified 4 to 5 degree phase error, precisely characterised at 4.73 degrees, is corrected either in software or in the low-pass filter, most likely the former, with each unit individually calibrated after assembly.[409]

This creates a genuine cost trade-off at design time: accuracy can be built into the resistors, or it can be calibrated in later, and the choice is largely about where the money is spent — on better parts, or on calibration labour.[1190] Per-unit calibration and test is real recurring cost, budgeted in the order of ten dollars per unit in one bill-of-materials breakdown of a bench arbitrary waveform generator.[1679]

## Adjustment hardware: pots versus closed-case calibration

Older and cheaper instruments are adjusted with trimmer pots. A 1980s portable oscilloscope carries around twenty visible adjustment pots plus more on the main board and trimmer caps for compensation.[208] A separate multimeter design uses a ten-turn trimmer for DC volts, another pot for AC volts, a further adjustment pot, and trimmer capacitors for frequency and capacitance adjustment.[1378] Some instruments make this deliberately convenient: one carries its calibration instruction sheet printed on a plate that doubles as internal shielding, with a numbered sequence to follow.[658] Trimmers set at the factory are commonly locked with lacquer, both to prevent vibration and to show the unit was calibrated.[940] Where trimmers sit behind the case, a manufacturer may trim the front end through access holes after the case is fitted.[790]

Closed-case calibration, where constants are written to non-volatile memory rather than set by hand, is the modern approach. It requires a processor and an EEPROM designed for the job; a bare-bones single-chipset multimeter has no facility to store calibration settings and must use external trim pots, which is a poor choice in a modern instrument.[853] A $25 meter holds its calibration and setup values in an external EEPROM alongside the chipset.[1007] A later multimeter revision removed all adjustment pots in favour of better references and divider resistors, with calibration presumably moved to a serial interface.[1378] Where calibration lives in firmware-adjacent memory it becomes a firmware compatibility problem: calibration data may sit in a protected memory region, yet changing chipset register settings to fix a functional issue can upset the calibration and force a recalibration of the meter or of particular ranges.[121] Product configuration should not be able to corrupt calibration and DC offset, and a design that permits it is a robustness failure.[1418]

Instruments that expose calibration to the user typically protect it. A bench multimeter requires a secure code before calibration can be performed and reports how many times its calibration string has been written.[489] Others hide the entry mechanism behind a rod reached with a screwdriver.[1012] Instruments with battery-backed calibration RAM are a liability; an EEPROM-based design avoids the pain of battery-backed calibration values entirely.[1012] A 1980s oscilloscope survived a repair with its calibration intact only because its Dallas non-volatile RAM was still alive.[1203]

## Field and user calibration

Many measurements require a user-performed compensation before the instrument means anything. Impedance measurement demands open, short and load compensation at the end of the actual test leads, because the leads and fixture contribute inductance that would otherwise be counted as part of the device under test.[1117] A vector network analyser is quantitatively meaningless without its calibration kit and the open-short-load compensation it enables.[1101] A handheld LCR meter performs open and short calibration to null out the test leads.[137] An LCR tweezer prompts for closed then open tips and saves the result, with a more advanced path that allows the calibration file itself to be edited over USB.[1335] A spectrum analyser's tracking generator is normalised by storing its own response as a reference trace, which is cheaper to implement in software than building a ruler-flat generator.[343]

Some sensors self-calibrate continuously. A wearable ESD monitor auto-zeros and auto-corrects all day to maintain reasonable calibration, which suffices because absolute accuracy to the volt is unnecessary and a few tens of volts is fine.[1567] A thermal imager runs a compensation and calibration cycle at intervals that are not time-based, apparently triggered by ambient thermal ramp.[670] An inertial sensor computes bias parameters during a boot period and requires no calibration afterwards.[1523] A soldering station using a drive scheme that tolerates arbitrary tip mass and type has no calibration mode at all.[1106]

Where a calibration reading is taken from a noisy output, filtering the value first is necessary — calibrating on a rippling output captures the ripple rather than the true level, which a Kalman filter on the reading avoids, provided the filter is disabled afterwards or the supply appears frozen.[1264]

## Temperature and stability

Calibration is only valid near the conditions under which it was performed. Specifications quoted as valid within plus or minus 5 degrees of the calibration temperature do not mean the instrument is spot-on inside that band and wrong outside it — things drift all the time.[835] A rapid 5 degree change is drastic in calibration terms, though a good transfer standard will still read close to spot-on through it.[374] Residual offsets drift observably in the least significant digits and can be nulled with a zero function, only to drift again with temperature.[1693] Thermal design exists partly in service of calibration stability: shielding, thermals and the avoidance of fan vibration coupling into the measurement are all part of keeping a calibration meaningful.[1624]

## Maintaining calibration without a laboratory

Formal calibration with a certificate is not always warranted, and a bench can be maintained on transfer standards instead.[374] The method is cross-referencing: with two or three multimeters measured against each other and against basic voltage and resistance standards at intervals, agreement over six months or a year gives strong confidence that nothing has drifted, since identical drift across several independent instruments is improbable.[374] A DC voltage standard paired with a 5.5-digit multimeter is more than capable of checking the calibration of good handheld meters because it is an order of magnitude better than they are.[374] Even bare-bones, a single quality meter bought new can be trusted to meet its factory specification and used as the house reference.[374] A 0.01% metal foil resistor used this way is not a genuine calibration but a transfer standard, and at 1 k that tolerance is 0.1 ohms — the least significant digit of the meter reading it.[216] A precision voltage and current generator good enough to calibrate any 4.5-digit meter can be had second-hand for a few hundred dollars.[576] Where a formal check is wanted for something outside the electrical domain, a NATA-certified laboratory does the work.[177]

Traceability at the top of the chain is institutional. A metrology standards laboratory holds the low-frequency standards, thermal voltage converters, DC cells and resistance standards; general-purpose gear is calibrated elsewhere in the facility, and the calibrators used there are themselves calibrated in the standards laboratory.[1041] Any large organisation with a serious measurement requirement runs a metrology department whose purpose is to calibrate meters and trace them over time; that accumulated history is what produces measurement confidence in a particular instrument, and it is distinct from raw accuracy.[ay9wFQAW19Y][6] A calibration house may also calibrate competitors' instruments, since it has to work on site, with limits — a timebase specified for 30-day stability cannot be calibrated against a system not designed for it.[424] Test laboratories in other domains apply the same discipline: SAR test equipment sent away annually for calibration is additionally subjected to a confidence check against a known reference signal before each test session, with results required to agree within 10%.[201]

## Certificates, stickers and second-hand equipment

Instruments ship with widely varying evidence of calibration. Some low-cost oscilloscopes include a certificate showing the instrument was calibrated against a named RF calibrator.[704] Certificates may list only the instruments used and the date without any measured values.[323] A decade resistance box's calibration certificate is an optional extra costing $74 and includes a full test report.[211] An LCR meter offers a standard certificate at $79 and an accredited one at $150.[229] Meters supplied with a calibration sticker and a full test report of every reading, under a national accreditation scheme, cost accordingly.[46]

A calibration sticker on used equipment is weak evidence and should be read for what it is. Ex-defence and ex-corporate gear is frequently scrapped simply because it has gone out of calibration or become obsolete rather than because it has failed.[559][655] A retest interval of five years on a laboratory instrument is not a plausible calibration cycle.[450] Against that, high-end bench meters are well built with good long-term drift characteristics, so a unit that powers up and is described as tested is a reasonable gamble on meeting its specification even without calibration.[1184]

## Calibration and repair

Repair intersects with calibration in both directions. Ordinary cleaning does not disturb it, because what determines a meter's calibration is the voltage reference, the ADC and the hybrid resistor divider network — none of which are affected by cleaning up battery leakage.[qmcSEJehqZM] Removing a shield from a bench multimeter likewise does not put it out of calibration.[485] But replacing the processor in a meter would require complete recalibration, which may not be possible on a home bench and can make an otherwise viable repair not worth attempting.[9s9LXOBknck] Unscrewing rigid coaxial connectors inside a 20 GHz spectrum analyser risks the calibration because at those frequencies even connector torque matters.[470] Recalibrating a repaired instrument is slow work: rechecking and recalibrating after a fault takes considerable time.[727] A repaired oscilloscope that powers up with an uncalibrated error forces a full calibration whether or not that was the intent.[1418][1448]

After any repair the appropriate first move is a spot check rather than a full calibration: comparing a repaired meter against a second meter and finding agreement within one least significant digit establishes the repair, with a fuller range-by-range check to follow.[JI4b-7vpIDc] Exhaustive calibration checking across every range is tedious, and a spot check on each range that comes in well within specification is usually sufficient to characterise a newly acquired instrument.[1012] Where a fault is suspected to be firmware corruption rather than analogue damage, confirming that the calibration is still exact is itself diagnostic evidence — an instrument that measures correctly went through its factory calibration cycle intact.[mkZFHEeQkQI]

## Production calibration

Calibration is a production step, not only a service one. Precision instruments carry factory test stickers recording each stage of production testing, with calibration and trim-pot adjustment as a distinct and important stage before high-voltage and burn-in testing.[281] Qualification builds are run through the calibration process specifically to gather statistical data across a significant number of units.[PcxEO3fA_Ls] A modern instrument board may receive little bare-board test, with the burden shifted onto thorough software-defined test and calibration after assembly.[470] Products distributed as part of a measurement network must each be individually calibrated so their readings are comparable, and units built by third parties would need to be flagged as uncalibrated to keep the aggregated data honest.[686] Consistency of the sensing element matters as much as the calibration itself in such networks.[686]

Calibrating an uncompensated sensor by hand is laborious and is a strong argument for spending more on a compensated part: an uncompensated pressure sensor required a circuit with six interacting control pots to trim offset, gain and temperature effects, and a test setup that cycled the transducers through thermal chambers.[686] Automated compensation and calibration is precisely the value that a purpose-built instrument adds over an equivalent passive component in a box, and it cannot be replicated by fitting resistors.[545]

## Judgment

Calibration is a science of measurement rather than an act of adjustment, and the myth of the grey-bearded technician tweaking trim pots is explicitly dispelled by the people who do the work.[420] The practical corollaries follow from the ratio and drift arguments rather than from ritual: buy or build a reference an order of magnitude better than what it must check, treat the reference's stability as the property that matters, and re-measure at intervals to build history. Where a design requires the user to calibrate before it will produce trustworthy readings, that is a cost imposed on the user and a legitimate mark against the product.[1056] Where a design eliminates the need — through better references, closed-case calibration, or a measurement scheme that is inherently insensitive — it has removed a recurring source of error and of labour.[1106][1378]
