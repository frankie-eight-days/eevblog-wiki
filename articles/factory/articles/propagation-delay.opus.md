# propagation delay

Propagation delay is the finite time a signal takes to travel from one point in a system to another: through the internal logic of a chip from input to output, or along a length of PCB trace or cable.[sr1DOHnJi8I][790] It is the parameter that ultimately sets the maximum clock frequency of a digital design, because every stage of gates a signal passes through adds its own delay and slows the whole machine down.[lJ3q9RHIatU] It also governs whether two signals that are supposed to arrive together actually do, which is why it dominates both high-speed PCB layout and multi-channel oscilloscope measurement.[1530][1735]

## The datasheet parameter

In logic datasheets the figure appears as TPD — T for time, P for propagation, D for delay — expressed in nanoseconds, and the datasheet states exactly which pins it applies to, such as from the A or B input to the Y output.[sr1DOHnJi8I] Treating the input edge as infinitely fast, TPD is how long the output takes to change in response.[sr1DOHnJi8I] More detailed parts break the figure down by transition direction: TPHL is the propagation delay for a high-to-low output transition, and such combinatorial parameters fold the output transition time in with the propagation time itself.[sr1DOHnJi8I]

The summary table at the front of a datasheet gives a typical operating voltage range and a typical TPD alongside the output drive capability, but it does not tell the full story; the real specifications further in, with their asterisks and footnotes, are where the usable numbers live.[sr1DOHnJi8I] A 74HC part might list 12 nanoseconds typical, and that figure varies with supply voltage, with temperature, and with other conditions.[1247] For worst-case system design the maximum column is what matters.[sr1DOHnJi8I] Designing to the typical figure produces a system that works at 25 °C on the bench and then develops weird, hard-to-diagnose errors once it is boxed up and running at 40 °C, or at 0 °C in winter — a failure whose root cause is unaccounted-for propagation delay.[sr1DOHnJi8I] Alongside TPD, logic datasheets carry the related timing specifications a design has to respect, including setup time and maximum input rise and fall time.[941]

Delay figures differ by logic family through process technology, though parts of the same family are broadly consistent between manufacturers.[941] The practical consequence is that a family swap is a legitimate speed fix: 74F fast TTL has a shorter propagation delay than 74HC, and boards otherwise built from 74LS jelly-bean logic sometimes carry one or two 74F parts dropped in exactly where a fast-propagation-delay problem needed solving.[1247][1053] Historically, a propagation delay of 12 nanoseconds was respectable for early resistor-transistor logic operating at a nominal 3.6 V.[867]

## Delay through a system of chips

In a discrete-logic machine — a TTL computer where the processor is built up from individual packages — every chip and every register contributes its own propagation delay, and those delays accumulate along whatever path the data takes.[1247] With a 25 nanosecond window to move data across, plus setup time at the destination, the budget disappears quickly.[1247] In a ripple counter the effect is structural rather than incidental: after a clock edge, the delay through the internal gates must elapse before the Q output changes and before the stage can be clocked again, and that Q output then ripples on into the next stage.[1208] Propagation delay in that context is a system-implementation concern, distinct from parameters like clock pulse width or input slew rate that constrain the part in isolation.[1208]

It is possible in principle to simulate a discrete design, work out the worst-case propagation delay, and calculate the clock frequency at which the architecture breaks.[1247] For a machine of a few dozen chips this is not worth doing.[1247] The practical method is to build it, run it, and wind the clock up until it stops working — a design that runs at 10 MHz but not 11 has answered the question empirically.[1247]

Inside programmable logic the same principle shapes how clocks are brought on chip. Routing a clock through general-purpose I/O and the logic fabric adds extra delay and consumes logic resources that the design could otherwise use; feeding it into a dedicated global clock pin distributes it efficiently across the device, giving lower propagation delays and higher achievable speeds.[193]

There is a corresponding argument for logic minimisation that has nothing to do with parts count. Every extra stage of gates adds propagation delay and lowers the maximum frequency, which is why simplification techniques remain worth knowing even when the target is an FPGA, an ASIC, or a processor rather than a board full of discrete gates.[lJ3q9RHIatU]

## Delay on the PCB

Copper imposes its own delay independent of the silicon. A working rule of thumb for signal propagation is about 15 centimetres per nanosecond, which is why trace length matters once speeds get high.[790] A PCB calculator gives the figure precisely: for a typical trace on the top layer of a board with a dielectric constant of 4.5, propagation delay works out to roughly 57.6 picoseconds per centimetre.[1247] The number is remarkably insensitive to the dielectric — dropping Er to 4 moves it only from about 57 to 54 picoseconds per centimetre, and even an extreme value of 3 does not shift it enormously.[1247] The engineering judgement that follows is that a design whose function hinges on that level of precision in the dielectric constant is either operating at the bleeding edge or is simply being done wrong; ordinary designs should carry enough margin that a rule of thumb suffices.[1247]

Critically, propagation delay along a trace does not change with frequency. Sweeping a calculator from 500 MHz down to 100 MHz leaves the delay untouched, because the delay belongs to the signal's transit, not to its rate.[1247] What does change it is how the signal is routed — which layer it runs on, and what reference plane sits beneath it.[1247] Even the glass weave of the dielectric matters: a trace running along the direction of one set of weave fibres sees a different effective dielectric constant than one crossing them, which shifts the propagation delay and the signal integrity with it.[1247]

The standard remedy where arrival time must be equal is length matching. Parallel data buses running to a sampling ASIC or FPGA are routed with serpentine sections so that all the output data reaches the destination and is latched at exactly the same instant; if the lengths differed, so would the propagation delays.[790] The same technique appears on scope front ends, where channel 1 is snaked around rather than taking the direct path so that its propagation delay matches channel 2 exactly.[1530]

Timing skew can also arise where a design changes signals on two different microcontroller ports in sequence, since the port writes do not happen simultaneously. Where the update rate is low — around 100 Hz for an LCD backplane — a fast microcontroller makes that delay negligible in practice.[1045]

## Delay in measurement

Every probe and cable adds propagation delay, and two probes on the same point will show a timing difference if their delays differ.[1733] Correcting for this is called probe skew correction, and it is a prerequisite for time-correlating channels in any serious multi-channel measurement.[1733] Scopes provide the adjustment directly: a per-probe propagation delay is entered — 5.3 nanoseconds in one instance — and many instruments perform the de-skew automatically.[1081] The compensation can be applied for a probe of different length, or used to cancel skew originating on the board under test instead.[1081]

The magnitude is easy to underestimate. Two ordinary cables can differ by around 10 nanoseconds, visible as roughly half a division at 20 nanoseconds per division, all of which calibrates out.[1735] The figures also predict well: taking a datasheet propagation delay of 5.05 nanoseconds per metre, a 45 mm length difference between two coaxial cables works out to about 227 picoseconds, against a measured correction of around 262 picoseconds.[652]

Delay is not always a parasitic. Glass delay lines exploit acoustic transit time as their function, with a nominal 64 microsecond delay measured on a scope at 63.83 microseconds using cursors against the trigger point.[386] Time of flight is likewise the measurement itself in pulsed LIDAR: a laser-encoded signal is fired at the ground and the propagation delay of the return gives the range, with encoding allowing several units to operate in the same arena without interfering — usable from about 20 centimetres out to 40 metres before other sensors take over.[766]
