# color temperature

Color temperature is the description of a light source's apparent whiteness expressed in kelvin, and for white LEDs it is set by the balance between the blue emitter's spike and the phosphor's re-emission across the rest of the visible spectrum.[361] It matters in two distinct ways: it determines whether a workspace is comfortable to work in, and it determines whether a camera set to a fixed white balance will record accurate colors.[1700][1252] It is also one of the most commonly misstated numbers on LED lighting products, which is why measuring it rather than trusting the label is treated as standard practice.[1343][p4cK-Cks0NQ]

## Physical origin in white LEDs

A white LED is a blue emitter with a phosphor over it. The blue die produces a sharp spike, and the phosphor converts part of that energy into longer wavelengths; the shape of the phosphor emission — how far it extends toward the red — combined with the size of the residual blue spike is what sets the resulting color temperature.[361] A cool LED shows a large blue spike with comparatively little phosphor contribution, while a warm one, around 3,000 K, shifts the balance the other way.[361] Spectra of nominal 6,000 K panels show this structure directly: a blue peak at 450 nm, a green peak at 532 nm, and a broad phosphor hump peaking around 600 nm in the yellow.[915]

Color temperature is independent of color rendering index, and the two are routinely quoted together on lighting products — a panel may be specified as 5K 90, meaning 5,000 K at CRI 90.[1303] Panels reaching quoted efficacies as high as 70 lm/W are generally doing so at an extreme 6,500 K with an overwhelming blue content.[361]

## Values in practice

For office lighting, task lighting, paperwork and electronics work, 4,000 K is widely regarded as close to ideal.[361] Domestic lighting sits lower, generally between 2,700 K and 3,500 K with a common preference near 3,000 K.[361] Studio and portrait photography and videography cluster higher, around 5,000 to 5,500 K, with 5,500 K being the most common rating for softboxes and similar studio fixtures.[764][p4cK-Cks0NQ] Adjustable desk fixtures commonly span the whole domestic-to-daylight range, 2,700 K to 6,500 K, by switching between banks of differently colored LEDs — orange and yellow emitters mixed in with white ones.[1286] Tri-color tube lamps work the same way, offering a switch that selects 3,000 K, 4,000 K, 5,000 K or 6,000 K by turning different color groups on and off.[QxMmEAb2JME]

Fluorescent tubes carry the same specification; a daylight tube is 5,000 K, and quad-phosphor lab tubes are available at 5,000 K.[Lrhee7hz53U][307] Older tubes with no color grading measured around 4,000 K, consistent with cool white 4,200 tubes that may also have drifted in color with age.[307] Industrial high-bay LED modules are sold at 5,700 K.[773] Architectural LED strip is commonly 4,000 K.[1617] Single-die white LEDs used in tube retrofits, such as the Philips Lumileds Luxeon 4014 low-power part, are specified at 4,000 K with 80 CRI.[533]

Not every application cares. A general-purpose everyday-carry flashlight has no meaningful color temperature requirement, unlike a video light where both color temperature and CRI matter.[1690]

## Matching a lab to a camera

The core discipline is that every light in a room is set to a single color temperature, and the camera's white balance is then fixed to that value rather than left on automatic.[1252][1451] A lab standardized at 4,000 K keeps its camera permanently at 4,000 K; when the lights were later moved to 4,200 K, the camera followed.[336][764][414] Standardizing on 5,000 K across ceiling panels, wall panels, softboxes and adjustable studio lights means the camera can be locked at 5,000 K and no white balance correction is needed in the edit.[1451][ddQF9U5CRNA][QxMmEAb2JME] Working this way, high-CRI 5,000 K studio LEDs are also reused in ancillary fixtures such as a magnifier lamp and a homemade photography light box so that everything stays matched.[RT3godBXkOg][6ZH2KeplSrs] The same fixed white balance is what allows a monitor under test to be judged as genuinely blue-tinted rather than mis-rendered by the camera.[1758][OdovWOP7ik4]

Where a fixture is replaced, matching drives the choice of replacement as much as brightness does. Existing 4,000 K tubes were deliberately paired with 4,000 K LED replacements to avoid mixing them with 5,000 K tubes elsewhere in the room.[531] A later upgrade went the other way, replacing 4,000 K battens with 5,000 K units specifically so the whole space would be color-matched, gaining output from 2,200 to 3,300 lumens per fixture in the process.[QxMmEAb2JME] When a 4,200 K panel was unavailable and only 4,000 or 5,000 K were offered, 5,000 K was chosen because it sits nearer the daylight photography range.[764]

Mismatch is the failure mode this discipline exists to prevent. Running a mixture of color temperatures in one room makes accurate color reproduction impossible and shows up on camera as a persistent cast.[1173][p4cK-Cks0NQ] Perceived brightness is also colored by color temperature, so lighting changes are not judged on lux alone.[362]

## Measurement and mislabelling

Advertised color temperature is frequently wrong. Panels sold as 5,000 K repeatedly measure otherwise, to the point that finding a genuine 5,000 K ceiling panel is difficult — suppliers overwhelmingly stock 4,000 K or 6,500 K daylight, and 5,000 K is something of an oddball value in that market.[1303][p4cK-Cks0NQ] Concrete cases: strip lights advertised at 5,500 K measured 6,400 K in one installation and 6,800 K in another; a 48 W panel specified at 5,500 K came in around 6,700 K; and nominal 5,000 K T8 tubes measured out of spec.[p4cK-Cks0NQ][1252][1343] By contrast, tubes claimed at 4,000 K have measured about 4,200 K, which is close enough to be usable when mixed with other lab lighting.[362][531] The engineering conclusion is blunt: LED lighting specifications should not be taken on trust.[1343]

Two measurement approaches are used. The cheaper one is a professional camcorder's manual white balance function against a proper white balance card, which returns the scene color temperature in kelvin directly.[p4cK-Cks0NQ][1206] The more capable one is a dedicated spectrometer such as the HP 320, which reports color temperature in kelvin alongside CRI, lux, chromaticity coordinates and the full spectrum with its peak wavelength.[1700] Without a specialized instrument, the actual color temperature and CRI of an unknown light source are simply unknown.[1700]

## LCD backlights

LCD backlights are a deliberate exception to normal color temperature practice. Because the panel's red, green and blue color filters subtract heavily from the backlight, the backlight itself is specified at an extremely high color temperature — in the region of 12,000 to 13,000 K — so that the filtered output lands on accurate screen colors, prioritizing color gamut over the raw spectrum.[1700] A backlight salvaged from a dumpster-found LCD television measured 12,569 K with a CRI of 76.5, which makes such assemblies unsuitable for repurposing as room or work lighting despite their high output.[1700]

## Related specifications

Color rendering index accompanies color temperature on lighting specifications and is a separate quantity: greater than 80 is common and unremarkable, while high-CRI studio LEDs reach 90 or even 98.[QxMmEAb2JME][1303][WW_-Clzs2Bo] For general lab use where video is not being shot, CRI can reasonably be ignored while color temperature is still checked.[1252] Unified Glare Rating is a third figure on panel datasheets, running from 0 to 40 with lower being better; a UGR below 19 marks a low-glare panel, which reduces reflections on products under test and eye fatigue.[1303] Illuminance is separate again — office standards call for something like 300 lux minimum at the bench, while over 1,000 lux is needed for usable video.[QxMmEAb2JME][1286]
