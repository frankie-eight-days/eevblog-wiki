# solar cell

A solar cell is a semiconductor junction device that converts incident light directly into electrical energy through the photoelectric effect: photons striking a PN junction generate a current, termed a photocurrent.[716] Electrically, a silicon solar cell behaves as a diode that produces voltage when photons hit it, yielding about 0.6 V per cell.[1426] Cells are wired together into photovoltaic panels and power everything from calculators and watches to multimeters and satellites.[1093][JWr5E2ej5cg][1608][519]

## Operating principle

The photoelectric effect requires photons of a specific energy and frequency to generate current flow through a PN junction; any semiconductor junction is light-sensitive, which is why the same phenomenon underlies camera sensors, infrared remote-control receivers, and solar cells.[716] As a circuit element, a solar cell is a DC voltage source in the same category as a battery, thermocouple, or Peltier device, and like any practical source it must be modeled with an internal series resistance rather than as an ideal source.[1009][1397] Solar cells have a characteristic power-versus-voltage (PV) curve, and extracting maximum power requires operating at the maximum power point rather than at open circuit or short circuit.[1383][1276]

## Panel construction and shading

A typical residential panel contains 60 individual cells in a 6 by 10 arrangement, all wired in series, so that 60 cells at roughly 0.6 V each produce about 36 to 40 V at the panel terminals.[1426] Because the full string current must flow through every cell, shading even one cell is disproportionately destructive: the shaded cell is still forced to pass the string current, and panels incorporate bypass arrangements to route around disabled sections.[1426] A small, fuzzy shadow covering only part of one cell — for example from a guy wire or antenna mast — is sufficient to cut the output of an entire multi-panel string by 10 to 20%.[1426][NoIjTK249D0] Manufacturing trends over successive panel generations include physically larger cells, smaller gaps between cells, and wider silver bus strips.[1385]

## Irradiance, efficiency, and losses

Solar irradiance at the Earth's surface is about 1,000 W per square metre in the best case, while outside the atmosphere it is about 1,357 W per square metre.[896][1591] A photovoltaic cell converting 25% of incident energy is doing very well.[1591] Reflection is a controllable loss: bare silicon reflects over 30% of incident light, modern processed cells without an anti-reflective coating reflect only 4 to 8%, and with the coating reflection falls to one or two percent; anti-reflective coatings are worth on the order of a couple of percent up to perhaps 5% efficiency.[1575] Surface texturing and oxide coatings also alter the reflection figure across the spectrum.[1575] The dominant loss mechanism, however, is thermalization: sunlight that is absorbed but not converted to electricity simply heats the cell, and most of the losses in a panel appear as heat rather than reflection.[1575] Output also follows a cosine law with angle of incidence, so a cell can be many degrees off-axis before output falls materially — only a seriously off-angle orientation drops collection to 50% — and precise aiming matters only when chasing fractions of a percent.[362]

A practical figure for real-world energy yield, derived from measured data and data sheet values for a top-end cell and assuming 40% losses including glass, is 380 watt-hours per square metre per day as a best case.[681]

## Low-light and indoor operation

Cell output scales roughly with illuminance, and indoor light is about three orders of magnitude weaker than full sun: a cell delivering 10 to 12 mA in full sun delivers only about 10 to 30 µA under typical bench lighting.[48] This is why solar-powered calculators are possible at all — they pair the cell with extremely low-power custom ASICs operating at only tens of kilohertz, and the cells themselves generate virtually no current.[48] Measured behaviour bears this out: the Casio FX-260 Solar II begins operating at about 20 lux (and not at 10 lux), and a PowerFilm flexible indoor cell delivers about 2.3 V but only 60–70 µA at around 70 lux, which is adequate only for ultra-low-power energy harvesting.[1093][1094][S-UqkM8iDKM] Amorphous cells are specifically designed for indoor use at illuminances of a couple of hundred lux.[1276] Short-circuit current is a convenient relative measurement for comparing cell output across lighting conditions without locating the maximum power point.[48][1276]

Supporting circuitry in solar-only devices is minimal but deliberate. A reservoir capacitor on the power rail determines how long the device runs after light is removed — in one calculator, enlarging it to 220 µF extended operation to about two minutes but added a one- to two-second power-on delay — and a diode may be used to clamp the cell's output voltage to a safe level for the load IC.[1094][962]

## Cell types and manufacture

Cells encountered in practice include monocrystalline parts (such as surface-mount IXYS devices rated 1.5 V at 12 mA in full sun), amorphous cells for indoor use, extremely thin fragile cells that shatter into sharp shards if mishandled, and printed flexible cells.[48][1276][673][991] Conventional wafer production saws thin slices off a thick silicon boule, turning much of the material to dust; alternative processes using particle accelerators to produce very thin wafers have been proposed to avoid that waste.[IbYtn420QBM][1025] For space applications, triple-junction cells are used, and a modest array can produce about 93 W in the space environment.[1711][896]

## Applications

Solar cells power credit-card and scientific calculators, watches in which the dial face itself is the cell, and pocket multimeters combining an amorphous cell with a lithium manganese dioxide rechargeable battery.[48][1093][JWr5E2ej5cg][1608] In satellites, cells feed a power supply module with charge management, current-consumption measurement, and battery-state monitoring.[519] Multi-source energy-harvesting evaluation boards combine a solar cell input with thermoelectric and piezoelectric generators.[534][945] Betavoltaic devices use a solar cell to convert the phosphor glow of a tritium source into a few microwatts of electricity.[1595] Cells can also be optimized as receivers for near-infrared laser wireless power, where the cell must be designed for the specific laser bandwidth rather than the full solar spectrum.[1092]

## Limits and engineering judgment

A number of measurements establish hard practical limits. Covering a cell with a textured glass road surface cuts short-circuit current by roughly half.[1276] Vehicle-integrated photovoltaics are supplemental only: a car offers at most about five square metres of area, and even a two- or three-fold improvement in cell efficiency cannot close the gap to self-sufficiency — solar-powered cars are not viable as primary-energy vehicles.[1480][QkFioBbH5aM] Night-time generation by coupling a thermoelectric generator to a solar cell's radiative cooling yields about 50 mW per square metre, roughly 0.04% of the cell's daytime output — energy-harvesting territory, not useful power.[1467] More broadly, attempts to power ordinary microcontroller-class loads directly from small cells fail on current budget: even covering half a product's surface with high-efficiency cells cannot supply a dot-matrix LCD drawing 100–200 µA indoors, which is why such designs fall back to charging a supercapacitor or battery rather than direct operation.[97][1741] Deployments that place cells in inherently poor locations — flat, under thick glass, exposed to grime and abrasion — remain inferior to conventional rooftop mounting regardless of future improvements in cell cost or efficiency, because the unfavourable geometry and losses do not change.[681][1276]
