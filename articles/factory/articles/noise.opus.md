# noise

Noise is the random fluctuation present on every real signal, and it is a practical reality of real-world circuits rather than a defect that better construction can remove.[941] It sets the floor beneath which a measurement carries no information, it decides how far a signal chain can usefully be amplified, and it turns clean logic decisions into chattering ones.[572][941] Because it is unavoidable, most of the engineering around noise is a matter of budgeting it: choosing where in a chain it enters, how much bandwidth it is allowed to occupy, and how much of the design's headroom is spent suppressing it.

## Noise in amplifier chains

In a cascade of amplifier stages, the noise contribution of the first stage dominates, so the front end should carry the maximum gain when noise matters.[572] The reason is that noise sources add as the root sum of the squares rather than arithmetically. With 1 microvolt of RMS noise at the input of a times-ten stage, 10 microvolts appears at its output; adding a second times-ten stage that itself contributes 1 microvolt does not yield 11 microvolts referred to its input, and the total is only marginally worse than a single times-1000 stage would have given.[572] Cascading op amps to gain bandwidth therefore worsens noise only trivially.[572]

Composite amplifiers exploit the same asymmetry deliberately. By trading gain between the two stages, the total noise of the composite can be brought below the worst-case 100 nV per root Hz of one of its members, because sufficient gain in the first amplifier suppresses the referred contribution of the second.[1609] The same technique lets input impedance, offset, bandwidth, output drive and temperature drift be combined from different devices, at the cost of stability problems if implemented carelessly.[1609]

Noise also limits how far a sense resistor can be shrunk. Lowering a current-sense resistor to reduce voltage drop is legitimate and can be compensated by raising circuit gain, but the value cannot be made arbitrarily low, because the signal eventually descends into the noise along with the input offset voltage.[221][232] Where a residual noise problem remains after the sense amplifier, a simple RC low-pass filter ahead of the following stage is the ordinary remedy.[232]

## Noise and switching thresholds

A comparator's input threshold is effectively infinitesimal: the input need only exceed the reference by some indeterminate but vanishingly small amount — a microvolt, a nanovolt — and the output switches.[941] Since the input signal always carries noise, a slowly transitioning waveform crosses the reference several times within the noise band, and the output oscillates through the transition.[941] Along with insufficient input slew rate, this is one of the two classic failure modes of comparator circuits.[941]

The remedy is hysteresis. With a Schmitt trigger, a transition through the lower threshold does not flip the output back until the input reaches the upper threshold again, no matter how many times noise crosses the lower threshold in between; the difference between the two thresholds is the margin that eliminates the problem and yields clean switching.[941] The same effect appears in analogue control loops, where an indicator driven near a decision point drops out at the low end of its adjustment range because noise is straddling the threshold.[224]

## Measuring noise

Noise is not a repetitive waveform, so oscilloscope measurements defined per cycle — cycle RMS, cycle standard deviation — return nothing useful. Plain standard deviation over the acquisition is the correct statistic for comparing the noise floors of two instruments.[1223] Front-end design dominates the result: an oscilloscope whose input path is built for 1.5 GHz is inherently slightly noisier than one designed to roughly half that bandwidth, even when both are band-limited to 20 MHz for the measurement, though the difference between two such instruments may amount to only a few microvolts.[1529]

Digital oscilloscopes have a reputation for being noisier than analogue ones, which is not necessarily the case: their update rate and capture depth let them display more of the noise that is genuinely present.[442][601] An analogue instrument shown the same signal with 30% added noise displays what looks like a clean sine wave, because the noise is uncorrelated with the sweep and appears only briefly on the phosphor; photographing the screen at a raised shutter speed reveals the dim phosphor the eye cannot see and the noise superimposed on the waveform.[601] Intensity-graded digital displays make the same distinction directly — turning the intensity down removes the uncorrelated noise and leaves only the persistent waveform.[601] The practical caution runs the other way as well: an analogue instrument can hide common-mode noise that a digital one would show plainly, so claims about the relative noise of the two architectures should be treated carefully.[442]

Averaging modes attack noise differently. Traditional mathematical averaging and high-resolution or boxcar averaging can differ from almost nothing to a difference large enough to change the measured accuracy or the apparent shape of the waveform, and the distinction is subtle enough to be widely misunderstood.[878] For logged measurement data rather than waveforms, noise showing up as large spurious spikes is a reason to prefer the median over the mean, since the median discards outliers that would otherwise drag the average.[1658] Multimeter trend charts and histograms make the residual fluctuation of the least significant digits visible even when the displayed reading appears static.[489]

Instruments frequently provide noise as a stimulus as well. Function generators offer noise as a waveform type, sometimes without any control over its character,[323] and arbitrary-waveform modes may allow anywhere from no added noise up to 100% noise superimposed on the arbitrary waveform.[209] Where added noise is specified in volts, whether the figure is peak-to-peak or RMS is not always stated on the instrument and must be checked in the units setting.[1552]

## Coupling, shielding and the environment

Noise usually arrives by a path, and identifying the path is more productive than treating the symptom. A cathode ray tube radiates its scan rates into nearby instruments; a probe held near one picks up the 56.1 Hz vertical rate and the 25 kHz horizontal rate plainly, and in the high-precision environment such instruments are designed for, that kind of pickup can ruin a measurement. A grounded metal screen bonded to the case removes it.[524]

Supply rails are the other common route. In an electret microphone, the internal JFET operating in its constant-current region presents a very high impedance, so any noise on the bias rail is passed straight through — a real hazard when the bias comes from a PC.[611] In an op amp biased to an artificial mid-rail, any noise on the virtual ground reference is amplified and appears on the output, which is why a large bypass capacitor across that node is standard practice.[600] Removing an input bypass capacitor from a precision current source raises the measured noise on its sense output immediately, even though the input voltage itself makes no difference to the output.[567] Not every mechanism couples into the signal path, however: noise injected into a ground reference may leave the measurement largely undisturbed, since the reference remains the reference and the op amp's power supply rejection handles the rest.[1328]

Thermal effects can masquerade as electrical noise at high resolution. During development of a precision instrument, engineers laid a handkerchief over the circuit board under test because subtle differences in air temperature passing over certain parts moved them by a tenth of a degree and showed up as noise in the DC measurement, with the fluttering of the air over the parts visible in the readings.[1032] For similar reasons, a low-noise measurement of a fixed resistor would ideally be performed on an instrument in a thermal chamber.[835] Slow wander in an overnight logging run may likewise be temperature drift rather than noise, which is why recording temperature alongside the measurement is necessary to establish correlation.[489]

Bandwidth is itself a noise mechanism. Broadband low-noise amplifiers of the HEMT type used in deep-space receiving are robust and need no tuning, but their very wide bandwidth brings noise covering essentially the entire spectrum, which is a problem when the target is a narrow part of that spectrum.[1547]

## Power supplies

In a linear supply driven by a filtered PWM set point, inadequate filtering lets the ripple and noise on the control node propagate directly to the output; the effect is dramatic enough that removing output capacitance sends a 100 mV disturbance straight through to the rail.[225] Adding a second RC section — 10K and 100 nF — reduces the ripple very significantly even with practically no filtering elsewhere, and restoring the output capacitance removes it almost entirely.[225] Residual spikes that remain after this are typically ground bounce and probing artefacts rather than supply noise.[225] Output capacitor value matters directly to the visible noise: with 22 µF the response is clean, with 100 nF it degrades badly, and with no capacitor at all it is unusable.[224]

Ripple attenuation by an RC filter in the base of a pass transistor is a straightforward trade-off. Raising the filter resistor from 1K to 10K starves the transistor of base current and increases the drop across the pass element, but improves the RC ratio enough that the ripple essentially disappears into the broadband noise floor.[1116] Noise is also the characteristic weak point of inexpensive supply modules whose other performance and flexibility are otherwise good.[1265]

## Noise as a selection criterion

Datasheet noise is one of the small set of parameters that decide op amp selection, alongside offset voltage, supply voltage, gain-bandwidth product, supply current, bias current, rail-to-rail operation, recovery times and slew rate.[1325] It is regularly the parameter that redeems a device weak elsewhere: a chip with four times worse typical input offset voltage need not be disqualified if it wins on bandwidth, noise or price.[1325] Chopper and zero-drift architectures are advertised on ultra-low broadband noise and zero flicker noise, the latter arising because the chopping action moves the amplifier's operation above the region where flicker noise dominates.[1325] Replacing the MAX4239 in a low-current front end with the OPA189 gives significantly lower noise and higher bandwidth for essentially no change in power, and is close to a drop-in substitution.[1328]

The criterion is not universal. The reason to specify a jellybean part such as the LM358 is availability, longevity and second sourcing; the design by definition does not need high bandwidth, low offset or low noise, or it would not be using a jellybean part.[1697] A common linear regulator is chosen the same way, its noise and power supply rejection being more than adequate rather than exceptional.[1438]

Where noise performance is claimed as a differentiator, instrument vendors do publish it: one precision multimeter is marketed on having lower noise and injected current than three unnamed competitors, one of which is described as too noisy to measure.[485] At the opposite extreme, an instrument that shows substantial noise on a channel with nothing connected to it has a defect, not a specification.[1127] A specified figure gives the scale involved — a 4.5-digit microvolt-class meter with a 40 ppm typical reference and 5 pA input current specifies 2 microvolts peak-to-peak noise over its 200 mV range, with accuracy guaranteed to ±1 count over the full 20,000 count span.[777]

## Working at the noise floor

Several measurement techniques fail simply because they run out of signal. Determining an oscilloscope ADC's true full-scale range by feeding in a signal small enough to produce a one-bit change is possible in principle but lands the measurement down in the noise, where it becomes impractical; reading the manufacturer's figure or reverse-engineering the schematic is more reliable.[6qjqhnQiQXQ] Impedance analysis near a deep resonance suffers the same way — the signal level drops so far that noise dominates the trace, and the fix is to raise the excitation magnitude or narrow the receiver bandwidth.[1103] In vibration work, a coherence measurement between drive and response is the diagnostic that separates genuine system behaviour from noise and vibrational modes; without establishing coherence first, quantitative results cannot be trusted.[1443]

Noise appears in adjacent disciplines by the same mechanism. Opening the aperture on a high-speed camera to gain depth of field forces the gain up and the image becomes visibly noisy, which is why high-speed work demands very large amounts of light.[947] Current probing is valued precisely because it makes peak currents and noise issues on a product under development directly visible.[1413]

Diagnostically, a noise problem is at least a problem that offers feedback: an oscillating op amp gives something to track down, unlike a project that simply fails to start.[500] Noise that resists explanation is common enough — intermittent content on a captured trace that cannot be reproduced,[2FlQnPsZCdE] or periodic-looking activity on a control line that may be a genuine packet structure or may be pickup.[1360] Careful designs in noise-critical products show the attention in the grounding and layout rather than in any single component.[1090]
