# logic analyzer

A logic analyzer is a multi-channel digital acquisition instrument that samples many signals at once and displays each as a stream of ones and zeros. Its defining advantage over an oscilloscope is channel count: a scope gives two or four analog channels, while a logic analyzer gives eight, sixteen, thirty-two or more.[44] Its defining limitation is that it discards everything about the waveform except which side of a threshold it fell on, so what appears on screen is an interpretation of the circuit rather than a picture of it.[44][MC9_4mpqVgU]

Most designs do not need one. Deep-memory oscilloscopes have absorbed much of the traditional workload — SPI, I²C and RS-232 debugging are routinely done on a scope with a serial decoder — and for most developers a logic analyzer is not a necessary purchase.[44] Reaching for one generally signals that a design is already ninety-nine percent working and the remaining fault is a subtle timing problem.[44] It is the right tool for protocol work; a broken, shorted or floating line is a job for a scope probe instead.[689]

## Timing analysis and state analysis

There are two modes of operation, and the distinction determines what an instrument can and cannot do.[44] Timing analysis runs from an internal clock, taking fast samples and showing inputs changing against time much like an oscilloscope.[44] State analysis samples on an external clock supplied by the circuit under test, so acquisition is synchronised with the target and every captured sample corresponds to a real bus cycle.[475][MC9_4mpqVgU]

State analysis therefore requires an external clock input. An instrument without one — such as the GW Instek GDS-2000A logic analyzer pods, which carry no external clock connection — is timing-analysis-only regardless of what else it offers.[475] The same restriction applies to essentially all mixed-signal oscilloscopes: they capture digital channels on the scope's own timebase and cannot be clocked from the target.[MC9_4mpqVgU] Low-cost USB instruments are frequently in the same position; lacking an input latch, the Saleae Logic is effectively timing analysis only.[436] State analysis has become less common in ordinary work, but it remains a genuine capability line between a real logic analyzer and a digital option bolted onto a scope.[MC9_4mpqVgU]

Timing analysis carries its own artefact: because sampling is asynchronous to the signal, edges land wherever the sample clock happens to fall, producing apparent jitter.[UDGsZcAWgL8] Undersampling is worse. A 2 MHz square wave captured at 12 megasamples per second reads back as alternating 2.4 MHz and 2 MHz because there is insufficient temporal resolution to place the edges; dropped to 8 megasamples per second, whole sections of the waveform vanish entirely.[1140]

## Thresholds and the limits of a digital view

What appears on screen is what the input latch believed the signal to be at the instant it sampled.[44] The real input may be noisy, overshooting or undershooting, and the instrument reduces all of it to a decision against a logic-low and a logic-high threshold.[44] Probing itself influences that decision.[44] A logic analyzer returns a one or a zero against a fixed threshold and takes no account of signal integrity, so a circuit with a marginal analog problem can produce plausible-looking digital results and send an investigation chasing faults that are not where they appear to be.[MC9_4mpqVgU] Working with a logic analyzer means largely trusting it, which is among its major pitfalls; an oscilloscope, correctly probed, shows what is actually present.[44] Some instruments do display waveshape as well, effectively acting as a multi-channel crude oscilloscope, but they sit at prices that place them outside ordinary bench budgets.[44]

Threshold flexibility separates price tiers. Fixed thresholds are the norm on low-priced instruments.[44] A $69 sixteen-channel USB unit defaults its threshold to zero volts and offers adjustment up to 6 V, but sets it globally rather than per channel — expected at the bottom of the market.[1720] Threshold range also bounds what can be measured at all: a Padauk programmer proved undecodable because the supply voltage changed between programming stages, moving above the analyzer's input range, and inserting a voltage divider pushed the earlier samples below the threshold instead.[1144] Threshold handling can also simply be wrong. A Rigol MSO5000 displayed multiple pulses on two channels at 50 MHz that were not present in the signal, consistently, at a 1.4 V threshold, while a Rigol 7000 fed the identical signal showed none.[UDGsZcAWgL8]

## Memory and compression

Sample memory is the other axis on which logic analyzers are separated. Compression sampling — storing a sample only when something changes — is extremely valuable because it maximises the use of available memory.[44] Its trade-off is that a change on any one channel forces a sample across all channels, so a single fast-clocking line can consume the entire buffer before a slow, widely spaced packet of interest arrives.[44] Instruments using compression are commonly fitted with only a couple of kilobytes of sample memory on the assumption that compression will cover it.[44]

Without compression, raw depth is what remains. The Digilent Analog Discovery provides sixteen digital channels at 100 megasamples per second with 4 k of channel memory and no compression, which is thin for logic work.[692] Capture depth is a practical constraint rather than a specification detail: analysing a NiMH charger's multiplexed MOSFET drive ran into the limit of what a single acquisition could hold, requiring deeper memory, sample compression, or segmented memory to see further.[812] Implementing compression is not free — it needs hardware and firmware capability in the instrument, usually inside an FPGA.[290]

Architecture also determines whether data can be lost. An instrument that buffers into its own memory and uploads afterwards is immune to host contention but bounded by buffer size; a streaming USB device has effectively unlimited memory provided the data rate stays within the transfer capability, but depends on a low-latency port.[436]

## Instrument forms

Three forms are worth distinguishing.[44] Traditional bench instruments are as large as or larger than a bench oscilloscope, cost from around $5,000 into the tens and hundreds of thousands, and consume bench space that is hard to justify without a specific need.[44] Instruments of this class are still built for cases that demand them, including units with 136 channels.[358] The second form is the combined scope and logic analyzer, typically two or four analog channels plus eight or sixteen digital.[44] The third is the USB instrument that hands display and analysis to a PC.[44]

For logic work specifically, the PC form has substantial advantages: a large screen, a mouse, a keyboard and better search than any front-panel interface can provide.[MC9_4mpqVgU][89] Sixteen digital channels plus four analog crammed onto a scope screen is a genuine usability problem, and mixed-signal capability, however convenient, is fiddly by comparison.[MC9_4mpqVgU] The reverse holds for analog work, where physical knobs beat a PC interface.[MC9_4mpqVgU]

Against that, the mixed-signal instrument gives time correlation between analog and digital channels in one acquisition.[MC9_4mpqVgU] That correlation can be reconstructed with separate instruments by feeding the scope's trigger output into any spare input channel of the logic analyzer, which requires no dedicated external trigger input — less convenient, since the two displays live on different screens, but functional.[MC9_4mpqVgU] Where a spare pin exists on a design under development, firmware can be made to assert it as a trigger marker for whatever instrument is attached.[1331]

## The cost of the digital option

On oscilloscopes the logic analyzer is almost always a paid option, and it is priced aggressively. The eight-channel option on the Agilent 2000 X series ran about $700, and on the 3000 X series the digital option exceeded a thousand dollars.[143][149] Siglent charged $469 for a sixteen-channel probe and software licence on the SDS5000X.[1220] The GW Instek eight-channel option was around $570.[474] Rigol asked $400 for MSO5000 probes.[UDGsZcAWgL8] On the Tektronix 4 series the digital capability consumes one of the instrument's flexible input channels rather than adding to them.[1218]

Those figures sit against standalone instruments in the same range or below. A capable standalone USB logic analyzer could be had for under a thousand dollars against a scope option costing over one.[149] The consistent engineering judgement is to buy the base scope, take the software serial-decode option, and add a separate logic analyzer if and when the need appears — unless the mixed-signal correlation is specifically required or budget is not a constraint.[MC9_4mpqVgU]

Capability at the low end varies considerably. A ChronoView LA8 in the sub-$200 category offered no sample compression, rudimentary triggering and no state triggering, though it did the job it claimed.[290] ZeroPlus hardware runs from around $3,000 to $6,000 for a 64-channel unit, but the same software — carrying the widest set of serial decoders of any instrument of its kind, down to SD card protocol — ships with models around $139; the software itself is unpolished, lacking edge snapping on cursors and sensible time readout.[1018]

## Probing and practice

Input front end and probing are decisive. A cheap front end and cheap probes produce the same result on a logic analyzer as on an oscilloscope: not a serious tool.[44] Inexpensive USB units are adequate for I²C and similar low-speed buses, but at 50 to a few hundred megahertz they do not hold up.[44] The act of connecting probes can alter the circuit, and on a multi-channel system probing one line can make a marginal fault appear or disappear.[44]

Two habits follow. Hook-up is laborious — every channel must be attached, verified for contact, and identified — and labelling each channel in software before capture is worth the few minutes it costs on anything beyond one or two channels.[44] Where signals are physically hard to reach, soldering fine mod wire to convenient vias creates accessible tap points, avoiding the impossibility of holding several probes on a fine-pitch package at once.[879]

Typical bench use is protocol work: SPI, I²C, CAN and one-wire buses, rather than the wide parallel buses of the era when boards carried hundreds of interconnected logic chips.[MC9_4mpqVgU] Concrete applications run from verifying that an SPI bus is carrying the data the firmware intends before the fault is sought elsewhere,[240] to capturing an infrared remote control's code output for protocol reverse engineering,[506] to confirming that a microcontroller is emitting the expected data before it is connected to the display hardware it drives.[VTAwzrKPjeE] The standing recommendation is a simple USB logic analyzer kept in a drawer for the occasions that need it: valuable, infrequently used, and cheap enough that it need not earn its bench space.[44][89]

## Implementation and internals

Inside oscilloscopes, the digital acquisition path is visible as a distinct block: input connector, comparators, level shifting and latching, feeding the main capture FPGA.[1309][587] Because the option is so commonly unsold, the depopulated footprint is a recurring sight — the Siglent 1000X, the Uni-T UPO3000 and the Rigol DHO800 all carry unfitted logic analyzer circuitry, and an Agilent 2000 X series board laid out for sixteen channels was fitted with only two of four devices.[800][1231][1563][144] Some designs move the analog front end off the mainboard entirely; the Rohde & Schwarz HMO1202 has no comparators or threshold circuitry on the main board because the level detection lives in the HO3508 logic probe pod.[842]

The instrument class is also a long-standing hobbyist project target. A 32-channel PC-based design published in *Electronics Australia* in October 1996, drawn up in 1995, ran 40 MHz in timing analysis mode and 20 MHz in state analysis, was TTL/CMOS compatible, used a PC parallel port interface, and provided latch trigger word masking fully maskable across all 32 channels plus optional glitch capture, with address and data disassembly in software.[747][1326] Its control and trigger functions were split across two devices because fully maskable triggering — high, low or don't-care per channel, set from software — needed dedicated logic, ultimately implemented in a CPLD/FPGA part.[747][1249] Elektor published a near-identical design in May 1996, independently arrived at on the other side of the world.[1326] Such designs are also the reason timing diagrams matter: a timing diagram is simply digital transitions plotted against time, the same view a logic analyzer produces from real hardware.[1249]
