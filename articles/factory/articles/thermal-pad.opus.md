# thermal pad

The term *thermal pad* covers two distinct things that both exist to move heat out of a component. The first is a feature of the semiconductor package itself: an exposed metal pad on the underside (occasionally the top) of a surface-mount chip, soldered to copper on the board so the die's heat escapes downward rather than through the leads.[326][497][259] The second is a compliant sheet of thermally conductive material — commonly known by the trademarked name Sil-Pad — placed between a hot device and a heatsink or metal chassis to bridge the microscopic air gaps between two imperfect surfaces while keeping them electrically isolated.[105][607] Whether a package carries a thermal pad at all is one of the fastest ways to judge how much power a circuit was designed to dissipate.[1263][1461]

## The package thermal pad

Modern QFN, MLF, MSOP and power SO packages route heat out of the die through a large metal slug on the bottom of the body. A 12-pin MLF measuring only 3 mm by 3 mm carries such a pad, alongside a DC-to-DC converter die that would otherwise have nowhere to send its dissipation.[346] The same construction appears on an MSOP-8 buck converter,[434] on automotive DC-to-DC controllers in packages with power tabs,[958] and on custom ASICs where the bottom pad is the only meaningful heat path off the die.[993]

The pad is useless on its own — it has to be coupled through the board. The standard arrangement is an array of thermal vias directly under the pad, tying it to copper flood on the opposite side: nine vias under a stepper driver on a 3D printer control board,[326] "dozens and dozens" scale arrays on oscilloscope acquisition chips,[217] and exposed copper with heavy via stitching visible on the underside of a function generator's output amplifiers.[497][475] Where the exposed copper and via field are visible on the back of a board, the chip on the other side almost certainly has a thermal pad.[497][1231] Semiconductor datasheets normally supply the recommended footprint, via pattern and the calculations behind them, and following that example layout is the intended design route.[326][805][407]

Because the pad is usually tied to ground, it becomes a large ground connection as well as a thermal one, and pins can short to it during assembly — a continuity check from every pin to the pad is a sensible post-reflow step.[346] Some parts ship in variants that differ only in which face the pad is on: the STA350BW carries its pad on the bottom, while a sibling part places it on top, and the choice depends entirely on which heatsinking scheme the board uses.[1322] An SO-8 with no pad at the bottom is, by construction, not intended to dissipate meaningful power; a package nominally rated for several watts will run extremely hot if actually asked to do so.[1461] The absence of pads on the underside of a board is therefore evidence that the design is low-power,[1263] and a board whose devices lack back-side pads can only shed heat through the pins and the copper traces attached to them — which fails badly when those traces are thin.[735]

## Soldering and desoldering pad packages

The thermal pad complicates both assembly and rework. Solder paste is the practical route, because getting solder underneath the pad with an iron first leaves a lumpy layer that has to melt into place afterwards.[1322] With drag soldering and hot air, solder reflowing visibly on the pins is the indicator that heat has already travelled through the bottom pad and the part is attached — there is no need to wait for anything further.[434] Under hot air the pad reflows along with the pins, and packages are built to survive oven profiles of a couple of hundred degrees for an extended period, so overheating during hand reflow is not the main risk.[346]

Misalignment is. Surface tension at the large pad can overwhelm the self-alignment that the fine pins would otherwise provide, pulling the chip visibly off-centre or lifting it, and a badly proportioned pad on the layout makes this worse.[346][1322] Removal is correspondingly difficult: a QFN dissipating only about a watt still needs a large tip, higher temperature and more airflow to release, and tilting the board on its side can be what finally frees it.[E1IqcGcZKHE] Done properly, the whole package lifts with minimal heat applied to the surrounding pads, leaving neighbouring components essentially untouched — often a better technique than heating the entire area.[1322]

## The interface pad

Two nominally flat mating surfaces — the back of a power transistor and a heatsink face — contact only at microscopic high points, and the trapped air between them adds thermal resistance to the path.[105] The two remedies are thermal compound, which fills the gaps as a paste, and a silicone rubber interface pad, which does the same job as a solid sheet.[105] Its defining advantage is that "you don't have to add heat sink compound" — the pad alone is the interface, and adding grease on top of one is redundant.[1761] The pad also provides electrical isolation, which is why heatsinks carrying multiple devices at different potentials use one: it transfers the heat while keeping the metalwork off the live tabs, and it removes the need for insulating bushes around the mounting screws.[607][745][644]

That isolation is not free. The pad is one more thermal resistance in a chain that already includes junction-to-case, the vias, and any heat transfer bar, and the design goal is to keep as few elements in series as possible — going straight from device case to heatsink, when the topology allows it, gives a substantially lower junction temperature.[744] In a marginal design the stack of junction-to-case plus vias plus the insulating pad can push a diode close to its 150 °C maximum junction temperature with no forced airflow.[895]

Pad quality matters as much as its presence. Bubbles and voids under a pad leave air gaps that defeat the point; the material conforms to surface irregularities but not completely, so a flat mating surface is still worth having.[607] Adhesive-backed pads are the usual answer for small PCB-mount heatsinks that have no mechanical attachment provision at all.[940] Clamping is preferred where possible — a clip or secondary plate pressing the device against the pad is common in high-quality equipment, and some designs dispense with per-device mounting hardware entirely in favour of pads plus a pressing plate.[745][1434][199][261]

## System-level use

Interface pads are the mechanism by which a chassis becomes a heatsink. Instrument and appliance designs commonly mould or machine the metalwork so that raised bosses come down onto each heat-generating package, with pads engineered to the right thickness to close the remaining gap — a spectrum analyser's aluminium plate is shaped so every major dissipating package lines up with a pad,[470] and the same approach appears in an early HD DVD player where the metal is depressed specifically to reach down onto the chips.[1011] A fanless rugged laptop uses its magnesium alloy shell the same way,[925] and a TO-220 device can simply be wedged between the board and the case bottom with a pad for insulation, making the extrusion itself the heatsink.[644] Where a package is on top of the board, a pad and a copper plate can still route heat down to the base of the enclosure; placing the processor on the underside of the board with a pad to the base is the more direct arrangement.[734][914]

Scale ranges from a single chip to an entire assembly: one isolated oscilloscope design uses a single large pad covering the whole board, drawing heat out of a 12-layer stack with heavy copper that spreads dissipation from an ADC running at about 1.5 W and a 500 MHz clock generator.[1753] At the other end, M.2 SSDs and memory modules routinely ship with a small pad under a thin heatsink,[zNG_oNhKCBg][MiqxUaWkGYY][1690] and Raspberry Pi cases include pads to couple the processor and regulator to the shell.[917][fpDnVSKfhew]

The pad only works if the far side of it is actually a heatsink. In a plastic-cased power supply, pads over the switching MOSFETs help marginally but cannot function as a dissipation path with no metal backing.[1606] Mechanical arrangement constrains the approach as well: a slide-on enclosure makes it hard to establish good contact between an internal heatsink block and the case, which is achievable with a screw-down case but not a sliding one.[1709]

## Failure and service considerations

Pads are a recurring nuisance during repair. They stick to whichever surface they were pressed against, so disassembly frequently leaves the pad adhered to the metal plate rather than the chip, and a pad that has come loose from a device suspected of overheating is itself a possible cause.[1154] Pads tear when a heatsink is lifted,[1717] go crusty with age and heat,[Ij6r6uXr2Mo] and in dense camera assemblies must be peeled back to reach the components beneath and then pressed carefully back down onto the die during reassembly.[1433][1428] Their one service virtue is cleanliness: a heatsink mounted on pads rather than compound comes apart without any goop to clean up.[1004]
