# dropper resistor

A dropper resistor is a resistor placed in series with a load to absorb the surplus between a fixed supply voltage and the voltage the load actually wants, thereby setting the current through it.[908][RT3godBXkOg] It is the cheapest possible current-setting element, and it is the reason a single LED can be run from a 9 V plugpack, a 24 V strip, a 170 V nixie supply, or a 240 V mains rail without any active circuitry at all.[392][948][435] Its ubiquity is matched only by its inefficiency: everything the load does not use is dissipated as heat in the resistor, and the current it sets holds only for the supply voltage it was calculated at.[621][1285]

## Sizing

The calculation is Ohm's law applied to the leftover voltage. The voltage across the resistor is the supply voltage minus the voltage the load drops, and the resistance is that difference divided by the desired current.[908][1491] For an LED run from a 3.3 V rail with a 1.8 V forward drop, the resistor must absorb 1.5 V at whatever current is wanted.[1491] A red LED is taken at roughly 1.8 V for this purpose, which leaves enough headroom to light it even from a 2 V supply.[471] Where an exam or a datasheet supplies only a nominal LED drop, that figure is what the dropper calculation has to be run with.[1427]

In a Zener shunt regulator the same arithmetic gives the dropper value RZ from the input voltage minus the Zener voltage, divided by the Zener test current, since essentially all the current flows through the Zener.[908] The Zener's own dynamic resistance — 5 ohms at a 50 mA test current for a typical 5.1 V part — is at least an order of magnitude below the dropper value and can be dropped from the equation without material error.[908]

The tolerance demanded of a dropper is usually low. When a resistor's only job is to set LED current, its exact value is not critical and a nearby preferred value is acceptable.[204] Where the current does matter, a decade resistance box substituted for the final resistor lets the working value be found empirically before the design is committed: at 170 V, a 20 K box setting produced 1.67 mA through a nixie tube, which lit and switched cleanly.[948]

## Driving LEDs and displays

Good practice gives every LED, or every segment of a multi-segment display, its own individual dropper resistor.[1491] Sharing one resistor across paralleled LEDs is explicitly avoided; a second LED added in parallel gets its own dropper rather than being tied directly across the first.[182] Boards with no dropper resistors at all on the display lines light up, but segment brightness then varies with how many segments are turned on at once.[1491] Where a display is multiplexed, effective brightness depends jointly on the dropper values and on the fixed rail feeding them, so a sagging 5 V rail shows up directly as a brightness change.[621]

Practical implementations follow the same pattern across widely different products. A digital clock ties a permanently lit decimal point through its own individual dropper straight to the rail, and drives a colon as two diodes in series with a single dropper.[801] A shotgun microphone indicator uses 1 K droppers with 4.5 V across them, giving 4.5 mA per LED — high for a 9 V battery, though battery life remains acceptable.[182] An infrared proximity sensor drives its two transmitter LEDs through a pair of droppers, whose junctions double as convenient scope tap points.[377] A mains socket tester is nothing more than a diode in series with an LED and a series dropper, three such branches arranged in a triangle.[1598] An LED panel lamp may contain no circuitry whatsoever beyond one dropper per LED fed from the plugpack.[392] Microcontroller I/O pins driving indicator LEDs get a dropper each in the ordinary way.[831]

Some drivers make the resistor unnecessary. Older counter/display chips such as the 4026 have buffered outputs able to drive LED displays directly without droppers.[801] The LM3914 bargraph driver has programmable LED current, adjustable from 2 mA up to 30 mA, so no dropper resistors are needed and the component count falls.[204]

Omitting the resistor without such a driver is destructive. A 1 W LED connected straight across a switched output with no dropper drew 250 mA.[135] Applying 9 V to a seven-segment display without a series resistor destroys the segment.[s2KkgI-kyK0] Some hobby kits shipped with the dropper resistors physically mounted on the display's own little board, visible on top, precisely because the omission was such a common way to lose a part.[s2KkgI-kyK0] A display drive scheme that relies on splitting a fixed source current between segments rather than using droppers should be treated as having no current limiting, and would not survive a design review on that point.[1493]

## Nixie tubes and high-voltage loads

High-voltage gas displays are driven the same way, with the dropper doing the work of turning a stiff HV rail into a defined tube current.[948] A 22 K dropper on a 170 V supply gives about 1.6 mA through a lit digit; the same 22 K on a 120 V rail gives roughly 2 mA.[950] Because a nixie digit is a single shaped cathode rather than an assembly of segments, one dropper serves one digit at a time.[948] Where high-channel-count drivers of 32 or 64 outputs are used, each tube still needs its own separate dropper, which constrains how outputs may be allocated across tubes.[950]

Not every electrode tolerates the same current. A nixie decimal point has a smaller surface area and a limit around 0.3 mA, so an additional series resistor is added on that line beyond the digit dropper.[952] On the PCB these resistors are numerous and awkward, and are typically massaged into the leftover space, including underneath the tubes themselves.[974]

Extreme supply voltages push dropper values into unusual territory. A homebrew optocoupler in an electric fence controller uses a 1 megohm LED dropper, which by the rule of thumb of 1 mA per kilovolt sets the LED current from the fence output voltage.[1277]

## Mains and off-line supplies

Where a small amount of low-voltage housekeeping power must be pulled from the mains, a large dropper resistor feeding a rectifier and Zener is the traditional approach.[1481][435] Such a board sits at mains potential throughout and cannot safely be probed with an earth-referenced scope.[435] A resistive dropper of this kind is wasteful by construction: power is burned in the resistor and again in the Zener to deliver very little output current, which is why a capacitive dropper is preferred where orders of magnitude less waste is wanted.[1285] The substitution has its own failure mode — an X2 class metallised film capacitor serving as the dropper for a Zener is a documented failure point.[1486]

Purely resistive dropping also appears in cheap mains-powered lighting and chargers, where a bridge rectifier plus dropper resistors and an output capacitor stands in for any regulation or constant-current drive at all.[1278][592] A battery charger built this way has no regulation whatsoever, and leaving cells on it too long can cook them.[951] The same shortcut appears on lower rails: a 17.5 V rail feeding logic through nothing more than a dropper.[1360]

## Other roles

The dropper idea extends beyond LEDs. High-value input dropper resistors set the attenuation ratio and limit input current on the front end of a high-voltage differential probe, four megohms per side, in place of galvanic isolation.[932] A power analyser attenuates its inputs with four dropper resistors per leg, attenuating so heavily that no additional input protection is required regardless of transient voltage.[589] A dropper resistor in a lab supply's reference chain sets the current range from 0 to 1 A.[224] A 10 ohm resistor in series with a device's supply is enough for basic powerline analysis with nothing but a scope probe.[771] Changing an indicator's dropper value also changes an RC time constant where the LED shares a node with a timing capacitor, so the resistor cannot always be treated as an isolated component.[242]

## When not to use one

A dropper resistor holds current constant only against a constant supply and a constant load drop. Where LED current must be well defined, a constant-current driver removes both the resistor and the need to calculate and specify it, reducing the design to a two-chip solution with no droppers anywhere.[513] Where 145 RGB LEDs must be run from a 12 V rail whose compliance voltage forces many short parallel strings, the alternative is a dropper in every string.[928]

The tradeoff is not one-sided. On 24 V LED tape, a strip using a single dropper per segment measured 19.2 W per metre and met its specification precisely, while a strip of the same form factor using an active constant-current chip driver failed to reach its rated 32.6 W per metre even after cable losses were accounted for.[5mueaTsus8o][1617] The resistive strip is also expected to dim more linearly as the supply is reduced, though it still exhibits a compliance voltage below which the string drops out.[1617] For a quick hack running LEDs from a 7.2 V camera battery, where consistent output, low-voltage cutoff and protection are all unnecessary, a series dropper calculated for the wanted current is the appropriate answer.[RT3godBXkOg] Decorative LED reels driven through a plain dropper are cheap and plentiful but do not deliver the high brightness a single long series string can.[1343]
