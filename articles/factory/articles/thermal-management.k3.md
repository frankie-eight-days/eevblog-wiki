# thermal management

Thermal management is the engineering discipline of moving waste heat from where it is generated to where it can be harmlessly rejected, and it routinely determines whether a product is reliable at all rather than merely how it performs. It shapes the physical layout of a system as much as the circuit design does: enclosure size, heatsinking, airflow paths, and component spacing are all set by the need to dissipate power within a given package.[236][291] In long-life products it is the dominant constraint — an LED luminaire designed for 40,000 hours continuous operation and a 3% light-output loss over five years treats thermal design as its most critical aspect, limiting temperature rise to around 20 °C.[773]

## Heat transfer paths

Heat moves by conduction, convection, and radiation, and which paths are available depends on the environment. In space there is no convection because there is no air, so hot spots can only be cooled by conduction to other structure and by radiation; the thermal problem in orbit is less about keeping warm than about controlling heat, with a low-orbit satellite cycling from roughly −40 °C to +80 °C over a 92-minute orbit.[519] Radiation damage is considered a harder problem than thermal design for space electronics, because the wattage a chip dissipates and the means of removing it are known quantities, whereas radiation tolerance is not predictable without testing.[897]

Within an atmosphere, conduction is the workhorse at the board level. Heavy copper electrodes in a power component package can act like miniature heat pipes, pulling heat out of a small die and enabling higher power handling in an ultra-miniature package.[9V99J22aiLE] For cylindrical battery cells, the lowest thermal resistance path is axial — out the top and bottom of the cell — while extracting heat through the sides faces higher thermal resistance, and a thin terminal tab is a thermal choke point even with a large slab of copper behind it.[1340]

## Board-level techniques

The PCB itself is frequently the heatsink. Exposed copper pours on the board underside with dense arrays of vias indicate that the chips above have thermal pads and are being heatsunk through the board.[497] Low-dropout regulators fed from multiple voltage taps are chosen to minimise dissipation in each device specifically because only the PCB copper is available as a heatsink.[497] Spreading dissipation across multiple paralleled power devices — more than the current rating strictly requires — distributes the heat and improves reliability, since on-resistance and the resulting power dissipated are the limiting factors rather than raw current capability.[607]

Aluminium-backed (metal-core) PCB is the classic construction for LED assemblies and other power packages, where heat must be extracted through the bottom of the board; the metal layer acts as a heatsink and can be bolted to a larger one.[1259] Sliding an aluminium-core LED PCB directly into a channel in an extruded aluminium housing turns the entire housing into a heatsink.[1343] Where heat must cross from an SMD power part to an enclosure, surface-mount thermal jumpers provide a pick-and-place, reflow-solderable path that ordinary SMD heatsinks — which simply dump heat into still air inside a small case — cannot.[744][1347] At the chip level, heat is commonly extracted through a bottom pad and thermally bonded to a machined aluminium block that acts as the heatsink.[993] In a fanless multilayer design, a large thermal pad covering the whole board can suck heat into the copper layers of a 12-layer PCB, spreading the output of a 1.5 W ADC, a 1 W clock generator, and power-hungry 1 GHz op-amps.[1753]

## Heatsinks, enclosures, and passive cooling

The enclosure itself is often the intended heatsink, and designs fail when that coupling is neglected. A studio monitor whose entire chassis is the heatsink, bonded to the die-cast frame, is thermally sound.[1156] Conversely, a 5 W device whose PCB backing plate is not thermally connected to its extruded aluminium case, relying on radiation alone, represents a missed opportunity.[1286] A rubidium oscillator runs hot enough — over 40 °C on the case within minutes — that mounting it in a sealed plastic box is inadvisable; a die-cast alloy or extruded aluminium case is needed to get the heat out.[235]

Sealed enclosures are a recurring failure environment. Oyster-style ceiling light fittings have no airflow at all, so drivers inside them run hot for their entire service life, and even capacitors rated at 105 °C only gain lifetime relative to lower-rated parts — they still have a finite lifetime under sustained heat.[GbADsyp9wM8] A resistor that fails from its own dissipation inside a sealed oyster fitting has been given a harsh life the design never accounted for.[1423] A board reaching 70 °C in free air will run far hotter once installed in an enclosed fitting with no ventilation, compounding the use of cheap low-temperature PCB laminate.[735] Small enclosed drivers also benefit from generous component spacing rather than dense packing, because the thermal performance inside a mostly enclosed case would otherwise suffer.[1091]

Orientation and geometry matter. Heatsink fins should align with the airflow so that air moves across them from cool inlet to hot outlet.[1499] Passive convection designs use vertical fins and an open chimney path; the Apple G4 Cube packed a G4 processor and graphics card into an 8-inch cube with no fan, using a top vent and a passive heatsink with fins in the correct direction.[1211] A mobile LED light can rely on motion-generated breeze across finned heatsinks and a solid aluminium backing plate to stay cool in service, reaching around 57 °C on the bench in still air but dropping with airflow during actual use.[1010][gTTsvYbQOT4] Internal air circulation without through-flow still has value: a fan stirring air inside a sealed box spreads warm air so it can conduct through the case top, though coupling components directly to the case with a thermal pad would be better.[1709]

## Forced-air cooling and airflow architecture

Deliberate ducting distinguishes engineered cooling from an afterthought. Rack and server equipment channels air from a clean inlet, across the electronics and heatsinks, to an exhaust, using air guides and partitioned zones; Xeon servers achieve adequate cooling with modest heatsinks because the processors are efficient, conservatively clocked for 24/7 operation, and the ducting is designed properly rather than oversized.[691] The Power Mac G5 used a clear acrylic airflow guide with separately sealed sections for drives and processors.[783] Fan and vent placement follows a consistent logic: an outlet fan at the back with inlet vents low on the edge draws air up across the board and heatsinks, whereas inlets at the top would short-circuit the path.[864] Bench power supplies intended for rack mounting suck clean lab air in through the front, over the board and through a channelled finned heatsink, and out the back.[1005] Side-vented instruments cannot be stacked directly against each other without blocking their own cooling.[1691][1499]

## Worked example: linear regulator dissipation

Power dissipation in a linear regulator is the voltage drop multiplied by the output current, and the Rigol DP832 supply illustrates the consequence of ignoring it: a regulator dropping 7 V at 0.7 A dissipates about 5 W, far beyond what the fitted heatsink can handle even with good airflow.[512] The unit's heatsink was measured at 90–110 °C in the case and up to 130 °C with the case opened (which itself removes airflow and inflates the reading), with the main 5 V logic rail regulator running near 100 °C nominal and triggering thermal shutdown.[512] After a redesign, the same measurement through the case side showed about 58 °C.[549] The design judgment is blunt: no competent design review should pass a 5 W dissipation on an undersized heatsink, and simply touching the part would have revealed the problem.[512]

## Temperature and component lifetime

Sustained heat shortens the life of nearby components even when the hot device itself survives. Electrolytic capacitors placed within 5 mm of a MOSFET heatsink will not be kept cool, to the detriment of their service life.[446] LED products illustrate the payoff of doing it right: a luminaire engineered for a maximum 20 °C rise claims 40,000 hours of operation and only 3% output loss over five years.[773] High-brightness torches and bike lights legitimately run hot — around 57–70 °C at full output — and depend on use-case airflow rather than bench conditions.[gTTsvYbQOT4] Processors selected for upgrade must match the thermal design power of the original part so that the existing power supply and cooling remain valid.[1280]

## Thermal effects in precision instrumentation

Thermal management in metrology gear is about stability rather than survival. Fan airflow must be kept off voltage references and sensitive front ends so the ambient around them changes only gradually; one 7.5-digit multimeter encloses the analog section in a plastic carrier with a spiral cutout baffle to isolate it from circulation, an alternative to soldering a pre-binned reference directly to the main board.[731] A precision multimeter places a thermistor directly at its press-fit input terminals, with deliberately massive traces to those terminals, to compensate for thermal EMF effects at the metal contacts.[417] Fan vibration also couples into sensitive measurements, so instrument mechanical design must manage vibration alongside airflow and shielding to preserve calibration stability and noise levels.[1624] Enclosing a photosensor even in aluminium foil changes the thermal properties of its case, a real consideration when a sensor dissipates significant power through small fins.[869]

## Batteries and cells

Cell internal resistance is a direct thermal load: at 20 mΩ series resistance and 10 A draw, a lithium cell dissipates 2 W internally, on the order of a 5% loss.[1340] Large pouch cells dissipate heat more readily than cylindrical cells, and pack-level thermal management has historically relied on thermally conductive material around cell sides, an inefficient path compared with axial extraction.[1340] Grid-scale battery containers use pumped liquid cooling loops over racks of cells, combined with per-rack monitoring and protection.[1411]

## Thermoelectric devices

A thermoelectric generator only produces power while heat is actually flowing through it; once a small heatsink reaches equilibrium with the heat source, the temperature differential across the plates falls to zero and output ceases entirely. TEG design is therefore fundamentally a thermal management problem — the cold side must have adequate sinking to maintain the gradient indefinitely, not just for the first half hour.[664]

## Measurement practice

Thermal performance of a fan-cooled system must be measured with the enclosure assembled. With the lid off, air enters at the nearest opening to the fan instead of being drawn through the designed path over the electronics and heatsinks, and the measured temperatures no longer represent the product as built.[622] The DP832's 130 °C heatsink readings were taken with the case open and are correspondingly higher than in-service temperatures, a caveat that applies to any such measurement.[512] Thermal imaging of an assembled product in its real environment — still air versus moving air, sealed fitting versus open bench — is the meaningful test, since service conditions routinely differ from lab conditions by tens of degrees.[735][gTTsvYbQOT4]
