# processor

In an electronic product the processor is the general-purpose programmable device that runs the firmware or operating system, drives the display, reads the keypad, and coordinates whatever dedicated silicon does the actual measuring, sampling or signal handling.[1717][1571][800] It is rarely the part that performs a product's headline function: in a multimeter the measurement is done by a dedicated chipset and the processor merely receives the result,[1571] and in a modern oscilloscope the acquisition happens in a custom ASIC while the processor runs the user interface.[1717] Because the processor is nonetheless the thing that boots, blinks, beeps and moves mechanisms, whether it is running is the single most useful early observation in troubleshooting almost any powered product.[1602][1053][905]

## Division of labour with dedicated silicon

Instrument architecture is usually a partition between a programmable processor and hard-wired acquisition or display silicon. In a Siglent 1000X oscilloscope the acquisition ASIC funnels data directly into a display ASIC/FPGA, bypassing the processor entirely; the processor handles maths and similar functions rather than waveform updating.[800] The same split appears in a Rigol design where a custom ASIC does the sampling and talks to the 500 Mpoint memory, while a Rockchip device runs the OS and the entire GUI.[1717] Keysight's MegaZoom generation moved the display into the digital ASIC itself; on the higher-end 4000 and 6000 products a processor with a camera interface takes the ASIC's output as video and overlays higher-resolution information, because the ASIC's resolution is hard-coded and this was the only way to extend the chipset's life.[1032]

Where that partition falls has direct consequences for behaviour. On a Rigol MSO7000 the FFT will not update while a front-panel control is being scrolled, because the same processor handles both the FFT and the user interface; architectures that put FFT and serial decoding in hardware do not stall in this way.[CMoBGGqojqs] Model-to-model updates often change only one side of the partition: between the Agilent 2000 and 3000 X-series the processor is an identical part with an identical specification, while the Xilinx FPGA grew from a Spartan 3 500 to a Spartan 3 1200.[148]

Not everything can be absorbed into the processor. Oscilloscopes continue to carry 4000- and 7400-series logic alongside their processors and custom analog blocks, because there is a limit to what modern integration can swallow and a small circuit element from the jellybean logic families is still needed.[1611] The same argument justifies logic minimisation inside FPGAs, ASICs and processors, where extra gates cost silicon and add propagation delay that lowers the maximum achievable clock frequency.[lJ3q9RHIatU] At the other extreme, a product can omit a processor entirely: an all-analog electronic load implements even constant-resistance mode without digital switching circuitry, specifically so the load contributes no radiated or conducted noise to low-level measurements on the supply under test.[749]

## More than one processor

Multi-processor partitioning is common. A GoPro Hero 4 Silver contains what amounts to three processors — two ARM Cortex-A9 cores plus an ARM11 — along with caches, USB 2, real-time clock, UARTs, NAND drivers, DDR memory drivers and separate video and image DSPs.[672] A bench power supply design can split the work between an isolated USB-side processor handling USB PD, USB HID and serial communications, and a second processor doing primary-side housekeeping.[1264] Cameras follow the same pattern, with a main board processor alongside a separate front-panel control processor.[1433] Instruments frequently place a processor on its own board, separate from the measurement hardware.[1382][1639]

## Reset and power-on

A processor held in reset does nothing useful even though parts of it are alive: with the reset line low the oscillator does start up and run, but the device cannot execute its program.[1245] Forcing that reset pin to the positive rail in an IBM PCjr keyboard controller drew an extra 6 mA and brought the device to life.[1245] The conventional way to generate the reset is an RC time constant into a Schmitt-trigger input, holding the processor in reset for some milliseconds while the supply rises so it gets a clean start rather than doing funny business at marginal voltage.[1406] Where a device relies on an external reset and that input has failed, no amount of pull-up will help — an intercom controller with a damaged reset input produced no initial reset pulse and would not run, having no usable internal reset of its own; briefly shorting the reset pin to the positive rail through a resistor started it and the LCD initialised normally.[710]

Soft power switching ties the processor into its own power-up. A processor commonly drives the gate of a series power MOSFET through a latching transistor arrangement, so that once it boots it holds its own supply on and the momentary switch is no longer needed.[1672][f-LTv1GqCMw] A failure to boot therefore looks identical to a failed power switch. Products with a programming header often feed power directly through to the processor, bypassing the front-panel off switch entirely, which is why they can be programmed in the switched-off position.[JI4b-7vpIDc][1520]

## Supply quality, brownout and resets

Processors are sensitive to supply disturbances that most other circuitry shrugs off. In a Rigol DP832 an overheating regulator dropping out produced ripple that ordinary 5 V logic or a downstream 3.3 V local regulator would have tolerated without complaint, yet something in the processing circuitry reset on it; the 5 V rail did not plummet to zero or collapse to 3 V as expected, which made the fault look for a while as though the regulator theory had been wrong.[512] The opposite extreme also occurs: in a Stanford Research SR650 the processor kept running with the rail sagging to 3.9 V, far below the nominal 4.75 V lower limit of a 5 V rail, with the display visibly dimming.[621]

Inrush is a related mechanism. In a Goal Zero Yeti 400, connecting a mains inverter with a large input capacitance drew enough surge current that the processor hiccuped — starting up, shutting down and retrying in a repeating cycle visible as a flashing indicator — while loads with little input capacitance and hence little inrush caused no such trouble.[1707]

## Power and thermal behaviour

Processor dissipation is usually the dominant thermal load in a compact product, and is often the reason a case gets warm at all.[734][402] It is not automatically the hottest part, though. On a failed Raspberry Pi Compute Module CM4 board the whole assembly drew a very consistent 6.3 W at 5.1 V with no video output at all, and thermal imaging put the DC-DC converter at about 101 °C and the failed Ethernet chip around 90 °C while the processor sat near 40 °C — a temperature entirely normal for it.[fpDnVSKfhew][E1IqcGcZKHE] Comparing an emissive plastic package against a bare metal can requires correcting for emissivity, for instance by taping the metal lid, before any such comparison means anything.[fpDnVSKfhew]

Where a processor is capable of thermally destroying itself, cooling is not optional: a single-board computer processor may throttle, shut itself down, or be damaged if worked hard without a stick-on heatsink.[883] In laptops and desktop machines the practical maintenance item is the same interface — clearing accumulated dust and replacing degraded thermal paste on the processor.[1345][1357]

At the low-power end the constraint reverses. In energy-harvesting work a storage capacitor charged from a thermoelectric generator can supply only a couple of milliamps, and the regulator output feeding the processor may be limited to 4 or 5 mA, while the processor itself might draw 10 mA on wake-up and considerably more if run continuously at 20 MHz — so short active bursts must be supplied from output capacitance rather than from the harvester directly.[664] A solar calculator's processor, by contrast, kept running with its 220 µF reservoir capacitor shorted out, indicating the cell alone was sufficient under that illumination.[1094]

## Firmware and silicon defects

Every type of processor on the market ships with silicon bugs, and the errata sheet for the specific device must be read before committing to it in a design, so that a feature that does not work or behaves incorrectly is discovered up front rather than late.[53] Firmware corruption is a distinct and real field failure mode: the leading explanation for one instrument that stopped working after passing factory calibration was that the flash in the internal processor had been corrupted or incorrectly reprogrammed after test, since the calibration itself checked out as still correct.[mkZFHEeQkQI]

A processor that fails to boot can leave peripherals in their power-on default state. A multimeter that displayed every LCD segment at once had simply never got far enough to send the command clearing the display; reflashing the processor through the on-board programming header restored normal operation with the calibration intact to within one least significant digit of a reference meter.[JI4b-7vpIDc] Across one manufacturer's meters the majority of observed faults have been outright processor failures, often failing in unusual ways rather than dying cleanly.[f-LTv1GqCMw]

## Diagnostic use

Evidence that the processor is executing is the first branch point in most repairs, and it can usually be gathered without opening anything. A floppy drive seeking, a transport mechanism moving, an LED flashing, a beep on a keypress or a boot delay followed by a display turn-on all require the processor to be fully operational.[1053][1602][918][1425] Serial output is stronger evidence still: a device emitting a decodable 9600 baud shutdown message on power-off demonstrates the processor is working.[1061] Conversely, a device drawing its expected running current with nothing on screen and no serial activity narrows the fault towards the display path — a dead LCD, a failed LCD driver, or in older architectures a separate video memory chip.[918][1564][808] Where a programmer refuses to detect the processor at all, the first question is whether the processor is receiving its supply, since even dead silicon would still show voltage on its pins; measuring 3.2 V on a rail that never reaches the processor points at the delivery path rather than at the chip.[1520] The converse also holds: a programmer that reports an insertion error rather than a communication failure shows both processor and LCD driver are alive.[1522]

Physical inspection focuses on the processor's solder joints, which are worth reflowing when no schematic is available and other candidates have been eliminated.[9s9LXOBknck] Processors can also be responsible for a system shutting itself down deliberately: a monitor speaker's amplifier chip reported a fault on its interrupt line and the processor, correctly implementing the design, reset the amplifier rather than let it continue.[1322]

## Design considerations around the processor

Modern high-pin-count processors and FPGAs come with strict, extensively documented requirements for bypassing and power-rail sequencing, running to dozens of pages, in contrast to older parts where a couple of bypass capacitors would do.[1512] Low-inductance capacitor technologies are favoured near high-end processors and FPGAs precisely for their inherently lower inductance than aluminium electrolytics, with multiple case sizes easing placement close to the device.[9V99J22aiLE] Board layout also has to keep high-voltage rails away from data pins entering a large BGA processor, or provide a grounded guard trace to shunt any breakdown to ground.[1222]

The processor's ground is generally common across the digital section, which permits single-probe debugging with one ground lead attached; the measurement chipset side of a multimeter is a different matter, with multiple non-common grounds where that assumption fails badly.[1520] Ground also leaves the product: the USB shield ground reaches the processor's ground on the board and, at the other end, mains earth through the PC.[279]

Where a design needs to remain dormant for a long period and then act reliably, a processor-based system must be continuously watchdog-reset so it cannot latch up or drift into an unrecoverable state.[1131] For general project work a hard processor is often preferred over an FPGA, with the caveat that some projects are better suited to an FPGA and some can only be solved with one.[131] Cost reduction pressure commonly lands on this part: selecting a lower-cost processor and reducing parts count was central to hitting a price point while still meeting expectations such as a colour LCD.[1032]

## Historical forms

Before single-chip integration, a processor could be a board-level or multi-chip assembly. A 1970s Compucorp calculator split its processor across four separate ASICs and needed two more for the display processor, most likely gate arrays with a limited logic budget rather than fully custom devices.[663] HP calculators used a BCD-based processor with a 56-bit register in the HP 41, an architecture that was the precursor to the later Saturn family.[582] Vintage computers commonly used identical processor boards in different roles, distinguished only by their position and the memory board next to them.[1404] Older machines also placed the processor in a socket, making reseating of socketed chips a legitimate first step when a system will not boot.[1348]

Old and new silicon age differently from the parts around them. In a Tektronix 2465B whose logic-board electrolytics had leaked and corroded the surrounding board, the processor remained fully functional with its calibration data intact in a Dallas non-volatile RAM whose battery is internal to the SRAM package and therefore unmeasurable.[1203] In a Compaq Portable the mask ROM, the RAM, the processor and the video card all still worked after decades, the silicon itself proving quite robust.[1354]

## Identification in practice

Processors are frequently identifiable only by inference. A device sitting under a thermal pad with a heatsink, surrounded by flash and DRAM, is the applications processor; a large Atmel part with memory, DRAM and flash around it is almost certainly running a Linux-based system.[1174][1286] Package markings are often deliberately obscured, sanded off, or simply unreadable without a microscope.[1110][940] Some parts are covered by non-disclosure agreements that prevent the specific device from being named at all, even when the programmer for it is available.[1667][JI4b-7vpIDc] Others are genuinely undocumented: a Motorola XC390176 in a vintage MicroTAC phone appears to be a processor of some kind with no available information.[492]
