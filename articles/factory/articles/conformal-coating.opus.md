# conformal coating

Conformal coating is a thin polymer film applied over a populated printed circuit board after assembly, following the contours of the board and its components.[355] Its purpose is environmental: it keeps moisture off the laminate and out from between component leads, where condensation would otherwise combine with dirt and contamination to create low-impedance leakage paths that eventually short things out.[1190][1126] It is standard practice in equipment expected to survive climates, weather and thermal cycling that a consumer board would never see — military hardware, automotive safety systems, marine electronics and outdoor power equipment.[1435][821][517][1499]

## Why it is applied

The failure mechanism the coating defends against is progressive rather than sudden. Moisture forms on the board and works its way between components, the film picks up dirt and gunk, and what began as a clean insulator becomes a conductive contaminant bridge until circuit nodes are effectively shorted.[1190] Described in creepage terms, the coating stops moisture spreading across the PCB and so stops leakage tracking across the board surface.[1126]

The requirement follows the service environment. An airbag controller is coated because the units must work across an enormous range of climates in different countries, and reliability cannot be traded away.[517] A hybrid solar inverter mounted outdoors is subject to sun, cold, thermal cycling and internal dew, so at least a partial coat over the components is expected.[1620] A portable battery station used camping, in a garage, in a trailer or in an RV warrants a coat for the same reason.[1499] A marine navigation instrument gets the underside of its digital processor board coated specifically for the marine environment.[821] An outdoor intercom board is coated to keep moisture out over the long term.[710] A regulator intended for an outdoor RC aircraft carries one.[895] High-voltage circuitry is another natural case: a high-voltage multiplier board is coated because moisture and crap on the board would upset its operation.[1149]

Coating is not universal even in demanding markets. A high-quality automotive board can be built to a very high standard — good fiberglass laminate, top-quality parts, careful engineering — with no conformal coating finish at all.[958] Intrinsically safe instrumentation does not require coating as a condition of that rating, though it is frequently present anyway.[603]

## Identifying a coated board

The usual visual tell is shine: a coated board carries a gloss or sheen that the bare solder mask does not, most visible on the sides and tops of chips where the coating has pooled over a non-planar surface.[456][1126][1489] Raking light held nearly horizontal to the board brings it out.[1620]

This test is not infallible, because some solder masks are themselves very glossy. The reliable discriminator is coverage: a conformal coating is sprayed onto the board after the components are loaded, so it appears over the chips and passives as well as the laminate, whereas solder mask stops at the bare board.[355] A multimeter board that looks coated at first glance turns out under a microscope to be high-gloss solder mask with nothing at all over the components.[1692] The same ambiguity arises with a vintage meter showing a faint luster that resists classification.[930] Older assemblies can also carry post-solder coatings that are neither ordinary solder mask nor an obvious lacquer, applied over the joints after soldering.[481]

## Partial and selective coating

In practice coverage is often incomplete, and the pattern of what was covered reveals how it was applied. A brushed-on coat leaves uneven patches with bare regions between them and the tops of chips and capacitors uncoated — plainly not a dipped board.[517][1190] Marine gear may have the solder side coated and the component side left entirely bare.[821] An LED driver may have coating on the primary side only, with the rest masked off.[1252] In a large battery product one board can be thoroughly coated while an adjacent board is not, the fan controller board coated and the bottom board apparently not.[1499]

Deliberate selective coating is a separate matter from sloppy coverage. Masking is used to keep coating off contacts and mechanisms that must stay clean: two pins masked off on a coated processor board, and the areas around tactile switches masked on a coated hardware wallet.[821][1006] A power window controller has no coating over most of the board but a deliberate bead of blue coating placed around one microcontroller.[h9V0qJ4p3Aw] Gunk applied only around the perimeter of a chip may serve as a local seal against dust or moisture rather than as board-level protection.[52] On an active probe, coating over a section that is not the sensitive amplifier suggests a motivation other than moisture ingress.[1715]

Where a board is coated only partially in an assembly that already has a potting box around it, the engineering judgment is that the half-measure is not worth taking: pot the whole thing in a solid or re-enterable compound, or coat it properly, rather than pouring some coating over the top and calling it done.[1190][821] The boundary between the two techniques can blur — a very thick conformal coat, with a few components such as a current sense resistor left exposed, does much of what potting does without turning the assembly into a solid brick.[1483] Full potting, by contrast, ends the discussion: a potted sensor PCB simply cannot be examined.[603]

Coating also appears on things that are not conventional assemblies. The winding of a precision decade resistance box is coated to protect wire finer than a human hair, most likely by dipping the whole wound element after winding.[461] A rubberized pedometer combines a coated board with a potted battery and an outer seal, an arrangement that would make the product completely waterproof.[412] Contract manufacturers offer conformal coating alongside assembly and test as a standard production service.[1686]

Because coating is an additional production process with real cost, it is essentially absent from consumer computing hardware, and partially coating a notebook logic board against known water ingress paths would count as a drastic over-engineering measure.[1222]

## Consequences for service and repair

Every property that makes conformal coating good for reliability makes it hostile to repair. The coating is an insulator, so a probe tip resting on a coated pad with moderate pressure reads nothing at all; the tip must physically pierce the film, which requires genuinely sharp probes and real force.[710][1491][1485] Coating and other gunk over relay pins must be pierced the same way before a coil resistance measurement will read.[1481] Chemical strippers can remove coating selectively or across the whole board as an alternative to piercing.[1491]

Anything more invasive than probing means removing coating mechanically. Scraping the coating off a pin before it can be probed is the standard preliminary step on coated military power supplies, and on dense assemblies this turns troubleshooting into a slow, unpleasant exercise.[821] Repairing coated military equipment means scraping it all off, replacing possibly one part, and replacing it with the exact part rather than a functional equivalent.[1435] Coating between pins and around traces can be scraped out with a scalpel under a microscope when contamination is the suspected fault — a test that, when it changes nothing, usefully eliminates board leakage as the cause.[710] Removing a coated EEPROM is markedly harder than removing an uncoated one, even with low-melting-point alloy, and risks damaging the part.[517]

Coating also obstructs identification. Part numbers are difficult or impossible to read through it, which is why coated boards are photographed with the illumination almost horizontal and the board turned to catch the marking at the right angle.[1620][1489] Failing that, the marking is scraped clear or read under a high-quality stereo microscope.[517][710][1388] Where a manufacturer has already sanded the markings off a controller, coating on top compounds the problem, though adjacent devices such as a bridge rectifier may still be readable.[1423] Coating over an unknown micro can defeat photography entirely without better lighting.[1499]

A coated board is also mechanically bonded in a way that is easy to forget. Coating bridges component bodies to the laminate, so lifting a part aside to see the trace beneath it — bending a resonator up roughly thirty degrees — can crack the coating or the part.[710]

## Applying coating during repair

Coating is a repair material as well as a manufacturing one. Where corrosion — from a leaking alkaline cell, for example — has been cleaned back to bare copper, the exposed copper must not be left open; the rot is stopped and then the area is protected with either conformal coating or a UV-curable liquid solder mask.[1497] An aerosol clear conformal coating is the convenient option for this, with the alternative of masking the board off properly first and using photo-imageable solder mask.[qmcSEJehqZM][1497] Boards that have already been coated once tend to look rough after rework and cleaning, since the coating interferes with wicking and cleanup.[1353]
