# ptc thermistor

A PTC thermistor is a resistor whose resistance rises with its own temperature — positive temperature coefficient — and it is the standard slow-overload protection device sitting in series with the voltage input of a multimeter.[373][W2o8otvVML4] Under normal measurement it is a low-value series resistor of around 1 k that affects nothing; under an overload the current through it dissipates enough power to heat it, its resistance climbs into the megohm range, and the current collapses before the front end is destroyed.[373][151] Along with MOVs, a bridge rectifier and HRC fuses, its presence is one of the quickest ways to judge whether a meter's input protection was designed seriously at all.[373]

## Operating principle

The device is a thermistor; PTC is the common shorthand for the positive-temperature-coefficient type.[373] Its schematic symbol is a resistor — zig-zag or rectangular — with a small dot alongside, and the reference designator is RT rather than R, which is the usual giveaway on a board or schematic.[373][1655][832][1388] On the Fluke 87 schematic the same idea appears as a plus-T marking next to the part.[1655]

The mechanism is entirely thermal. An overload drives excess current through the device; by Ohm's law the power dissipated in it rises, the part heats, and its resistance goes up, which is a self-protecting action.[373][W2o8otvVML4] Because it depends on heating a physical mass, it is slow, with the same kind of thermal inertia as a fuse.[373] Plotted against time, the input current jumps almost vertically at the moment of the fault and then rolls off as the PTC warms, until the resistance is high enough that essentially no current flows and the meter is protected.[373]

That thermal lag is also the device's principal weakness. Immediately after a fault the PTC is still at its low nominal resistance, so a large inrush current flows until it has had time to heat.[1378] Against a fast high-voltage transient it does nothing useful at all: a 1000 V CAT III meter is designed to survive 6 or 8 kV transients, and the PTC does not have time to heat and raise its resistance on that timescale.[373] It is there for the slow stuff — for instance a 1000 V meter being ramped slowly up to 2000 V.[373]

## Place in a multimeter input chain

A conventional input chain puts a high-voltage, high-power series resistor permanently in line with the input jack, followed by the PTC.[373] In the Fluke 27 that resistor is a 3.5 k wirewound part, high temperature and high power, feeding a nominal 1 k thermistor.[373] The typical modern equivalent is a 1 k resistor, often specified as fusible, in series with a PTC.[W2o8otvVML4][1655][1692] Multimeter chipset datasheets specify the arrangement directly, in one case calling for a PTC plus a 100 ohm resistor.[W2o8otvVML4]

The series resistor is not optional padding. When a MOV exceeds its rated voltage — 430 V in the Fluke 27 — it clamps hard, goes low impedance and shunts the fault current, which is what heats the PTC in the first place.[373] The MOV does not absorb that energy by magic: it flows through the series resistors as well, so they must survive the dissipation during the interval before the PTC has risen in value.[373] A design with four PTCs and three MOVs but no high-voltage surge resistor in series is therefore still deficient, because every clamp in the circuit is relying on the PTC to heat up.[1378]

Downstream of the PTC, a Zener clamp typically holds the sensitive node at a low voltage — of the order of 7 to 10 V — while the PTC handles the energy.[1667][1655] Some designs clamp both input paths down to the common terminal with MOVs instead, each path consisting of a 1 k high-voltage input resistor and a PTC of roughly 1 k to 1.5 k.[1667] Higher-voltage instruments stack PTCs: a 2000 V meter used two in series on each of two paths because a single part with an adequate voltage rating was not available.[1734]

## Ohms and low-impedance modes

Because a PTC is in series with the input on the ohms range as well, a decent meter's ohms range tolerates high-voltage overload and behaves much like a dedicated low-impedance mode.[1655] This is the basis for discharging a charged capacitor bank with a multimeter: switching to ohms places an internal PTC across the capacitor, and the bank is bled down safely without damaging the meter.[1655] In ohms mode the path runs through a PTC and a 1 k resistor, often a fusible one, before reaching the clamping circuit.[1655] A meter with an explicit low-Z mode does the same thing deliberately, switching a 1 k series resistor and a PTC across the input.[1655]

One low-Z implementation puts two 1.5 k PTCs across the input, which is close to ideal for the job: the value is low enough to discharge the bank but not so low that current is excessive, and the resistance rises as the parts heat.[1655] Fixed 1.5 k resistors in the same position would burn up quickly, whereas the PTCs self-regulate as their dissipation rises.[1655] The arithmetic is worth doing — 240 V across roughly 2.25 k of series resistance — which is exactly why the resistance must be allowed to climb, and why the physically larger of two PTCs on a board, being able to dissipate more heat, is the one that belongs in the low-impedance path.[1667] Applying 240 V mains to a meter in ohms mode causes the PTCs to warm noticeably during the test.[1731][uC-q-FjXEsA]

Conversely, in a fault where the meter was in ohms mode, the input protection resistors and PTCs make it implausible for enough current to pass to arc a range-switch contact.[m3sQHx5aMmU]

## As an indicator of design quality

On the voltage side the PTC is one of the two keys to input protection, the other being the MOV.[373] A meter opened up and found to lack a PTC, a MOV, a bridge rectifier and HRC fuses is not built to the standard of a quality Fluke; at minimum, if there is no PTC or thermistor in series with the input — and the trace is usually easy to follow — the design is not fit to be trusted.[373] Even a two- or five-dollar meter normally has a single PTC in series.[W2o8otvVML4]

Observed implementations span the range. The Fluke 27 uses a black PTC thermistor for the slower overload condition alongside five MOVs, four of them in series to raise the working voltage.[372] The Fluke 287 has a fusible input resistor, a PTC and three MOVs, with a further PTC position left unpopulated.[1592] The Keysight U1282A puts a PTC thermistor on the ground terminal next to a four-diode bridge, and another on the positive input feeding a large heat-shrunk power resistor, with gas discharge tubes shunting to ground on either side of it.[832] A 50 dollar meter was found with two PTCs and five MOVs against three MOVs and one PTC in the Fluke it was compared with, the extra PTC apparently serving an additional input path.[1731] Other units carry three MOVs and one PTC[1723], three MOVs and a couple of PTCs[3kdYGneg9xI], or two PTCs and three MOVs at a lower CAT rating.[dv0B_WqL7w4]

At the thin end, several meters offer a single PTC and nothing else — one with carbon film resistors and no MOVs at all[99], one presented as CAT III 600 V with a single 1.5 k PTC[1704], one relying on a lone PTC placed on the switched side of a latching relay[1096], and one budget unit whose only protection device was a PTC tucked on the far side of the board by the battery contact.[1007] Others have input protection resistors but no MOVs or PTCs whatsoever.[648][775] An oscilloscope-multimeter was found with no PTC and no input protection of any kind on its volts-ohms-amps jack, the input running straight into the resistor divider.[W2o8otvVML4]

## Physical construction and failure

Because the part works by getting hot and often sits at mains potential, its mechanical treatment matters. Good practice is heat shrink over the body and generous spacing[1083][832]; poor practice shows up quickly. A PTC leg left within less than a millimetre of an input jack will move with rough handling until it is effectively touching, and although a PTC is low resistance in normal operation, a short across it in that position defeats it precisely when a fault occurs.[151] In one 2000 V meter the input PTC sits barely 4 mm from ground with no creepage slot, which works until solder mask degrades or moisture gets under it.[1734] A production bench meter was found with a flying lead running from a power resistor to the PTC, on a footprint intended for a surface-mount part.[829] One PTC was seen chipped away on its underside among crowded, folded-over input components[99], another had a dry solder joint on the input side[e98cYNPUxcg], and in a drop-test teardown a PTC had fallen out of the board entirely, leaving the input unprotected.[868] Under a deliberate high-energy overload, MOVs blow and the PTCs are visibly the worse for it.[373]

## Other applications

The same device appears outside meter front ends. It is used in series with the mains ahead of a bridge rectifier in switch-mode supplies, in coffee machines and instrument power supplies alongside the X- and Y-class capacitors and common-mode chokes.[1452][1161][1431][1388] That position is a common source of misidentification, since the visually similar part in a mains inrush role may be an NTC — negative temperature coefficient — rather than a PTC.[e98cYNPUxcg] PTCs also appear in battery packs, mounted in good thermal contact with a cell terminal and covered with heat shrink, in series with the cell string.[XpZVIWdXliY]
