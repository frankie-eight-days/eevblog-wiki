# usb

USB is the near-universal wired interface for connecting electronics to a host computer, carrying both a data pair and a 5 V power rail on the same connector. For bench electronics it plays three largely separate roles: a low-power supply for small circuits and tools, a control and file-transfer link for test instruments, and a programming and firmware-update path for microcontrollers.[158][285][704][1306] Each of those roles brings its own constraints — a hard current budget, an unavoidable tie to the host's mains earth, and a data rate that dictates PCB practice.[158][279][12]

## The 5 V rail and its budget

The bus rail is 5 V nominal with a ±5% tolerance, so a compliant port may sit as low as 4.75 V.[158] A standard port supplies 500 mA at 5 V, or 2.5 W, which is the ceiling any USB-powered design must be engineered around.[158][285] A device is supposed to negotiate for that current and signal back to the host that it wants 500 mA, but in practice almost all ports will supply it regardless of whether the negotiation happens; doing it properly is a nicety of the standard rather than a precondition for getting the current.[827]

Cable and connector losses erode the rail further. A DC-DC module specified for a 4.5 V minimum input can easily be pushed below that limit by the drop of a USB cable under load.[324] The same margin problem appears in linear regulators fed from USB: an LM317-class part drawing 500 mA at room temperature drops around 1.75 to 1.8 V, so producing 3.3 V requires about 5.1 V in — technically more than a 5 V USB port is obliged to deliver, and a worst-case design would be characterised at 4.75 V input.[158] The practical consequence is that USB-powered supplies are viable only for small loads: a nominal 8 W soldering iron cannot reach 8 W from a 5 V 1 A outlet, delivering perhaps 5 W before the source's protection intervenes.[1113]

Higher-power operation depends entirely on the source rather than the connector. A USB PD portable iron rated at 18 W, or one advertised at 65 W, will only reach that figure if the battery bank or adapter behind it can support it.[1319][1543] Where a design carries its own PD negotiation, it is typically handled by a dedicated processor on the USB side, separate from the one running the user interface.[1264]

Charging behaviour is a common design failure point. A product with a micro USB connector that draws power should charge from a standard half-amp port even if only at a reduced rate; devices that will only do a data connection when plugged into a PC, and demand their bundled 1.8 A adapter to charge at all, are functionally broken in the field.[220] Downstream power budgets can also collapse: a Raspberry Pi Power over Ethernet HAT powered the board correctly but shut down the USB ports as soon as anything was plugged into them.[1122] Power should flow only outward from a hub to its ports — a hub feeding its wall-wart voltage back up to the host is a fault condition with serious consequences for the PC.[97]

## Signalling, enumeration and throughput

The interface uses a differential pair, data plus and data minus, but the lines carry more than differential data: the speed grade is signalled by pull-up and pull-down resistors on those lines, and is established when the host first talks to the device rather than being negotiated later in software.[ql7J_tFBVfY] Because devices on one port share the data lines, the bus must arbitrate priority between them — a webcam and a mouse on the same port are competing for the same bandwidth, in a scheme with priority levels comparable to Ethernet.[ql7J_tFBVfY] Contention is visible in practice as jerky or laggy behaviour when several devices share a bus.[VTAwzrKPjeE]

Enumeration is a precondition for function, not a formality. An infrared optical touchscreen monitor produced no signals at all on its internal buses until the USB link to the PC was connected and enumeration completed.[1346] At the firmware level, a transmit routine will find the bus busy and simply return failure, leaving retry logic to the layer above.[-W5zEtqJJIo]

Throughput sets the character of USB-based instruments. A USB 2 logic analyser is limited to something on the order of 20 MSa/s rather than the gigasample rates of dedicated hardware, but in exchange it has effectively unlimited memory because it streams into the host's storage.[MC9_4mpqVgU] The same trade applies to USB oscilloscopes, where streaming directly to the PC substitutes for deep internal capture memory.[521] The weakness of a streaming architecture with only a small intermediate buffer is that another device hogging the port can cause a buffer overrun and dropped samples; an instrument that loses data without telling the software is committing a cardinal sin, and this is the argument for a real logic analyser with its own capture memory.[436] USB 3 runs at 5 Gbit/s, and pushing close to the limits of the data rate makes the physical path matter — an adapter or a ribbon-cable extension can be enough to stop a high-rate device working.[207][1488]

High-speed signalling also constrains the board. A hub built on cheap single-sided phenolic rather than FR4 has no ground plane and no controlled-impedance routing for the high-speed lines, and pairing that with a ceramic resonator in place of a crystal to save a fraction of a cent removes what little margin remains.[12] Cable quality is similarly load-bearing: a device that refuses to connect on an arbitrary cable will often work immediately on the thicker one supplied with it.[1720]

## Ground reference and isolation

A USB connection ties the device to the host computer's mains earth. Connecting a USB lead to an otherwise battery-powered board instantly earth-references it, which is the mechanism behind a great many destroyed oscilloscope front ends and damaged power supplies when a probe ground lead is then attached elsewhere in the circuit.[279]

Instrument designers therefore isolate the port or the circuitry behind it. A function generator's USB is placed on the main processing side and separated from the outputs by digital isolators with their own supply crossing the barrier.[497] In a programmable bench supply, the USB, Ethernet, RS232 and logic outputs share a common ground that is optically isolated from the supply outputs, with a small dedicated transformer tap powering that section.[511] A bench supply's USB may still be electrically connected directly to mains earth even while being optically isolated from the output.[315] Development boards sometimes ship in an ISO variant whose only difference is an isolated USB port.[900]

Isolation becomes impractical as speed rises. GPIB and RS-232 can be isolated cheaply because they are simple UART-like interfaces that a protocol chip can carry across a barrier, but USB and LAN cannot be isolated at their operating speeds, which forced at least one instrument family to relocate its processing to a grounded back end next to the connectors.[1032] USB 3 is harder to isolate than USB 2, and instruments running the full 5 Gbit/s generally do not isolate it at all.[207]

Safety certification pushes the same way in handheld instruments. Multimeters generally do not carry a USB port, because any externally accessible metal is a potential safety hazard on a CAT-rated instrument and would not pass third-party certification.[D2PANd9Hu3U] Where a USB connector does appear on a high-voltage instrument, it tends to be deeply recessed so the user cannot touch it or plug in an unapproved adapter.[468]

## The instrument interface

The standard arrangement on modern test gear is a USB device port for host control alongside a USB host port for a memory stick, often duplicated front and rear so the front panel can be kept clear.[143][823][199] The host port is used for screen captures, waveform and setup files — commonly saved as PNG rather than bitmap — and increasingly wired to a single-button quick-save action.[704][1566][1638] It is also the normal firmware upgrade path, with the image loaded from a stick as an alternative to a network update.[209][1545]

For programmatic control, instruments present as a USB Test and Measurement device and accept SCPI commands, though the USB hardware may need to be enabled in the instrument's own menus before the host will even see it enumerate.[862] The link is not confined to PC control: an oscilloscope can drive an external function generator over USB to perform frequency-response sweeps, selecting between an internal generator, USB or LAN as the source.[LbqnHtNPt9Y] A host port that accepts arbitrary HID devices extends further — a mouse for on-screen navigation on an inspection microscope, or a USB-C touchscreen monitor acting as the touch input for a scope, with a hub tolerated on a single port.[1125][r_BYYgCqScE]

The most user-friendly implementation puts the control software on the instrument itself, so that plugging it in presents a drive from which the application runs with no installation on the host machine.[876] The opposite failure is equally instructive: drivers must generally be installed before the device is first plugged in, or the software will not find it.[817]

## Programming, bootloaders and serial bridges

Much of USB's presence in hobby and professional electronics is as a serial replacement. A USB-to-serial bridge provides a simple serial interface to a USB port, which is why such chips became the default way to add USB to a product whose microcontroller already has a UART.[676] An Arduino talks to the PC through a standard USB port that behaves as a serial port, with a separate dedicated device — an ATmega8U2 alongside the main processor, for instance — handling the USB end.[45][326] The same pattern appears in instruments where an FTDI part simply dumps a sample buffer out to the port with no processing in between.[1104]

Many microcontrollers remove the bridge entirely by exposing a bootloader over USB. STM32 parts ship pre-programmed with a bootloader and support Device Firmware Upgrade, so a blank board can be programmed by connecting it to USB and talking to the bootloader directly, without a dedicated programmer.[1306][1264] Programmers themselves are USB devices, and the port is usually able to power the small target circuit as well as program it, which is convenient enough to be the normal working arrangement.[158][635]

## Data paths and security

Because everything crossing the USB link is visible to a potentially compromised host, hardware wallets are designed around keeping secrets off the bus. The recovery seed is generated inside the device and displayed on its own screen, and never travels over USB, so malware on the computer cannot read it during setup.[1048] Where a backup is written to a microSD card, it is written directly from the device's internal processor to the card rather than passing through the host, meaning an attacker would need physical possession of that card.[1075] The corresponding weakness is any secret that must be typed on the computer: a PIN or password entered on the host travels over USB and is therefore weaker than entry on the device's own screen.[1075] Firmware upgrade over USB is still supported on such devices, but arranged so that flashing unsigned firmware wipes the stored keys.[1006]

## Connectors and mechanical practice

The connector standard is only as useful as its implementation. A camera that charges internally and ships with a USB cable, but only works with its own proprietary variant of that cable, discards the entire benefit of a standard interface.[578] Reversible micro USB cables solve the orientation problem mechanically, with bevelling on both sides of the plug and symmetrical contacts in the centre so either orientation mates correctly.[839] Layout matters too: putting a high-speed USB serial interface in the far corner of a board, remote from the devices it must talk to, is a poor decision when the routing could be short.[137] At the extreme, industrial design can eliminate the port altogether — a machine made thin enough that a traditional USB connector will not fit is also thin enough to have unupgradable memory and storage.[k4zrmTsZj_s]

USB generation is also a useful dating clue for unknown hardware; Dave Jones treats the presence of USB 3 as a minimum bar when deciding whether a discarded PC is worth recovering.[1615]
