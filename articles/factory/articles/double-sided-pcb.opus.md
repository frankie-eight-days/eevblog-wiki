# double sided pcb

A double-sided PCB, also called a two-layer board, carries copper on both faces of the substrate with no internal layers.[116][1176] It is the workhorse construction for most low- and medium-complexity electronics: cheap enough to be near-free from prototype houses, dense enough to route a complete microcomputer, and limited enough that its shortcomings — no dedicated ground plane, no buried power distribution — define a whole body of layout practice.[1176][1081][244] The term is ambiguous in one important respect: it can mean two copper layers, or it can mean components loaded on both faces, and these are independent choices.[936][478]

## Construction and the plated-through-hole distinction

The defining feature is copper on top and bottom, with the two sides joined electrically. Modern boards use plated through holes, so a hole doubles as a layer-to-layer connection.[1705][111] Older and cheaper boards frequently skipped the plating: the board was etched on both sides but the vias were formed by hand, with a wire link soldered on both faces, or by a component lead soldered top and bottom to serve as the interlayer join.[256][438] One keyboard board used exactly this compromise, taking the double-sided process purely to get a ground plane on the top face while declining to pay for through-hole plating, so every layer transition became a soldered link.[256] A related trick is a board that is single-sided in the true sense but built up with a second, separately applied copper layer for jumpers, bridged and masked as an extra process step rather than drilled and plated.[936][541]

Board thickness is a design variable rather than a constant. Standard stock is 1.6 mm, but thin two-layer boards down to 0.8 mm or 0.5 mm are used where the finished rigidity requirement permits.[243][130] Etching resolution for home-made double-sided boards using pre-sensitized photoresist and a transparency overlay reached roughly 8 thou track and 8 thou spacing, and turnaround could be an hour or two.[1197][1259] Commercial fabrication of a double-sided board can be turned around in 24 hours, against 48 hours for a four-layer and about six working days for an eight-layer board, most of that engineering and tooling rather than processing.[358] Milling machines can produce double-sided boards without plated through holes or solder mask, which makes assembly harder.[1565]

## Orthogonal routing

The dominant layout convention for two-layer boards is to dedicate each layer to one routing direction: all traces predominantly horizontal on one side, predominantly vertical on the other, with vias handling the turns.[410][452][955][759] This is what makes a large, densely populated digital board routable on two layers at all; attempting it without a directional discipline collapses quickly.[1313] The pattern is visible in essentially every classic through-hole digital board — the Apple 1, the Amiga 500, the TRS-80 Model I, the Gigatron TTL computer.[911][438][645][1176] Autorouting algorithms are built around it, and CAD packages expose the per-layer preferred direction as a setting.[410][1313]

Layer count changes the character of the problem entirely. With eight layers, routing options open up dramatically; on two layers, the constraint is severe enough that resistors are deliberately used as jumpers, letting a single component hop four, five, or six traces at once and materially improving routing density.[244] A working goal on a two-layer layout is to route as much as possible on the top layer before resorting to the bottom, keeping the second layer free for the connections that genuinely need it.[244][974] Some two-layer designs come so close to being single-sided that the difference amounts to a dozen jumper links.[244]

For fine-pitch BGA parts the layer count determines whether a fan-out is even possible. A 36-pin BGA with only two rings of pads can be completely fanned out on one layer, which is what allows it onto a double-sided board rather than forcing a multilayer stackup.[193]

## Autorouters on two layers

Automatic routers perform poorly on simple double-sided boards, and the simpler the board, the worse the result tends to be.[93] A fairly average double-sided board of modest complexity, with a few awkward cross-routing situations, defeated a commercial autorouter in direct comparison with a manual layout of the same design.[975] A dedicated AI routing service, whose beta supported two layers, produced output that failed the basic orthogonal-direction rule that governs double-sided routing.[1535] Autorouters are capable tools under heavily constrained setups — potentially a week of constraint definition before the button is pressed — but they are not a substitute for placement and manual routing judgment.[1535][975]

## Electrical limitations

The central weakness of a double-sided board is that neither layer can be given over entirely to a solid, low-impedance, low-inductance ground plane once signals must be routed.[1081] Both faces are signal layers with ground reduced to flood fill and occasional stitching, so return-current loop areas cannot be kept tight across a whole board.[1081][1176] With two dozen chips spread over a large board it is not possible for every trace to have a short loop area, and loop area is the governing quantity for radiated emissions.[1176]

Measured against an electrically identical four-layer version of the same layout, a two-layer Gigatron board showed roughly 15 dB more broadband noise on near-field probing, and would perform worse in a full compliance test across the spectrum.[1176] The blunt summary of two-layer EMC behaviour is that it radiates like buggery.[1176] Bypass capacitors cannot repair this: switching artefacts traceable to a shared ground return are a structural property of the board, not a decoupling deficiency, and would be largely suppressed by stitching the pins together on a four-layer board.[1081]

This does not make two-layer boards unusable. Careful — and somewhat lucky — layout can approach four-layer performance on two layers.[1176] The useful practices are keeping ground and power traces stacked directly over one another wherever possible, not splitting the ground, and flood-filling ground as the default rather than star-pointing it.[1176][500] On a two-layer board the VCC track to a bypass capacitor should be routed as a priority, because there is no plane to fall back on.[1365] Where a big ground plane is wanted under a chip, a double-sided board allows planes on both faces to be strapped together.[23] Via stitching between the two layers is also used for current handling: for high-current traces, doubling the copper across both layers helps, and although stitching does little for resistance it does assist thermal dissipation.[1559][1034]

There are designs where two layers simply will not do. Fast ECL logic drawing large switching currents relies on the power and ground planes acting as one distributed low-inductance capacitor; such a circuit would almost certainly not work on a two-layer board because the edges are too fast and the current gulps too large.[452] Compliance requirements have historically forced the same conclusion: an early portable computer built on a double-sided board with no substantial ground plane was re-spun as a multilayer design to meet FCC limits.[955]

## The cost boundary against single-sided

Single-sided board remains cheaper in production, and the gap is large enough that high-volume consumer products go to remarkable lengths to stay on one layer.[LAbWsqS51Tk] Zero-ohm jumpers are the usual instrument, and one speaker board used what amounted to a whole reel of them: "I have never seen so many zero-ohm jumpers before in my life."[LAbWsqS51Tk][1672] The same calculation appears with hand-fitted jumper wires on a single-sided consumer product, and with a run of wire links added because a decent ground could not be got across the layout — extra assembly labour judged cheaper than a double-sided layout.[752][1243] A keyboard design avoided the double-sided board by moving to a carbon process, which also removed the gold flash and discrete resistors from the bill of materials.[1108] Going double-sided also costs more than copper alone: on a thermal design it brings via holes, thermal bonding to a heatsink, and the associated process steps, all of which can be avoided if a single-sided pad is adequate.[533]

Counter-intuitively, a single-layer board is sometimes quoted higher than two layers, because the manufacturer's stock material is double-sided and the unwanted copper must be etched away.[1259] At prototype quantities the cost of two layers has collapsed to the point where a professional double-sided board with solder mask and silkscreen costs a few dollars, against roughly $500 for a single board when a full panel had to be purchased.[9iXFhKUa1BU][mW-1FMGpsFg] The standard prototype order is a two-layer board with top and bottom copper, both solder masks and both silkscreen overlays, with no paste stencil when assembly is by hand.[990] Two-layer flex is fabricated the same routine way.[1262]

Layer count also propagates back into component selection. Placing an LCD driver chip directly against the display pins can allow a two-layer board where a four-layer board would otherwise be needed, and at consumer volumes that difference in cents matters.[1074]

## Double-sided component loading

Separately from copper layers, "double-sided load" means components populated on both faces. It is a near-universal consequence of small form factors: a smartwatch, a compact audio interface, a densely packed test instrument, an oscilloscope module board with bypass caps that must sit directly under the BGA.[761][1405][478][342] The usual pattern is active devices on top and passives on the bottom.[478] The trade-offs are additional assembly cost and thickness — laying components on both sides of a board adds on the order of 30 to 40 percent to the assembled thickness, which was one of the compromises separating an early analogue mobile phone from a later single-sided-load design.[243]

For that reason, single-sided loading is often specified deliberately. Professional equipment intended to be serviced may forbid chips on the bottom, allowing passives only, which constrains placement severely.[598] A board that must sit flat in its case is easier to design with everything on top.[833] Where a heatsinked surface-mount part could be handled by flipping the board over, the cost of doing so is precisely that it forces a double-sided load.[744] Cost comparisons between competing instruments turn on this too: a single-sided-load design has lower construction cost even where its components are individually more expensive.[1447]

## Assembly, rework and repair

On double-sided through-hole boards the solder should wick completely through the plated hole and appear on the opposite face; that through-wicking is the mark of a sound joint.[183] Even a very small solder feed of 0.46 mm wire is enough to achieve it on a normal DIP pin.[183] A low-power iron can manage a DIP package on a double-sided board while failing entirely on a ground plane.[1113]

Desoldering effort scales with copper mass. Two layers of ground fill around a power package already represent substantial thermal mass on both faces, and hot-air rework must supply enough energy to heat all of it; a multilayer board connected to internal planes is considerably worse.[1322] Desoldering stations need noticeably longer dwell on multilayer boards than on a double-layer board, and may require raising the tip temperature from 350 °C to around 380 °C.[542]

## Historical prevalence

Double-sided construction with no internal planes was the standard for late-1970s and early-1980s computing and instrumentation, and the layouts achieved on it are often remarkable given the constraint.[116][1021] Examples run from the TRS-80 Model 100 processor board and the TRS-80 Color Computer through the Cambridge Z88, the Sinclair flat-screen pocket television, the Apple IIc, the Olivetti Programma 101, and Intel's 8085 design kit.[116][1021][382][554][788][1705][1308] Early boards of this type were hand-taped, laid out with black tape on film rather than in CAD.[1126] Older instrument boards show tin roll-plated traces applied before solder mask, producing the characteristic crinkled raised tracks, or the classic green crinkly solder mask finish.[645][265] Where boards were populated densely on two layers, the routing was frequently close to optimal and remains difficult work to reproduce.[645][899]
