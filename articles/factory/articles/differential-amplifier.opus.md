# differential amplifier

A differential amplifier is an amplifier that measures the voltage difference between its two input terminals and multiplies that difference by a gain, without reference to ground.[1368][1521] There is essentially no ground reference at the input: a differential amplifier with a gain of 10 fed 1 V of differential signal produces 10 V out regardless of where that pair of inputs sits in the circuit.[1521] It is one of the most-used analogue building blocks in instrumentation, appearing wherever a small signal must be pulled out from on top of a large or noisy reference — current shunts, thermocouples, strain gauges, oscilloscope front ends and high voltage probes.[221][419][722][675][932]

## Relationship to the operational amplifier

An op-amp is itself a differential amplifier: two inputs, one output, and an internal gain applied to the difference between them.[600] That gain — the open-loop or large DC voltage gain, quoted in data sheets in dB and working out to roughly 100,000 for a common part — is enormous, drifts with temperature and varies between devices, which is exactly why a bare op-amp cannot be used as a practical differential amplifier despite being one.[600] The gain has to be tamed with negative feedback before it becomes useful.[600]

Taking the inverting configuration and replacing the fixed bias voltage on the non-inverting input with a second signal input turns the circuit into a differential amplifier whose gain, as in the inverting case, is set by the resistor ratio −R2/R1: 10 k over 1 k gives a gain of −10, so 1 V and 1.1 V at the inputs — a 0.1 V difference — yield −1 V out.[600] The circuit is modest but serviceable: "It's not a terrific differential amplifier, but it works."[600] This single op-amp topology is the default roll-your-own diff amp and turns up throughout commercial designs.[221][1415]

## Topologies

Beyond the single op-amp version, several arrangements recur:

- **Three op-amp (instrumentation amplifier).** The traditional form used in cold junction compensation ICs, where the three op-amp differential amplifier is packaged alongside a trimmed internal temperature sensor and a compensation network matched to a particular thermocouple type such as type K.[419] An instrumentation amplifier responds to differential variation between two conductors while ignoring common mode changes entirely, so substantial common mode noise on the pair appears nowhere at the output.[616]
- **Discrete transistor.** Discrete differential amplifiers are common in analogue video and RF gear, including vacuum tube implementations in older microwave equipment.[574][XqakD0dXdjM] Matched dual transistor pairs — parts intended for ultra-low-noise audio work — are used to give a well-matched differential input stage.[731] Scope front ends favour a matched JFET pair as a roll-your-own JFET input differential amplifier.[932]
- **Dedicated parts.** Off-the-shelf instrumentation amplifiers such as the AD620 buy precision at a price, and are not always worth it.[221] Purpose-built current sense amplifiers are differential amplifiers with a fixed gain: a MAX4080F measures across a high-side shunt at a fixed gain of five to 0.1% and outputs a ground-referenced voltage.[232] Some converters integrate them, as with a three-channel 16-bit sigma-delta part carrying built-in differential amplifiers and a 4 ppm reference.[828]

The choice between an instrumentation amplifier and a rolled-own differential amplifier built from op-amps turns on signal level: for very low-level differential sources such as a strain gauge, the instrumentation amplifier — often with adjustable gain — is the right part.[500][722] The choice between a differential and a single-ended front end is a cost and geometry question; where the current shunt sits on the same board right next to the amplifier chip, the differential input earns less.[72][87] Differential amplifiers are also intrinsically noisier than a good single-ended amplifier, which is a real argument against reaching for one by default.[616]

## Common mode rejection

Any differential amplifier circuit, whether an op-amp or a discrete transistor design, has a common mode rejection ratio.[1521] CMRR is simply the ratio of the amplifier's differential gain to its common mode gain.[1521] It is normally taken on faith from the data sheet rather than verified, though it can be measured directly.[1521]

In a high voltage differential probe — which is nothing more than a differential amplifier with a gain of ×10 or ×100 chosen to offset the input attenuation — the dominant limit on CMRR is the matching of the input resistor divider strings.[932][1368][1744] If one string is unbalanced against the other, CMRR suffers, which is why such probes carry a CMRR trimmer alongside a gain trimmer.[1415] Ordinary component tolerance in the divider and in the amplifier gain is enough to throw a CMRR reading well out unless every element is tweaked.[1744] At higher frequencies the divider resistors become hard to compensate and the response degrades; above 10 MHz, rejection falls off sharply, which is why manufacturers stop quoting figures at 50 or 70 MHz.[1521][Dez9KG6whb0]

The rolloff of rejection with frequency is not a differential-specific pathology — it is ordinary amplifier behaviour.[1521]

Measurement wiring matters as much as the amplifier. Untwisted input leads let the common mode signal differ between the two conductors, and the resulting error can wreck a low-level measurement regardless of whether the front end is a probe, an op-amp differential amplifier or a discrete transistor circuit.[1521]

## Current measurement

The most common application is reading the voltage across a current shunt. A very low value shunt — 0.1 Ω or 10 mΩ — is placed in series with the load so that it dissipates little in normal operation, and a differential amplifier across it converts the shunt drop into a usable, ground-referenced signal.[1688] With a 1 Ω shunt, 1 A produces 1 V across the resistor and 1 V at the amplifier output.[221]

A single op-amp differential amplifier is often good enough here. In a bench supply design an LM358 was used deliberately in place of a precision low-offset part, because the required current resolution did not justify the precision; the LM358's several millivolts of offset was then compensated by raising the shunt value.[259] The same output feeds a microcontroller ADC channel directly, giving 0 to 1 V for 0 to 1 A, or 1 mV per milliamp, off a 1 Ω shunt.[259] Combined with an INA219, that arrangement produced a five-range current measurement capability spanning 1 A down to 10 µA resolution.[259] A similar µSupply design pairs a 1 Ω shunt with an ordinary rail-to-rail automotive-grade op-amp as the differential amplifier rather than a dedicated high-side current shunt amplifier.[1561]

The same pattern recurs across instruments and consumer hardware: a 10 mΩ four-terminal sense resistor feeding a differential amplifier in a multimeter[173], a four-terminal shunt in a 600 V PSU routed back to a diff amp[814], AMP-03 differential amplifiers reading the output shunt in a source measure unit[718], an external diff amp with a gain of 50 on a development board[900], and integrated shunt-plus-differential-amplifier front ends inside lithium battery charger ICs.[176] Where the shunt and its amplifier are separated by a connector and cable rather than sitting together, the arrangement works but is not the preferred practice: the differential sense amplifier belongs right at the shunt.[814]

Battery capacity measurement needs two differential amplifiers — one measuring the cell voltage directly across the cell, one measuring current — with both logged over time so watt-hours can be accumulated.[141]

## Signal chains and front ends

Oscilloscope input channels are built around a differential stage. A typical front end AC-couples the input into a JFET amplifier with a low-impedance emitter-follower output, which then drives the differential amplifier whose differential output goes straight into the ADC.[675] Bandwidth limiting is sometimes implemented inside the differential amplifier itself, by switching series resistance in the stage — a 200 Ω versus 680 Ω difference between otherwise identical paths marking the 20 MHz bandwidth filter.[675] Because the differential amplifier drives the downstream ADC board from a low source impedance, running that analogue signal across inner layers to another board is not an interference problem.[1639]

High voltage differential probes follow a fixed architecture: high voltage resistor divider string, FET input buffer, differential amplifier, then an output driver into the scope BNC.[1415][1414][1631] Because the two halves are symmetric, reverse engineering one half is sufficient — the other is a duplicate.[1415] Implementations vary in the input devices, from discrete FETs to high-speed FET input op-amps such as the THS4631.[1415][1414] A probe lacking that structure — no differential input, no FET input into a differential amplifier configuration — is not a differential probe at all, whatever its other merits.[1557]

Elsewhere, differential amplifiers appear as the low-noise input stage of a power meter, shielded inside a metal can, with the amplified low-impedance output driven out to the ADC where interference no longer matters[1693]; as the video input stage of an analogue TV IF modulator, where it prevents hum from being superimposed on the video by hum current in the ground line[574]; in delay line output amplification[386]; in timer-counter input stages alongside comparators and dividers[265]; and as the front end of ECG instrumentation, where the useful probing point is the final scaled output rather than the differential amplifier itself.[660][1008]
