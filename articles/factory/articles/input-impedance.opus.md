# input impedance

Input impedance is the effective impedance a measuring instrument or amplifier input presents to the circuit it is connected to. Because that impedance appears in parallel with whatever is being measured, it changes the circuit under test, and the resulting error is one of the most common traps in practical measurement.[1399][286][1747] The consequences run from a few least-significant digits of error on a low-impedance source to readings that are wrong by an order of magnitude in high-impedance circuits, so the number matters as much as any accuracy specification on the front panel.[1067][1747]

## Circuit loading and the order-of-magnitude rule

A digital multimeter does not have infinite input impedance; it is roughly 10 megaohms for most instruments.[286] Placing 10 megaohms across a 1 megaohm resistor in a divider causes a measurement error.[286] The rule of thumb is that one order of magnitude between the circuit impedance and the meter impedance gives roughly 10% error, two orders of magnitude gives about 1%, and once the instrument is up in the gigaohms the loading is not really going to affect the result at all.[1399] In a divider of 1 megaohm and 166.6k, an 11 megaohm meter across the 1 megaohm leg produces a calculated 1.428 V where the unloaded division would give more, which is exactly what a meter reads out.[1399]

The same arithmetic explains why the effect is invisible most of the time. With 100 ohms in series feeding a 10 megaohm meter the error is negligible; the source impedance has to fall to around 100k before that 100 ohms costs about 0.1%.[72] Conversely, a meter with only 120k input impedance placed across a node dominated by a 10 megaohm path collapses the reading — 10 megaohms in parallel with 120k is essentially 120k, and a 5 V reading drops to 0.33 V, then to 0.08 V on a lower range, then to zero.[1747]

Loading also interacts with leakage. A reverse-biased PN diode with roughly 473 nanoamps of leakage develops nearly 5 V across a 10 megaohm meter input, which is why a diode that is supposed to block appears to pass voltage; a Schottky with 10 nanoamps of leakage produces far less.[1747]

## Multimeter practice

Ten megaohms, or thereabouts, is the standard on almost every voltage range, AC or DC.[1747] Real instruments cluster around 10 to 11 megaohms depending on the front-end design.[1399] Measured examples include 11.1 megaohms on a Brymen BM786,[1399] 11 megaohms on the 2 V range of an Amprobe AM220 falling to 10 on higher ranges,[91] 20 megaohms on a Uni-T UT892 2000 V meter,[1734] and 9.9 megaohms measured in circuit on a Keysight U1273AX with the possibility of about 11 megaohms total once a second resistor is included.[1564] A buying guide criterion is at least 10 megaohms; cheap meters down at one or two megaohms should be avoided.[75] Because the front end is high impedance, the series input protection resistors ahead of the chipset do not need to be precision parts — 1% is adequate.[853]

Millivolt ranges are frequently the exception, and are often higher: 27 megaohms on the millivolt range of an Amprobe AM220 and 17 megaohms on an Extech EX330, both dropping to about 10 megaohms elsewhere.[91] A top-of-the-line meter will have very high input impedance on the low millivolt ranges — greater than 1 gigaohm below 1 volt on an Agilent U1253A.[56] Not every meter has it, and some handhelds advertised as having it do not deliver: one $50 meter measures 10 megaohms on millivolt mode where the comparable Fluke is genuinely high impedance.[1731]

MOV-based overload protection sits across the input and leaks, so the nominal impedance is not perfectly constant with voltage. On a Brymen BM2257 the nominal 10 megaohms measures 10.02 megaohms at 1100 V and falls only to about 9.63 megaohms at the top of the range, which is not a meaningful departure.[rT0g1QmKE5E] A degraded connection inside the input path is a different matter: on a Tektronix DMM916 a loose fastener created a high-impedance path in a 10 megaohm divider and produced readings that were slightly low.[1576]

## High-impedance and low-impedance modes

Precision meters offer a selectable high-impedance mode, usually presented as a choice between 10 meg and auto.[1399][489] In auto the input resistance is effectively removed and the impedance is set by the FET input amplifier — gigaohms, specified in one data sheet as greater than about 2 gigaohms.[584][1399] The mode is not always available across the full range set: one 6.5-digit Agilent meter allows effectively infinite input impedance up to the 10 V range but not on the hundreds-of-volts ranges,[584] and a Prema 6047 has it on the 200 mV, 2 V and 20 V ranges but loses it at 200 V.[613] Some bench meters do not offer it at all despite otherwise good specifications.[658] Bench meters are widely assumed to be high impedance on more ranges than they actually are; a Keysight bench meter sits at 10 megaohms on ordinary voltage ranges.[1379]

The trade-off runs the other way. A very high input impedance picks up everything: with the leads flapping, readings drift with lead position, hand proximity and location in the lab, and twisting the leads significantly reduces the pickup.[1379] On a high-impedance input the display charges up and can walk all the way to overload with nothing connected.[489][1379] Multimeter low ranges can be effectively open — many gigaohms — which is why contamination matters, since flux residue, dust, grime and fingerprints all form leakage paths across a sensitive input node.[478] A junk-box panel meter with 100 megaohms input impedance shows the same behaviour, drifting with capacitive build-up and responding to a wiggling hand.[9lIC3ZzIht4] Some of that noise is not an impedance problem at all but an integration-time problem: the same 10 megaohm input on a bench meter is quiet at one power line cycle and wildly noisy at 0.1 NPLC.[1379]

Low-impedance (low-Z) modes solve the opposite problem, deliberately loading the input to kill ghost voltages. Implementations vary: a Hioki sits around 900-odd kilohms at 1.5 V test voltage,[973] a Brymen BM2257 measures 2.3k where the earlier BM235 reads 10 megaohms because its low-impedance mode does not engage until about 6 or 7 volts,[1667] and the 121GW uses a thermistor directly across the input that starts near 2k cold and rises as mains current heats it.[121] The impedance in these modes is not constant — it decreases as the applied voltage increases.[973]

## Analog meters and ohms per volt

Analog meters specify input impedance in ohms per volt, because the load is set by the movement sensitivity and the range multiplier rather than by a fixed divider.[1067] A 20,000 ohms-per-volt movement corresponds to 50 microamps full scale and is typical.[1067] On a 12 V range a 10,000 ohms-per-volt meter presents 120k.[1747] Because the figure scales with range, the highest range can be better than a digital meter: 1,000 volts times 20k is 20 megaohms, twice a typical digital meter, though every lower range is far worse.[1067] A 100k ohms-per-volt movement gives 100 megaohms at the 1000 V range,[633] and higher sensitivity movements were a significant selling point.[634][1013]

Vacuum-tube voltmeters were valued precisely because the tubes made them active and therefore high impedance, comparable in spirit to the fixed 10 megaohms of a modern digital meter.[1013] The FET VOM that replaced them fixed the input impedance and nothing else: a Micronta 22220 gives a fixed 10 megaohms on DC volts only, with the same non-linear backwards ohms scale, the same zeroing problems and the same accuracy limits as any analog meter.[1067]

## Oscilloscope and probe inputs

The standard oscilloscope input is 1 megaohm.[453][279][1566][1545] A times-one probe is essentially just a length of coax straight through to that 1 megaohm input, so the probe presents 1 megaohm at DC.[453][1367] A times-ten probe adds a 9 megaohm series resistor to give a nominal 10 megaohms.[453][1367][778] The times-ten label is a misnomer — the probe divides the signal by ten, and the marking is a reminder to set the matching factor in the channel menu.[778] The bandwidth penalty of times-one is severe: 40 nanoseconds rise time against 900 picoseconds for times-ten on the same probe.[453]

Selectable 50 ohm input impedance is standard on RF-capable and higher-end instruments and appears on some inexpensive scopes as well.[797][876][695][1203][ByUiOk00K0U] Where it is absent, an inline 50 ohm terminator serves.[1566] Termination also reduces displayed noise: on one instrument, switching from 1 megaohm to 50 ohm roughly halves the visible noise at 1 mV per division.[199] It does not cure noise that originates elsewhere in the front end — a channel set to 50 ohms with nothing connected can still show substantial noise, and terminating the input makes no difference.[610] Spectrum analyser RF inputs are 50 ohm,[1109] with a 75 ohm option on some models,[891] absent on others.[1477] Real hardware does not always meet the nominal figure: one USB oscilloscope measures 55 ohms on its 50 ohm setting, about 10% out.[1709]

Input impedance is only the DC half of the story. Input capacitance dominates at frequency: 1 picofarad of probe capacitance is 159 ohms at high frequency, so a 1 megaohm probe becomes a 159 ohm load and can seriously load the circuit.[1715] A times-ten passive probe is around 16 picofarads,[453] the highest-bandwidth passive probes reach 3.9 picofarads at the same 10 megaohm DC impedance,[1367] and active FET probes hold 1 megaohm with under 3 picofarads,[1715] one example combining 1 gigahertz bandwidth with 1 megaohm and 1.2 picofarads.[1368][Y7t6BIhBZhc] A purely resistive high-frequency probe reverses the trade: adding a 450 ohm series resistor gives the same 10:1 division but with about 500 ohms input impedance instead of 10 megaohms, which loads the line heavily at DC while contributing almost no capacitance.[1367][1718]

Scope loading is a real constraint on measurement, not a theoretical one. A peak detector with a 1 nanofarad hold capacitor droops visibly at 1 kilohertz purely because of the 10 megaohm scope probe, even though the op amp's input bias current is only 1 picoamp.[490] A slow-charging capacitor node in an overload detector cannot be observed at all with a 10 megaohm times-ten probe, because the probe discharges the node faster than the circuit charges it.[471] Enabling a second channel on a 1 megaohm input can itself change the fidelity of the signal on the first.[1220] Logic analyser probes are lower impedance still — a nominal 100k with 8 picofarads is typical.[UDGsZcAWgL8]

## Design considerations

On the design side, high input impedance is what makes several standard techniques work. An amplifier or ADC front end whose input impedance is essentially infinite draws no input current, so a shunt resistor can be used as a Kelvin sense tap with no voltage drop across the tap path.[931] A unity-gain buffer is high impedance in and low impedance out, which is what makes it useful for isolating one part of a circuit from another.[xUKf-4rv_sQ] Inverting amplifier configurations, by contrast, draw input current through the input resistor and should not be driven from a high-impedance source, since the source resistance enters the gain equation.[600] Composite amplifiers exist partly to let a designer combine high input impedance with bandwidth, offset, drift and output drive characteristics that no single device provides.[1609]

Impedance can be too high as well as too low. A successive-approximation ADC cannot be driven from an arbitrarily high source impedance, because the external impedance sets how long the internal sampling capacitor takes to charge; a 12-bit converter must settle very close to the final value or the conversion carries additional error, and at 10k source impedance a 0.1% LSB deviation target becomes unachievable, which is why buffer amplifiers are placed ahead of the converter.[232] Chopper-stabilised amplifiers suffer charge injection from their internal switches, and the fix is to lower the input and feedback resistances as far as possible.[24] Floating a 4000-series CMOS input is dangerous for the same reason it is convenient — the inputs are effectively infinite impedance, so 10 megaohm pull-ups work fine and dissipate essentially nothing, but a floating input on a long line acts as an antenna.[801]

At the extremes, condenser microphone preamplifiers demand staggeringly high input impedances together with very low leakage currents and very low parasitic capacitances, which is a classic JFET application.[354] Electrometer-class instrumentation goes further still, using 250 gigaohm resistors and hermetically sealed low-leakage relays.[1017] Picoammeters invert the usual concern entirely: manuals specify minimum input impedances, but what that really constrains is a minimum input voltage, so that the instrument's burden voltage does not dominate the measurement.[406] High-voltage probe design can also relax the requirement — measuring a high-energy source with low source impedance does not call for ultra-high input impedance, so a divider built from five 10 megaohm resistors in series, each rated at 3.5 kV, is adequate.[85]
