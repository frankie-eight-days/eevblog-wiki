# mains voltage

Mains voltage is the alternating supply voltage delivered to a domestic or commercial outlet, and it is the single environmental parameter that most equipment on a bench is forced to live with unmodified. It is not a constant: it drifts by several volts over the course of a day, varies by region and by street, and is specified as a nominal figure with a wide tolerance band rather than as a fixed number.[1426][1507][1714] Because almost everything mains-powered derives its internal rails from it, the actual voltage at the socket propagates into transformer secondaries, regulator dissipation, fuse ratings and component stress.[549][861][gqzZHbEfWDU]

## Nominal values and real values

Australia is nominally 230 V with a tolerance of plus or minus 10 per cent, having been changed from a 240 V nominal a decade or two earlier.[1507][31gkF0q9VfE][vNKcRs3zDBI] The nominal figure and the delivered figure are not the same thing. Dave Jones's lab and home in Sydney both sit near the top of the allowable band: readings of 240 to 245 V are typical, 246 to 248 V is common, and it reaches almost 250 V at times.[1507][549][uC-q-FjXEsA][1305] The legal ceiling in Australia is around 253 or 254 V, and the local supply runs right at that top.[vNKcRs3zDBI] A wrist-worn meter probed directly into an outlet read 254 V AC.[1706] Elsewhere the corpus records 110 or 120 V nominal supplies, and equipment sold into those markets is built accordingly.[1636][1619][280]

Other territories present the same gap between label and reality. A US-supplied instrument arriving in Australia is typically still configured for 115 V, and some dealers will reconfigure it to 240 V before shipping.[620][755][1189] A 110 V supply feeding a 2,000 W copier sagged to 80 or 90 V, enough for the machine to hiccup and stall; the cause was the wiring around the wall rather than the service entry, since plugging in at the incoming point cured it.[280]

## Variation through the day

Mains voltage rises through the day by a couple of volts, tapers off, and then curiously rises again around 7:00 p.m.[1426] The drift is large enough to show up downstream: an amplifier rail measured 48 V one day and over 50 V the next, with no change to the circuit.[1714][1714]

Distributed solar generation is a plausible driver of the daytime rise, since exported power pushes up the local grid voltage. A grid-connected inverter reached around 250 V, and at times 252 V, on its own front-panel display.[1426] Where the supply already sits at the top of the band, this is close enough to the trip threshold to be suspected of curtailing output. Logging a 121GW multimeter at 10-second intervals and overlaying that against 5-minute solar production data showed that mains voltage went *down* at the exact moment output plummeted by 15 to 20 per cent, which ruled mains voltage out as the cause.[1426][1426][1426] Even when a correlation had appeared, correlation would not have established causation.[1426] The actual culprit was the diffuse shadow of a coax antenna mast crossing the array at 9:40 a.m.[1426]

A hybrid inverter installation logged an AC output excursion down to 181 V that correlated with grid frequency, suggesting a protective setting rather than a supply fault.[vNKcRs3zDBI] A line-interactive UPS handles the same problem in hardware, indicating over-voltage and switching in a transformer tap to pull the output back down, or the reverse tap when the mains is sagging.[504]

## Equipment configured for a specific supply

Much bench equipment is single-voltage only, because its transformer is wound for one supply and has no taps. Soldering stations from several makers are built this way, so buying a unit from a foreign market means buying the wrong one.[596][1063] A 120 V soldering station plugged into 240 V billowed smoke out the bottom within seconds, with the enamel burned off the thin primary winding; the absence of a fuse meant it could have caught fire unattended.[1152][1152] A DIY reflow oven controller was 240 V only and not offered in the US at all.[558]

Where selection is provided it takes several forms: a rear-panel voltage selector switch,[210][804] the old-style plug-in voltage selection block on vintage instruments,[1716] internal jumpers or straps that put two primary coils in series for 230/240 V or in parallel for 115 V,[1189][1189] or genuine dual-range operation with no configuration worry at all.[829] The primary resistance measured with the coils in series is more than double the parallel value, since the parallel measurement returns half of a single coil.[1189] Fuse rating is part of the configuration and differs per mains voltage — a rear-panel label specifying half an amp for 240 V.[861] The practical rule is to download and read the manual before buying, since it will state whether the unit can be configured for other regions.[1189]

Running equipment at a higher voltage than it was designed for stresses the parts even when it does not destroy them outright. Linear supplies designed for 220 V and run on 240 V put that extra 20 V across the regulator transistors, dissipating substantially more power.[gqzZHbEfWDU] A bench supply whose transformer tap was never changed continued to deliver an unnecessarily high input to its LM317 because the lab supply ran at 247 V.[549][549] A fume extractor was not rated for the 240 or 245 V present at the bench.[1305] A linear supply with a 220/240 V inlet and no selection switch simply will not work on 110 V.[314]

## Measurement

Measuring mains directly calls for the right instrument. A dedicated high-voltage probe is the proper way to safely measure mains voltage on an oscilloscope; 704 V peak-to-peak corresponds to 245 V AC RMS.[1283] Socket testers report the line-to-neutral voltage and, more usefully, the neutral-to-earth voltage, which indicates not merely that neutral and earth are bonded but how well — effectively the resistance of the neutral-earth path.[1598] The chipsets used in such testers are multimeter ICs that also integrate a non-contact voltage circuit.[kU7zSSuy9WQ]

Many multimeters include a non-contact electric field function that indicates the presence of mains voltage when brought close to an energised 240 V or 110 V cable, though detector sensitivity varies and some units fail to register on unloaded mains even on their low range.[1636][1378] Meters also measure mains frequency in hertz, and a 50 Hz mains period appears as 20 milliseconds across a screen at 5 ms per division.[1636][1059] Some oscilloscopes derive line sync capacitively: a two-pin header with wires laid alongside the active and neutral inside heat-shrink, with no electrical contact, couples enough signal for the processor to measure mains frequency and adjust automatically.[564] Mains voltage can also be logged continuously over a serial interface for later analysis.[1684] For deliberately testing the upper and lower limits of a mains power supply, a programmable AC source allows the voltage to be dialled in, with frequency selectable at 50, 60, 100 or 400 Hz.[1698]

## Hazard and protection

Mains is a low-impedance source with a great deal of energy behind it, which is why X-class capacitors placed directly across it are designed not to fail short and not to catch fire.[1183] The same energy makes instrument input protection non-negotiable: a pocket multimeter blew up in the hand while measuring 240 V mains,[94] another failed outright on mains applied to its ohms range,[1084] and a scope-meter with no input protection at all should not be used on the mains under any circumstances.[W2o8otvVML4] Well-designed instruments protect against a user mistakenly plugging 240 V mains into the sense terminals,[829] and a robust handheld tolerates 240 V applied to a millivolt range without damage.[1692]

Historic live-chassis televisions connected all internal metalwork directly to the mains, placing it at 240 V potential with only the plastic case separating the user from it; even the shield of the coax at the antenna connector sat at mains potential, isolated only by a capacitor.[104][866] Present-day hazards are more mundane: an instrument whose pop-out foot leaves a gap through which a screwdriver can reach the mains input circuitry,[480] an appliance with mains taken straight to the element and no earth on its metalwork,[893] and abandoned equipment with mains-rated conductors and heatsinks left exposed.[YaTldV7-uXo] When probing a powered device, the mains side is off-limits and only secondary-side heatsinks should be touched.[CK5nbC_dBWk] Circuits that derive a low-voltage supply straight from the mains through series resistors and a bridge rectifier are non-isolated, and testing them requires an isolation transformer.[534] On a board carrying mains, everything on the primary side — bridge rectifier, filter caps with their bleed resistors, common-mode chokes, Y-class capacitors and varistors — is at mains potential.[1364][1499][1519]

## Mains as a design input

Loads are frequently connected straight across the mains and switched by a relay, as in a heater whose controller does nothing more than close a relay on the element,[1481] or modulated by a triac switching whole or half cycles of the 50 Hz waveform.[1059] Devices intended to operate from the mains directly need high-voltage parts: a jellybean bipolar with a 400 V collector-emitter rating covers most mains-type applications at 220 mA continuous collector current.[1599]

For low-current loads, a small regulator fed from 240 V mains through a rectifier and capacitor draws orders of magnitude less current than a Zener-based alternative.[1285] Constant-current drive removes the supply voltage from the equation altogether: an LED lamp with a constant-current driver produces the same brightness whether it is fed 110 V, 230 V or the 245 V present in the lab.[1688] Not all mains-powered lighting is so forgiving — an LED tube driver can drop 50 to 100 V of the 240 V available, and a tube whose PCB is inserted the wrong way round applies the mains to the output.[362][362] LED tubes rated for 230 V operated in a lab sitting above 245 V are being run at the top of their limit.[531]

True zero standby power is difficult on a 230, 240 or 110 V mains set precisely because switching the mains input itself requires a solid-state relay with adequate current handling and, in the case of SCR-based devices, significant input drive current.[971] Measuring standby consumption after a 12 V plugpack, rather than at the mains input, hides the plugpack's own quiescent draw.[971]

Higher mains voltage is not uniformly a disadvantage. An EV charger drawing a fixed power at 240 to 245 V rather than 230 V draws less current, giving lower copper losses in the cable — a 6 mm² radial measured roughly 10 °C above ambient at 32 A.[31gkF0q9VfE] A portable EVSE on a 15 A outlet delivers 15 A at whatever the supply voltage happens to be.[1507]
