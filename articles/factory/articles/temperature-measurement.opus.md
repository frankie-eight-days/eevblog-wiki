# temperature measurement

Temperature measurement is the routine bench practice of determining how hot a component, assembly, or environment is, and it underpins most thermal design verification, fault-finding, and product characterisation work in electronics.[622][957] It divides into contact methods, dominated by the K-type thermocouple plugged into a multimeter, and non-contact methods, dominated by infrared thermometers and thermal cameras.[75][587][1636] Both are easy to perform badly: a reading is only as good as the thermal coupling, the emissivity setting, and the mechanical state of the object under test at the moment the number is taken.[622][664][742]

## Thermocouples and multimeter temperature functions

A K-type thermocouple probe is little more than a wire sensor on the end of a lead; it is cheap, simple, and plugs directly into any meter with a temperature function.[75] Its tip measures the temperature of whatever it touches — air, metal, heat sink surfaces, or liquids — and the measurement range extends to hundreds of degrees.[75][1636] Meters generally display in both Celsius and Fahrenheit.[1636][534]

Resolution and accuracy are separate specifications and should not be confused. A mid-range handheld may offer 0.1 °C display resolution while specifying accuracy of only about ±1 °C, which is still useful because the fine resolution supports relative work: a delta temperature mode nulls out the starting reading so that only the change is displayed.[249][99] Doing temperature properly inside a meter requires cold junction compensation on the thermocouple input, along with a dedicated offset trim pot for the temperature channel — a level of investment that is unusual in a handheld and marks out a meter that treats temperature as a first-class function rather than an afterthought.[853]

Some meters expose an internal temperature sensor rather than an external probe.[mkZFHEeQkQI][489] That sensor is typically a calibrated PN junction on the PCB, and in some designs may be inside the processor itself, since on-die temperature sensors are common in microcontrollers.[mkZFHEeQkQI] Reading it is a practical way to track the internal temperature of the instrument itself, for example when cycling a meter between hot and cold to chase a thermally dependent fault.[mkZFHEeQkQI] Bench instruments expose the equivalent: a 6½-digit DMM can report internal or ambient temperature directly.[489]

## Temperature as a multimeter buying criterion

Temperature capability is treated as a significant feature in meter selection rather than a novelty, and its absence is a real deduction.[75][99] A meter that is otherwise excellent but cannot measure temperature is simply unsuitable for anyone who needs temperature.[99] On a meter approaching US$90, omitting temperature while retaining a transistor tester is judged an oversight severe enough to change the overall verdict on the product.[1378] Fluke's product lines have historically been differentiated on exactly this axis, with the temperature function among the few things separating adjacent models.[64] In at least one case the temperature hardware is present and only the firmware distinguishes the cheaper model from the dearer one.[1448]

A recurring limitation is that meter temperature inputs are single channel, when differential and multi-point measurements are frequently what is actually needed; two or three blade-type thermocouple inputs would address this.[184] Where a probe is not supplied with the meter, the user has to source one separately.[75]

## Infrared and thermal imaging

Non-contact instruments give a fast survey of a whole board, but every reading is conditioned on emissivity. Bright or unfinished metal is the classic problem: an un-anodised transformer reads cool and silver heat sinks read low unless emissivity is set for the surface.[828][587] Shiny surfaces also reflect, and the operator's own hand can appear in the image as heat reflected off aluminium capacitor cans, so thermal work requires care that reflected energy is not being mistaken for the object's own emission.[742] One remedy is to paint the surface of interest black.[742] Where the material is not in the instrument's preset list, emissivity can be found experimentally by adjusting the setting until the image agrees with a known-good reference — for example a soldering iron tip whose temperature has already been established by other means, which in one case settled at around 0.35 to give 246 °C.[1064]

A thermal camera's on-screen temperature figure is typically the maximum across every pixel in the frame, not the temperature of any particular part.[593] It is therefore excellent for spotting hot spots — a display ranging from 23.8 °C room temperature up to nearly 70 °C localises a fault immediately — but identifying the temperature of a specific device still means reading individual points.[593][622] Fusion or MSX-style overlays help by superimposing visible-light outlines so that individual chips can be identified in the thermal image.[593]

## The lid-off trap

The most common mistake in thermal performance measurement is testing with the lid off.[622] In any product with forced-air cooling, removing the enclosure destroys the airflow path the design depends on, so the temperatures observed are not the true temperatures of the devices in service.[622] Airflow direction and the position of vents matter concretely: with the case open, air forced in at the rear flows out over regions that would ordinarily be cool and exits at the wrong point.[622] Blocking one of two exhaust vents changes the direction of heat spreading across the board and lowers the maximum-to-minimum spread in the image.[622] Ballpark lid-off readings remain useful for orientation, but they are explicitly not valid characterisation data.[622]

## Making the measurement trustworthy

Getting a genuinely accurate number is laborious. Characterising a thermoelectric generator properly requires correctly coupled probes on both the hot and cold sides, and a quick ballpark setup will not do it; a full day of setup may precede any decently accurate measurement.[664] The same ambiguity appears with soldering irons, where the tip temperature, the internal sensor reading, and an external thermocouple all disagree, and the tip-to-sensor thermocouple coupling is itself part of the problem.[1646] The practical answer is that the tip is what matters, but the coupling still has to be accounted for.[1646] Instruments should also be given time to settle; an hour of operation is a reasonable interval before assuming a chassis has thermally equalised.[zoyaHOqp9gI]

Sensor placement is equally decisive in products themselves. A power supply that reports a temperature but does not place its thermocouple on the heat sink is measuring the wrong thing, a cost-saving compromise that undermines the reading it presents to the user.[1298] The contrary example is a battery emulator that instruments the temperature of each output connector individually, with a sensor bolted through a solid brass block.[1550] Attempting four significant digits of temperature in a data logger is ambitious given how hard the underlying measurement is.[1686]

## Diagnostic and characterisation uses

Comparative thermal measurement is a standard technique for locating a short. Where a rail is shorted and the bypass capacitors have been eliminated, measuring the remaining candidate devices and looking for the outlier is the obvious next step — but the technique fails when all of the candidates are faulty. Four ASICs on a 3.3 V rail measured at essentially the same temperature, and only a 3 to 4 °C difference between one chip and the others, which was far too small to be explained by the extra dissipation of a single short, and which ultimately indicated that every one of them was shorted.[401][405]

For thermal characterisation, the meaningful figure is usually the differential above ambient rather than the absolute reading. An oscilloscope running overnight settled at 39.4 °C against a lab ambient near 24 °C, a 15 to 16 °C rise, at a roughly constant 35 to 36 W regardless of channel count or processing load.[pwS6HnM9PDo] Reducing that instrument's fan drive from 8 V to 5 V cost only about 2 to 2.5 °C.[zoyaHOqp9gI] A linear supply's output transistor peaked at just over 100 °C under a worst-case short-circuit dissipation test, while a breadboarded regulator at 1 A settled at about 43.5 °C on the device case and 41.5 °C on the heat sink against 28.5 to 29 °C ambient.[828][224] Instruments may also protect themselves, warning that internal temperature is approaching a damaging point and shutting down.[587]

Temperature is also a useful auxiliary axis in efficiency work: attaching a thermocouple to a converter or its heat sink allows temperature to be plotted against efficiency, output, and load dissipation, which exposes designs that behave correctly at lab ambient but not elsewhere.[957] Determining die temperature is a recognised technique in its own right.[1619]

## Dedicated sensors and where temperature is the wrong measurand

Beyond thermocouples, a range of dedicated devices appear in equipment: thermistors driving PWM fan controllers with configurable low, high, and alarm thresholds, ramping fan duty from 10 % to 100 % linearly between them; the DS18B20 one-wire digital thermometer; and combined temperature and humidity parts.[-9l41zNWlxY][f_SdM6sXHD4][377] Data loggers built around a small microcontroller commonly cover −40 to 125 °C, storing readings as 8- or 10-bit values.[294] Radiosondes carried aloft under balloons measure temperature and barometric pressure with an external probe, since the Styrofoam housing that keeps the electronics warm would otherwise isolate the sensor from the air it is meant to measure.[970]

Occasionally the thermal effect of interest is too small to measure directly. Mobile phone SAR testing is nominally about how much a tissue-simulant liquid is heated, but the temperature changes involved are too small for accurate measurement, so the E-field is measured instead and SAR is computed from the dielectric parameters of the liquid.[201]
