# aliasing

Aliasing is the artifact produced when a sampling system is fed a signal whose frequency is too high for its sample rate: the instrument returns a coherent, plausible-looking waveform at a completely different frequency from the one actually present.[j49T1E4UEv4] The essential point is not that the display becomes messy but that it stays believable — the trace is still a clean sine wave, just the wrong one, so a capture taken without attention to the time base and sample rate can be silently and entirely wrong.[j49T1E4UEv4] It is a property of sampling itself rather than of oscilloscopes in particular, and it affects every sampled instrument: scopes, logic analyzers, USB acquisition hardware, and integrating digital multimeters alike.[j49T1E4UEv4][1140][1144][1379] Aliasing is one of the big problems with digital storage oscilloscopes.[143]

## Mechanism

The governing requirement is Nyquist: the sample rate must be at least twice the signal frequency, and below that threshold the result is aliasing.[1379] Content above the limit does not disappear — frequencies fold back into range, producing components that never existed in the input.[j49T1E4UEv4] The consequences extend past simple waveform viewing into any analysis performed on the captured record.[j49T1E4UEv4] In an oscilloscope the failure is normally provoked by winding the time base out, because at slow sweep speeds a fixed record length forces the sample rate down until the input is grossly undersampled.[430][797][199]

The aliased frequency displayed depends on the arithmetic relationship between input and sample rate, which makes the effect strongly sensitive to the exact input frequency. A 10 MHz signal on a scope sampling at 2 GSa/s can appear as a 1 to 2 Hz waveform at 5 ms/div.[j49T1E4UEv4] Sweeping the source down through 9, 8, 7 and 6 MHz leaves the alias present throughout, but an oddball, non-round frequency behaves differently: at 8.1 MHz the trace aliases instantly, while 8.105 MHz merely shifts the apparent ripple frequency and 8.123 MHz or 9.12345 MHz smooths the display out so that no alias is apparent at all.[j49T1E4UEv4] The same distinction explains why 250 MHz on a 180 MHz instrument shows a clear beat against the sample rate rather than a random-looking artifact.[1582]

Display processing complicates interpretation further. A stopped acquisition can look significantly different from the same signal running, because the stopped-display algorithm and the real-time display algorithm need not be the same, and the stop lands on a different set of samples.[j49T1E4UEv4] Reconstruction algorithms can turn an aliased record into elaborate patterning that bears no resemblance to either the input or a simple low-frequency alias.[I3FhAhxet7s] Every scope handles the situation differently.[j49T1E4UEv4]

## Sample rate, bandwidth and memory depth

Two separate ratios matter. The first is sample rate against front-end bandwidth. A four-channel 100 MHz scope that splits 1 GSa/s across all channels delivers 250 MSa/s, or 2.5 times its bandwidth — in theory just enough to prevent aliasing given sin(x)/x interpolation, but marginal, with pulse waveforms and similar signals the likely casualties of running full bandwidth on all channels.[704] The same 2.5-samples-per-cycle figure on another instrument is judged technically adequate and not an aliasing risk, while a good scope would provide roughly ten times bandwidth.[1355]

The second ratio is memory depth against time base, and it is usually the one that decides whether a given screen aliases. With only 14k points a scope aliases at slow sweeps and simply cannot do otherwise; with 14 Meg points the same signal is captured without trouble.[797] A 4k record aliases where 500k does not.[487] Reducing an Owon's record to 10k produces wobble even while the waveform is still nominally on screen, and at 1k the trace is gone entirely.[480] The MDO4000 aliases readily at short records but not when a 1 Meg depth is selected.[199] The pathological case is an instrument with no acquisition memory beyond the display buffer — roughly 320 samples across — which cannot be zoomed and aliases a 100 MHz input outright.[1260]

Automatic memory depth is therefore the practical defence. A scope with auto memory depth selects a record length appropriate to the time base and refuses to alias on signals that defeat manual-depth instruments, and it does so even with any dedicated anti-alias mode switched off.[797] Its absence is a genuine shortcoming: requiring the user to select memory depth by hand is what turns an otherwise capable scope into one that aliases.[797] A 20 Meg record makes aliasing hard to provoke but not impossible.[1146]

## Anti-alias filtering and warnings

Most oscilloscopes contain no anti-aliasing hardware filter, and the undersampled input reaches the digitiser unattenuated.[j49T1E4UEv4] Where such filtering exists it is worth noting: one instrument switches an 800 MHz anti-alias filter with an audible relay.[1709][lXvXBdTP7Tg] Some scopes apply anti-aliasing behaviour only in automatic acquisition mode, so the mode setting must be checked before concluding an instrument is immune.[j49T1E4UEv4] A separate mitigation is warning rather than filtering: at least one scope detects the condition and tells the user that the capture is aliasing.[p-eLu1z7-cs] Most give no warning at all.[I3FhAhxet7s]

A hardware frequency counter is unaffected by the acquisition path and continues to read the true input frequency while the trace shows an alias, because the signal is fed to dedicated counter hardware directly.[j49T1E4UEv4] A software counter derived from the sampled record is not independent in this way, and its digit count falls as the time base is slowed.[143]

## Instrument behaviour

Aliasing behaviour under a slow time base with a fast sine wave is a standard bench check, and many otherwise good scopes fail it.[876] Instruments that hold up include a 2000 X series scope which, given a 20 MHz sine wave, still does not alias at 1 second per division;[143] a benchtop USB instrument that passes cleanly where many better scopes have failed;[876] an $80 handheld that cannot be made to alias at all;[1540] a 50 MHz handheld that resists aliasing with the available generator and has more than enough memory for its purpose;[1597] and a handheld DMM/scope combination that shows no aliasing on a 1 MHz square wave and simply displays nothing above its limit.[1723]

Instruments that alias under the same test are more numerous: a 12-bit Rigol at slow enough time bases, which is expected behaviour rather than a fault;[1566][I3FhAhxet7s] an R&S HMO1002;[793] a tablet scope where a 101 MHz input reads as roughly 43 MHz;[1317] a LeCroy at extended time bases;[792] a Red Pitaya;[858][2] a $5 scope at 20 MHz;[1492] a Fluke 91 Scopemeter, which presents a 10 MHz square wave as an apparently perfect sine wave at 50 µs/div;[430] and a handheld that aliases a 100 kHz signal, an unusually low frequency at which to fail.[D2PANd9Hu3U] A 10 Hz sine on a low-end handheld is barely readable, and by 100 Hz the display is either mistriggering or aliasing outright.[81]

## Beyond oscilloscopes

Logic analyzers alias in the same way, and the effect worsens as the sample rate drops: at 8 MSa/s whole sections of a captured bitstream contain nothing at all, and even at 24 MSa/s a signal of two distinct duty cycles is misreported across most of the capture.[1140] A USB instrument with a slow ADC produces heavy aliasing on a serial line, recoverable only by zooming in.[1144]

Integrating multimeters are subject to the identical constraint through their aperture time. Measuring a 1 V peak-to-peak 50 Hz signal at 0.2 power line cycles — a 4 ms aperture — produces a modulated reading that is classic aliasing, the sampling artifact of an aperture too short relative to the signal.[1379]

Bode plotting by sweeping a generator into a scope is bounded at the top end by the same limit: the sweep must stay well clear of the region where the scope begins to alias.[396]

## Practice

Aliasing is a condition to watch for in any sampling system, not a scope-specific quirk, and an unwary capture can come a cropper.[j49T1E4UEv4] The defences are ordinary: keep the sample rate at least twice, and preferably around ten times, the highest frequency of interest;[1379][1355] use automatic memory depth where the instrument offers it, and select a long record manually where it does not;[797][199] cross-check any suspicious frequency against a hardware counter rather than the trace;[j49T1E4UEv4] and treat a clean sine wave appearing at a slow time base with suspicion, since that is exactly what a successful alias looks like.[430][j49T1E4UEv4]
