# transmission line

A transmission line is a conductor pair analysed as a distributed structure rather than as an ideal wire, in which the signal propagates as a wave with a finite delay and is governed by the line's characteristic impedance.[1439][1728] Any conductor behaves this way at sufficiently high frequency: coaxial cable, twisted pair, PCB trace, component lead.[652][1085] The practical consequences are reflections, ringing, standing waves, and amplitude and phase errors when the line is not correctly matched at its ends.[652][1718][316]

## When a conductor becomes a transmission line

There is no separate class of transmission line wiring; effectively every trace on a board is a transmission line, and whether it must be treated as one depends on the edge rates and frequencies present.[1085] What matters is not the nominal frequency of the signal but the slew rate of its edges — a 1 kHz signal with a fast enough rising edge demands the same matching discipline as a high-frequency sine.[316] A step function is the extreme case, because by Fourier decomposition it contains a fundamental plus a large set of harmonics, which is precisely why a switch closing onto a pair of wires is a transmission line problem and not a lumped-circuit one.[1439]

At the system scale, the same theory covers 500 kV power transmission lines, where the relevant property is the series resistance of the conductors: current flowing through them dissipates real power as I²R or copper losses.[1730] Apparent power in volt-amperes, rather than real power in watts, is the figure used to size transmission line conductors and the windings of substation and pole transformers, because it is the current the upstream source must actually supply.[1730] Real power is only ever dissipated in resistances — never in a pure capacitance or inductance — across loads, transmission lines, generators and transformers alike.[1730]

## Characteristic impedance

Characteristic impedance is a term reserved for distributed-element transmission lines and is distinct from the impedance of a circuit element or network.[1728] Saying that a coaxial cable's impedance is 50 ohms is therefore incorrect; 50 ohms is its characteristic impedance.[1728] The distinction has a measurable consequence: the characteristic impedance of a transmission line typically does not vary with frequency, whereas the impedance of an ordinary circuit element does.[1728]

Characteristic impedance follows from geometry and dielectric. Two parallel wires spaced one metre apart form a line of roughly 800 to 900 ohms characteristic impedance.[1439] Older twin-lead flex was built at 300 ohms.[lBycH31K-E8] Coaxial cable is commonly 50 ohms, and RG58 C/U is a representative type.[1367][1266] Propagation delay along coax is on the order of 5.05 ns per metre, so a 45 mm difference in cable length between two channels produces roughly 227 ps of skew — a figure that agrees with the 262 ps measured in practice.[652]

PCB structures have their own well-defined forms, and impedance calculators enumerate them: microstrip, coplanar waveguide, grounded coplanar waveguide, rectangular waveguide, coaxial, coupled microstrip, and stripline for inner layers sandwiched between ground planes.[253] Modern layout tools accept transmission line constraints alongside safety spacings and track ampacities and check the board against them automatically.[1032] Controlled-impedance work is one of the standard burdens of high-speed layout, along with EMC requirements and thermal packaging.[244]

## Termination and reflections

An unterminated line reflects. A 1K series resistive probe must be terminated at the scope end, because without termination the reflections make it unusable as a probe.[1718] With the 1K in series against a 50-ohm termination the division ratio works out at about 21:1 rather than the nominal 10:1 for a conventional probe.[1718] Maximum power transfer and impedance matching are the same underlying idea: a 50-ohm transmission line is matched to a 50-ohm source to prevent reflections.[1401] Source and load impedances are matched for the same reason when terminating a differential preamplifier or a scope input for ripple and noise measurement — proper 50-ohm source impedance driving a 50-ohm load.[594] Feeding a signal generator output into a 50-ohm terminated load removes transmission line problems and the reflections that come with them from a measurement setup entirely.[1521]

The failure mode when inputs are left unterminated is not subtle. At around 35 MHz on an unterminated scope input, both amplitude and phase change substantially, the two can swap over, and the behaviour shifts with the coax impedance, the coax length, and the load being reflected back.[652] Disconnecting one channel visibly changes the amplitude on the other.[652] More generally, an incorrectly matched line, output impedance, or attenuator chain compromises the measurement.[316] In a USB 3.0 eye diagram, an imperfect transmission line shows up as overshoot and level errors at the receiver.[340]

## Coax, probes, and measurement hardware

Coaxial cable — centre conductor plus outer braid — is a transmission line as far as high-frequency content is concerned, and viewing high-frequency ringing requires a proper transmission line of this kind.[1266] BNC-to-banana and BNC-to-clip adaptors are convenient for plugging into systems but are called transmission line probes for a reason: hooked to a high-frequency signal without correct termination of the 50-ohm coax, they misbehave.[1367] A ×1 probe measures as a short at DC because the coax passes straight through, while its transmission line behaviour governs what happens at speed.[453] Switchable ×1/×10 probes carry extra tip capacitance and extra transmission line to contend with, which is why fixed ×10 probes — with the 9 Mohm resistor placed right at the tip — can outperform them.[453] In a probe fault investigation, the nine-meg resistor plus the cable capacitance of the transmission line forms the impedance load seen across the offending element.[983]

Even a well-built pulse generator is limited by this: long coax adds inductance, and while it works as a transmission line, it can only go so far before signal integrity dictates the outcome, which is why such instruments are shielded down to the battery box.[306] Inside RF instruments the discipline is total — on a spectrum analyser or RF front end, everything visible on the board is a transmission line.[199] Board-level examples include a synthesiser local oscillator routed out through a transmission line into an inner layer[391] and four analog channels length-matched by snaking traces so that all four arrive at the buffers at the end of the transmission line together.[790] A discrete RF assembly may implement the line in air, coupling to the antenna through plate capacitors formed with air dielectric.[970]

## Return current and bypassing

Transmission line behaviour explains where return current actually flows. Current always takes the lowest-impedance path from source through circuit and back to ground.[1085] At low frequency that is the lowest DC resistance route; at high frequency the return path is not back to a distant bulk decoupling capacitor but directly beneath the signal trace, because that path is lower impedance at those frequencies.[1085] The result can be shown from field equations, and the practical consequence is that a bypass capacitor placed at the chip becomes the local source for the fast transitions.[1085] The effect is demonstrable on a single-sided board with copper tape traces over a solid ground plane, driving a 50-ohm transmission line and load from 5 V to produce the large switching currents that make bypassing matter.[1085] Insufficient bypassing produces visible ringing and substantial ripple on a 5 V rail even where the load is only a driven capacitive line.[1085]

Long interconnects between boards are transmission lines whether or not they were designed as such, and driving them raises common ground, line buffering, and synchronisation issues that a simple single-ended 5 V TTL solution may not survive.[1365] A clean square wave at the driver can be badly degraded by the time it crosses a poor line, and the problem compounds when tens to hundreds of milliamps of gulped current cross the same path and bounce the ground.[1365] Probing such a line properly is a prerequisite to seeing what is happening at all.[1365] At the extreme, distributing power and data over a 10 km towed seismic array with 24-bit ADCs every 30 m makes transmission line behaviour and the power budget the dominant design problem.[500] Verification hardware for such assemblies uses a reed-relay switching matrix to measure any pair of conductors, including hydrophones, induction coils, and transmission lines.[1632] Cable assemblies of this kind require measurement of wire resistance, wire inductance, twisted-pair transmission line performance, and insulation resistance of every wire against all the others.[38]

## Modelling and simulation

The standard analysis is the lumped element model, in which each unit length of line — a centimetre, an inch, a metre, the choice is arbitrary — is given a shunt capacitance, a series inductance, and a series resistance, and the section is repeated toward infinity.[1439] Every conductor in electronics has some inductance, so the inductor is always present in principle.[1439] For demonstration purposes a single section, or two, is enough to show the mechanism.[1439] After the transients have died away and all the waves on the line have settled, analysis shifts to DC steady state, a different mode in which current simply flows through the loop.[1439]

SPICE tools ship transmission line elements directly: LTspice offers an ideal lossless transmission line and a separate lossy transmission line, the latter being necessary when the object being modelled — such as a deliberately lossy scope probe — is not lossless.[1445][516]

The much-discussed problem of a battery, a lamp, and two wires one metre apart running out half a light-second in each direction is a transmission line problem set up deliberately as one.[1439][lBycH31K-E8] The lamp begins to conduct after roughly one metre divided by c, because that is the spacing between the wires and there is capacitance between them.[lBycH31K-E8][1439] Within that window the ends of the line are not yet seen as open circuits by the circuit, the simulation, or the real line, because the disturbance has not reached them.[1439] The line does not strictly need to be modelled as a distributed structure — two wires and their mutual capacitance suffice — and it is the cable capacitance rather than the inductance that produces the effect.[1439][lBycH31K-E8] The fraction of battery voltage the bulb initially receives depends on the impedance of the lines and the impedance of the bulb, and invoking impedance is already invoking transmission lines.[1439] In practice the capacitance and inductance are tiny and the power delivered to the lamp in that first interval is minute, but it is real.[1439] The energy does flow in the fields outside the wire under AC conditions, which is why coaxial cable and transmission line theory on PCBs exist at all; at DC steady state the Poynting vector points back into the wire and there is no radiation.[1439] To an engineer the result is unremarkable — it is a transmission line, explained by cable capacitance and ordinary transmission line behaviour, with no need to reach for field-theoretic hand-waving.[1439]

## Related structures

A glass delay line functions as a high-fidelity transmission line, its surfaces chemically roughened so that shear waves stay shear waves and remain within the body of the material to reflect correctly.[386] Signal integrity matters there because the payload is analog colour video, not digital ones and zeros.[386] The standard references for the digital case are Howard Johnson's *High-Speed Digital Design: A Handbook of Black Magic*, treated as the industry bible for high-speed design and transmission lines, and its follow-up *High-Speed Signal Propagation*.[2] Distributed transmission line parameters and characteristic impedance also appear among the worked problems in classic engineering calculator handbooks.[1571]
