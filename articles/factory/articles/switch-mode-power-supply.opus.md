# switch mode power supply

A switch mode power supply (SMPS) converts electrical power by chopping it with a transistor that is either fully on or fully off, storing the energy in an inductor or transformer, and filtering the result back into a smooth DC rail.[90] Because the switching element is either saturated or open, it dissipates very little power in either state, which is the whole reason a switcher is more efficient than a linear regulator that drops the surplus voltage continuously across a pass transistor.[90] The topology is close to universal in modern electronics: it is what allows a mains-powered appliance, instrument or plug pack to be small and light instead of carrying a mains-frequency iron transformer.[289][272][542]

Despite a reputation for complexity, the basic step-down converter is not structurally far removed from a linear regulator; it is a control loop around a series element, with the difference being that the element is pulsed rather than held in its linear region.[90][95] The schematic looks busier — a diode, an inductor, output capacitors, sense resistors and loop compensation components surround the controller — but the added parts are all in service of that same regulation task.[90]

## Operating principle

In a buck (step-down) converter the switch connects the input to the inductor, current ramps up and energy is stored in the magnetic field; when the switch turns off, the current continues to circulate through a freewheeling diode, so the circuit simply alternates between two conduction loops.[90] There are only two states, off and on.[90] The same energy-storage idea scales up: an offline supply rectifies the mains to a high-voltage DC bus, switches that bus into a transformer primary, and rectifies the secondary.[1301][1364][525] In a mains electric fence controller the first stage generates roughly 700–800 V across parallel 900 V capacitors, and a second switching stage takes energy from those capacitors and drives a transformer to boost it further.[1277]

A step-up (boost) arrangement uses the same inductor but dumps its collapsing field above the input rail, and the load therefore receives current in bursts rather than continuously.[708] Where the input is always higher than the output — a 12 V automotive system feeding logic rails, for example — a buck converter is the safe default choice.[1475]

## Efficiency and where linear still wins

Efficiency is the reason switchers exist, but it is not a constant: it depends on load, on input voltage and on how the converter was optimised.[90] A converter is designed around a particular input voltage and a particular output power band, and efficiency outside that band is simply whatever it happens to be.[1031] In product design this is rarely a problem, because the designer knows the product's consumption and can size the inductor and transistors for that operating point.[1031]

There are cases where a linear regulator wins outright. With 6 V in and 5 V out, an LDO is already operating at high efficiency, and a switcher offers little or nothing.[90] Conversely, where the input range is wide or the headroom is thin, the switcher is the only sensible answer: a 9 V battery supplying a 5 V rail through a linear regulator stops regulating around 6 V input, wasting most of the cell's capacity, whereas a switch mode converter will run the battery down to nearly 0.8 V per cell.[722] The same reasoning applies to battery-powered product design generally, where changing from a linear regulator to a switching converter is one of the standard levers for extending run time.[1331]

Device technology sets the ceiling. Gallium nitride transistors combine very fast rise and fall times with high breakdown voltage, which permits extremely efficient DC-DC converters and is why modern high-power bricks can be physically tiny.[o2NxHu5Bsnk] At the other end, the choice of rectifier matters: ordinary PN junction diodes drop 0.5 V or more and rise further with current, so they waste significant power as heat, which is why Schottky diodes — measured at around 0.14 V in comparison with half a volt for a 1N4001 or 1N4148 — are the standard part in efficiency-critical, high-frequency switch mode supplies.[1747] Higher-efficiency designs go further and replace the diode entirely with a secondary-side synchronous rectification driver.[1461]

## Components and control

MOSFETs dominate the switching element because their on-resistance is far lower than a bipolar equivalent for the same current, making them the natural choice for switch mode converters and similar high-current switching work.[1461] Selecting one is where a converter design becomes exacting: static parameters suffice for a simple power switch, but dynamic characteristics — gate charge, input and output capacitance, reverse recovery — matter only in switching converters, and it is exactly those parameters that separate a 95% efficient converter from a 90% efficient one.[1461] Finding an equivalent part for a converter's bill of materials therefore demands extensive testing rather than a parametric search.[1461] At the high-voltage end, superjunction devices with integrated fast body diodes are marketed specifically for resonant topologies in high-power supplies such as servers, telecom and EV charging stations.[1294]

Control is often built from generic parts. The TL494 is a general PWM controller rather than a dedicated regulator IC, containing a reference, two summed error amplifiers, an oscillator, a dead-band control and a steering flip-flop, and its primary application is switch mode power supplies.[272] The TL431 adjustable shunt reference is used in practically every isolated mains switch mode supply as the feedback reference element, to the point that manufacturers publish application notes on its use in that role.[1438] Combination parts bundling comparators, op-amps and a reference are likewise pitched at switch mode supplies, battery chargers and over/under-voltage sensing.[1464] Integrated multi-rail controllers exist for board-level power: a five-channel switching converter can generate the assorted rails an FPGA and its support circuitry require from a single DC input.[1693]

Electrolytic capacitors in a switcher are stressed differently from those in a linear supply. Equivalent series resistance, not capacitance, determines their performance, and it is ESR that rises as the dielectric dries out with heat while the measured capacitance may still read correctly — which is why the correct diagnostic instrument is an ESR meter, not a capacitance meter.[365] Replacements must be low-ESR types rated to 105 °C; ordinary or 85 °C parts are not adequate.[365][347] Ceramic capacitors in a switching supply can also become a mechanical problem: piezoelectric multilayer ceramics vibrate, and in one case nearby MLCCs in a switching supply disturbed a MEMS oscillator on a compute module.[1607] Audible inductor squeal from a switching converter is common and is a physical noise rather than an electrical fault.[1613]

## Noise and its consequences

The defining drawback of a switcher is the noise it injects. Ripple in any supply is the charge-discharge cycle of its storage element, but where a linear supply produces ripple locked to the mains at 50/60 Hz — and can therefore be line-triggered on an oscilloscope — a switcher runs at a free-running frequency that defeats that technique.[594] Observed switching artefacts include a 120 kHz fundamental on a 360 W bench supply's output[1691], spikes at 315 kHz with visible harmonics inside an instrument[1557], and a spurious tone at about 39.4 kHz coupling from an internal supply into the input of a measurement instrument, indicating a layout problem.[692]

Switching noise also propagates through the mains. A cheap switching plug pack sharing a power board with test equipment injected a 66 kHz spike into measurements, and removing it from the board eliminated the interference entirely.[441] A set of LED light panels driven by poor-quality switching converters produced severe noise that disappeared the moment they were disconnected.[765] Mitigation inside the product is a matter of shielding and magnetics: an electrostatic screen between transformer primary and secondary lowers interwinding capacitance and hence leakage of switching noise into sensitive analog sections[731]; common mode chokes are fitted to the input of isolated DC-DC converters[810]; and inductors are placed at cable entries to keep converter noise from escaping a probe's enclosure.[932]

For this reason switch mode conversion is deliberately excluded from certain circuits. RF signal generators run switch mode supplies for the chassis but keep the analog board itself entirely linear.[823] A low-noise oscilloscope front end will not be powered from a switcher.[1503] The best bench supplies are linear precisely because their noise and ripple performance is very hard to match with a switch mode design.[166][828][1173] Handheld multimeters commonly accept the wasted battery energy of a linear regulator rather than put a switching converter next to a dual-slope A-to-D.[1533] Op-amp noise measurement setups run from internal batteries, not from any external or switching supply.[528] Where a switcher must coexist with sensitive circuitry, the noise problem can become a project of its own: spurs from the supplies powering high-power ASICs found their way into the output of a 120 MHz function generator and required a full power supply redesign.[1032]

## Mains-side behaviour: power factor and standby

A typical modern product with a non-power-factor-corrected switching supply presents a capacitive, badly non-linear load to the mains, drawing large narrow current spikes rather than a sinusoid.[1413][1191] The consequence is that voltage times current does not equal true power, and correcting it requires adding reactance of the opposite sign — capacitor banks for the inductive loads of a factory, inductance for the capacitive character of a switch mode supply.[1730] These spiky waveforms also have a high crest factor, which is the class of signal that stresses true-RMS measurement circuits.[972]

Standby consumption is regulated in several markets, and products cannot be sold without meeting efficiency requirements — which is why switching plug packs had to be brought under a watt of idle draw, given how many sit permanently powered in a typical home.[1369] Consumer brick supplies are commonly specified below a watt in standby, though architectures that keep a transformer engaged to hold auxiliary circuitry alive set a floor on how low the figure can go.[1032] The same trick appears inside larger equipment: an amplifier with a big linear transformer for the audio stages uses a separate very low power switch mode converter, on the order of 0.1 W, to run the soft power button while a relay disconnects the main transformer.[378]

## Layout and construction

Switch mode layout is a specialised skill and one of the strongest arguments against automatic routing; tight loops matter and an autorouter has no notion of them.[93] A high-frequency DC-DC converter built on solderless breadboard will perform badly because of inter-contact capacitance and stray inductance, and is not a valid test of the design.[568][111] Considerable design effort goes into laying out a compact switching board.[697] Where FPGAs are involved the converters must be characterised dynamically as well as at DC — output impedance differs between the two, and with only tens of millivolts of allowed rail tolerance the difference is significant.[1216]

Physically, vertical riser boards are a recurring construction pattern inside switch mode and lab supplies, used to place control circuitry — op-amps, feedback and protection — off the main power plane crowded with pass devices, heatsinks and bulk capacitors.[1697] Cheap consumer supplies are built to the last cent and use single-sided board.[289] A switcher is easy to identify visually: magnetics are the tell, so an inductor plus high-value capacitors on a board indicates a switching converter, and the absence of any magnetic component indicates there isn't one.[444][1202][1701] Once a design goes switching, the massive heatsinks associated with linear regulation disappear.[1513]

## Faults and repair

Electrolytic capacitor degradation is the classic failure, with rising ESR in the output capacitors dissipating power internally at high output current.[612][365] Not every failure is on the secondary side, though. A supply that repeatedly tries and fails to start — hiccup behaviour — is characteristically a primary-side problem with the converter unable to start up, and three parallel output capacitors are not where that symptom originates.[1423] Where regulation is performed on the secondary side and fed back to a primary-side controller, the presence of a small controller IC on the primary is itself diagnostic of the arrangement.[1423] Diagnosis is frequently obstructed by manufacturers scrubbing part numbers off controller ICs.[1423] A failed switch mode supply can take out downstream regulators on individual boards while leaving rails that are known good untouched, which narrows a search considerably.[538] A completely open mains input reading suggests a blown input fuse rather than a supply fault.[1388]

## Measurement and probing

Probing a live switcher is hazardous and awkward, and several instrument classes exist specifically for it. High-voltage differential probes are essential for measuring high-side switching transistors that sit above signal ground, since an active or ordinary passive probe would destroy the oscilloscope.[1557] Their limitation is bandwidth: common mode rejection that is −50 dB at 1 MHz degrades to −40 dB at 10 MHz, so measuring the high side of a 1 MHz converter is marginal — the case for optically isolated probes with roughly 100 dB of rejection, particularly for high-efficiency, high-voltage work such as EV charging.[1557] Isolated current probes and clamps allow current waveforms to be taken from inside a mains-powered supply without breaking into traces, though bandwidth is typically limited to hundreds of kilohertz to a few megahertz in the affordable classes.[812][1368][769] The positional current probe is used to trace switching waveforms and measure switching frequency directly on a PCB trace without cutting it, with finger guards keeping hands clear of live heatsinks.[296] Probe technique matters as much as the probe: an impedance mismatch in the cabling can substantially amplify apparent ringing and produce a result well off the true figure.[1266] Anyone designing supplies of any kind should also have a precision DC electronic load.[281]

## Prevalence and availability

Switch mode supplies are near-universal inside consumer appliances, instruments and computing hardware, and are among the most reusable parts salvaged from discarded equipment.[289][641][1298] The market for low-cost switching bench and module supplies has expanded substantially, with sub-$100 units offering capable user interfaces and specifications that would have been implausible in a linear design of the same size — a 360 W, 0–60 V, 6 A buck module for around $53, for example.[1298][1265] Density has risen accordingly: fully integrated custom supplies now pack 85 W into a fraction of a small chassis, with the caveat that a custom supply cannot be replaced if it fails.[1635]
