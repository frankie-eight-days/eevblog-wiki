# overvoltage protection

Overvoltage protection (OVP) is a circuit or firmware function that stops a supply rail from exceeding a set ceiling, either by shutting the source down or by clamping the excess.[1293][509] On a bench supply it exists as a settable limit independent of the output voltage setting, so that a knob bump, a wrong keystroke, or an internal failure cannot put more voltage on a load than the load can survive.[1293][439] Inside equipment it appears as a clamp, a trip comparator, or a block of an integrated regulator, and it is one of the standard companions of overcurrent and overtemperature protection.[90][1541]

## Why a separate limit exists

The case for OVP on a bench supply is economic rather than theoretical: a prototype board being powered for the first time may represent tens or hundreds of thousands of dollars of parts and a project schedule that collapses if it is destroyed.[1293][509] The protection setpoint is deliberately separate from the output setting, so that a passer-by who twiddles the front panel — a manager, a marketing person, or simply someone who did not read the label — cannot raise the rail past the ceiling.[1293][509] Overvoltage and overcurrent limits are for this reason treated as lifesaver features when first powering a prototype or anything expensive.[509]

The conventional setpoint is a small margin above the nominal rail: a 3.3 V board is protected at 3.4 V, a 5 V board at something like 5.25 V or 5.5 V.[509][1293][1030] With the limit armed, an attempt to set or slide the output past it is refused — the output switches off, or the supply simply beeps and reverts to the previous value rather than delivering the higher voltage.[509][1691] On a multi-channel supply the ceiling is set per channel.[439][1606] Instruments that store presets may carry a separate overvoltage and overcurrent limit within each preset.[1606]

## Software limits versus hardware trips

Most consumer and mid-range bench supplies implement OVP in firmware: the controller reads back the output and shuts it down, with no dedicated hardware crowbar behind it.[1691][1606][1030][1265] The weakness is explicit — if the software fails, locks up, or otherwise misbehaves, a firmware-only limit will not save the load from overvoltage.[1265] Whether a given supply's protection is software or true hardware is not always documented and is a distinguishing question when evaluating one.[1173] Where the protection is genuinely independent, it is visible as discrete circuitry: hardware overvoltage protection trip and detect stages sitting alongside current monitors, done in hardware rather than software.[1550] Some supplies place dedicated overvoltage protection in the PWM control section purely to stop the output going berserk after an internal component failure.[272] The ideal a well-equipped supply aims at is protection that remains effective even when the regulating path itself has failed.[439]

Related architecture appears in instruments whose front panel and regulation are handled by different processors: overcurrent and overvoltage protection is the job of a local controller close to the output, not of the main applications processor, for reasons of speed, consistency, and separation.[1550]

## Crowbars and clamps

The classic hardware response is a crowbar, which shorts the rail on overvoltage and clamps it down.[905] It is a sacrificial defence: a crowbar that fires on, for example, a supply left switched to 110 V and plugged into 240 V may destroy itself, but it saves the rest of the equipment.[905] Vintage linear supplies commonly carry one across the main rail.[905]

A cheaper clamp is a Zener across the output — a 12 V Zener on the output of a 0–10 V bench supply prevents dangerously high voltages from reaching the load, though whether the Zener survives depends on the capability of whatever is driving it, and no single clamp caters for every scenario.[233] A transient voltage suppressor, sold variously as a TVS or transorb, does the same job with more energy capability; a 12 V part such as the SA12AG clamps output overvoltage while also providing reverse-polarity diode protection.[238] In the design of the output stage of a DC-DC converter, the feedback divider itself can be used as a ceiling: lowering the upper feedback resistor sets an absolute maximum the converter can reach, an inherent safety feature rather than an added circuit.[260]

At meter inputs the clamping elements are metal oxide varistors, chosen with nominal ratings above the meter's rated input — 560 V and 620 V parts appear in a 1000 V handheld front end, clamping anything above the meter's rated range.[1667] Multimeters also carry dedicated overvoltage protection on the ohms path specifically.[426] High-voltage sections rely additionally on isolation slots milled through the PCB, which appear even where the working voltage is modest.[1023][1530]

## Integrated and embedded protection

Linear regulators, LDOs, and switching regulators routinely include overcurrent, overvoltage, and overtemperature protection on-die as added value.[90] Boost regulators are sold with overvoltage protection and soft start as headline features.[259] Battery-charge and power-path controllers integrate overvoltage and overcurrent detection along with charge detection and on-die temperature sensing.[1541] Automotive H-bridge motor drivers add undervoltage and overvoltage protection, thermal protection, cross-conduction protection, and protection against loss of ground or VCC.[413] Audio power stages signal these conditions on a fault interrupt line and disable their outputs when tripped.[1322] Camera and consumer power sections show the function as a named block — a DC-DC converter followed by an overvoltage protector — in the block-partitioned schematic style used for complex systems.[1429]

The distinguishing feature of industrial-grade I/O is that every input and output is protected, typically with current limiting and overvoltage clamping.[979] Vehicle body-controller nodes carry overcurrent protection, automotive transient protection, and overvoltage protection on all inputs and outputs.[1627] PC motherboards expose the function in firmware as anti-surge support, enabling undervoltage and overvoltage protection, and enable it by default.[462] Even inexpensive device programmers advertise overvoltage and overcurrent protection as a way of protecting both the target device and the programmer.[411]

## Lithium cell protection

Small lithium-polymer packs carry a protection PCB whose devices provide overvoltage cutoff and, usually, undervoltage cutoff, switched by MOSFETs on the board.[412][295] A cell protection circuit with overvoltage, overcurrent, and low-voltage functions should, in principle, make the cell impossible to kill — it will disconnect rather than allow the abuse.[1613] The presence of such a board is a basic quality indicator in a teardown of any lithium-powered device.[295][412]

## Trip behaviour and recovery

A tripped limit may latch, requiring explicit operator action to clear: some supplies display an overvoltage protection fault and require a shift-key sequence on the OVP preset key to clear the OVP status and reset before the output can be re-enabled.[439] Latching can have awkward side effects — on a multi-channel supply, an armed or tripped limit on one channel may block turning on any channel at all.[439] Switching controllers more often respond by retreating into hiccup mode, restarting periodically and shutting down again on each attempt; the behaviour is common enough that it is documented in controller datasheets, and its presence during a repair indicates the switching stage is alive and receiving voltage but is meeting an overvoltage or overcurrent condition.[1726] Hiccup is also specified as the deliberate overload response in AC power converters, alongside a stated output overvoltage protection threshold such as 280 V AC enforced by internal supply limiting.[449]

An unexplained output collapse under load can be mistaken for an overvoltage trip, and distinguishing a real protection event from an instability or design fault requires establishing that the trip conditions were actually met.[315] Documentation is frequently inadequate on this point: manuals may say nothing about how the overcurrent and overvoltage protection operate, or whether the setpoints are settable at all.[315]
