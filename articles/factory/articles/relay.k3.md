# relay

A relay is an electromechanical switch in which energizing a coil of wire pulls an armature across, moving a contact from one position to another, so that a low-power control circuit switches a separate, often mains-level or high-current, circuit with full galvanic isolation.[1409] Despite being one of the oldest switching technologies in electronics, relays remain everywhere in modern equipment: they select ranges and input paths in oscilloscopes and multimeters, switch transformer taps in linear power supplies, connect mains to heating elements and EV charge cables, and provide mandated disconnection in grid-tied inverters.[1004][544][1643][1620]

## Construction and coil drive

Inside a typical sealed relay are the coil and the contacts; activating the coil pulls the armature across and transfers the contact.[1409] A relay has a minimum coil turn-on voltage, typically specified on the data sheet as a percentage of the nominal coil voltage, on the order of 80 percent; a 24 V relay starved of coil voltage will fail to actuate even while the rest of the control electronics works normally.[1409][1481] Coils are driven from transistor stages, from high-voltage Darlington driver arrays such as the DS2004 (which integrate the catch diodes), or directly from open-collector comparator outputs rated to tens of volts and tens of milliamps.[606][1464]

A relay coil is an inductor storing energy in its magnetic field, so interrupting the coil current produces back EMF: by Lenz's law the induced voltage opposes the change that caused it, and the resulting spike can destroy the drive transistor unless a freewheeling (clamp, flyback, or back EMF) diode is fitted across the coil.[1409] A demonstration of this uses a 2SC2610 transistor rated at 300 V specifically to survive the spike when the diode is removed.[1409] The same mechanism appears at the contacts when a relay switches an inductive load such as a motor: opening the contacts can produce high-voltage arcing across them, which is why a clamp or a resistor-capacitor snubber may be fitted across the switching element as well.[1409]

## Signal switching in test and measurement equipment

Oscilloscope front ends use relays to select the 50 ohm versus 1 megohm input path and to switch AC or DC coupling.[1510] The attenuation range changeover is audible as a relay click — for example at the 100 to 200 mV/div boundary on both a GW Instek GDS-1000B and a Keysight HD3, where two major attenuation ranges cover the input span.[824][1639] Models without 50 ohm termination simply omit the relay, as on the Rigol DHO800 relative to its stablemates.[1563] Relay counts in scope front ends run high: four relays per channel in the Siglent SDS2000X, and relays "everywhere" in the Owon XDS3202A, including eight for the arbitrary waveform generator and pass/fail outputs.[864][1004] A current probe switches between its 10 A and 100 A ranges with a relay rather than electronic switching because of the parasitics a solid-state switch would introduce.[1413] Selecting 50 ohm output impedance on an arbitrary waveform generator does not insert a physical 50 ohm resistor with a relay; the resistance is always in circuit and the high-impedance option only rescales the permitted output voltage in software.[1560]

Precision instruments place the heaviest demands on relay quality. The Fluke 5450A resistance calibrator contains 34 relays, documented on an internal relay map listing which relays energize per range; the low-value decades require top-quality low-contact-resistance relays, with multiple contacts paralleled to bring contact resistance and long-term error within specification, while the megaohm ranges use Coto reed relays where contact resistance no longer matters.[544][545] Reed relays also serve RF and switching-matrix applications, with Pickering relays specified for switching from DC to 65 GHz at prices up to $10 per relay.[358]

In multimeters the autoranging or jack-switching relay is often audible and can dominate measurement speed: the Agilent 34461A's fast ohms reading includes the time to physically switch its relay, without which it would be near-instantaneous.[489] Autoranging relays that flicker back and forth under 50 Hz mains pickup are a genuine design defect — a meter needs a settable number of power line cycles so the relays are not worn by continuous hunting.[1382][1379]

## Power switching

At the mains end, relays switch heating elements directly across the line in fan heaters and ovens — a 3 kW oven element visibly cycles on and off under relay control — and a pair of relays reverses polarity across a Peltier device to select heating or cooling in a thermal chamber.[1481][gAu8CvMjDrU][101] The Sinclair C5 used a single relay as its entire motor speed control, giving strictly binary on/off drive.[501] Linear bench supplies use relays to select transformer secondary taps, keeping headroom across the pass transistor reasonable; the Rigol DP832 substitutes triacs for this function.[30][TQcV3ftPLgA][512] Multi-output supplies use relays for internal series/parallel and tracking interconnection so channels can be combined without external shorting bars.[828][439][1174]

An EVSE is not a charger; it is essentially a controller, a PWM pilot signal, and relays that connect mains straight through to the vehicle, which contains the actual charger — both active and neutral are switched.[1643][1507][5rZk8mNvYUc][1437] In grid-tied hybrid inverters, two relays are wired in series on the grid connection because one failure mode of a relay is the contacts arcing over and welding permanently short; the redundant series contact is a regulatory disconnection requirement.[1620] A mechanical automatic transfer switch rated at 63 A transfers in 50 milliseconds with an audible clunk, whereas critical loads such as server racks use electronic versions with effectively zero switchover time.[1500] In a defibrillator, relays hold the patient terminals isolated until the SCRs and IGBTs sequence the discharge.[909]

## Latching relays

Latching relays retain their state with no coil power, which suits battery-powered instruments: the ANENG Q1 multimeter uses a latching Hongfa relay specifically so no current is wasted holding the coil energized.[1096] In mains equipment the choice is more debatable; an EVSE fitted with latching relays holds its contacts closed through a power loss, and the engineering preference there is a normally open non-latching relay that drops out and requires the control electronics to actively maintain the energized state.[1507]

## Reliability, failure modes, and troubleshooting

Relays are the classic electromechanical weak link, yet in at least one long-term railway PA amplifier application they proved "ridiculously reliable," even when socketed in a high-vibration environment.[354] Wear-out is real under rapid cycling: a stepper motor sequenced at speed by PLC relays in ladder logic would destroy the relays within days.[940] Particulate contamination can prevent contact closure entirely — a single grain of dust in a relay was among the failure mechanisms behind NASA's adoption of clean rooms around 1958.[BUW6h88weXU][Uk5F3rQNUkU] Contacts can also be damaged by breaking substantial current or short circuits, an experiment that destroyed one channel of a lab power supply.[861]

The audible click is a first-line diagnostic. Its absence when a load should energize points immediately to missing coil drive — in one heater repair, a degraded X2 capacitor in a capacitive-dropper supply left the 24 V rail too low to pull the relay in while the micro and display kept working.[1481][1482] Conversely, hearing relays click during power-on tells the troubleshooter the standby supply, soft-power logic, and much of the digital chain are alive before any display appears.[1394][536][682]

## Components and construction practice

Brand matters: NEC, Omron, and other Japanese relays are consistently noted as the quality choice, with shielded Omron parts, TE Connectivity Schrack types, and Japanese relays in instrument front ends singled out favorably, while unbranded or obscure relays attract suspicion.[1004][1709][1263][1679][440] Relays are also a significant bill-of-materials item, and removing them is a recognized cost-saving move in lower-tier instrument variants.[1355] Magnetically shielded relays with mu-metal shields exist for sensitive front ends; stick-on shields over unshielded relays are a cheaper afterthought.[1355] A stated bench preference favors designs that solve switching problems with relays rather than solid-state alternatives — a self-described "relay fanboy" position — on the grounds that an audible clunk of relays at power-up instills confidence.[1004][781] Relays are also among the worthwhile salvage items from scrapped power supplies, printers, and UPS units.[1302][1168]
