# ram

RAM is fast volatile read/write memory: the working store a processor scribbles in while it runs, as distinct from the ROM or flash that holds the program.[410][1111] The distinction between a microprocessor and a microcontroller rests on it — a microprocessor needs external memory, both RAM and program memory, while a microcontroller has both built in along with the IO.[1111] That built-in memory is what let a single chip replace a board of parts and drive the bill of materials down far enough to put a microcontroller inside a $20 toy.[1111] Because RAM is volatile, it is also the thing that must be kept alive by a backup cell, or lost when power goes.[1662][947]

## The canonical architecture

A vast range of equipment reduces to the same three blocks: a processor, some ROM, some RAM, plus whatever IO the job needs.[641][645] A TRS-80 Model I is a Z80 with its RAM and its ROM and essentially nothing else but 74LS series logic.[645] A dumpster cash register needs a processor, some RAM and some ROM, a thermal printer driver and a communications interface, and little else.[641] The same layout appears in an HP 3457A multimeter alongside the main processor,[426] in a Stanford Research instrument built round a Z80 with ROM, RAM and a GPIB interface,[621] in a 1990-vintage instrument with one RAM and two ROMs implying a 16-bit bus,[1008] in an old-school industrial controller around a Hitachi 6303 with an 8255 for peripherals,[745] and on an off-the-shelf 6809 controller card carrying serial IO, RAM, ROM and a few miscellaneous chips.[1097] An HP 85 is unusual in having ROM and RAM and no glue logic at all, because HP did all their own custom ASICs rather than the address decoders an IBM PC of the period needed.[903]

Where double-sided board fabrication was expensive, memory chips were stacked physically on top of one another — RAM stacks or memory stacks — with only the chip select or one address line bent out, since every other pin-out is identical.[680] It is ugly but it routes easily.[680]

## Capacities, historically

Early micro RAM was measured in bytes. An Intel MCS-85 8085 design kit shipped with a processor, a monitor ROM, one RAM chip and a keyboard/display controller;[1308] a second RAM chip brought one example to 512 bytes total, and the extra port expansion added a further 256 bytes.[1308][1308] A Motorola 6804 mask-ROM micro in a 1980s voice recognition product had 1.6K of program ROM and 30 bytes of RAM;[713] the PIC16C57 running a MicroTAC's charge algorithm had a 2K-word one-time-programmable program store and 72 bytes of RAM, one 8-bit timer, and no UART or ADC.[492] A Hitachi HD 63 P0 had a couple of hundred bytes of RAM and piggyback machine pins on top for the EPROM.[993]

Personal computers of the period were measured in kilobytes. The ZX81 had 1K built in, with the US-market Timex Sinclair 1000 doubling it to 2K,[872] against a $99 price.[872] An HP 85 came standard with 8K expandable to 64K via rear expansion packs,[903][904] though at least one example carries 16K on the main board where 8K was the specification.[905] A TRS-80 Color Computer eventually shipped with 64K at a $399 launch price in 1980 dollars.[1021] A Cambridge Z88 had 32K built in, expandable with 128K packs up to around 4 meg.[382] An Apple IIc came with 128K and a built-in 140K floppy.[788] An IBM PCjr was $669 for 64 kilobytes or $1269 for 128 kilobytes, and in either case came with no storage at all — a large part of why the machine failed.[1053] A fully populated PCjr with the extra 64K card and a 500K expansion reaches 640K.[1053][1053] An Amiga 500 had 512K,[438][438] an Amiga 2000 2 meg in two banks,[Gbn_51IoJiM] a Sharp X68000 1 meg,[746] an Acorn Archimedes A3000 2 meg,[507] a Mac SE 4 meg across four SIMM slots at 1 meg per slot,[414] and an IBM L40SX 18 meg, of which the 16 meg was directly usable and the extra 2 meg available as EMS.[639] An HP 9845B took 512K boards, three of which expanded it to 1.5 meg, while another of its memory boards used 4116 chips organised 16K x 1.[1404] Portables followed the same curve: a Dulmont Magnum was about $3,000 Australian for the 96k model or $4,000 for 256k,[949] a PowerBook 165C had 14 megabytes,[W91qP8GpgjM] a Psion Organizer II from 1986 8K,[320] a Psion 5 two megabytes in 1993,[729] and an HP 48G 32K.[979]

## RAM in modern microcontrollers

Even current parts are RAM-poor by desktop standards, and the number is a primary selection criterion. A Padauk part in the 150 series has 64 bytes; the next one up doubles that to 128, of which a trivial program used four.[1141] A 3-cent micro in SOT23 carries 64 bytes alongside a comparator and internal reference but no ADC.[1132] An MSP430F2013 in a bench power supply has 2K of flash and 128 bytes of RAM, chosen for its 16-bit sigma-delta ADC with internal reference rather than its memory.[440] Larger parts move into kilobytes: an STM32 with an LCD driver offers 32K of RAM against 256K of ECC flash,[900] an ARM Cortex-M0 board 8K RAM with 64K flash,[715] an ESP8266 module roughly 64K, enough to run applications without an external micro,[998] a PIC24FJ128GL303 8K,[1538] and an STM32-class Cortex-M4 in a smartwatch 256K plus a separate 4K.[761] Where a part is executing from RAM rather than flash, its quoted run-mode current can differ sharply — a headline figure measured with code running from RAM may be on par with or worse than a competitor once the code runs from flash.[1539]

RAM also turns up outside the processor. An LCD driver chip's internal display RAM can hold the frame data so a memory-limited microcontroller does not have to, and can be read back later for manipulation.[1074] A socket-tester LCD controller carries 32 by 4 bits of RAM for the same purpose.[1598]

## RAM in instruments

In a logic analyser the acquisition RAM is filled continuously through a circular address counter while the instrument waits for a trigger, so pre-trigger data is already captured; the trigger, after any delay, flips a flip-flop and stops the fill.[747] One such design used 32K of memory.[747] Scope memory depth is a cost lever: cut-down models appear with RAM omitted from the board,[1563] or with half the RAM of the model above.[1355] A high-speed camera holds a capture in RAM until it is explicitly saved and warns before a new recording discards unsaved data.[947]

Magnetic core memory is the ancestor: a sense line reads back the state set or reset in each ferrite ring, with an access time around a microsecond, putting it in the region of 1 MHz.[648][paQ3zIsz1-8]

## Electrical and physical behaviour

RAM is not a passive presence on a board. Under thermal imaging, the RAM in an 8085 design kit runs hotter than the processor itself — 47 °C for the lower device against 42 °C for the upper, with the difference attributed to which one is actually being accessed.[3zAWgNjx5Ew][3zAWgNjx5Ew] Its address lines are among the most active signals present, which makes them a useful EMC probe target: a near-field probe held over the RAM picks up high-level signal, comparable to holding it over the ROM or the address decoder.[1188] A single address line from the driving chip to the RAM chip is the natural signal to compare between a two-layer and a four-layer version of the same board.[1176] Removing every bypass capacitor from the side of a board carrying the instruction register, the RAM and the ROM did not stop the circuit working, though the clock lines were another matter.[1081]

Physical damage tells: small amounts of surface wash fluid around the legs of RAM chips leave visible residue even in a 12 V section of a board, while the same fluid at 120 V levels elsewhere etched arcs clean through the PCB.[354]

## Cost, scarcity and obsolescence

RAM was expensive when these machines were new, and stayed valuable enough that expansion RAM boards turn up with the chips pilfered.[1060] ROM and RAM are by far the most common parts to go obsolete in long-lived instrument designs, and a DDR substitution is not a drop-in: DDR has registers that must be configured, so the firmware changes with the part.[1032]

## Modern PC practice

Module count and channel configuration matter as much as total capacity. A four-channel board is populated with a set of four 8 GB modules of a matched speed grade.[1098] Adding a second 8 GB stick to bring a machine to 16 GB also enables dual-channel operation.[NLwgFYt1pjA] Boards report a memory mismatch when banks disagree,[NLwgFYt1pjA] and a BIOS may simply refuse a module it does not like — five beeps on power-up, cured by fitting a different stick.[Lhmg1jUNxVc] Older machines with DIP switches for memory settings were superseded by BIOSes that auto-detect how much RAM is fitted and ignore the switches entirely.[1348]

Because it is one of the few socketed parts left, RAM is both the first thing stripped from a discarded machine and a standard diagnostic swap.[466][1212] Swapping the RAM along with the CPU and thermal compound revived one machine, and the original RAM then worked as well, pointing at a contact problem rather than a failed module.[oGNyQ_A5kZI] A machine that resists every substitution — CPU, power supply, video and memory — indicates a dead motherboard.[7mDglCZTC9o]

The consistent engineering judgment is that capacity requirements are routinely overstated for ordinary use: 8 GB is enough for a general office machine,[okaYXwzHzoY][1342] 16 GB is plenty for anything a small N150-class mini PC will run,[1656] and 20 GB in a recovered all-in-one is not worth upgrading.[esJN76rykfQ] Shipping a machine with only 4 GB, or charging a large premium to move from 4 GB to 8 GB, is treated as a rip-off.[kF7sJbAQRHY][1212] Video rendering is the exception where large capacities genuinely apply, with 128 GB in a dual-Xeon workstation.[871][3J1JdeHBKd8]

Soldered memory is the standing objection to modern designs. Where a machine is made thin enough that neither the RAM nor the storage can be upgraded, the eventual failure of the flash renders the whole product scrap.[1407] Screwed-in and socketed assemblies are preferred wherever the mechanical design allows it.[1727]

## Backup and retention

Because the contents vanish with power, RAM in portable equipment is commonly held up by a small secondary cell. A Tandy 200's rechargeable battery exists specifically to protect the RAM,[1662] and a Toshiba T1000LE carries a sub-battery of series N cells that keeps RAM alive through a soft power-off and a main battery change so the machine resumes its previous state.[1527] Even after decades, the silicon generally survives: a Compaq Portable board pulled out and run from a bench supply still had working mask ROM and working RAM.[1354]
