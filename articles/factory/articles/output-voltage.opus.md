# output voltage

Output voltage is the potential a source, regulator, converter, or amplifier presents at its terminals, and it is the parameter around which most power and signal-source design is organised. It is rarely a single fixed number: it is set by a resistor ratio or a digital word, it moves with load and with supply, it is bounded by the device's own headroom, and it is specified with a tolerance that real hardware sometimes fails to meet.[158][366][579][f_SdM6sXHD4] Because almost every other specification — efficiency, compliance current, dropout, accuracy — is quoted at a particular output voltage, the number chosen up front propagates through the whole design.[139][1031]

## Setting the output

In a classic adjustable linear regulator the output voltage is fixed by a two-resistor divider from the output back to the adjust terminal. For an LM317 targeting 3.3 V, the lower leg is a nominal value — 240 Ω is the usual datasheet figure, though 220 Ω with a variable upper leg works equally well — and the upper resistor is then calculated from the standard formula.[158] Switching converters work the same way: a DC-DC output of about 7.6 V follows from a 51 kΩ upper resistor over a 10 kΩ lower one, plus one, multiplied by the converter's 1.245 V internal reference.[366] The same principle extends to high-voltage supplies, where a single 680 Ω external resistor sets a 170 V rail from a 12 V input.[948]

Because the divider is the only thing that defines the output, two otherwise pin-equivalent parts that share the same output-voltage setting arrangement can be substituted directly, though output capacitor ESR must still be checked for stability.[1475] Conversely, when an unknown part must be identified, knowing the input voltage and the intended output voltage narrows the search substantially, since the resistor values can then be worked back through the candidate datasheet formulas.[1475]

Where the output must be programmable, the reference is replaced by a DAC or a PWM signal. A 0–20 V output range — or 20.48 V, which falls out neatly on DAC steps — is a convenient design target because it needs only a doubling of the DAC-stage gain.[259] Driving the control node from a PWM output works down to very small duty cycles, with the output tracking the duty ratio: 20 % of a 6.6 V control span through a times-two amplifier gives the expected 1.3 V.[225] Such a chain can go wrong in ways that show up only as a scale error — an output moving 200 mV per intended one-millivolt step points at an unintended times-two amplifier stage rather than at the DAC itself.[240]

## Accuracy, tolerance, and deliberate offset

The setting resistors do not tell the whole story. Current out of the LM317 adjust terminal flows through the upper resistor and adds directly to the output: taking the worst-case 100 µA rather than the 50 µA typical, across 363 Ω, adds about 36 mV, shifting a nominal 3.3 V output to 3.336 V.[158] The conventional choice of 240 Ω for the lower resistor exists largely to keep this adjust-terminal error small.[158] Ordinary 5 % resistors are enough to land a predicted 7.6 V output at a measured 7.723 V.[366]

Instrument-grade supplies are expected to read back what they are set to — 4.97 V displayed against a 4.9 V setting on a repaired bench supply is normal behaviour — and to hold that after abuse, with output voltage and current still within specification after a full sequence of load tests.[1036][439] A default 1 V output sitting about 3 mV under nominal is unremarkable.[1293] A factory-fresh process meter that delivers 7.9 V when commanded to output 8.000 V, and roughly 0.7 V when set to 1 V against a 0.1 % specification, is out of calibration and fails outright.[f_SdM6sXHD4]

Not every deviation from the nominal figure is an error. A DIN-rail 5 V supply measuring 5.15 V is doing so on purpose — "you don't want it actually precisely 5" — because cable drop between the supply and the load subtracts from whatever leaves the terminals, so starting slightly high means the load still sees 5 V.[1656]

## Limits of range

No output stage reaches its own supply rails. An OPA277's minimum output voltage is the negative rail plus roughly half a volt, so with the negative rail at ground the output simply cannot approach zero, and the operating point must be lifted above that floor — about one silicon diode drop, 0.6 V, suffices.[579] Output swing also depends on the supply: reducing an op-amp's rails from ±5 V to ±2.5 V drops the output voltage while the input stays constant, because gain-bandwidth product is not independent of supply voltage.[572]

Published maxima are sometimes conservative. A high-voltage differential probe specified at 7 V maximum output does deliver ±7 V, and in fact clips only above about 8 V — over specification on the voltage actually presented to the scope input.[1414] A later model in the same family reduced the output swing to ±3 V from the ±7 V of its predecessors, with consequences for noise floor.[1631] Function generator and source ranges are quoted the same way: ±12 V from an integrated function generator, 0–35 V from a 6641A-class supply, 0–100 V (or 200 V in series) from a 100 V bench supply, and roughly 200 V at 1 A from a general-purpose source-measure unit.[876][655][1402][718] A newer 800 W supply doubles both the output voltage of its predecessor, with a 64 V option, and the current.[1293]

Devices with a fixed or menu-selected output offer a discrete set instead of a range: 1.8, 2.5, 3.3 and 3.6 V from an energy-harvesting front end, five levels from 3.3 down to 1.2 V on a programmer's supply rail, or a continuously adjustable 4.8 V to 12.5 V on a model-aircraft regulator.[534][991][895]

## Output voltage as a selection parameter

Datasheet performance curves are plotted at a specific output voltage, and applying them at any other output is a trap. Characteristic curves drawn for Vout = 1.8 V have no bearing on a 5 V design, and a graph that omits the output voltage entirely is useless; the correct approach is to find the curve family explicitly labelled for the intended output — Vout = 5 V — which will also list the inductance and input/output capacitance values at which it was measured.[139] A boost converter advertising low input voltage and 94 % efficiency is disqualified immediately if its fixed output voltage is 3.3 V and the requirement is 5 V, an outcome a parametric search sorted on output voltage would have caught earlier.[139]

Efficiency itself is strongly a function of the output voltage chosen. A buck module from a 30 V input reaches roughly 95 % at 24 V, 12 V and 5 V outputs, but a 1.5 V output would fall to around 60 %; the peak is placed where the designer optimised the output power level.[1031] The same module from a 40 V input can only produce its two highest outputs, up to a maximum of 32 V.[1031]

## Behaviour under load and in circuit

Output voltage is meaningful only alongside the source impedance behind it. A Cockcroft-Walton multiplier that reads high on a light load falls to 75.6 V into 10 MΩ and to 70 V into 1 MΩ, a load most circuits would consider negligible.[469] A regulator's own output is also affected by what is connected: a rail measured at 14.7 V with a load disconnected is the correct starting point for deciding whether that load should then function.[1087]

Switching the output on and off does not necessarily take it to zero. A soft-latching switch leaves about 500 mV at the output after turn-off, which then decays, rather than snapping to ground.[262] In a supply where a series pass MOSFET performs the switching, the gate waveform and the 30 V output waveform can be watched together on two channels to confirm that the switch, not the regulator, is what is acting.[512]

Feedback arrangements make the output voltage of one stage a dependent variable of another. In a composite amplifier the precision front-end op-amp no longer sits at zero once the loop senses the buffer's output: its own output moves to −55 mV to compensate, which is the mechanism by which the buffer's offset is driven to zero at the sense point.[1609] A peak detector driven too fast, or loaded too heavily through its discharge path, shows overshoot on its output voltage.[490]

Sources that are not regulators still have an output voltage worth reasoning about. A thermoelectric generator produces a voltage from the temperature difference across its plates, and reversing the differential reverses the polarity of the output; the current, however, comes from the heat actually transferred through the module.[664] Even a poor, unregulated rail can be useful if its output voltage clears the headroom of a following regulator — 3.64 V from a charge-pump inverter built with 1N4148 diodes and small capacitors is enough to feed a 3.3 V low-dropout regulator at a couple of hundred microamps and produce a clean supply.[483]

## Measurement and diagnosis

Monitoring output voltage alongside input voltage is what allows firmware to know whether a regulator still has enough headroom to regulate, so an instrumented supply digitises both the output voltage and the incoming source voltage rather than the output alone.[232] The same pairing is used on the bench: feeding a regulator's output from a second supply while watching input voltage, output voltage, and the current flowing back in characterises behaviour at switch-on and switch-off.[233]

A working output is often the fastest confirmation that a repair is sound — 12.6 V present after replacing marginal capacitors settles both the repair and the choice of 16 V parts.[1726] Output voltage is equally capable of misleading: an anomalous integrator output voltage in a microvolt DMM proved to be a red herring unrelated to the actual fault.[777] Prototype converters are verified the same way, with 15 V out from a 5 V input confirming the topology before output ripple is examined under load.[110] Where a source is being characterised rather than used, input current and output voltage are measured together to check the claimed 0.2 % figure.[197]

Bench supply displays are built around this set of quantities: measured output voltage, measured output current, and measured output power at 1 mW resolution, with the set values shown separately.[509][1265] A supply that switches its display between measured output current and the adjustable current setting depending on whether the output is enabled removes the need to short the terminals to set a current limit.[315]
