# pcb footprint

A PCB footprint is the copper land pattern, solder mask openings, hole sizes and silkscreen outline that a component physically occupies on a board, held in a CAD library and linked to the schematic symbol that represents that part.[254][974][952] It is the point where an abstract netlist becomes a manufacturable object: the footprint determines whether the part can be soldered, whether it can be tested, whether the traces around it can be routed, and whether the assembled board works at all.[974][1306][555] Getting a footprint wrong is one of the most common and most expensive prototype errors, because unlike a schematic mistake it cannot be patched without physically abusing the part or spinning the board.[555][357]

## Land pattern standards and pad sizing

The IPC publishes standard land patterns, and they are issued in three density levels rather than as a single recommended geometry.[186][1306] The three sizes are described as normal, least, and most pad size, and the choice between them is driven by the density of the board — a dense layout takes the smaller pads, a sparse one can afford the larger.[186][1306] The difference is essentially how far the pad overhangs the end cap of the component.[1306]

For boards that will be hand soldered, the larger pad options are preferable, because they leave exposed copper beyond the component body where a chisel tip can make thermal contact with the pad and the lead simultaneously; with flux, the joint then forms on its own through surface tension.[186] The same extended pad doubles as a flying probe test point, which makes generous pads worth the board area on any design headed for flying probe test.[1306] There is a limit: pads long enough to suit a bare prototype board are longer than is sensible in a production footprint.[186]

Solder mask expansion between adjacent pads is part of the footprint, not an afterthought. A pad that is too wide leaves too little mask between pins, the mask breaks through, and solder bridges during hand soldering or wave soldering; a gap of four or five thou of mask between pads is a workable target, and narrowing the pad is the usual fix.[244]

Where a manufacturer publishes a recommended pattern — tactile domes and similar mechanical parts are typical — that pattern should be used as given.[1291] The patterns are fairly tolerant of small deviation, but departing from them without a specific reason is not worth the risk.[1291] Recommended footprints are also printed in device datasheets alongside the package dimensions, and those dimensions are exactly the inputs an IPC footprint wizard in a layout package asks for when generating the land pattern.[sr1DOHnJi8I] A correctly followed manufacturer footprint shows up in the reflow result: a battery holder pad barely larger than the tab itself still reflows cleanly on the edges at a peak of just over 230 °C.[562]

## Library discipline and failure modes

Having every schematic component carry a correct, verified footprint before layout begins is the first step of a board design, and time spent sorting the libraries out beforehand is recovered during layout.[244][974] A board built entirely from existing, proven parts goes much faster than one that needs new footprints, precisely because the footprints are already known good.[244] Creating a footprint from scratch is not the bottleneck — a schematic symbol and a land pattern each take a minute or two, and dimensions can be reconstructed from photographs and partial data when a mechanical drawing is incomplete.[952]

Pinout errors are the classic footprint failure, and they are worse than a reversed silkscreen because they cannot be worked around by orientation alone.[555] Transistors are the worst offenders: "Always double-check your transistor footprints" is a standing rule, since the same three-lead package is issued with different pinouts by different manufacturers and part types.[555][357] Recovery from a wrong three-pin footprint means rotating the part on the pads, flipping it upside down and bending the leads, or some combination — workable on a rush prototype, but it must be repeated on every affected device on the board.[357]

Dimensional errors are subtler and scale with package size. A footprint whose pin pitch is slightly off from the specified 0.6 mm places the first pins acceptably and then accumulates error along the row, until the outer pins sit visibly off-centre; on a small part it is survivable, on a large chip it becomes a real assembly problem.[689] Because errors of this kind are invisible in the schematic, checking a suspect footprint against the datasheet mid-layout is routine practice, and doubling as a pass to consolidate hole sizes.[244]

Jellybean parts warrant the opposite of minimalism in the library: a general-purpose transistor such as a 2N3904 is available in DFN, SC-70, SOT-23, SOT-223 and TO-92 among others, down to packages of roughly 0.6 mm by 0.8 mm, and building the same part into the library in all of those footprints means a design constraint on size never forces a change of part.[1599]

## Footprints as a manufacturing variable

The footprint set is a design-for-manufacture lever in its own right, distinct from consolidating the bill of materials or substituting cheaper parts.[1307] Consolidating footprints — reducing the number of distinct land patterns a board uses — matters because the pick-and-place machine, not the schematic, pays the cost, and ignoring this can make a device more expensive to build than it needs to be.[1307] The specific case is package size taken too far: 0201 capacitors are half the size of an already small 0402, and specifying 0201 for a 1 µF part with no space pressure to justify it is a poor trade.[1307]

The footprint name is carried through to the assembler in the pick-and-place file, alongside the reference designator and the component's X-Y location relative to a datum that is usually but not necessarily the bottom left corner of the board.[127] The file can be CSV or effectively any format the manufacturer accepts, and it is supplied together with the overlay diagram showing where each designator goes.[127]

Company-standard footprints and approved spacings, enforced through DRC constraints, are a real force on layout at established firms, and constraints carried forward from a decade earlier can still be shaping boards being designed today.[1512] In contract layout work, the schematics handed over generally include most footprints already, sometimes with alternatives for the layout engineer to consider, along with mechanical requirements and placement information for connectors and critical parts.[795]

## Placement, routing and optional footprints

Footprint geometry constrains routing directly. Where four traces must pass between unused pads and a 15 thou clearance rule leaves no room, the options are to relax the clearance or to edit the footprint — narrowing the pads vertically, or converting rectangular pads to rectangles with rounded corners, to open the channel.[974] Modifying the land pattern to serve the route is a normal part of layout rather than a last resort.[974]

Pad size also affects serviceability. Large footprints on a board that is not densely populated make it easy to get a probe onto a node, which is a deliberate advantage of not shrinking packages further than the layout requires.[330] Board space is finite, though, and a discrete crystal oscillator circuit costs three components' worth of area plus the clearance a high-impedance node needs around it.[1614]

Footprints need not be populated at all. Laying down multiple capacitor footprints and fitting only as many as a given model requires lets one PCB serve a 50 W and a 100 W variant of the same product, with one or two capacitors in the low-power build and four in the high-power one.[742] Unpopulated footprints are also a visible tell in commercial products: an empty footprint identical to a fitted one next to it, positioned behind an unused front-panel cutout, indicates a planned option that the particular model does not carry.[1639] Kits and collectible boards are sometimes shipped with the footprints bare and the parts left to the builder to source.[1471][951]

Where two boards share an identical footprint and mounting hole pattern, mechanical options follow: the same board can be mounted to the bottom of an enclosure or flipped and mounted to the top, and stacked assemblies become possible without a second layout.[258]

## Footprints in CAD packages

The mechanism for assigning a footprint to a schematic symbol differs sharply between packages and is a frequent source of friction. In Altium the footprint is a model attached to the component, and building a custom part — schematic symbol, footprint, simulation model, 3D model — is done from a single panel.[754][974] KiCad historically split the job across a separate association program that read the netlist and matched footprints to symbols after the schematic was finished, rather than offering a footprint picker inside the schematic editor itself.[254][253] The expectation from an Altium background is a button in the schematic that opens the PCB library, chooses the land pattern and pushes it through to the board; typing a footprint name into a text field is a poor substitute.[254]

Terminology compounds the problem: KiCad at the time called a footprint a module in some dialogs and a footprint in others, including a footprint editor labelled the module editor, on the grounds that "nobody calls a PCB footprint a module".[254] Filtered footprint lists — offering only the plausible patterns for a capacitor or a DB9 connector — are useful to a beginner, and the full unfiltered list remains reachable, but an unfiltered list can run to hundreds of entries for a single 8-pin DIP.[254] Automatic footprint association exists and may serve as a decent first pass, though automated assignment in a CAD program invites distrust.[254] Missing library entries surface on netlist import as footprints not found, which for demonstration projects shipping with the tool is a poor first impression.[254]

Other tool behaviours around footprints include selection filters that let footprints be excluded while tracks and vias are retained, which is what makes bulk layer swapping on a four-layer board possible.[1193] Some open-source layout editors can generate footprints on the fly from a scripting language or a dynamically generated element definition, though the scripting support is weaker on Windows than on Linux.[125] Data-exchange viewers built around standards such as IPC-2581 expose footprints as a browsable category alongside components, highlighting every instance where a given pattern appears, in the manner of a CAD system.[349] Board-level tools also vary in how they carry footprint metadata; an Eagle design may hold device footprints without any link to distributor part numbers, which limits how much of a BOM can be recovered from the file.[1374]
