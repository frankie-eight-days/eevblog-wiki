# output capacitance

Output capacitance is the capacitance placed across the output terminals of a regulator, converter or reference. It does three jobs at once — it filters ripple, it supplies charge during load transients faster than the control loop can respond, and it forms part of the loop's frequency compensation, so its value and its ESR determine whether the circuit is stable at all.[1115][1147][222] Because those jobs pull in opposite directions, and because stored energy in the output capacitor escapes the control of any current-limiting circuit upstream of it, choosing the value is a trade-off rather than a case of more being better.[224][511]

## Stability: a minimum, and sometimes a maximum

Regulators with an internal error amplifier — linear regulators, low-dropout regulators and voltage references alike — can oscillate if they are not loaded with the correct amount of capacitance, the correct type of capacitor, and the correct ESR.[1147][567] LDOs are the classic case, since the PNP or P-channel pass element they use to achieve the low dropout voltage is inherently more unstable, and a minimum output capacitance is required to hold the loop together.[1147][664] Data sheets state that minimum explicitly; one part specifies 2.2 µF as the minimum for stability.[664][224]

The requirement is not always one-sided. Some parts specify a *maximum* output capacitance rather than a minimum, going unstable above it.[827] The TL431 shunt reference is stable only over part of the capacitive-load range: it has a stable region and an unstable region, so a capacitor cannot simply be attached to the output on the assumption that it will help.[1438] Stability is a property of the whole loop, not of the capacitor alone — adding an op-amp buffer inside the feedback path changes the poles enough that a circuit which needed a 470 µF output capacitor to behave becomes stable with that capacitance removed entirely, at the cost of somewhat more noise.[577]

In practice the data-sheet minimum is a guideline rather than a hard threshold. A design specifying 2.2 µF minimum of ceramic has been run stably on 1 µF of a non-ceramic type; removing the output capacitance altogether left the rail working but noticeably noisier, with a 0.47 µF part enough to clean it up again.[224]

## Ripple, transient response and the sizing calculation

Greater output capacitance improves load transient response: the droop is smaller, though recovery takes longer.[222] The effect is directly measurable — a design with 1 µF of output capacitance showed a 50 mV step for a quarter-amp load change, and adding 47 µF shrank the transient substantially.[224]

For a switching converter the output capacitor is sized from the ripple specification. With an output current of 100 mA, an on-time of 7.3 µs and a target of 100 mV peak-to-peak ripple, the standard expression gives 66 µF.[110] The controller data sheet for an LED driver, working the same calculation in reverse, needs roughly 470 µF on the output to hold ripple current to 30% — itself a large figure.[1253]

Charge-pump converters make the dependency awkward. The ripple curves published for the 7660 family assume a nominal output capacitance of 10 µF and a switching capacitor of 10 µF, so reading a figure such as 50 mV peak-to-peak at 10 mA off the graph and expecting it in a real circuit is a trap: the result depends on the capacitance actually present, on the ESR of the capacitor chosen, and on the derating of that capacitance under DC bias.[1115]

Not every converter needs much. A DC-DC converter tested with no output capacitance at all held 4.93 V from a 4.9 V input right up to its maximum load, needing only a bypass capacitor.[324] At the other extreme, a physically tiny boost converter has extremely limited output capacitance and peak current capability by virtue of its size, leaving the load's own internal decoupling to handle pulse currents.[751]

## Lab power supplies: keep it as low as possible

The strongest practical constraint appears in bench supplies with a constant-current mode. The output capacitor sits after the current-sensing and regulation circuitry, so the charge stored in it is not current-limited.[1402][511] When the supply transitions from constant voltage into constant current, that energy dumps into the load before the loop has time to respond, delivering a current spike above the set limit — precisely at the moment the current limit was supposed to be protecting the circuit.[1293][1691][549] A 2200 µF capacitor added for transient performance therefore buys better regulation and a worse current limit.[222] The design goal for any supply with constant-current capability is to minimise output capacitance while still keeping ripple low.[511][1691][1298]

Teardowns show the principle applied with varying discipline. One bench supply carries a single small ceramic across the output.[1174] Another uses 22 µF, with some surface-mount ceramics on the reverse side.[1402] Against these, a supply found with 1000 µF on the output is very high for the class, though whether that value was forced by a stability requirement cannot be determined without analysing the loop.[511] Excessive output capacitance is visible from the front panel: the output terminals continue to read voltage for a long time after switch-off as the capacitors discharge, particularly with the load disconnected.[1265][1030] The converse also shows — a supply whose voltage setting steps essentially instantaneously has little output capacitance doing anything.[1691] Slow, curved edges when the voltage setting is swept down are the expected signature of the output capacitance.[1606]

## Secondary effects

Output capacitance shapes several behaviours that are not obviously about the capacitor. In a current-limit circuit that works by pulling the regulator's adjust pin low, the output voltage cannot drop instantly because of the output capacitance, so the loop servos around an average value rather than snapping to it.[221] Measurements of a supply under repair start out low and rise as output capacitance somewhere in the circuit charges.[1036] In an energy-harvesting regulator the value must be split between rails: a small minimum-value capacitor on the rail that must come up quickly, and a much larger one on the rail expected to supply pulse currents, since anything oversized has to be charged before the output is usable.[664][945]

Output capacitance also appears as a parameter well outside power conversion. Logic-gate propagation delay depends on it, and data sheets that quote transition times without specifying the load capacitance leave the figure incomplete.[sr1DOHnJi8I] MOSFET data sheets list output capacitance alongside input and reverse transfer capacitance among the dynamic characteristics — figures that matter for switching converters and are largely irrelevant when the part is used for logic or simple power switching.[1461] The presence of an inductor, a diode, a controlling element and output capacitance together is enough to identify an unknown circuit block as a switching converter during reverse engineering.[837][1741]

Sizing claims on commodity supplies are worth checking against the rating on the box: a unit claiming 384 W of continuous output power with a token amount of output capacitance is unlikely to deliver anything close to its stated figure.[893]
