# datasheet

A datasheet is the manufacturer's own specification document for a component, and it is the authoritative source for every parameter a designer is entitled to rely on.[1180] It is where the pinout, the guaranteed limits, the test conditions, the characteristic curves, the recommended external components and the orderable part numbers live, and a design that has not been checked against it is not a finished design.[1285][1307] Almost every stage of practical electronics work — part selection, calculation, PCB layout, purchasing, troubleshooting and reverse engineering — runs through the datasheet at some point.[139][36][710]

## Structure of the document

A typical semiconductor datasheet opens with headline features and an application circuit, then moves through a block diagram, absolute maximum ratings, recommended operating conditions, electrical characteristics tables, characteristic curves, timing diagrams, package drawings and ordering information.[sr1DOHnJi8I] Even a device as simple as a 74HC00 quad NAND gate requires a page-by-page walk to cover every parameter; a microcontroller or FPGA datasheet expands the same structure by orders of magnitude.[sr1DOHnJi8I] On large parts, the detail a designer needs — which internal register selects a multiplexer input, for example — is often buried hundreds of pages in.[900]

Not everything in the document carries equal weight. The block diagram is illustrative, not specified: the LM3914 block diagram shows a ladder of ten resistors each marked 1K, implying 10 kΩ, while the electrical characteristics elsewhere in the same datasheet give the divider resistance from pin 6 to pin 4 as a typical 12 kΩ.[204] The electrical characteristics table is the only part that matters for design values.[204]

## Absolute maximum ratings versus electrical characteristics

The absolute maximum ratings and the electrical characteristics answer different questions, and a parameter appears in one or the other depending on which kind of statement it is. Emitter-base breakdown voltage, VEBO, is a maximum overload figure rather than an operating characteristic, so it is found in the absolute maximum ratings of most transistor datasheets and not in the specification tables.[1157] Few general-purpose transistors specify a high value; the 2SC3326 is one of the rare parts that carries a specific 25 V VEBO as a headline parameter.[1157]

Absolute maximums are also easy to mistake for operating specifications. An LED array datasheet quoting 182 W input power is quoting an absolute maximum, not a design point.[773] A MOSFET's banner current rating — 17.6 A on one part — is given at ambient temperature, while the device inside a real product runs hotter and cannot deliver it.[1461] Zener surge ratings behave the same way: a nominally 1 W diode with a 900 mA surge rating at 5.1 V clamping implies about 4.5 W, but the footnote restricts that to 10 ms, with derating curves supplied for other conditions.[908]

## Banner specifications and the tables behind them

Top-level specifications are marketing surfaces and are chosen to flatter the part.[528] Op-amp noise figures are selected for the best possible banner number, so comparison between devices has to be done from the graphs and the individual data rather than the front page.[528] A gain bandwidth product printed as 6.5 MHz turns out, in the table, to be measured into a 10 kΩ and 100 pF load at 100 kHz, with the gain at which it was measured left unstated.[1325] Deep sleep and watchdog currents cannot be taken at face value either, because the achievable figure depends on which wake sources the application actually needs.[1242]

Parametric tables are frequently split by operating condition. One op-amp datasheet gives separate tables for different supply voltages, so a design at ±5 V must read the ±5 V table.[572] Regulator parameters can vary with the programmed output voltage, and reading the 3.3 V table for a 15 V output is a route to wrong numbers.[1285] A single datasheet often covers a whole family of devices, whose graphs, parameters and specifications differ from one another despite sharing the document.[139] Some closely related parts share a datasheet entirely: the LP2902 and the LM324 are near-identical and are often documented together, differing only in details such as an extra internal current source.[95]

Temperature is a common hidden variable. A microcontroller quoted at a low microamps-per-megahertz figure will not hold it at 50 °C or 125 °C, and current consumption can rise sharply with temperature; where the datasheet does not characterise this, the only recourse is to build the circuit and measure it.[1539]

## Notes, footnotes and conditions

A large amount of the real specification is in the notes, and they have to be read.[500] Line and load regulation figures for voltage references commonly exclude the effect of self-heating, which is stated only in a note; excluding it makes the headline specification look better, with the justification that the manufacturer cannot know the thermal environment.[500] A resistance standard specified at 2.5 ppm/°C on the 10 kΩ range carries an asterisk restricting that figure to operation outside ±5 °C of the calibration temperature.[834] Common-mode rejection ratio can be quoted input-referred or output-referred, a distinction worth several tens of dB in the printed number; a good datasheet states which.[1521]

Unit notation deserves the same care. Per mil and parts-per-hundred notations differ from parts per million by nothing more visible than the number of zeros, and a missed zero turns an ordinary specification into an apparently terrible one or vice versa.[1300]

## Graphs and curves

Curves carry information that no table can. The first graph in the 7660 charge-pump datasheet shows the ripple consequence of the device changing its mode of operation, which matters directly for data converter and audio amplifier supplies.[1115] Startup voltage versus load current determines whether a boost converter will start at all on a partly discharged cell.[139] Reverse leakage curves let a measured few microamps on a Schottky diode at 20 V and 25 °C be confirmed as normal, against nanoamps for a PN junction — three orders of magnitude apart.[1747] MOV leakage against applied voltage, which determines what a multimeter's nominal 10 MΩ input impedance becomes at 1000 V, is obtainable entirely from the characteristic curves.[rT0g1QmKE5E] An averaging-responding multimeter's error against waveform shape comes from a table in the converter datasheet: zero error for an undistorted sine wave and +11% for a symmetrical square wave.[1448]

Curves are also where a full parametric picture is often missing. Zener dynamic resistance frequently has to be worked from a single test current, IZT, because a complete parametric graph is not supplied.[908] Battery datasheets are a recurring offender: some give voltage against time rather than the discharge-current family that is actually needed,[1732] and some omit characteristic curves entirely and fail even to state the current at which a claimed 8,000 mAh capacity was measured.[9FCzAgJhRdc] Where curves exist, they can substitute for measurement — a 100 mA discharge over 86,400 seconds can be planned against a cell datasheet to land at a 1 V end voltage.[RFb3TwWzza0]

## Thermal data

Junction temperature is the quantity that matters and the one that cannot be measured directly, so it is calculated from datasheet thermal resistances.[158] A power MOSFET datasheet listing 3.13 °C/W junction-to-case and 62.5 °C/W junction-to-ambient allows a measured 87 °C case temperature at 10 W dissipation to be converted to a junction temperature: 10 W multiplied by roughly 3.1 °C/W gives a 31 °C rise, putting the junction at about 118 °C.[105] Set against a maximum junction temperature of around 125 °C for a typical LM317, that calculation is what establishes whether a part is being operated within its ratings.[512] Datasheets may also condition their thermal ratings on airflow: a switching regulator specified for a given airflow over its heatsink will reach 161 °C in still air.[895] Package thermal tabs are electrically live — on many transistors the reflow-soldered tab is the collector, a fact stated in the datasheet and easily fatal to a layout that assumes otherwise.[744]

## Application circuits, layout and internal behaviour

Manufacturer application circuits are widely used as-is. The TDA2086A datasheet's typical universal motor application matches a commercial product's circuit exactly, and the same document supplies a detailed theory of operation.[435] Designers work from these circuits directly, sometimes with a considered variation — one mains switching product follows the first-page application circuit almost identically except for moving the solenoid into series with the bridge rectifier.[1164] The consequence for repair is that an unknown board built around an off-the-shelf amplifier chip will usually turn out to follow the datasheet's application note closely.[1072]

Layout guidance is a further reason to read datasheets. Reference layouts show thermal vias and the thermal pad arrangement needed to get heat out, where the decoupling capacitors must be placed, and why each choice matters — material that is otherwise hard to learn.[326] Internal circuit diagrams sometimes appear too: a crystal oscillator datasheet showing the internal inverter between the X1 and X2 pins explains a topology common to nearly every chip with those pins.[1614]

Some internal behaviour is systematically absent. Input ESD protection structures exist on every modern CMOS part and are usually mentioned in text, but very few datasheets draw the diodes; the NXP, ON Semi, Diodes Incorporated and TI versions of the same 74HC00 all omit them.[831] The resulting phenomenon, where a chip is powered parasitically through its input pins, is not explained anywhere in a 58-page datasheet.[831]

## Timing diagrams

Timing diagram conventions are not standardised. The same signal state may be drawn with long dashes, short dashes, small letters, doubled lines or nothing at all, and two datasheets for the same chip can use different notations.[1249] Conventions propagate by copying, with newer manufacturers adopting the style of established ones, producing de facto rather than actual standards.[1249] Break symbols indicating omitted time or omitted bits are a related convention: a diagram will show bit zero, bit one and then bit seven, implying that the intervening bits behave identically.[1249] Read carefully, timing diagrams are directly checkable against measurement — a captured 24-bit access with an 8-bit command field and 16 bits of data can be matched against the write cycle drawn in the datasheet.[879]

## Ordering information and part marking

Datasheets for configurable parts normally carry an ordering table giving the exact orderable part number so that a specific configuration cannot be ordered wrongly; where one is missing, as on some segment LCD controllers, the configuration must be specified and confirmed with the supplier directly.[1074] Suffix letters frequently encode grade: an A suffix on an op-amp may denote a better offset voltage than the basic grade.[1697] Those suffixes are not always explained — one LMV321 variant's S, B or T suffix has no stated meaning in the datasheet, while parts with different suffixes differ by roughly half an order of magnitude in offset voltage, up to 190 mV.[1057]

Markings on the package are not the same thing as the part number. The second code on many SMD parts is an internal manufacturing production code identifying line, facility, date and process, and it will never appear in the datasheet; searching for it is wasted effort.[1708] Part numbers can also carry more digits than the datasheet covers, leaving a four-digit device documented only by a three-digit datasheet whose pinout nevertheless matches.[1522]

## Revisions and errors

Datasheets are revised, and revisions can change specifications. A jellybean audio op-amp first released in November 1979 was still being revised in December 2025; comparing revision K against the January 2015 revision J shows a real specification change following a process node change, including the removal of a 26 V typical peak-to-peak output swing into 600 Ω.[1752] Major manufacturers list the changes between revisions inside the document itself.[1752] Fuse characteristics behave the same way: an old datasheet and the current one for the same unchanged HV620 series part differ significantly in their graphs, including an added decade.[1677] The practical consequence is that a critical parameter must be taken from the manufacturer's latest datasheet rather than from a distributor's parametric search, where errors are common.[1180]

Datasheets are not infallible. One boost converter datasheet contains a graph with an axis running from zero to one percent efficiency and duplicate curves with contradictory labels — an outright error that would produce a non-working design if read at face value.[139] The defence is expectation: knowing what a curve should look like, comparing it against the same graph elsewhere in the document and against other manufacturers' datasheets.[139] Errors also run the other way — one oscilloscope datasheet understated its own display intensity grading as 16 levels when the instrument has 256.[1639] Development kit documentation can simply be wrong, requiring jumper modifications to make a supported chip work at all.[63]

## What datasheets omit

Some effects are real, important and largely undocumented. The capacitance loss of class-2 ceramic capacitors with applied DC voltage — a 16 V Y5V part measured at 1.5 µF where its nominal value is far higher — is rarely mentioned in datasheets, and for a given manufacturer can be next to impossible to find.[626] Solderless breadboard datasheets do not give an inter-contact capacitance; figures circulating outside the datasheets range from 2 to 25 pF per contact strip.[568] LED datasheets quoting efficacy in lumens per watt — 132 lm/W for one high-efficiency emitter — say nothing about efficiency, and therefore nothing about how much waste heat the device produces; that has to be derived from a theoretical response curve.[50] Waveform update rate is absent from some oscilloscope datasheets and must be measured, while a competing model's specification sheet quotes up to 280,000 waveforms per second outright.[1478]

Manufacturer claims are also checkable and sometimes wrong. An LED strip specified at 32.6 W per linear metre draws 27.25 W over a precisely one-metre, twelve-segment length at its rated 24 V, and remains short of specification even after accounting for about 0.4 W of cable loss.[5mueaTsus8o]

Register-level omissions are common in complex parts. Undocumented registers accessed by a manufacturer's own software may exist outside the datasheet and app notes entirely, obtainable only by asking the manufacturer for supplementary spreadsheets.[1262] A microcontroller advertised at top level as supporting USB power delivery may have no USB transceiver in it at all, with the correct datasheet simply noting that data is to come.[1262] Fault reporting can be half-documented: a power amplifier chip's readable fault registers are described while the polarity and meaning of the fault interrupt output are not.[1322] Where a datasheet is silent on a test pin, the safe default is to leave it open unless explicitly instructed otherwise.[1541]

## Availability and access

Not every part has a public datasheet. Touchscreen controller ICs, security processors, DSP chips and GPS front ends are routinely held behind a non-disclosure agreement or supplied only on contact with the manufacturer.[384][1048][1150][1229] Some parts have no datasheet at all, which is itself a reason to select a different device.[1461] A power management chip with no public datasheet leaves failure analysis to inference, and even the full document would offer only a guess at which internal circuit is latching up.[716] Some documents named as datasheets turn out to be brochures with no technical content.[1242] Even where the document exists, it may not describe an accessory that ships with the part — an infrared module datasheet showing a clip-on micro shutter in a photograph while the text mentions only support for an external shutter.[670]

Language is a lesser obstacle. Where only a Chinese datasheet is distributed, the manufacturer's own website often carries an English version.[1132] Part-Chinese, part-English documents are workable,[761] and a block diagram plus a pin list is frequently enough to identify a five-pin constant-current LED driver and confirm its external component values.[1617] A Chinese-only datasheet that omits the required output capacitance and ESR range simply leaves that judgment to the designer.[1147]

Requiring registration before releasing a datasheet is treated by Dave Jones as grounds to design out the manufacturer's parts entirely and use a competitor who publishes freely.[47][100]

Historically, access was harder. Before manufacturers published on the web, magazines devoted substantial page counts to datasheets because they were otherwise difficult to obtain,[142] and manufacturers mailed datasheets, application notes and articles on request.[416] Old parts remain surprisingly well served: datasheets for devices such as a 1970s black-and-white TV IF amplifier have been scanned and remain findable.[830]

## Use in design

Part selection typically begins with a parametric search, which produces a shortlist of part numbers; the datasheets for all of them are then opened and compared against the governing requirement, such as input offset voltage, alongside constraints like temperature range and package.[72][87] The winner is provisional until the rest of the document has been read.[139] Comparing several candidates is ordinary design engineering work and consumes a large share of a design engineer's time.[139]

The datasheet also drives calculation. Component values that look arbitrary on a schematic often come from datasheet formulas — a 1.1 kΩ resistor setting a 160 V high-voltage supply output cannot be substituted with 1 kΩ because a non-trivial formula in the datasheet fixes it.[952] Compensation network values for a switching regulator come from equations in the datasheet, and re-deriving them is how a bill of materials is consolidated onto fewer distinct values.[1307] Verifying a circuit by inspection — reading the schematic and the datasheet, calculating where currents flow — gives a guaranteed result for simple designs, subject to effects such as regulator instability that inspection will not catch.[1285]

Stability and external component requirements come from the same place. A DC-DC converter datasheet may specify a minimum capacitance for a given load along with a minimum, maximum or window for ESR, and those requirements have to be met by a real capacitor with its own resistance and lead inductance.[742] A regulator datasheet may call for a 2.2 µF ceramic output capacitor as a stability minimum.[224] Some circuits misbehave without the specified output capacitors, making that section of the datasheet worth close reading before substituting a part.[1475]

Datasheet numbers feed directly into measured results. A precision current source reading 1.0027 A instead of 1 A resolves to a nominal 2 mA drawn by the VoutS sense pin, a figure stated in the datasheet; removing that error brings the output to 1.0006 A.[577] A DAC output that will not match its expected value is explained by the datasheet's statement that the output is always referenced to 4096 counts regardless of whether the device is 8, 10 or 12 bit, together with a typical gain error of −0.1% of full scale, an offset error up to 20 mV, and a maximum of 1%.[240] A boost converter rated 350 mA at 3.3 V out from a 2.4 V input, and requiring 3.3 V in for 5 V out at the same current, behaves accordingly on the bench.[320]

Interface levels come from the tables rather than from nominal supply rails: a device specifying a high input level of 4.5 V and giving no figure for 5 V operation leaves a 3.3 V driver on the margin.[1365] The same applies to instrumentation — the maximum crest factor a true-RMS converter can handle is in its datasheet,[1417] a ×1 oscilloscope probe may be limited to 10 or 20 MHz,[594] and a bench multimeter's high-impedance mode may be specified as greater than about 2 GΩ.[584]

Operating a part outside its published limits is possible but is an engineering decision rather than an oversight. Manufacturers are sometimes conservative, and a converter specified for a ±2.4 V minimum supply may work fine below it; doing so in production requires either full characterisation or clearance from the manufacturer.[972] In the opposite direction, a comparator whose common-mode input range extends to 1.25 V below the positive rail may work in a circuit where the datasheet says it should not, which is not a basis for shipping it.[471] Certain applications remove the discretion entirely: datasheets commonly state that parts are not suitable for implantable or life-support devices, and use in those applications requires specific manufacturer permission.[1027]

Beyond the electrical content, datasheets carry mechanical and process data. Enclosure datasheets can include downloadable interactive 3D models that import into a PCB CAD package to check board and front-panel fit.[258] Magnetics datasheets sometimes lack the dimensional data needed to model a part at all.[xTZpGM2P9u4] Solder paste datasheets specify the reflow thermal profile to be programmed into an oven, ramping to peak temperature at around 180 seconds.[415]

Datasheet content flows into design documentation. Extracting the relevant tables and diagrams by copy and paste into a design document is standard practice, so that the comparison of flash and SRAM sizes across candidate microcontrollers sits alongside the rest of the design analysis rather than having to be searched for again.[1561] The same applies to design reports, where block diagrams and internal descriptions taken from datasheets communicate the design far better than redrawing them.[174] Purchasing depends on it too: the designer, who has read the datasheets and tested the parts, owns the bill of materials, and the purchasing officer buys that exact part number from a reputable supplier.[1697]

When schematic symbols are drawn, the datasheet pinout should deliberately not be copied. Following a physical pin order that places half the drains on one side and half on the other produces an unreadable schematic; symbols should be arranged so that signals flow, with outputs grouped and features such as open-drain outputs conveyed by the symbol.[DNlA4X5_S30][952]

## Use in repair and reverse engineering

For an unidentified board, obtaining the datasheet is the step that makes progress possible; without a pinout, the alternative is many hours of guesswork.[710] With one, the analysis becomes bounded — a fault reduces to a small number of candidate causes.[710] Reverse engineering starts by identifying each chip and pulling its datasheet, which in turn leads to application notes and further parts.[36]

Concrete repairs turn on specific datasheet facts. A backup-battery SRAM measured at 7 V is confirmed as faulty because the datasheet specifies 5 V.[1527] A processor held in reset is known to have its oscillator running because the datasheet says the oscillator starts in that mode, which makes a current measurement while forcing the reset pin high a valid test.[1245] The reset signal on a PC motherboard is traced to pin 11, the power-good output of the 8284 clock generator, using its datasheet.[1348] An LCD's V0 pin is known to need a nominal −7.5 V rather than the 2.3 V measured.[1202]

Datasheets are a reference, not a description of the board in front of you. On a Keithley 177, the datasheet shows pin 7 connected through to +15 V while the instrument's own schematic grounds it — a discrepancy resolved by trusting the measurement and the instrument schematic.[777] Analysis can equally reveal that a datasheet does not fully apply: until an OTP microcontroller is programmed, its documented pin functions are not guaranteed, since fuse settings can change them.[1144] Reading detailed sections rather than the summary is what reveals constraints such as a reset pin that must be freed or an ISP interface shared with an SPI bus.[238]

Multimeter and instrument front-end chipsets are an unusually rewarding case: some datasheets document the complete input switching topology that makes a modern multimeter work, in enough depth to serve as a tutorial on the architecture.[832][894]

## Datasheets as a learning path

Taking any product apart, identifying each chip and reading its datasheet is a route to learning what building blocks exist and what they do, with each datasheet leading on to application notes and further devices.[36] Reading datasheets is a standard part of the engineering that no amount of modularity removes, alongside PCB layout, bill of materials, design for test and design for manufacture.[zyuRcsM0gjI] Where behaviour is subtle — the exact decay and threshold behaviour of an automatic gain control, for instance — the chip manufacturer's datasheet is the direct source rather than the product manual.[271]
