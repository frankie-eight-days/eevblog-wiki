# arbitrary waveform generator

An arbitrary waveform generator (AWG, or "arb gen") is a signal source that plays back a user-defined sequence of samples through a digital-to-analog converter, rather than synthesising only the fixed shapes a conventional function generator can produce.[1560][475] The distinction is a hard one: a plain DDS generator cannot do arbitrary waveform capability at all, because the shape is baked into the synthesis engine rather than held in memory.[475] Because the waveform is just data, an arb gen can reproduce a captured signal, a mathematically defined curve, a physiological waveform, or a static DC level, which makes it one of the more broadly useful boxes on a bench.[209][972][1560]

## How the signal is produced

Modern arbitrary waveform generators do not generate the signal in the analog domain the way older instruments did; they clock samples out of memory into a DAC, typically a 16-bit part, with even inexpensive units using 14- or 16-bit converters.[1560] The depth of that sample memory sets how much waveform detail is available: an isolated oscilloscope design used a 4K arbitrary buffer, which is not large but still supports a useful range of shapes, and it was implemented not in the FPGA but in an Analog Devices part with an arbitrary waveform generator built into it, giving a 0 to 65 MHz range at very low distortion.[1119] A scope-internal arb gen of the same era offered 10 bits of vertical resolution, 100 megasamples per second and 8K of sample memory.[209]

Physically the output chain is simple and repetitive across designs: a DAC, an LC reconstruction filter under a shield can, a set of relays for range and coupling switching, and a 50 Ω buffer driver into the BNC.[875][1563][1004] One 14-bit scope used two Burr-Brown DACs driven by a dedicated Spartan-6 FPGA, with a substantial bill of materials spent on relays and analog conditioning for the arb output alone — no fewer than eight relays serving the arb gen and pass/fail outputs.[1004] Elsewhere the whole function is folded into a custom ASIC, as with the ADG 395C that drives a 50 MHz arbitrary waveform generator and most likely the pattern generator output alongside it.[1477][qE-SwtYQM6E]

## Standalone instruments

Dedicated arb gens span an enormous range. Mid-range bench units include an 80 MHz, 500 megasample per second function/arbitrary waveform generator,[497] a two-channel 160 MHz, 500 megasample per second model with two separate outputs plus two sync outputs and a hardware frequency counter,[323] and a 120 MHz, 1.2 gigasample per second touchscreen model.[797] At the top of the mainstream range sits a four-channel, 500 MHz, 2.5 gigasample per second generator with a 10-inch touchscreen.[fM4bbKVw2og][1625] Beyond that are laboratory instruments running at 24 gigasamples per second and weighing around 20 kilograms.[358]

A related class combines several sources in one chassis. The Agilent 81160A is a pulse function arbitrary noise generator — a pulse generator, function generator, arbitrary waveform generator and noise generator in one two-channel, 500 MHz instrument with gold BNC outputs — costing around $20,000, with an optional 660 Mbit pattern generator adding roughly another six thousand.[309][308] Modular USB instruments follow the same idea in a stackable die-cast form factor, pairing an AWG with a scope and a 26.5 GHz VNA in matching enclosures.[u22duJf4n1Q]

## Arb gens built into oscilloscopes

Integrating a waveform generator into a scope is a well-established practice, but the integrated version is almost always a reduced one. Most scope-internal generators are limited to about 20 or 25 MHz of bandwidth, which becomes a real constraint when the internal frequency response analyser is driven from the internal source — the sweep can only go as high as the generator.[LbqnHtNPt9Y] A 100 MHz built-in generator is unusual enough to be worth noting.[LbqnHtNPt9Y] Some designs improve on the norm: 50 MHz built in rather than 25 and integrated rather than supplied as an external box,[1309] or dual 100 MHz arb gens on a dedicated board identifiable by its relays.[1717]

Even where the shape memory is present, the surrounding feature set is often thin. A 50 MHz integrated generator with sine, square, ramp, pulse, noise, DC and arb — with an audible relay click on switching to DC — had no modulation capability at all, which is a significant omission given that modulation is one of the main reasons to reach for a generator during scope work.[1309][1220] Another integrated generator offered AM and FM modulation but no FSK.[1638] A firmware-added arb capability that could store a captured waveform and replay it repetitively, automatically inheriting the captured amplitude and setting a 200 Hz repetition rate, still lacked single-shot output and was not a full-featured generator.[209] The engineering conclusion is consistent: an internal arb gen is a genuine convenience and worth having, but a serious high-performance arb still means a dedicated instrument.[209]

Where the arb gen is absent it is usually a deliberate cost decision. Scopes have shipped with unpopulated cutouts for the two extra BNCs an arb output would need,[1503] with the hardware fitted but the function locked behind a software licence,[1717][CMoBGGqojqs] or with the capability physically absent as one of the features dropped to reach a $699 price point.[1510] At least one 500 MHz scope offered no arb gen even as an option, making it a deliberately barebones bandwidth-for-price product.[792] Others sell the function as an external doohickey that plugs into the scope over USB and integrates with its software — a $268 option for 25 MHz and 125 megasamples per second.[1220][1309]

## Waveform libraries and editing

Beyond sine, square, ramp, pulse, DC and noise, the standard built-in set typically includes sinc, exponential rise and a cardiac or ECG waveform, plus a range of built-in math functions such as exponentials, window functions and trigonometric shapes.[CMoBGGqojqs][972][1638][1309] Waveforms can generally be uploaded over USB or LAN, or loaded from a text or CSV file.[1309][876] More capable software front ends allow the waveform to be defined by an equation edited directly on the graph, or assembled from multiple segments.[1701] At the small end of the scale, an LCR tweezer's signal generator accepted an arbitrary waveform of up to 100 points edited as a text file on a PC.[1335]

Providing an arb gen with a file loader but no built-in waveform library is a design failure, particularly in an educational lab environment where a built-in set is exactly what users expect from an arbitrary function generator.[876]

## Using an arb gen as a precision DC source

Because the output is a DAC, an arbitrary waveform generator can serve as a fine-resolution DC voltage source when no calibrator or spare supply is available, and most units have a DC option that goes unnoticed.[1560] With a 14- or 16-bit converter, even a no-name unit bought for around $100 can produce precisely stepped small voltages.[1560] The one caveat is the 50 Ω series output resistance, which must be accounted for if the output feeds an external voltage divider, or the resulting voltages will be wrong.[1560]

## Cost structure

The gap between the parts in an AWG and its retail price is large but explicable. A bill-of-materials analysis of a $5,800 four-channel generator put the all-in cost to design, develop, test and assemble each unit at well over five or six hundred dollars, into $800 and possibly $1,000 — not $80, and not $2,000.[1679] Non-recurring engineering dominates the difference: assuming around ten employees for a year or two, at least $500,000 in labour and company overhead before management costs, and plausibly a million or several million.[1679] On top of that sit packaging and accessories, dealer margins that are certainly not five percent, and headroom for the manufacturer's own profit and for later discounting, all of which is captured by a roughly 2.5× cost multiplier.[1679] The same arithmetic explains why an arb option on a mainstream scope carried an $1,100 list price when a standalone dual-channel 20 MHz generator with arbitrary capability could be had for well under a thousand dollars — the option is priced against the platform, not the parts, and at one point amounted to five dollars' worth of components sold for a thousand.[383][1218][149]
