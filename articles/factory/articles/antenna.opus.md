# antenna

An antenna is the structure that couples electrical energy between a circuit and free space, and in a radio system it is the component that decides what the rest of the design has to cope with. It is fundamentally simple — in its most basic form "Just a long wire"[LzAUa_FtuC0] — and it is indifferent to what is riding on the signal: "An antenna is an antenna, it'll pick up any signal that is broadcast"[LzAUa_FtuC0]. Because every decibel captured at the antenna is a decibel of gain that does not have to be added later, with its accompanying noise, in television, two-way radio and communications generally "the antenna is everything"[684].

An antenna only radiates from a changing field: "you can't just put DC on an antenna and have it transmit something"[1417]. The signal has to oscillate, and if it oscillates as a sine wave it represents one pure frequency[1417]. Radiated power falls off with the square of distance, so for an antenna with a 360-degree radiation pattern such as Wi-Fi, the power crossing a square centimetre a metre away is a small fraction of what left the transmitter[4vHx-UyIM9M].

## Size, wavelength and matching

Physical length is tied to wavelength. A UHF walkie-talkie operating at 477 MHz has a wavelength of roughly 0.6 m, so its spiral-wound internal element is a quarter-wave structure rather than a full-length whip[1275]. An FM bug carrying a proper half-wavelength element, sited high with clear line of sight and radiating somewhere between 10 and 30 milliwatts, was received 27 kilometres away on a car radio[280]. Conversely, an early crystal set required a long strung wire before it would produce audible reception at all[1654].

A missing or disconnected antenna is not a small degradation. Removing the antenna connection from a 121.5/243 MHz emergency beacon leaves the transmitter running but radiating almost nothing — inefficient by orders of magnitude, enough that the transmission goes nowhere[368]. The same effect appears as a fault: an FM transmitter with an unsoldered antenna wire produced clear audio at almost no signal strength, and reconnecting the single wire restored full strength[280].

Antenna behaviour also changes with its surroundings. A beacon's characteristics shift depending on whether the element is vertical or laid flat against a grounded bar, and the resulting change in the AC-coupled signal at the feed point is detectable by the on-board circuitry[368]. Reflected power measured at the feed is a direct indicator of this: a bare wire used as an antenna will change its measured characteristics simply from being touched or moved[1101]. Enclosures matter as much as position — a lunar rover built entirely of aluminium needs its Bluetooth antenna mounted outside the body to get any reception[885], and a lock electronics module with the antenna integrated into the chip inside a steel safe works only at around half a metre of range, a limitation accepted rather than designed around with an external element[7xF1RCVw4Fc].

## Diversity reception

Multipath is the dominant impairment for mobile receivers. Signals arriving by different paths can land 180 degrees out of phase and cancel, producing a dead spot where reception drops out entirely[1416]. The spots are physically small: a location that drops out may be perfectly receivable 30 centimetres away[1416].

Diversity solves this by separating antennas in space so that they genuinely receive different signals, giving the receiver a choice of which to use[1416]. In a dual-diversity receiver, two antennas feed an RF switch that selects between them[1416]. True diversity goes further, duplicating the entire receive chain — two sets of filtering, two amplifiers, two mixers — so both signals are demodulated simultaneously in real time and the better one can be selected practically instantaneously[1416]. The technique extends beyond two antennas[1416]. Where a diversity receiver appears to have only one visible antenna, the second is generally hidden elsewhere in the product; in one bodypack design the second diversity feed is taken from a pad associated with the audio jack[571].

Feeding DC up the antenna line through an inductor, rather than taking RF out, indicates power being supplied to a remote RF amplifier at the antenna itself[1416].

## Unintentional antennas

Any conductor of sufficient extent behaves as an antenna, and much practical engineering consists of preventing that. A solderless breadboard is unsuitable for high-frequency and switching work precisely because its loose wires form large loops that act as antennas[568]. The same rule governs bypassing: the larger the current loop area and the higher the frequency, the more strongly it radiates as an antenna, producing EMI and jeopardising CE and FCC compliance — hence the standing instruction to minimise loop area[1085].

Test equipment suffers from this directly. An oscilloscope probe with its earth lead attached is itself an antenna, picking up high-frequency impulses that vanish when the lead is disconnected[1107]. Extended metalwork inside an instrument acts as a rather effective antenna, so probe pickup at the front of a CRT is enough to display the noise present[524]. Probing an RF design under these conditions produces waveforms that look like design faults but are artefacts of the pickup, which is why probing technique dominates signal-integrity work in RF[368].

Circuit inputs are equally exposed. A CMOS 4000-series input has essentially infinite input impedance, so a long unterminated switch line running alongside mains wiring readily picks up 50 Hz[801]. Long input leads on a product function as antennas and are a standard reason to fit RF beads at the input alongside ESD and surge protection[1365]. In EMC work, ferrite beads are applied along cables for the same reason, since a cable running to a metal chassis is itself an antenna[202]. Internal transformers and their AC coupling can act as effective low-frequency pickup antennas, which is why equipment that is switched off and disconnected from the mains can still show common-mode noise[441]. Poor RF system design compounds the problem: a transmitter using a tiny coil as an antenna alongside a receiver with no shielding at all raises the broadband noise floor rather than producing discrete interference[767].

The effect is sometimes deliberately exploited. Non-contact voltage detection in multimeters and socket testers uses a plain conductive strip along the top edge of the PCB as the antenna for picking up the electric field[1598][1704]. Regenerative receivers have been built using the tank coil itself as the antenna[767].

## Antennas in products

Integration approaches vary widely. Antennas appear as PCB traces and patch structures, as separate daughterboards feeding an RF system-on-chip[1453], as modules with the antenna printed on the board and castellated edge connections for surface mounting[722], and as inductors on a key fob PCB[cc_GqtBuP4c]. Dedicated stand-off antennas — physically separated elements rather than a patch or PCB antenna — represent a materially better implementation where cost allows[1514]. Metalwork can be pressed into service: a tablet with three antennas built into the back of the case connects to them via leaf spring contacts landing directly on the main PCB, one of them an NFC near-field antenna[321]. Antennas have also been integrated into the moulded plastic of a device's outer cap[1210], and formed as folded sheet-metal stubbies with the ground on one side and the two antenna feeds on the outer contacts[1313]. In a slim tablet-style enclosure the antennas may sit in dedicated plastic port sections with a matching element on the opposite side, feeding an internal wireless card[1211].

Older equipment shows the same functions built at larger scale — a coil concealed inside a plastic moulding on a 1990s handset[492], or an external whip stowed within the battery pack of a 1986 analogue phone[888]. An aircraft transponder rated at roughly 140 watts peak transmit power both receives and transmits on a single antenna[1352]. Satellite handsets use proper coax board interconnects to their antenna connectors rather than wires soldered to the board[721].

## Measurement and installations

Radiated emissions testing is performed in an RF anechoic chamber with the equipment on a turntable and the measuring antenna at a distance of one to four metres, against defined upper limits for the relevant FCC requirements; a different antenna type is used for measurements above 1 GHz[1516]. On a spectrum analyser, a simple whip antenna is enough to reveal harmonics radiated by digital equipment against a baseline noise floor around −105 dBm[645], and with a demodulator the same instrument can recover an FM signal off an attached antenna[891]. RF bug sweepers are wideband amplifiers with a detector diode covering a few hundred kilohertz to 2 GHz, working only in close proximity to the antenna, which is why a room is swept physically rather than swept in frequency[735][956].

At the transmitting end, an analogue television facility splits its output through a power divider into two feeders running approximately 180 m up the tower to what are effectively two antennas, an upper and a lower half; if one half is damaged by lightning or otherwise, the other can be switched into full operation at slightly reduced power[569]. The kilowatt-level RF from the exciter and power amplifier chain feeding the antenna is the genuinely dangerous part of such a plant[574]. Deep-space ground antennas are large enough that the structure rides on a film of oil at three pads, each roughly the height of a person, with the main spars carrying the load down from the elevation axis[1547]. Spacecraft antennas are manoeuvred as part of routine calibration — a magnetometer roll spins up the gyros and rotates the entire structure, a variation observable on the downlink from 15 billion kilometres away[1547].

Placement has consequences beyond reception. A roof-mounted log-periodic television antenna casting a shadow across a domestic solar array was enough to cost of the order of 20 percent of the array's output, sufficient reason to relocate the antenna entirely[1426][NoIjTK249D0].

## Wireless power claims

Antennas feature heavily in wireless-power marketing that does not survive a power budget. Claims of charging a phone at up to 9 metres via a transmitter plugged into a wall socket and a receiving antenna attached to the handset are inconsistent with the inverse-square falloff of radiated power[WiMe9peQbTQ][4vHx-UyIM9M]. Energy-harvesting modules genuinely exist and will extract power from an antenna across roughly 60 Hz to 6 GHz, performing best between about 100 MHz and 2.5 GHz — but a system pumping one watt into patch antennas and harvesting it into capacitors is a demonstration of harvesting, not of useful wireless charging[1408][MCyLO-1grEk]. The concept is old, having been attempted via air ionisation in the 1890s, and the modern restatement using antennas and RF sources does not change the underlying budget[MCyLO-1grEk].

Antenna-themed claims also recur in consumer scams: a device advertised as turning existing house wiring into a superior antenna trades on the fact that an antenna is only a long wire, while asserting best-in-class capture area and VSWR without measurement[LzAUa_FtuC0]. Claims that an entire metallic object constitutes a complete antenna emitting a 2.3 Hz signal, with the field amplified through motion, likewise have no supporting mechanism[uI62sAN5JzU].
