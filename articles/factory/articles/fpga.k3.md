# fpga

An FPGA (field-programmable gate array) is a programmable logic chip that comes as close as possible to designing a custom chip from scratch, allowing virtually any digital function to be implemented in a single universal device.[496] The defining difference from almost every other chip on the market is that an FPGA has no intended function when purchased: apply power and it does nothing at all until a configuration is loaded into it.[496]

## Architecture and operation

An FPGA contains no processor unless the designer puts one in; it is a "sea of logic gates" — configurable logic blocks joined by programmable routing, surrounded by sophisticated I/O blocks.[496] Structurally, the chip is a series of tiles of programmable logic elements connected together with wires and switches, so that programming the switches and gates turns the chip into any desired digital circuit.[8OaZ89TN0fo] The I/O blocks support multiple logic-voltage standards, slew-rate control, and embedded flip-flops and latches for tasks such as driving DDR memory.[496]

Most FPGAs are volatile devices: the configuration is held in transistor or latch "fuses" that vanish when power is removed, so an external configuration flash memory is required to reload the design at every power-up.[496] This loading takes time — sometimes seconds — so FPGAs are not instant-on devices, and power-up draws a significant surge current while the configuration fuses are programmed.[496] Some families, such as the non-volatile Lattice parts used in the Agilent 34461A multimeter, power up instantly without an external E²PROM.[485]

Modern devices integrate far more than the basic logic-block-and-routing fabric. Almost all decent FPGAs include distributed memory or dedicated RAM blocks that can implement dual-port memory; a typical contemporary part may carry 50 or 100 DSP cores scattered through the fabric.[496] Clock infrastructure includes PLLs and digital clock managers that multiply the external reference — a 20 MHz crystal can drive internal logic at a couple of hundred megahertz — along with phase shifting and complex clocking arrangements, at the cost of significant system-level clock-timing complexity.[496] Dedicated global clock pins offer faster routing paths than general I/O, and clock signals such as an SPI clock should be routed onto them; the number of global clock pins is what allows designs with multiple system clocks.[193][496]

Manufacturers offer soft processor cores — vendor-specific 8-, 16-, or 32-bit cores such as NIOS that can be placed anywhere in the fabric — because so many users were implementing processors anyway.[496] Parts with hard processors, such as the Xilinx Zynq with its dual ARM Cortex-A9 cores alongside Artix FPGA fabric, are more optimized and lower power than soft cores, need no recompilation of the processor with the design, and are guaranteed to work — something never true of user-designed FPGA logic.[496][2] High-end parts also integrate hard Ethernet transceivers and SerDes serial controllers around the soft fabric.[496][1018]

## FPGAs versus microcontrollers

The compelling advantage of an FPGA is massive parallelism. Where a processor must service pins sequentially, an FPGA with hundreds of I/O pins can drive them all at once, and dedicated logic can process an incoming signal in effectively real time and forward it out another pin — something impossible for a processor working on, say, a 100 MHz input.[496] Consequently, duplicating the function of an existing dedicated chip in an FPGA is a waste of money: an FPGA large enough to hold an AVR core costs more than simply buying the AVR.[496] FPGAs also cannot match dedicated silicon on power consumption, because the flexibility of millions of logic elements, routing paths, and configuration cells is not optimized for low power.[496]

## FPGAs versus CPLDs and gate arrays

CPLDs are less flexible than FPGAs — fitting even an 8-bit microprocessor core into a CPLD is impractical — but they are faster, lower power, and offer more deterministic internal timing, which is why a design may pair a large FPGA with a small CPLD for timing-critical functions.[111][37] FPGA routing is segmented and connected through switch matrices, which increases delay between logic elements compared with more regular structures.[1215] Historically, one-time-programmable CMOS gate arrays filled a similar role as a rudimentary form of FPGA for semi-custom logic before FPGAs with configurable logic blocks took over.[496][833][969]

## Disadvantages and design challenges

- **Cost.** Small FPGAs exist at a dollar or two but contain few logic blocks; high-end parts cost thousands of dollars each, and parts costing around $10,000 apiece are bought in quantity for applications such as radio-telescope beamforming.[496][818]
- **Power.** FPGAs are relatively high-power devices with a startup surge, making them unsuitable for ultra-low-power design.[496]
- **Packaging.** Logic density and pin count go hand in hand; high-density parts come only in large BGAs, and a million-logic-element device in a friendly 44-pin TQFP does not exist.[496][4] Even a small 0.4 mm-pitch BGA FPGA can force an entire board down to 4-thou track/space design rules and tiny drill sizes.[193]
- **Complexity.** A single device may ship with dozens of datasheets and application notes covering the logic blocks, I/O, clocking, serial interfaces, DSP cores, and secure boot programming; the design tools are multi-gigabyte downloads, and fitting and timing-optimizing a large design can take days.[496] Complex modern FPGAs can have 50-page documents devoted purely to power-up sequencing and rail bypassing.[1512]
- **Timing fragility.** Every recompilation re-routes the design, so all internal timing changes with even a small modification; marginal, board-to-board timing failures are a recognized failure mode.[58]
- **HDL difficulty.** The two major hardware description languages, VHDL and Verilog, are not intuitive to engineers trained in sequential languages like C or BASIC, because everything in an FPGA executes in parallel and clock domains must be managed explicitly; poor HDL practice produces race conditions and related faults.[496]
- **Maintenance.** Migrating an old FPGA design is painful: legacy devices were often schematic-based rather than written in HDL, modern tools do not accept those schematics, and FPGA documentation is rarely good enough to hand a design to a new engineer without the original designer's involvement.[1032]

## Power supply, decoupling, and layout

FPGAs demand multiple supply rails: a design may need a 1.0–1.2 V core rail plus several I/O voltages, with larger devices requiring three, four, or even half a dozen different rails such as 5 V, 3.3 V, 2.5 V for PLLs, 1.8 V, 1.5 V, and 1.2 V.[193][1679] Rails sometimes must power up in a defined sequence to avoid problems such as SCR latch-up inside the chip.[1691] Rail voltages below 1 V are common on modern parts — 0.75 V cores appear in current designs.[1124]

Power integrity is critical enough that vendors publish dedicated application notes, some around 100 pages, on powering and bypassing their FPGAs, because the devices gulp current at boot.[1477][1307] Switching at hundreds of megahertz with edge components reaching into the gigahertz range means bypass-capacitor impedance rise at high frequency is a real concern.[859] DC performance matters as much as AC decoupling: excessive ground-path resistance across even 20 cm of board can drop enough voltage to upset an FPGA, and with modern 0.8–0.9 V core rails the total margin may be only 30–125 mV, while a board carrying half a dozen large FPGAs can draw tens of amps to triple-figure currents.[1216] Because current draw depends entirely on what logic is loaded, how fast it runs, and how the I/O is configured, consumption is estimated with vendor power-estimator tools rather than read from a datasheet.[496][1216]

## Use in test equipment and products

FPGAs are the standard acquisition engine in modern oscilloscopes: ADCs stream raw samples into the FPGA, which performs downsampling, triggering, and protocol decoding in hardware, faster than any application-layer software could.[PcxEO3fA_Ls][674][1309] Some variation of the Xilinx Zynq appears in nearly every oscilloscope, combining hard ARM cores running Linux with fabric doing the capture work.[1545][1530][1042] Cost reduction in cheaper models is often achieved by substituting a smaller FPGA for a premium part such as the Artix-7.[1563][1510] Where an FPGA's fabric cannot sustain the required clock rate — FPGAs resist running past about 250 MHz — waveform pipelines are split into parallel paths and recombined.[1032]

In multimeters, FPGAs handle sampling and data formatting: the Agilent 34461A replaced the custom ASICs of the 34401A with a Lattice FPGA implementing the multi-slope IV conversion, a Keysight design uses an FPGA for high-speed delta-sigma ADC processing and FIR filtering, and the Siglent SDM3055 uses a small Lattice MachXO to buffer and forward its 150 readings per second.[485][1032][829][1190] Arbitrary waveform generators pair a large FPGA with fast DDR3 memory to feed multi-gigasample DACs.[1679] Logic analyzers use FPGAs for sample compression, SerDes-based inputs, and real-time protocol decode and triggering.[290][1018][207]

Classic applications include video generation — a small Spartan-3 with an SRAM framebuffer and a triple DAC forms a complete VGA display, and one FPGA can host multiple VGA cores driving multiple displays — and video processing such as gain, gamma, and color balancing in production switchers.[145][494][WDfzwCXK0mI] FPGAs appear in lane-keeping and obstacle-avoidance cameras, ultrasound machines, and beamforming radio astronomy, all workloads that demand parallel processing impossible to do sequentially.[818][1314] Even products with a capable main processor sometimes add a small FPGA for glue logic or display assistance, at a cost of a few dollars.[761]

## Development tools and learning

Vendor toolchains are notoriously complex, and simply getting a blinking LED running from scratch is difficult; loading the vendor's example projects and working backwards from them is the standard way past the initial grief.[636][111] Sub-$100 starter boards such as the Terasic DE0 Nano (Cyclone IV, roughly $80) provide an entry point.[636][494] Synthesis tools that once cost $10,000–$12,000 are now free for many devices, and fully open-source toolchains exist for some families — the IceStorm toolchain made the Lattice iCE40 programmable with open-source software, enabling boards such as the IcoBoard Raspberry Pi hat.[818][8OaZ89TN0fo] On debugging, large FPGAs with hundreds of I/O pins produce correspondingly long JTAG boundary-scan serial streams, since every pin's data must be shifted through the chain; FPGA configuration headers are recognizable by their pin count and their proximity and trace routing to the FPGA.[499][p-eLu1z7-cs]

As engineering judgment, the choice between the two dominant vendors matters little in practice — Xilinx and Altera offer similar competing technology, and what can be done with one can be done with the other.[89] FPGA knowledge splits naturally between hardware and software disciplines: it is entirely possible to design FPGA boards professionally while barely being able to write VHDL, leaving the HDL to software specialists.[83]
