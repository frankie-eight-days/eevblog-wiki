# trimmer potentiometer

A trimmer potentiometer, or trim pot, is a small adjustable resistor mounted on a circuit board and set with a screwdriver rather than a knob. It exists to absorb the tolerance of the parts around it — to pull a voltage reference, a gain, a bias level or an oscillator frequency onto its nominal value once, at manufacture or service, and then be left alone.[372][235][714][1413] It is the physical embodiment of analogue calibration, and its steady disappearance from new designs in favour of digital adjustment is one of the clearest markers separating older instruments from modern ones.[853][1378]

## What trimmers adjust

The most common job is setting a reference. A Fluke 27 uses a trim pot alongside its 1.23 V reference to set the value that feeds the ADC.[372] A Krohn-Hite DC voltage standard labels its first adjustment as pot number one on the board itself; it sets 6.5 mA through a zener, and the resulting measured drop is the source of the instrument's stability.[210] A rubidium frequency standard, for all its 0.1 ppm tolerance and 0.01 ppm aging, still carries an adjustment trimmer, because the physics package must be trimmed to the correct frequency before its stability means anything.[235]

Gain and offset are the next most frequent targets. Two resistor trim pots on an oscilloscope current probe set the gain of each channel, one per sensor, because the spread between individual Hall-effect sensors is too wide to be handled by fixed resistors.[1413] A 741-derived op-amp such as the OP07 has offset pins that accept a trimmer, taking the part to better specifications than its untrimmed figure.[1436] A 500 Ω trim pot installed as the bias resistor at an op-amp input allows the input bias current to be nulled out entirely, although the null holds only at the particular supply voltage and common-mode range at which it was set.[479]

Bias, sensitivity and frequency compensation account for most of the rest. A metal detector uses a 10-turn trim pot to set the DC bias level shared by two transistor bases, with a separate front-panel pot for sensitivity.[714] Logic analyser probes carry a trimmer for compensating the probe against the input.[1018] High-voltage differential probes use 10-turn pots for frequency compensation, with trimmer caps in the same role.[932] A differential probe design adds a dedicated common-mode rejection ratio trimmer, which compensates for mismatch between the upper resistor string and the lower divider resistor — mismatch that would otherwise degrade CMRR directly.[1415] Older scope probes and dividers used a trimmer on each resistor divider step, and each step could need separate high- and low-frequency compensation, which becomes ugly quickly.[6XpyOGw6RFM]

Character LCDs are a canonical application: a trim pot sets the contrast bias voltage, adjusted until the characters sit just below the point where they fade out.[313][1202] Modern controllers replace this with a register value written over the interface — a contrast register incremented and decremented by buttons rather than a shaft.[1699]

## Construction and grades

Single-turn carbon trimmers are the cheap end. A single 270-degree rotation covering the full resistance range gives very little mechanical resolution, and on a calibration adjustment the practical consequence is that the lightest pressure on the screwdriver moves the reading by a couple of digits either way.[1578] The wiper also finds natural resting places along the carbon track, so the element can effectively snap to a position and refuse a value in between.[1578] How much of this matters depends on how wide the designer made the adjustment window around the nominal value — a narrow window is what makes a coarse element usable.[1578]

Multi-turn trimmers, usually 10-turn and sometimes 20-turn, spread the same resistance range over many screwdriver revolutions and are the standard choice where resolution matters: ovenised oscillator adjustment,[1138][265] high-voltage supply primaries,[909] precision reference circuits around a REF02,[755] and current-shunt and 10 A range calibration.[866] Where both coarse and fine adjustment are needed, instruments provide both, as in a Philips timer counter with a 10-turn fine trimmer and a separate coarse adjustment elsewhere in the chassis.[265] A gap between coarse and fine granularity is felt immediately: a poor 10-turn adjustment gives a much finer range than a coarse one, and a proper multi-turn element is recognisable by feel alone.[459]

## Access, mounting and locking

Because a trimmer must be reachable after assembly, its position drives mechanical design. Good practice drills access holes through the board so pots obscured by an upper board can still be adjusted from the back with a screwdriver.[208] Instrument enclosures carry matching holes: LeCroy places front-end trimmer pots behind a row of holes in the bottom cover that are not ventilation, so the units are trimmed after the case is on;[790] Siglent cuts holes in the metalwork to reach the pots that tweak AC performance;[864] a Fluke 37's alloy case has the adjustment openings moulded in and aligned to the trimmers below.[1393] Where the holes are absent, the trimming must be done on the bench before final assembly, since the pots become unreachable afterwards.[628] Sealing a shielded compartment without provision for access can leave three trim pots inside that cannot be reached once the instrument is closed up, which undermines the point of having them.[1578]

Mechanical stress is a second design concern. Mounting trimmers away from the sensitive circuitry and running fine wires to them, with the pots rigidly coupled to the board, decouples the screwdriver force from the solder joints — pressing too hard on a pot soldered directly at the critical node is damaging.[977]

Once set, trimmers are commonly locked. Manufacturers apply lacquer, sealant or coloured compound over the element both to prevent vibration from moving it and to show that the unit has been calibrated;[940][648][268] some are sealed entirely so the setting is not user-serviceable.[481][769][1133] Adjustment itself requires care in the choice of tool: a metal screwdriver can upset the circuit it is adjusting, so plastic trimming tools are needed for trim pots and trimmer capacitors alike.[168]

## Manufacturing cost

Every trimmer on a board is a person and a step in the factory. A retro Canon still camera carries 40 trim pots on a single side of one board, each of which had to be adjusted individually.[937] An HD DVD player has three trimmers next to the triple laser diode assembly, set at the factory to align the optics.[1011] A Philips counter's expense is directly attributable to each unit having been hand assembled and hand tweaked, its coarse and fine trimmers set and the result soaked in and characterised.[265] A BWD MiniLab exposes a full bank of them, labelled for maximum frequency, minimum frequency, symmetry, gain, waveform, peak rounding adjust and sine wave only.[1724]

The same logic applies at the other extreme of volume. For a one-off piece of test equipment such as a PCB panel test jig, putting trim pots on the board and calibrating it by hand is entirely reasonable, because only one board will ever be built.[552]

## The move to digital calibration

The engineering judgment expressed consistently across the corpus is that external trim pots have no place in a modern instrument. Where a chipset lacks closed-case calibration — no EEPROM to store calibration settings and compensate — the design is forced back onto external trim pots, and that is poor form given long-term drift characteristics and what modern processes can do.[853] A meter of any quality should not need trim pots today, although decent instruments carrying them still exist.[75] Cheap designs betray themselves by the opposite: trimmer pots scattered everywhere, six or more visible in a low-cost meter, four adjustable from the bottom of the case, five on a single board.[712][91][99]

The transition is visible within single product lines. The Uni-T UT61E used a 10-turn trimmer marked for DC volts calibration, another pot for AC volts, a third adjustment pot, and trimmer capacitors for frequency and capacitance; the UT61E+ that replaced it has no adjustment pots at all, relying instead on a better reference and better divider resistors.[1378] Similarly, an LCR meter may retain a single trim pot as its only physical adjustment alongside a jumper that selects calibration mode,[115] or reduce to one tiny single-turn PCB-mount trimmer with everything else done in software.[229] A power analyser with one lone trim pot in the voltage path and none in the current path can be calibrated in software regardless, following a procedure in the manual.[589]

Even an eight-and-a-half digit DMM may carry a crusty single-turn trim pot on the board without that reflecting on its class, because the trimmer is not touching the parts that determine performance — the precision relays, precision resistors and hand-selected, hand-aged voltage reference elsewhere in the instrument.[613]

Calibration in the metrological sense is separate from trimming. Sending a multimeter away for calibration does not mean someone tweaks the trim pots inside; calibration is the science of measurement, and adjustment is a distinct activity that may or may not follow it.[420]

## Diagnostic value

The presence, absence or ambiguity of trimmers is itself information when working on unfamiliar hardware. Two shorted pins is a common trimmer footprint, so an unpopulated pad pattern with two pins tied together identifies a place where a trimmer was intended.[1202] Conversely, four trim pots inside a clamp meter whose manufacturer releases no schematics and no calibration procedures leave a repairer with no way to know what they do.[Yk_T-uCbg10] Adjustment ranges are frequently insufficient for a repair use they were never designed for: running a contrast trimmer to both extremes with no effect at all indicates the wrong topology rather than a faulty pot, and the fix in that case was to load down the V0 pin through a 5K trimmer instead of trimming the original series element.[1202]

Trimmers also fail and drift. A counter whose trim does nothing points to a fault in the trimmer inside the ovenised oscillator rather than in the oscillator itself.[1138] An unlacquered trimmer moved by hand upsets a calibration immediately and irrecoverably without the original procedure.[940] And trim pots that were not fitted at the factory are a reliable sign of prior modification.[1418]
