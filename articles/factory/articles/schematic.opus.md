# schematic

A schematic, or circuit diagram, is the symbolic representation of a circuit: components drawn as standardised symbols and their electrical connections drawn as nets, with no attempt to represent physical size, shape or position. A symbolic diagram and the working implementation of the same circuit bear essentially no resemblance to one another — the transistor symbol looks nothing like the small black package that embodies it on the board.[27aG9xhfk6s] The schematic is nevertheless the document from which the rest of an electronic design flows, and it is the single most valuable piece of documentation when a finished product has to be repaired.[1307][1520]

## Place in the design flow

The normal sequence is to draw the schematic first, run electrical rules checking on it, lay out the PCB, run design rules checking, generate the manufacturing files, and then have the board assembled.[1307] Bill of materials consolidation belongs in the same chain, before or around the rules-checking stage.[1307] Getting the schematic and all of the component footprints correct in the libraries is a prerequisite step: only once every symbol carries a footprint can the components be dumped onto the board.[244][974] If the footprints and nets are all correct and the electrical rules check passes, that component dump should in theory only have to be done once, although PCB design is inherently iterative and there is sometimes value in starting layout before the schematic is finished.[974]

Selecting parts, drawing the schematic, checking it, and placing the components is roughly ninety-five per cent of the work in a design; routing is the small remainder, which is why automatic routing saves far less time than it appears to.[93] An autorouter has no access to schematic information at all — it knows nothing about what the circuit does, only about connectivity and geometry — which is why it cannot make the judgements a human layout engineer makes.[1535]

Changes can flow the other way as well. Swapping two pins during layout to obtain an easier routing path is a legitimate edit that is then back-annotated into the schematic so the two stay in agreement.[245]

## Drawing a readable schematic

Readability is a design deliverable in its own right. Designators and values should be placed consistently — designator above, value below, for instance — across the entire sheet.[1129] Components should be physically lined up rather than scattered, and wasted white space closed up.[1129] A schematic in which designators are strewn about and wires run straight through the middle of chip symbols is unusable regardless of whether the underlying circuit works.[1127] Line weight matters too; excessively thick nets make an otherwise sound schematic hard to read.[1254]

Symbol design determines much of this. When drawing a component symbol, the pin order should not simply follow the datasheet pinout: a device whose drains are split half on one side and half on the other in the datasheet produces an awkward diagram if copied literally.[DNlA4X5_S30] Grouping all the outputs on the side facing the thing being driven, and putting power and ground where they will not obstruct the signal wires, produces a compact symbol that the surrounding wiring flows naturally into.[DNlA4X5_S30] Symbol detail carries information: drawing the open-collector or open-drain marker on the output pins conveys the electrical nature of those pins to anyone reading the sheet.[DNlA4X5_S30] Creating a custom symbol takes only a minute or two, so the absence of a part from the vendor library is not a reason to accept a poor one.[DNlA4X5_S30]

Structure at the sheet level matters as much as the details. A modular schematic, with functional blocks drawn out and descriptive boxes around them, lets a layout engineer see where each module goes and how the system fits together.[661][244] The failure mode is a schematic that is modular in name only and does not show the system flow, so the reader cannot follow a signal from input to output.[689] Circuits whose switch contacts are scattered across several parts of the sheet in different positions are particularly hard to follow, oscilloscopes being a classic example.[1243] Cramming a complete design onto a single A4 sheet compresses it at the cost of modularity.[232]

On multi-board designs, reference designators are conventionally allocated in blocks per board — the 200 series for one board, and so on — specifically so that designators are not duplicated across the whole design.[628]

## Notes on the schematic

A schematic is also the place to record design intent that layout cannot infer. Three electrolytics drawn in parallel say nothing about how they must be physically arranged, so a note on the schematic explaining the required layout configuration to the PCB layout person is standard good practice — and serves as a reminder to the designer, who may be the same person months later.[742] The same applies to star grounding: the diagram may show a star ground, and the layout engineer has to be told that it is deliberate.[1216][1110] A boxed design note with an arrow to the relevant node, explaining that a particular ground must return to a specific point because it is the reference, prevents someone unfamiliar with the topology from destroying it during layout.[1110] Without such notes, a problem such as forgotten power routing may only surface two weeks into a layout, when the fix is to rip the board up and start again.[1216]

Bypassing is a case where schematic convention and physical need diverge: specifying one bypass capacitor per supply pin on the schematic because it is the done thing leads to boards carrying far more capacitors than the circuit requires, to the point of leaving no room for silkscreen designators.[1512]

A theory of operation, formerly a routine companion to a published circuit, is largely absent from modern project releases that consist of a schematic, a PCB file and firmware dumped in a repository.[1254][921]

## Electrical rules checking

Electrical rules checking is the schematic-domain analogue of design rules checking on the PCB.[1307] It detects conditions such as two output pins shorted together, floating pins, and a power net driven by an output pin — errors capable of releasing the magic smoke on first power-up.[953][5eJorvyA708] With online checking enabled, offending nodes are flagged directly on the sheet with a red marker, and compiling the project produces the full error list.[953] Rules-check markers are conventionally annotations rather than editable schematic objects, and packages that make them real objects behave unusually.[253] Errors deliberately introduced to exercise the checker have gone undetected in at least one shipping release of a major package, so the check should not be trusted blindly.[5eJorvyA708]

Even a simple auxiliary board, such as active circuitry added to an otherwise wasted PCB panel, needs an associated schematic if it is to be rules-checked at all — which in a serious flow means individual board schematics plus a panel-level schematic.[552]

## When the schematic and the board disagree

Schematics are not automatically authoritative. A troubleshooting session can stall entirely because the pins shown as ground on the schematic — pins 19 and 20 — are LED standby and key input on the actual board, with 14 and 15 being the real grounds; the schematic simply did not match the PCB, despite carrying the correct model number.[XUyjRm1Upjs] In a similar case the discrepancy resolved as a symbol drawn upside down relative to the board, pin 1 corresponding to pin 33 and vice versa, because the schematic on hand was for a different regional version of the product.[1394] The general lesson is to follow anything that looks wrong rather than explaining it away: the initial symptom was simply a measured voltage that did not match expectation.[1394]

Internal inconsistency also occurs. One camera service schematic showed a backup supply rail entering an IC on one sheet while the sheet for that IC showed the connection going nowhere, a contradiction that cannot be resolved from the documentation alone.[1433] Schematics can also contradict a component datasheet — an instrument schematic showing a device pin grounded where the datasheet shows it tied to the positive rail — in which case the instrument schematic describes the hardware and the datasheet does not.[777] Revision errors are common enough in one's own work too, such as a current-sense amplifier output routed to the inverting rather than the non-inverting op-amp input on a revision B board.[240]

Silkscreen labelling is not a substitute for the schematic. Header pins labelled with their port names may still be shared with debug lines and connected to other circuitry on the board, so trusting the labels instead of reading the schematic produces signals that appear shorted to something else for no visible reason.[8]

## Schematics in repair

For fault-finding, a schematic converts an open-ended search into a bounded one. Without one, troubleshooting a complex analogue board becomes a matter of chasing one's tail indefinitely, particularly when the failure does not manifest as an obvious short drawing excess current.[540] Concrete time penalties are routine: three quarters of the effort in one multimeter repair would have been saved by having the schematic, and a significant analysis error in another would not have been made at all.[1520][1522] In another case the absence of a schematic would have led to a full reverse-engineering exercise on the board before the actual fault could be understood.[1672] Where no schematic exists, questions that should be trivial — whether a break-before-make contact arrangement implements a power-on reset, what the voltage supervisor functions in an intelligent power supply are, whether an off position physically disconnects the battery — simply cannot be answered.[1520][1527][1449]

Schematics are not always necessary. Faults that are visible on inspection, such as a failed component or a broken solder connection, are found and fixed without reference to any diagram.[884][1243] But it is at the point where measurement stops making sense — no voltage on a filter capacitor, a rail collapsing under load — that the schematic becomes the next step.[905][804]

Service documentation quality varies enormously. Some manufacturers publish complete service manuals with full schematics, including instrument makers whose manuals can be bought or downloaded outright.[620][358][523][808][667] Some vintage manuals include full schematics, calibration and servicing information decades after the instrument shipped.[406][613] Others provide extensive troubleshooting and block diagrams but omit the schematics entirely, leaving the service procedure to terminate at board-level replacement.[536][540] Documentation practice from earlier decades is markedly more thorough: fold-out schematic sheets, transparent colour component overlays registered to the circuit, internal block diagrams of the chips themselves, and full theory of operation.[863][ZBVyJaLAZDY][381] Consumer television sets once shipped with a circuit diagram behind the back cover so that an independent technician could troubleshoot the set at reasonable cost, and early personal computers came with full service information.[moa3YBdLepg][Y0SNOsQOBYk] Semiconductor design kits of the period likewise included schematics as a matter of course.[1308]

Related practice makes the schematic more useful at the bench: coding test points identically on the schematic and on the printed circuit board so that a point on the diagram can be located on the hardware immediately.[1364] Boards laid out without labelled test points make tracing a signal back through multiple ribbon cables far harder than it needs to be.[1672]

## Withheld schematics and reverse engineering

Some manufacturers decline to release schematics at all, including to their own dealers, which forecloses both repair and any analysis of the front-end design.[1667][1449][Yk_T-uCbg10][964] Instruments whose schematics were never released can be effectively unrepairable when the design is complex.[a4Xpsenpd6E] The extreme case adds physical obfuscation: part numbers ground off every device on the board so that neither a schematic nor a parts list can be reconstructed easily.[966][956] Announced open-source releases sometimes turn out to contain wiring harness drawings and modular block diagrams rather than board-level circuits, which is high-level system information rather than what is needed to understand or repair the electronics.[1581]

Where no schematic exists, it can be reconstructed. On a symmetrical design only one channel needs tracing, since the other is identical.[837] The immediate output of tracing is unreadable and has to be redrawn before it conveys anything — at which point features such as a screen grid tied to the plate, converting a pentode into a triode, become visible.[837] A practical starting method is a high-resolution photograph of the board, printed out, with traces marked off as they are followed; the process is not conceptually hard but is very time-consuming to get right.[1313] A partial, hand-drawn schematic representing what the circuit is believed to be doing, mapped onto the physical components, is a useful intermediate even before tracing begins, provided its provisional status is understood.[268]

## Release and openness

Publishing a PDF of the schematic together with Gerber files does not constitute open hardware: the original schematic and PCB source files must be released, along with the firmware, any PC software required to make the product work, the bill of materials and supporting documentation, so that the design can be modified and not merely reproduced.[195] Releasing a schematic on its own is nevertheless a real, if partial, step toward opening a design, and a licensing regime that lets a company open as much or as little as it chooses — provided it states which parts — is preferable to an all-or-nothing definition.[921] The open hardware gear logo carries no information about what has actually been released; a proposed remedy adds letters to it, S for schematic, P for PCB, F for firmware and M for mechanical CAD.[921]

A schematic released only in a proprietary CAD format forces every reader to install that package merely to view the circuit, which is why a PDF should always accompany the native files.[688][643] Providing a well laid out single-sheet PDF that can be printed and kept on the desk for reference is the practical standard.[1306] A schematic released without the design in electronic form, and without the software and mechanical files, can be close to useless in practice.[1wrin8FYtEo] Projects promoted as open source have in some cases had no schematic available on their own website at all.[858][2]

## Schematic entry as a design medium

Schematic capture has been used for logic design as well as circuit design. Older FPGA designs were entered schematically rather than in a hardware description language, and modern tools will not accept those schematics, which makes migrating such a design to a current part extremely painful — the documentation is never sufficient to hand the design to someone else, so the original engineer has to be involved.[1032]

## Learning to read schematics

Instructional electronics kits have long treated the schematic as the destination rather than the starting point. The early projects give a picture of the board and a wiring diagram showing which spring terminals to connect, and later projects progress to schematic diagrams, with explanatory text for each project.[54][riBwRC_CaAA][1135] The transition point is explicit: the first projects are built by rote from a pictorial layout, after which circuit diagrams are introduced and grow progressively more complex.[1135] Teaching material introduces the symbols individually — a nine-volt battery, an LED, a resistor with its value marked, in one common first circuit a 330 ohm resistor between the LED cathode and ground — and then how those symbols connect.[1163] Conventions such as the distinction between a junction, marked with a dot, and a crossing where two wires do not connect, are part of the same body of basic literacy.[27aG9xhfk6s]

Beginner kits are markedly more usable when a schematic is supplied alongside the assembly instructions and the component values are printed on the overlay, rather than only designators that force the builder to cross-reference a small schematic to find each value.[aQ2AVLs8_7k][1205]

## The hand-drawn era

Before CAD, schematics were assembled physically. Symbols were set as dry-transfer lettering and glued down individually, with over a hundred separate stick-downs on a single moderately complex circuit diagram, and the adhesive used eventually browned and destroyed the artwork.[280] Manuals of that period nevertheless contain beautifully executed hand-drawn circuit diagrams, including entire digital sections drawn by hand.[544] Hand-drawn schematics also survive as published artefacts in their own right, reproduced from the original notebooks in recreated project kits.[1034]
