# supercapacitor

A supercapacitor — also called a supercap, an ultracapacitor, or an electric double layer capacitor — is a capacitor whose capacitance is measured in whole farads rather than microfarads, achieved at the cost of a very low working voltage per cell, typically around 2.5 to 2.7 V.[1231][9V99J22aiLE][1483] The farad-scale capacitance makes it a charge *store* rather than a filter element: it is used where a circuit needs to keep running, keep counting, or shut down cleanly after its power source goes away, and where a battery would be the obvious alternative if it did not leak, wear out, or need replacing.[382][1231][757] Modern parts have made capacitance values that were once implausible into ordinary stock items — a 1 F part now fits in a cylinder about 6 mm in diameter and 9 or 10 mm tall, and surface-mount double-layer capacitors are available in tape and reel.[9V99J22aiLE][1242]

The distinction between one supercap and another is not cosmetic; construction, leakage, ESR, and derating behaviour vary enough between families that "A supercap is not a supercap."[1741]

## Voltage rating and series stacking

The low cell voltage is the defining constraint. Because nothing much runs below 2.7 V, a supercap bank intended to hold up a 3.3 V or 5 V rail has to be built from cells in series.[1511] Series operation raises a balancing problem: the cells do not share the applied voltage equally on their own, so balance resistors are placed across each cell to force the split.[WFVfbu1Xz3A] Omitting or losing one of those resistors leaves one cell overvoltaged, and a missing balance resistor has been found on a board whose series pair subsequently failed.[WFVfbu1Xz3A]

Series stacking is contentious as a design practice, and the alternative — a single cell followed by a boost or DC-DC converter — trades the balancing problem for a converter and its own set of requirements.[WFVfbu1Xz3A] Neither choice is wrong in principle; it is a trade-off, and the series approach is defensible provided the balance resistors are actually accounted for in the design.[WFVfbu1Xz3A]

Derating matters as much as balancing. Supercap life is strongly sensitive to both temperature and applied voltage, on the order of a halving of life for each 10 °C rise or each additional 0.1 V.[9V99J22aiLE] A part run at its nameplate voltage in a hot enclosure is being run at its limit in two dimensions at once.

## Memory and clock backup

The oldest and most common application is holding up volatile state across a power interruption. A supercap on a portable computer's main board keeps the contents of a 128K static RAM pack alive while the batteries are being swapped, which is only workable because SRAM — unlike dynamic RAM — draws almost nothing in retention.[382] The same role recurs across decades of equipment: a 5.5 V 0.047 F NEC part for RAM retention,[382] a 5.5 V 0.22 F NEC part standing in for the rechargeable or primary lithium cell that would otherwise be sitting in an LCR meter waiting to leak,[757] a 0.1 F part for backup in networking gear,[851] 5.5 V 0.47 F parts in salvaged industrial boards,[9VVTGE7ABIk][745] a 0.02 F part on a camcorder's DC input board,[375] and 4.7 F at 2.7 V used outright as the real-time clock battery replacement in an oscilloscope, glued down and paired with a soldered-in 32 kHz watch crystal.[1231] Similar backup parts appear in a professional GPS receiver,[918][917] a music keyboard,[256] telephone systems,[214][363] a multimeter whose time and date are lost if the batteries are out too long,[1592] and a high-security electronic lock that has no internal battery at all.[1483][1484]

Avoiding a battery is a substantial part of the appeal: a supercap does not leak electrolyte onto a board over twenty years the way a backup cell will, and needs no service.[757] Where a design has both, the supercap is doing a different job from the battery — the battery holds the clock, while the supercap is sized to keep the processor alive long enough to shut down in an orderly way.[619]

## Graceful shutdown and ride-through

The last-gasp shutdown case is a distinct application from long-term retention. In a mains-powered solar monitor, the supercap exists only to detect the power failure and supply the brief interval needed to close out writes so the stored data is not corrupted, and to send one final cellular report that power has been lost.[WFVfbu1Xz3A] Emergency-call systems use the same idea, holding enough energy for the single outgoing call after the vehicle's power has gone.[9V99J22aiLE]

Ride-through of the opposite kind — supplying a large transient rather than surviving a loss — also suits the technology, because a supercap's ESR is low enough to source current a battery of the same size cannot. Some trucks buffer an inverter with a large supercap tied to the alternator so that an oversized load plugged into the outlet does not disturb everything else downstream.[9V99J22aiLE] Slot car racing tracks use pairs of two-farad capacitors alongside lead-acid batteries to cover the current demanded on acceleration.[1214] The energy involved is not trivial: transferred into a part with the very low ESR a supercap offers, the charge held in an AA-sized cell is enough to do real damage.[1043] A 5 F 2.7 V bank removed from a failed board retained a hazardous charge for a considerable time after the equipment was dead, and required deliberate discharging — with visible voltage recovery afterwards.[1511]

## Energy harvesting

Supercaps are the natural storage element for harvesting circuits, where the source delivers microwatts to milliwatts and the load runs in bursts. Thermoelectric harvesting ICs provide a dedicated storage pin onto which a farad-class capacitor can be connected, accumulating energy from the generator in parallel with running the output.[664] Piezoelectric harvesters work the same way, and can be fed from a solar panel through a series resistor into the element's internal bridge; no series blocking diode is needed to prevent back-discharge into the chip, because the chip simply shuts down when the input voltage is insufficient to charge the capacitor.[534] Indoor photovoltaic evaluation kits offer a supercap in place of a battery, with charge curves plotted against storage capacitance.[S-UqkM8iDKM]

That last curve is the central design tension of harvesting: more storage capacitance means more energy available to the application, but a proportionally longer wait before the circuit has enough voltage to start up and do anything at all.[S-UqkM8iDKM] The related failure of the concept is straightforward energy budgeting — a solar-powered calculator built around an internal high-capacity supercap was abandoned because the cells could not supply even the 100 to 200 microamps the LCD needed, and a calculator that has to be charged before use is not a calculator anyone wants.[97]

## Where supercaps are the wrong answer

Against a battery, the supercap loses badly on energy per unit volume, and this rules it out of a large class of low-power designs. In a memory-LCD product drawing about 2 microamps in standby — roughly as low as a practical design gets, with maybe a factor of two available through further engineering but not an order of magnitude — a 1 F part yields a run time fifty times shorter than a physically large alternative, and the physically large alternative is too big to use.[1242] The conclusion is blunt: supercaps aren't any good at all if you need small size and long discharge.[1242]

Where a supercap or a rechargeable cell is used as the retention element, it must be isolated from the rest of the product with a series diode so that powering down the main circuitry cannot drain the store backwards through it; diode leakage then becomes part of the budget, at tens of nanoamps for an ordinary small-signal part such as a 1N4148.[1242]

Supercaps are also awkward in manufacturing. They are among the temperature-sensitive components — along with batteries — that cannot go through wave soldering or reflow and must be hand-soldered at final assembly, sometimes with preheating.[207]

## Failure modes

Supercaps are wet-electrolyte parts and fail like one. A series pair in a mains-powered solar monitor vented, and the electrolyte ran down the board under gravity and wicked into a connector, tracking across the PCB.[1511] The leak took out a diode, desoldering it completely, and reflowed a ceramic-bodied resistor so violently that the resistor snapped and resoldered itself in the opposite orientation.[1511][WFVfbu1Xz3A] The board burned through, and the PCB tracks in the affected area went black and began to corrode; where electrolyte has soaked into the laminate the affected material has to be drilled out because it can go conductive.[1511] The equipment continued to draw everything the transformer's primary side could supply, overloading a primary-side resistor, because there was no fusing on the output.[1511]

Contributing factors in that case were a missing balance resistor across the series pair and an installation on the afternoon-sun side of a fuse box, where the enclosure would get hot.[WFVfbu1Xz3A][1511] The parts themselves were otherwise consistent with a good-quality build.[1511] A degraded supercap does not necessarily read as failed: both cells still charged when tested, though extremely slowly, using nothing more than an ohmmeter.[1511]

## In pseudoscientific products

Supercaps appear routinely as the concealed energy store in products claiming an impossible power source. Wireless-power ventures repeatedly turn out to be RF energy harvesting into a bank of supercaps or batteries, which then trickle-charges the phone — energy harvesting on the order of milliwatts, presented as power transmission, and constrained by transmitters rated at 3 W that would not suffice even at 100 % efficiency.[1408][1532] A "nuclear diamond" battery marketed as never needing recharging pairs a roughly 100 microwatt source with a small supercapacitor at the top of an AA-sized package; the supercap is what allows any meaningful burst of current to be drawn, and the underlying source remains a couple of hundred microwatts at best.[1333] The word graphene is generally attached.[1333][1595][1633]

The tell is consistent: where a claimed generator produces microwatts and the demonstration delivers watts, the storage element is doing the work, and its presence in the teardown or the marketing copy is the giveaway.[1408][1532][1333] The same reasoning identifies a supercap-sized part as the likely storage in an automotive "voltage stabiliser", though in that case the component was probably too small to even qualify as a proper supercap.[951]
