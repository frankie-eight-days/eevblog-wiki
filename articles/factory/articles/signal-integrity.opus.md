# signal integrity

Signal integrity is the degree to which a signal arrives at its destination with the shape it left with — whether the edges are clean or carry overshoot, undershoot, ringing and settling artefacts.[1664][306] It is a property of the interconnect and the probing, not of the logic: a bus can be functionally correct and still have terrible signal integrity, and it can have beautiful signal integrity right up to the point where the edge rate outruns the board.[1664][155] It is one of the core competencies separating someone who can place parts on a board from someone who can design a high-speed board, alongside EMI, design for manufacture, and knowledge of how boards are actually made, assembled and tested.[83][1512]

## When it matters

Signal integrity is a function of edge rate rather than clock frequency.[316] A design clocked at around a megahertz does not need to worry about it much,[334] and a multi-layer board running a 68000 under 10 MHz with ground planes and abundant bypassing presents essentially no signal integrity problem at all — such a board can be handed to an auto-router without concern.[523][410] The same latitude applies to any board where the interconnect is deliberately not high speed: a board-to-board connector carrying a non-critical parallel interface can be built with signal integrity be damned and no controlled impedance whatsoever.[1239]

The concern rose with the interconnect. Older systems spread logic across large boards and backplanes, using one big ground plane and heavy decoupling; those traces were not controlled impedance and did not need to be.[410] Modern high-speed serial lines, controlled-impedance routing and terminations changed that.[410] In-circuit emulator design became a particularly hard case, where flight time down the interconnecting cables alone can ruin a design, which is why the emulation processor is placed as physically close to the target connection pins as possible.[410] Similar reasoning drove point-to-point coax runs inside older instruments when a signal could not survive the trip down an edge connector, through the motherboard and back up another board.[1237] The requirement is not exclusive to digital work: an analog delay line carrying colour video is judged as a transmission line, with reflections and bounces mattering exactly as they would elsewhere.[386]

## Board-level practice

The return path is the first-order concern. A ground plane that is not flood-filled under a trace forces the return current to take a longer route, and the consequence is blunt — the rule is never split your ground planes.[1247] Losing the return path destroys the timing budget along with everything else: "You've ruined your timing budget. You've ruined your signal integrity. You've ruined everything."[1247] A circuit whose ground return is completed only through mains earth — out through the power board and back — will function for something as slow as a UART while its signal integrity is entirely compromised.[155]

Where high-speed signal integrity is required on a multi-layer board, the standard construction is microstrip: the controlled-impedance trace on the top layer with the ground plane immediately beneath.[1323] Differential pairs are length-matched, including small serpentine wiggles added to trim one leg of a pair against its companion.[360] Local removal of a plane is sometimes deliberate — scope input channels may have the bottom ground plane cleared beneath them for signal integrity reasons.[360] A single-sided board carries a real performance penalty by comparison.[747]

DDR memory layout illustrates the full set of effects at once. Intersymbol interference arises when the bus runs faster than the line can settle, so that residual energy from the previous symbol corrupts the next.[1247] Parallel buses routed side by side couple into each other, and crosstalk becomes serious in a way that simple crossing traces never are.[1247] Even the glass weave of the substrate matters: a trace running along a weave sees a different dielectric constant than one running across it, changing propagation delay.[1247] The voltage reference is a case where signal integrity is decisive — its trace should be as wide as possible to reduce inductance, and with a 0.5 V/ns edge rate, 50 mV of VREF noise translates into 200 ps of timing error.[1247] Dave Jones's own DDR designs decoupled and inductor-isolated VREF heavily for this reason, and kept it spaced away from adjacent signals to avoid crosstalk.[1247]

Packaging and connectors contribute. A surface-mount pad is preferred over a through-hole lead where signal integrity is paramount, because the lead adds inductance.[304] BGA-mounted modules help for the same reason.[342] Through-hole pins are fatal for a millimetre-wave antenna array, where they cannot work at 28 GHz and beyond.[1369] A zero-insertion-force BGA test socket inevitably introduces signal integrity issues that must be designed around.[532] Insufficient bypassing produces ripple on the supply rail with ramifications throughout — glitches on circuit transitions and ground bounce among them[1085] — and bypass capacitors placed in a tidy row for appearance rather than adjacency are poor layout.[936] A missing coupling capacitor on an auxiliary rail entering from a video connector is enough to warrant a bodge wire on production boards.[795] Placing large copper on the outer layers is thermally superior but trades against signal integrity.[1347] EDA suites address this class of problem through simulation, impedance control and crosstalk analysis tools.[968]

At the interface level, an input whose incoming clock quality is unknown — particularly one arriving from an external board — should at minimum be buffered by a Schmitt trigger.[1365] Capacitive loading of a driven line is a related failure: one module may be driven fine while daisy-chaining several introduces intermittent faults.[1365]

## Probing

Probing is where signal integrity is most often lost, and in RF work probing is decisive — an antenna-like ground lead picks up enough rubbish to make a healthy design look broken.[368] The ground lead is an inductor, and getting rid of the long clip lead is the first step above all others when measurements are meant to be taken seriously.[1081] The same applies to logic analyzers, where short ground wires are essential and the probe set is the most valuable part of the instrument.[44] A cheaply designed logic analyzer input is not a serious tool, because so much bears on it: noise, skew across channels, transition times, metastability, input capacitance and inductance, rise and fall times, and crosstalk.[44] A logic analyzer also reports only a one or a zero against a fixed threshold and takes no account of signal integrity at all, so a poorly built circuit can produce plausible but wrong results and send an investigation chasing red herrings.[MC9_4mpqVgU]

Active FET probes put the buffer amplifier at the tip, which is how they reach roughly one picofarad of input capacitance; the shortest possible leads between probe and circuit remain necessary.[309] They are the choice when signal integrity at high frequency genuinely matters.[1368] A home-made resistive probe — a 1 kΩ series resistor into coax — can match the signal integrity performance of a multi-thousand-dollar, even ten-thousand-dollar, 10 GHz probe if built correctly, though there is considerable art in getting it right and characterising it requires the same class of equipment.[1367] In one direct comparison the do-it-yourself resistive probe recovered from undershoot faster than a $2,500 active FET probe, at the cost of loading the circuit far more heavily.[1368][1718] Resistive probes are also more tolerant of longer ground leads than active FET probes.[Y7t6BIhBZhc] Hands-free probing fixtures inherit the same grounding limitations as a conventional probe, but the tradeoff is usually worth it for debugging.[GS0WqUKZ-3c][1362] Fixed ×1/×10 probes avoid the attenuation distortion that poor switch contacts and impedance mismatches in a switchable probe can introduce.[LeVZ5n3K2io]

The instrument itself is part of the measurement path. Enabling a second channel on a scope has been observed to visibly change the fidelity of the waveform on the first.[1220] Conversely, a higher-bandwidth scope is what reveals a termination or signal integrity problem that a lower-bandwidth instrument smooths away.[487] Averaging or high-resolution acquisition modes can clean up what the probe delivered, but proper grounding technique remains the better answer.[1081]

## Knowing when not to care

A consistent bench discipline is to distinguish measurements from presence checks. When the question is only whether a signal exists and is doing roughly the right thing, an awful ground path is acceptable — clipping to the chassis, relying on the mains earth connection shared between scope and equipment under test, or leaving deliberately long wires attached for handling convenience.[1664][1452][879] The overshoot seen through a hideously long ground lead is then simply ignored, because data presence rather than edge quality is what is being examined.[1322][1320][689] Short leads still buy confidence: when leads are already short, a suspect capture can reasonably be attributed to something other than signal integrity.[1462]

The corresponding hazard is diagnostic. Unpopulated termination resistors and a plausible signal integrity story can absorb an entire debugging session — timing diagrams, protocol analysis, careful probing setup — when the actual fault is a hardware failure on a clock pin.[689] Signal integrity is a genuine explanation often enough to be a seductive wrong one.[689]

## Measurement of signal integrity proper

The eye diagram is the standard instrument for characterising high-speed differential serial links: the data waveform overlaid repeatedly against a clock recovered from the signal itself, driving the horizontal axis.[340] A clean eye reflects correct controlled-impedance routing, correct terminations, correct shielding and low clock jitter, and the demonstration cases represent an ideal a real system may never reach.[340] Serious characterisation is slow work — setting up a single critical measurement to standard, over a specified cable length and into a specified load, can take days, and fully characterising a product against a standard such as USB 3.0 can take weeks.[340] Signal integrity remains the practical fix at that level too: a USB 3.0 link that would not work reliably was resolved by removing the series components on the USB signals and falling back to USB 2.[1496]
