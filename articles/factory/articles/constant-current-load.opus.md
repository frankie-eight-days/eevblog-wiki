# constant current load

A constant current load is a load that draws a fixed current from a source regardless of the source's terminal voltage, in contrast to a resistor, whose current falls as the voltage across it falls.[140][141] It is the default load type used to characterise batteries and to test power supplies, because holding current fixed removes one variable from the measurement and reduces capacity accounting to current multiplied by time.[140][141] In instrument form it is normally realised as a low-side current sink: a power MOSFET regulated by an op-amp against the voltage developed across a sense resistor.[1688][102]

## Circuit principle

The canonical implementation is an op-amp driving the gate of a MOSFET, with the source returned to ground through a sense resistor and the op-amp's inverting input tapped off that resistor.[1097][102] The op-amp forces the sense-resistor voltage to equal the control voltage on its non-inverting input, so with a 1 ohm sense resistor a 1 V input sets 1 A of load current, giving an adjustable constant current load referred directly to an input voltage.[102] The whole thing needs only a MOSFET, an op-amp, and some means of setting and reading that voltage.[1097] Because the current is set by feedback rather than by resistance, the load is an active element, not a resistor.[1147]

Although the term speaks of a load, the same topology is a constant current sink, and it can be thought of as a resistor from the perspective of the source under test.[1688] High-side constant current sources and low-side sinks are two arrangements of the same idea.[1688]

Scaling up is a matter of paralleling pass devices. A commercial source measure unit uses four FQB8N60C MOSFETs — 600 V, 12 A, 1.2 ohm on-resistance parts — with drains commoned to the tab and each device given its own current shunt, so that each dissipates a quarter of the total power.[607] Range switching is done by selecting among sense resistors, including a 1 ohm resistor and two separate 1.0 ohm resistors.[607]

Adding a microcontroller converts a fixed-function current sink into a general programmable load. A PWM output generating the control voltage, in place of the manual pot, plus an ADC reading the sense-resistor voltage, allows constant current, constant power, constant resistance, or arbitrary pulse profiles to be synthesised.[102] Without that intelligence the same hardware is limited to constant current alone.[1097] Off-the-shelf hardware is sometimes repurposed this way: a hobby battery charger with custom firmware can be turned into a modest constant current, constant power, constant resistance dummy load, limited mainly by how much heat its pass element and heatsinking can shed.[397]

## Why linear-regulated products present a constant current load

A product powered through a linear regulator is, seen from the battery, close to a pure constant current load.[1533][ILIO5b1BliE][772] The reasoning is a basic property of linear regulators: essentially no current flows in the adjust or set pin, so the input current equals the output current.[772][ILIO5b1BliE][141] The regulator holds a fixed voltage across a load whose behaviour is steady on average — even a complex load such as a processor running the same task — so a fixed voltage produces a fixed current, and that fixed current is pulled from the battery.[ILIO5b1BliE][1533] A multimeter drawing roughly 5 mA is a worked example: 5 mA at the load means 5 mA out of the battery.[ILIO5b1BliE][1533] Raising the supply voltage does not change it; the draw stays at 5 mA because the load is constant current.[1533] The regulator's quiescent current adds to this, but for a milliamp-region load a microamp-region IQ can be neglected.[1533]

Switching regulators behave completely differently, and any load that changes state differently again.[1533] A switch-mode front end draws roughly constant power, so its input current rises as the battery voltage falls.[141][772]

## Battery capacity and the load-type problem

Battery data sheets typically assume a constant current discharge, which is why capacity is so often quoted in amp hours or milliamp hours.[140] That specification is not strictly correct, because it ignores the voltage change over the discharge and assumes the load is constant current when not all loads are.[140] Under those assumptions the measurement captures an area under the discharge curve that includes some region it should not and excludes some it should, the two errors partially cancelling.[140] True capacity is the full area under the discharge curve and is properly expressed in watt hours.[140]

Three load types appear on battery data sheets: constant current, which is the most common; constant resistance, tested literally by strapping a 10 ohm or 100 ohm resistor across the cell; and constant power, which is the most representative of most designed products and underlies the watt-hour rating.[140] Few real circuits behave as constant resistance.[140] Data sheets may additionally give pulse-load curves to simulate something like a toy being switched on and off.[102]

The practical consequence is that a constant current capacity figure is accurate but narrow. Measuring an AA cell at 250 mA constant current for about 7.5 hours yields 1875 mAh, a number known accurately for a 250 mA constant current load and largely inapplicable to a product that does not draw 250 mA constant current.[141] The most accurate approach is to log the discharge curve using the real product, which may switch between modes and so present neither constant current nor constant power.[772] Where a data sheet lacks a curve at the current of interest — a 9 V alkaline characterised at other currents but not at 5 mA — the value must be interpolated or measured.[ILIO5b1BliE]

Measuring amp hours needs only a constant current load, a stopwatch, and a multimeter across the battery to catch the cutoff voltage; measuring watt hours is harder, requiring a data logger that captures both voltage and current over time.[141] Discharge curves are also better understood in terms of loaded terminal voltage: the curve a data sheet gives for a constant power, constant current, or constant resistance drain is the loaded voltage, quite distinct from the open-circuit voltage seen by pulling the cell out and metering it.[779]

## Bench practice

Electronic loads used for battery work generally provide a cutoff: a constant current is drawn until the terminal voltage reaches a set threshold, at which point the test stops automatically, protecting the cell from over-discharge.[789][393] A capacity-based stop is the complementary mechanism — set a stop capacity of 1.25 Ah at 100 mA and the load ends the run once half a nominal 2500 mAh alkaline has been removed.[1274][j_eaXfmRB8Q] Discharge data is typically plotted against capacity on the x-axis, though time is often the more useful axis and can be recovered by exporting the data; a lithium cell test giving 59 minutes 11 seconds of depletion from 4.2 V corresponded to almost exactly 5 Ah.[393]

Applying a constant current load also exposes internal resistance directly. Switching a half-amp load onto a fresh 1.58 V alkaline drops the terminal voltage immediately, the step being the drop across the cell's ESR; removing the load lets the voltage recover, but that recovery represents chemistry and ESR rather than recovered energy, and reapplying the load collapses it again.[772][774] A nominally flat cell sitting at 0.56 V can still deliver a continuous 10 mA under constant current, whereas a constant power setting on the same load will not start, because constant power requires the instrument to compute and compensate while constant current is handled more directly.[774] Not every load offers both: some battery test modes support constant current only, forcing constant power tests to be run outside the built-in mode.[774] Some instruments do support constant power in battery mode, which better represents modern DC-DC-fronted products.[774][IXVTMUQGN5U]

Cell chemistry and cell size limit how hard a constant current test can be pushed. A AAA is not designed for 1 A constant current, and a 1 A discharge of a good alkaline AAA returns only about 263 mAh or 0.268 Wh.[1023]

Anomalous readings during long discharges are worth chasing rather than accepting: a string reading 0 V at the pack terminals while the load still reports tens of milliamps flowing, or a load delivering 50 mA when set to 100 mA, indicates a connection or contact problem rather than a battery result.[XDjyY48u0PU] Voltage drop in the test leads matters too, and remote sensing may be required.[XDjyY48u0PU]

For long-term storage or leakage experiments, a single small constant current load — on the order of 10 microamps — placed across a whole series string is a practical substitute for soldering individual resistors to each cell.[RFb3TwWzza0] An earlier leakage attempt using a fixed 1K resistive load across cells produced no leakage in any brand tested, and no strong evidence existed as to which load condition best provokes leakage.[1653]

## Testing power supplies

For supply characterisation the constant current load is the standard instrument. Ripple and noise rise with load, so measurement is done at maximum rated output current or at the intended operating current — a 5 V rail tested into 2 A, for example.[594] Load-step and turn-on behaviour is captured by switching a set current onto the output and single-shot capturing the transient: a 10 V output into 3 A showed no overshoot at all;[1030] a 15 V output into 5 A produced a visible kink in the rising edge without overshooting;[315] a 2000 W supply started into a 19.9 A constant current load exhibited a step lasting around 150 ms.[1298] Electronic loads are dynamic and some supplies cannot start into them at all, which is why a plain resistive load is sometimes used for the startup comparison.[1298] Because a constant current load and a purely resistive load do not stress a regulator identically, comparing the two is a legitimate diagnostic step.[439]

The same load doubles as a current-reading cross-check: a 1 A constant current load read as 1.008 A on one instrument and 0.999 A on another indicates agreement within specification,[1691] whereas a 0.6 percent discrepancy on a 1 A setting was traced to the load being left on its 40 A range, and ranges on such loads cannot be changed with the load switched on.[1023]

A constant current load is also a fault-finding tool. Applying a modest 0.1 A load to a suspect bench supply channel establishes whether the pass element can source any current at all.[861][755] Applying 50 mA to a device battery and watching the terminal voltage collapse to 3.03 V identifies a cell that cannot sustain even that draw,[1370] as does a pack that goes completely dead at a 200 mA draw.[XpZVIWdXliY] Recovery from short circuit can be characterised by switching a regulator from a short back to a defined constant current — 250 mA, then 50 mA — and observing whether it re-establishes regulation.[1147]

Beyond supplies, the constant current load serves as a controlled sink wherever a defined current is needed: two of them in parallel across a rectifier output to develop a comparable ripple voltage on two different 100 microfarad capacitor arrangements,[742] a 1 A load at 5 V as a clean example of 5 W instantaneous power in an energy-versus-power demonstration,[1009] and a 1 A current sink built from the same dummy-load circuit when a precision current source was needed and a ground-referenced output was not.[577] It is also the natural bench test for a mains-side or alternator-side unit rated for a continuous 4 A output.[720]

Efficiency characterisation is the case that most rewards automation: with an LXI-capable supply and load on the network, a script can set the input voltage, read back input current and hence input power, command the load's constant current setpoint, and sweep it, gathering points at the load's current resolution — around 1 mA — across the full range.[1031][102] Simulation is a further step removed; a constant current load can be swept over temperature in SPICE to expose the tempco of the arrangement.[301]

## Practical judgement

The DIY constant current dummy load is regarded as an instrument every bench should have, and the circuit is trivial enough to be built from junk-box parts.[1097][141][102] Where more capable hardware is available, a precision electronic load with programmable setpoint and readback of output power and voltage is preferred over cobbled-together arrangements, particularly at higher currents such as 8 A where lead resistance becomes significant enough to need attention.[895]

On the specific question of how much energy a constant current cutoff leaves in an alkaline cell, the difference between constant current and constant power framing is small: with a cutoff around 1 to 1.1 V, roughly 10 to 20 percent of the battery's energy is left stranded as an absolute best case.[772] A claim of eight times the usable life depends on the product being poorly designed, with a loaded cutoff as high as 1.4 V.[772] A separate claim — that because a cell takes longer to fall from 1.2 V to 1.1 V than from 1.5 V to 1.4 V under a constant current draw, the recoverable life must be larger still — is true as an observation about the slope of the discharge curve but does not carry the conclusion.[751]
