# ringing

Ringing is the damped oscillation that appears on the edges of a fast signal when parasitic inductance in the current path resonates with the capacitance around it, typically riding on top of overshoot at a rising edge and undershoot at a falling edge.[859][1365][453] It is the visible symptom of a signal integrity problem rather than a problem in itself: the same parasitics that produce it also produce ground bounce, broadband radiated emissions, and false logic transitions.[1085][1081][1365] Because it lives entirely in the high-frequency content of an edge, ringing has nothing to do with the nominal frequency of the signal — a slow square wave with a fast slew rate rings just as readily as a fast one.[859][1081]

## Origin

Any current loop with inductance and capacitance in it has a resonance, and a step of current excites it. The inductance need not be a component: bond wires inside a package contribute inductance between the die and the pins, and that alone is enough to put ringing on a signal.[859] On a printed circuit board the contributors are trace inductance, loop inductance, and the position of the source and load relative to each other — the loop area of the path the transient current has to take.[1735] The rule that governs all of it is blunt: "Any piece of wire of any length is an inductor."[1081]

In switching converters the same mechanism produces high-frequency noise: parasitic inductances distributed through the layout ring when subjected to large rates of change of current during the charging and discharging of the storage element. Because these parasitics are of much lower inductance than the intentional ones, they ring and generate noise at correspondingly higher frequencies.[594]

Ringing on a power rail is fast. Probed at 10 ns per division, the transients on a rail can carry content in the hundreds of megahertz, and the frequency follows the slew rate of the driving circuit — even ordinary microcontroller switching gets there.[1735] Where the rail is probed matters as much as what is on it, since the observed ringing depends on the decoupling present at that point and on the inductance of the loop between the point of measurement and the source.[1735] Physical layout of the circuit under test can dominate: a switching board held down against a ground surface rings visibly more than the same board raised on a stand.[1557]

## Consequences

The most direct failure is in logic. A clock line with enough ringing on it re-enters the indeterminate input region — for a 74HC-family part, the band between 1.5 V and 3.15 V — and if the excursion dwells there, the receiving gate can register multiple clock pulses from a single edge, with metastability in downstream flip-flops as a further consequence.[1365] Logic datasheets state the requirement plainly: routing and load conditions must be considered to prevent ringing, which is a statement about PCB layout and loading rather than about the part.[sr1DOHnJi8I] The risk is worst where drive is strong and the load is light, since high drive creates fast edges into light loads.[sr1DOHnJi8I]

The second consequence is emissions. Ringing on a poorly bypassed rail appears in the frequency domain as broad, high-amplitude content across a wide band; adding bypass capacitors knocks it down markedly between roughly 200 MHz and 500 MHz, and removing them brings it straight back.[1085] A circuit radiating that much broadband energy is unlikely to pass CE or FCC emissions compliance.[1085]

The third is rail integrity. Ringing driven by insufficient bypassing on a 5 V supply can amount to a substantial fraction of a 100 mV per division trace, which is a large amount of ripple to place on a supply feeding digital logic, with knock-on glitches and ground bounce.[1085] On a board with several chips and inadequate bulk capacitance, the supply impedance resonates and the result is large ringing rather than a small droop.[1365]

## Mitigation

Bypassing is the primary control. A local bypass capacitor placed as close to the pins as the package allows cleans up an edge that otherwise rings and undershoots, and a supply that rings on a poorly bypassed oscillator output can be made to look like a commercially built part by adding one.[859][1089] The limit is the inductance that cannot be removed — the bond wires inside the package, and the leads and series inductance introduced when the capacitor is not mounted directly.[859][1089] Adding leads and series inductors between the capacitor and the pin complicates the picture and undoes part of the benefit.[1089]

Splitting bulk and high-frequency decoupling between two capacitors of different value is a long-standing practice justified by the overlaid impedance curves giving a broader, lower-impedance path across frequency, and controlling ESR is one of the ways ringing is deliberately damped.[9V99J22aiLE] Low-inductance capacitor constructions change the calculus, potentially allowing a bulk capacitor plus one low-inductance part in place of a conventional pair; three-terminal feedthrough capacitors shift it further still, since such a part can offer 10 µF at very low inductance.[9V99J22aiLE]

Termination is the other control. An unterminated line rings on every edge; a length of coax driven without proper termination shows clear overshoot and ringing that a matched termination removes.[453][381] Even with a nominal 50 Ω termination, small mismatches remain visible — around 55 Ω against 50 Ω is enough to put noticeable ringing on the input of a fast digitiser.[1709] Whether the termination is a separate 50 Ω through-terminator or an in-line one can make a measurable difference at high frequency.[6XpyOGw6RFM] For an existing supply already built, ferrite beads placed across switching components such as the MOSFET are one route to taking the edge off ringing already present.[1266]

Construction technique matters as much as component selection at the extremes. A pulse generator built for the lowest possible inductance and plugged directly into the oscilloscope input, with no coaxial cable at all, produces almost no ringing; the same class of pulse routed through a long coax picks up ringing attributable to the cable's inductance.[306] A coaxial line works as a transmission line, but only within limits.[306]

## Measurement artefact versus real ringing

A large share of observed ringing is created by the probe, not by the circuit. The ground lead supplied with a standard passive probe is a loop of wire and therefore an inductor, and it will put ringing on the displayed waveform that is not present in the circuit; removing it is the first step in any signal integrity measurement, ahead of everything else.[1081] The same lead is what defeats high-frequency probe compensation: after adjusting the low-frequency trim at the probe tip and the high-frequency trim at the connector end, ringing remains on the displayed step until the long earth lead is replaced with a short ground spring or hook.[842] Traces annotated as showing ringing "due to our antenna earth lead", or ringing attributed to a long ground lead on an active FET probe, are of this class.[813][1368][Y7t6BIhBZhc][1493]

The distinction is testable. When high-fidelity probing technique is used and the probe's own contribution is negligible, the ringing and undershoot on a clock edge are real events in the circuit and should be treated as such.[1081] Conversely, ringing seen with a bandwidth limiter engaged and casual probing on a retro machine's processor clock is not evidence about the clock.[788] Probe quality itself shows up the same way: a benchmark step at 5 ns per division through a good probe shows a little overshoot and a little ringing before settling, and a probe under test is judged against that reference.[707]

Oscilloscope front ends have their own pulse response. A 100 MHz instrument fed a roughly 700 ps pulse through an in-line 50 Ω termination can be distinctly ringy, with pronounced overshoot and undershoot, while another instrument on the same signal is clean.[487] Some scopes show an almost ringy response to a step from a signal generator where others show only mild overshoot, so the signal source must be accounted for before blaming the instrument.[6XpyOGw6RFM] Turning off a bandwidth limit is what reveals the overshoot, ringing, and transmission-line and termination effects that were being hidden.[6XpyOGw6RFM] Ringing severe enough to make a waveform untriggerable forces the trigger to be taken from a cleaner source elsewhere in the circuit.[1557]

## Ringing as a diagnostic

Because ringing frequency is set by the resonance of a real physical loop, it carries information. Cursor measurement from peak to peak on the higher-frequency content within a ringing burst yields a specific frequency — on one oscillator output, about 29.4 MHz — distinguishing deliberate high-frequency components in the waveform from the settling tail.[1089] Ringing on a rail captured in single shot is sinusoidal in character and, correlated on a mixed-signal instrument against the digital lines, can be tied back to the switching transient that excited it.[1733] Changes in ringing on the coils of a driven load track loading conditions, the loaded case showing ringing on both the rising and falling edges with flattened top and bottom compared with the unloaded case at just over 13 V peak to peak.[284]

Ringing also appears deliberately as a test stimulus: a ringing clock is one of the standard training and demonstration waveforms built into oscilloscopes alongside sine with glitch, RF burst, and burst with infrequent glitch, precisely because it is a realistic hard case for triggering.[143]

## Judgement

Some ringing is unavoidable and tolerable; the engineering question is how much and where. On a linear supply's control loop, a small amount of ring and undershoot on the op-amp output as the circuit hits a limit, with a clean overall switch-on, is acceptable, though a long decaying ring on the op-amp is not desirable even when the output itself is well behaved.[224] High-frequency content on a supply rail traced to probing rather than to the circuit still points at something worth eliminating.[225] On a fast pulse output, ringing following overshoot degrades an otherwise respectable 250 ps average rise time, and imperfect signal integrity of that kind — a dip before the edge and undershoot and ringing at the tail — is usually traceable to the interconnect, a coaxial cable or a socket-type connector, rather than to the source.[316][306]

At high voltage and fast switching the tolerance narrows sharply. Winding up the switching voltage on a wide-bandgap experiment makes the ringing on the switch node progressively and severely worse, to the point of being a major problem rather than a cosmetic one.[1557]
