# temperature sensor

A temperature sensor is the element that converts temperature into an electrical quantity a circuit can act on, and in most equipment it exists not to display a number but to close a control loop or trip a protection threshold. Its accuracy is dominated less by the sensing element than by where it is placed and how well it is thermally coupled to the thing whose temperature actually matters.[1394][853] The recurring engineering problem is thermal path, not transduction: a well-chosen sensor bolted to the wrong piece of metal, or separated from the heat source by plastic and air, reports a temperature that is real but irrelevant.[1172][1392]

## Sensing elements

The cheapest practical element is a negative temperature coefficient thermistor, used wherever a rough absolute reading with a strong slope is sufficient — heater controllers, air conditioner panels, EVSE chargers.[1481][1360][1507] A thermistor is also easy to triage in a repair: an in-circuit reading of the right order, such as 11 k, rules out an open or shorted element even without a curve.[1481]

A plain silicon PN junction works as a sensor with a temperature coefficient of roughly 10 mV per degree C, and this is what appears in fast battery chargers, where cell temperature feedback right at the contacts is not optional — without it a fast charger will destroy the cells.[811] The same crude junction sensor is integrated on-die in microcontrollers and processors to report junction temperature, and although the technique is unrefined it is adequate for thermal management.[642][mkZFHEeQkQI] Laptops and similar hardware carry several such sensors and use them to drive fan speed.[1345]

Thermocouples are used where the sensed point is remote, hot, or numerous. A precision instrument may distribute them very widely: one design places roughly two dozen thermal sensors across the whole chassis, with a sensor per channel on the low-power and high-power output amplifiers, one per board, one per main chip, and several across different regions of the FPGA, all feeding an algorithm that sets fan speed.[1624]

Platinum resistance wire occupies the high-accuracy end. A meteorological radiosonde sensor of this type achieves a resolution of 0.01 degrees C with a response time of half a second, with a combined sounding uncertainty of about 0.3 degrees C below 16 km.[1207]

Integrated digital sensors combine the element, conversion and an interface. The SHT21 class of I2C parts measures relative humidity and air temperature together and turns up in everything from IO interface boards to network appliances.[377][729] Some precision references also expose a temperature output: the REF01 brings the bandgap core out on a temp pin whose voltage varies linearly with temperature, so it can serve both as a system temperature sensor and as the input to compensation of the reference itself.[1438]

## Cold junction compensation

Any multimeter that measures temperature with an external thermocouple must contain an internal temperature sensor, because the thermocouple only produces a voltage proportional to the difference between its measuring junction and the junction formed at the instrument's input terminals.[121][419] Without knowing the temperature of those dissimilar-metal junctions, the reading carries an offset error.[853]

The compensation can be done in software from the internal sensor's reading, commonly with a low-order polynomial, or in hardware.[419] Dedicated cold junction compensation chips exist: a conventional three-op-amp differential amplifier with a calibrated, trimmed internal temperature sensor and a compensation network tailored to one thermocouple type, such as type K.[419]

Placement is the whole game. In a properly executed thermocouple meter the compensation sensor is physically coupled through to the metal of the input jacks, and the schematic carries an instruction to the PCB designer to locate it near the inputs.[853] Cheaper implementations simply place the sensor close to the jacks without coupling it, which is better than putting it halfway up the meter but is not the correct method.[853] High-accuracy thermometry goes further still, with a calibrated precision thermometer and a large isothermal block at the terminals.[419] The same technique appears in unrelated places: a true-RMS converter section can use a device near the V terminal purely as a temperature sensor for thermal compensation.[1448]

## Reading the internal sensor deliberately

Because the cold junction sensor exists whether or not a probe is attached, it can be read directly. Selecting temperature mode and shorting the input with a shorting link or the probes makes the meter display its own internal sensor, giving an ambient reading with no thermocouple at all.[mkZFHEeQkQI][1392] Dave Jones treats this as a general bench trick and has verified it against a thermal chamber, where the displayed value tracks chamber temperature closely, and against meters left for hours in a refrigerator, where several instruments independently converged on the same low ambient.[1392][48J0m3dvpgA] The same access path serves diagnostics: a sealed unit with no external probe point can be monitored during a thermal-shock repair by reading its internal sensor.[mkZFHEeQkQI]

The important caveat is thermal lag. The sensor sits inside a plastic case and often inside a rubber holster, so ambient air takes a significant time to reach it.[1392] Instrumented in a thermal oven, a meter's internal sensor reads 35 degrees when the box is at 38, and just over 40 when the box is at 45.[249] Self-heating is a second error source: a meter with a built-in temperature display shows a visible rise after its internal battery has been charged, which is a design issue rather than a measurement one.[56] Built-in ambient sensors of this kind are common enough to be unremarkable, sometimes buried far enough in the menus that their existence is not obvious.[46][64][1420]

## Over-temperature and thermal protection

The most common single use of a discrete temperature sensor is heat sink monitoring for over-temperature protection, and the quality of its mechanical attachment is a reliable indicator of design care. Good practice bolts or screws the sensor directly into the side of the heat sink so it measures that heat sink's own temperature.[1698] One receiver design uses two dedicated PCBs solely to hold a TO-92 sensor against the metal, thermally coupling it to the heat sink.[1394] Sensors mounted directly on the heat sink appear in bench supplies, instrument switch-mode supplies, high-power PSUs and inverters alike, on both primary-side switching transistors and secondary-side output devices.[536][1185][814][1402][970][1265][1620] Where the heat sink is fan-cooled, the sensors also decide when the fan runs and how hard, so unobstructed radiant paths can raise the power available before the fan is commanded on.[1173][621]

Amateur and one-off designs solve the coupling problem differently — surface-mount sensors soldered to the board and press-fit under the heat sink when it is bolted down, which works but risks mechanical stress on the packages since the compression acts on a single axis.[661] Another approach cable-ties a power resistor and a sensor onto a chip to hold it at a stable temperature, cheap and possibly effective, though hot spots in the die and long-term aging remain unknown.[802]

Protection only works if the loop is closed properly. A hot air rework station whose fan seized destroyed its heating element even though a temperature sensor was fitted, because loss of airflow was never detected and shutdown was never commanded — a failure that could have been handled in firmware given a working thermocouple feedback loop.[1171] A related heater failure has the same root: with no air moving over the element, the thermistor sat in the wrong thermal environment and read low, so the controller kept delivering power while radiant heat continued to build.[1172] Sensor placement in cheap heaters is frequently poor, with the sensing element sited too close to the heating element to represent inlet or ambient temperature.[1481]

## Soldering irons and rework

Soldering iron performance is governed largely by where the temperature sensor sits relative to the tip. In old-style construction the tip is a separate part that slides over a ceramic heater element, with the sensor embedded somewhere in that element, so the tip is physically decoupled from both heater and sensor.[1064][1065] Cartridge designs integrate tip, element and thermocouple into one part, putting the sensor right at the tip next to the element; the control loop is faster and power is delivered sooner when the tip is loaded.[1106][1065] The three contacts on such a cartridge are the visible evidence of the integrated sensor, and they allow real-time tip temperature to be displayed on the front panel.[472][400] Compared side by side, recovery on the older decoupled construction is markedly slower, and the difference is attributable to tip design, coupling and sensor integration rather than to raw wattage.[1106][1065]

Verifying any of this is harder than it looks. Tip thermometers disagree substantially with the station's own display, and a displayed reading that barely moves when the tip is fully loaded indicates a problem with sensor location or the display path rather than genuinely tight regulation.[1063][1646][1058] Rework station handpieces combine the temperature sensor with stand detection for sleep mode.[1058]

## Thermal imaging

Thermal cameras need a reference temperature to calibrate against. A shutter used for calibration is only effective if it carries a temperature sensor directly on it, so its temperature is known precisely; proper instruments use a large matte-black-painted aluminium plate with an embedded sensor, giving a uniform known temperature across the field.[670] A clip-on micro shutter with no sensor on it cannot achieve the same performance.[670] Microbolometer modules also contain their own internal temperature sensor and can signal the host microcontroller when compensation is required, with the micro deciding whether to command it.[670] Separately, a thermal camera's imaging sensor and its visible camera are physically offset, producing parallax error at close range that requires a software distance setting to correct.[565]

## Environmental and atmospheric measurement

Sensors exposed to the environment face error sources that bench sensors do not. A radiosonde temperature sensor is made thin and tiny and held away from the body on a large air gap specifically to limit solar radiation error, since at altitude solar heating would otherwise raise the sensor above true air temperature; the small surface area also improves response rate.[1207] The same geometry limits evaporative cooling error after the sonde passes through cloud and picks up moisture, and the companion humidity sensor may carry a small resistive heater acting as a de-icer.[1207] Radiosondes report temperature, pressure and position and transmit the data to ground.[1034]

Multi-parameter environmental monitors bundle temperature with humidity, pressure, particulates, air quality and radiation.[1049][1079] Low-power wide-area nodes exist largely to get exactly this kind of reading onto a network, since LoRaWAN's long range and multi-year battery life suit low data rate sensor traffic.[1062] Even precision instruments now include temperature, humidity and pressure sensing internally, because at femtoamp current levels humidity genuinely affects the measurement.[1755]

## Temperature-stabilised systems

Where an oscillator's stability matters, a heater and a temperature sensor form a closed loop around the crystal. An ovenised oscillator carries the heater element and sensor on the outside of the can, regulating to a fixed point such as 79 degrees C, with foam insulation to make it immune to external thermal shock; the assemblies draw several watts to reach and hold temperature.[1049][265] The sensor is sometimes deliberately placed on the outside of the can rather than inside.[265] A cheaper approach compensates rather than controls: crystal temperature characteristics corrected by two thermosensors driving a varactor, producing a poor man's TCXO that is not temperature controlled at all.[574]

Peltier thermal chambers work the same way, with a sensor poking into the chamber and an impeller circulating air so the internal temperature stays consistent throughout the volume rather than only at the sensor.[101] Consistency of the medium around the sensor is a general requirement; a heated bath places its sensor in the path of the agitator's flow so a continuous stream passes over it.[hVIo7vmIExw]

## Multi-point and system-level sensing

Systems with distributed thermal risk need distributed sensing. Competition electric vehicle rules require at least 30 percent of cells to be monitored with no sensor further than 10 mm from a cell's negative pole, which for a typical traction pack of around 100 modules in series and 80 cells in parallel implies hundreds of measurement points.[917] Battery packs of all sizes carry a temperature sensor alongside the cells, often visible as an extra unlabelled contact or a two-pin lead.[923][Cn3DVQGmF9A][1593][1761] Planetary rovers likewise carry many temperature sensors alongside an inertial measurement unit, because knowing temperature is essential to operation.[896]

At the other end of the scale, an ambient sensor's usefulness depends on isolating it from local heat sources. A temperature sensor in a wristwatch is difficult to isolate from the heat of the wearer's wrist, which limits what such a reading means.[1391] A finger touching a small environmental board changes its reading immediately, which is both a fault-finding clue and a demonstration of how little thermal mass such sensors have.[1254]

## Practical consequences

Where the sensor is placed defines what is being controlled. In a stratified hot water tank the sensor location sets the effective control point — one installation places it at the 30-degree mark, which is where heat input stops.[1517] Meaningful thermal characterisation of such a system is not possible without proper water flow meters and proper temperature sensors in the right places.[1517] Multiple sensors plus an ambient reference allow the useful quantity to be extracted by subtraction rather than read directly: plotting the difference between each of three sensors and ambient, rather than the raw temperatures, reveals rises of up to about 13 degrees that the raw traces obscure.[50]

Cheap dedicated data loggers make the same measurement portable. A coin-sized sealed logger uses its own case as the sensor pad, runs for about ten years on an internal lithium cell, holds 2 K of sample memory and can be set to log anywhere from once per minute to once per year.[9] For bench climate control, a bench-mounted temperature sensor driving a hacked air conditioner over its Wi-Fi module can hold a small lab to roughly 0.2 to 0.3 degrees of hysteresis, tighter than the unit's own control system achieved — a result Ian Johnston attributes partly to the small volume being regulated.[a4Xpsenpd6E]
