# battery life

Battery life is the length of time a portable product remains functional on one set of cells or one charge, and it is set by the ratio of the usable energy in the battery to the average current the product draws.[1533][140] It is one of the few specifications that can decide a product's fate on its own: the choice of battery type and the runtime that follows from it can ruin a product or make it a complete success.[18] Because the number falls out of decisions made early — processor, display, cell format, cutoff voltage — it is a design constraint rather than a feature that can be added later.[e9cpKN69Avk][ILIO5b1BliE]

## Estimating runtime

The first-order estimate is capacity divided by average current, and it requires measuring the current the product actually draws rather than trusting the manual, which typically quotes consumption only.[1533] Measurement is the practical route because directly timing a product that runs for hundreds of hours is not viable.[1533] A worked example: 2.5 Ah of AA capacity divided by 150 µA gives 16,666 hours, or 1.9 years of continuous operation.[1539] Another: 650 mWh in a CR2032 divided by a 10 µW load gives 65,000 hours, about 7.4 years.[1383] A 100 mAh coin cell asked to last three years permits an average draw of roughly 3.8 µA.[1242]

Refining the estimate means choosing the right characteristic curve, because manufacturers publish service hours for constant-current, constant-power and constant-resistance loads, and a real product may be a combination of these plus pulsed activity.[140] Even then, getting an accurate figure from datasheet curves alone is difficult.[140] Temperature is a large and often overlooked term: for one cell at a fixed draw, published service life runs from about 2.5 hours at −10 °C to 9 hours at 21 °C, a factor of more than three across a 30-degree span.[1533] Datasheet curves also tend to stop at loads around 100 mW, leaving the ultra-low-drain region — the region that matters for products expected to run a year on a set of cells — poorly characterised.[774]

Duty cycle dominates everything else in intermittent products. Holding peak and active currents fixed while stretching the sleep interval moves the average current down proportionally: a device with a 15-second active event every 60 seconds yields about 43 hours, while the same device waking every 1000 seconds yields 560 days.[1331] The same principle drives design decisions elsewhere — a GPS tracker that logs on detected movement rather than continuously,[1351] a Z80 portable that sleeps between keypresses,[382] and a PIR sensor light pulse-width-modulated at very low current to reach eight years on its cells.[684]

## Cutoff voltage and usable capacity

A product's low-voltage cutoff determines how much of the cell's energy it can reach. Most equipment is designed to stop somewhere around 1 V per cell, but useful energy remains below that, and designing down to 0.8 V per cell maximises the extractable capacity.[779] Products intended to run on rechargeables must work down to 1.1 V per cell in any case.[751] Measured cutoffs vary widely: one meter holds down to 1 V per cell across four AAAs,[973] another quits at 1.1 V per cell,[249] and a 9 V LCR meter gives up at about 7.4 V, roughly 1.2 V per cell, which wastes a substantial fraction of the pack.[115] A Fluke 37 drops out between 6.4 and 6.5 V on a 9 V battery.[WWMXJLhPVdA] The correct way to find a cutoff is to feed the product from a bench supply and wind the voltage down, which is also the only practical way short of watching a battery discharge in real time.[779]

## Cell format and chemistry

Cell format is a volumetric energy decision. A 9 V battery contains four small cells internally and therefore stores considerably less energy than the AA cells that fit in a similar footprint, giving poor battery life for no volumetric advantage.[ILIO5b1BliE] Moving a multimeter from a 9 V battery to AA cells raised its rated life to 800 hours,[64] and one design was deliberately made four or five millimetres thicker to accommodate AA cells rather than AAAs, on the judgment that the extra runtime was worth the thickness.[ILIO5b1BliE] AAA cells shrink a product but cost runtime.[ILIO5b1BliE] C and D cells push capacity further but become unwieldy in handheld gear.[184]

Lithium primary chemistry occupies the extreme end. Two lithium thionyl chloride D cells of about 17 Ah each, powering a meter drawing 0.5 mA, work out to 34,000 hours — roughly 3.8 years of continuous operation.[WWMXJLhPVdA] Implanted and safety-critical equipment relies on the same approach: a neurostimulator's non-rechargeable lithium cell is specified to effectively the shelf life of the battery, on the order of ten years,[1027] and parachute automatic activation devices used certified Panasonic cells qualified for 10, 12 or 15 years while retaining low enough ESR to fire a heating element at end of life.[1165] Where a device must sit dormant for an unknown number of years, a lithium primary solution with a guaranteed ten-year-plus life is the appropriate choice rather than a rechargeable lithium-ion pack.[1136] A large primary pack can carry even an LED-display instrument for about a decade.[1491]

## Displays and backlights

Displays are usually the largest single load in a low-power instrument. An OLED multimeter display delivers exceptional update rates and contrast but costs most of the runtime: one such meter managed eight hours against the several hundred hours typical of a good LCD meter.[56][57] Later OLED instruments improved on this considerably, though an LCD version remains preferable for runtime, and OLEDs are additionally poor outdoors.[832][894] Backlights are a comparable penalty — 12 mA on low and 30 mA on high against a 1.7 mA base draw in one meter,[64] and 5 mA in another.[1692] The design response is either a fully reflective LCD with an optional momentary backlight,[1699] or aggressive power-down of the display supply and backlight rails so only the microcontroller remains powered.[473] Ultra-high-efficiency edge LEDs allow a front-lit e-ink reader to keep weeks of runtime with the light on.[370] Sharp memory LCDs consume roughly 4 µA statically, about double what a three-year coin-cell digital watch budget allows, with dynamic consumption not much above that.[1242]

Colour LCD instruments illustrate the cost directly: a combined scope-multimeter drawing 350 mA in oscilloscope mode returns under 20 hours in multimeter mode and under 10 hours as a scope from a 3400 mAh pack,[1597] while a 2000 mAh predecessor manages about 5.7 hours.[1540]

## Multimeters as the benchmark case

Handheld multimeters are the clearest worked example of battery life as a purchase criterion, and long runtime is treated as essential for a general-purpose meter.[75] The rough scale is: about 1000 hours at the top of the market and rare, 300 to 500 hours for a good meter, and 100 hours or less as poor.[75] Three hundred hours is taken as a decent figure, with 500 hours plus a reasonable target for a four-AA design.[184] Data-logging and graphing meters sit at the bottom of the range because memory and faster processors raise the baseline draw.[75]

Measured and claimed figures across the field bear this out. A Fluke 27 and a Fluke 37 are both rated at 1000 hours,[372][1393] and a Fluke 23 at 2000 hours.[1424] A Fluke 87V and a Fluke 117 claim 400 hours, with the 87V measured at 1.1 mA.[10][60] A Fluke 233 claims 400 hours; at a measured 5 mA from 2800 mAh AA cells the arithmetic gives about 560 hours, comfortably meeting the claim.[43] A Fluke 28 Series II claims 800 hours against an expected 3.5 mA and measures 1.7 mA.[64] A 121GW at about 5 mA on four AA alkalines works out to roughly 600 hours nominal.[ILIO5b1BliE] A Fluke 17B MAX claims 500 hours minimum at 5 mA and draws 2 mA.[1692] A wireless Fluke 87 variant on three AA cells quotes 250 hours minimum.[4UY3uI6tl1E] At the other end, a $25 meter draws 1.7 mA,[1007] a budget meter yields 50 to 70 hours from two AA cells,[1096] and a rechargeable novelty meter drawing 22 mA from a 130 mAh cell manages about five hours.[1238] A backlit rechargeable meter drawing 63 mA from a 1000 mAh cell returns roughly a dozen hours.[1704] The Fluke 189 shipped with about 50 hours, which the 289 raised to 100 hours — still far short of the several hundred hours of the general-purpose meters.[15]

LCR meters run shorter than multimeters as a class: 20 to 30 hours against 200 to 300 for a meter, so cells get changed often.[234] A 9 V LCR meter drawing about 22 mA gives roughly 45 to 50 hours.[137]

Standby draw matters as much as operating draw for instruments that spend most of their life switched off. A meter with 143 µA of standby consumption gives roughly six months on the shelf, which is high for the task,[91] whereas a 55 µA standby draw against an 800 mAh 9 V battery yields around 14,000 hours — effectively the shelf life of the cell.[320]

A recurring engineering position in this area is that battery-powered instruments should be deliberately over-engineered on runtime rather than merely adequate, since the cost is cheap and the failure mode — a flat meter at the moment it is needed — is the worst one.[197][1597] Dave Jones has repeatedly proposed a 10,000-hour bench multimeter, an order of magnitude beyond anything on the market, which corresponds to over a year of continuous 24/7 operation and is readily achievable by fitting a large primary battery into a meter with spare internal volume.[1371][1393]

## Rechargeable packs and cycle longevity

For rechargeable systems the relevant lifetime is the number of years the pack survives, not the hours per charge, and partial cycling is the standard lever. Limiting charge to 80% via a software cap extends pack life, with full charges reserved for long trips.[1432] Home storage installations are commonly run between a floor of 15 to 20% and a ceiling short of full, sacrificing usable capacity for longevity; one 15 kWh installation is run across 80% of its nominal capacity to that end.[gAu8CvMjDrU][5C_IT9F4ZkA][aANZNwmnC_4] Manufacturers advise against a full 100% discharge cycle every day.[eHCzTC9lku8] Equipment that offers no way to set a lower float target forces the pack to sit permanently at 100%.[8P8Af5SR57U] Pack lifetime is also the limiting term in payback calculations: solar panels last 20 to 25 years while the battery does not, so a storage system that takes 20 or 30 years to pay back cannot do so before the battery needs replacing.[1628][wTzQg1N-SLI]

## Claims and their verification

Battery life is a heavily marketed number, which makes it a frequent target of unsupported claims. The prominent example is a boost sleeve marketed as delivering an eight-fold increase in battery life; that figure holds only for the narrow class of products that cut out around 1.35 to 1.4 V, whereas the vast majority run to 1.1 V and would see improvements closer to 20%.[751] For some products the effect is negative, because the converter's own efficiency and quiescent draw can halve the runtime, and the maximum output current — the parameter that decides whether the device works in a high-drain product at all — went unpublished.[751] Practical testing bore this out: an MP3 player run on cells discharged to about 0.91 V returned a fraction of the claimed multiple.[pa8j-Lje_2o] A later 30% claim for a similar concept is a defensible number, and one that design engineers can already realise by incorporating the same boost topology directly into a product.[ixbLmBWUqcQ] Claims that a device eliminates keep-alive drain are similarly weighed against what already exists: a product cited as wasting nearly all its battery power already achieved a five-year battery life, and no chip turns that into 500 years.[971] A nuclear battery claimed to last up to 28,000 years without replacement was the subject of SEC fraud allegations; the supporting circuitry alone bounds any such figure.[1579]

## Consumer and portable equipment

E-book readers established the practical ceiling for consumer runtime, with e-ink devices claiming a month with wireless disabled, about three weeks with Wi-Fi on, and roughly a week on cellular.[108] A competing reader quoted six weeks or 14,000 page turns.[188] Continuous audio playback on the same hardware measured 24 hours.[108] Runtime falls sharply once an LCD is involved: a 7-inch tablet was rated seven or eight hours,[220] and another ten hours, short of a Sydney–Los Angeles flight.[322]

Laptops span the same range. Vintage machines with reflective LCDs and 4000-series CMOS logic reached seven days of operation from four AA cells, or 20 hours on the earlier model.[1662] A 1993 colour PowerBook offered about an hour from a full charge,[W91qP8GpgjM][802] and an x86 handheld a couple of hours, limited by thermal dissipation from a processor that ran hot in an inadequate volume.[914][913] Contemporary machines depend on configuration: an 80 Wh gaming laptop is rated 5.5 hours with a Ryzen and RTX combination against 7.5 hours for the Intel and GTX variant, while ultraportables claim 15 to 18 hours.[p287qKMXqxQ][Rmhu0Yvba2Y] Thickness is the usual trade: adding one millimetre to a tablet would allow at least 50% more cell capacity and potentially double the runtime, a trade obscured when marketing optimises for thinness at constant battery life.[5B6O1aFA5l8]

Elsewhere the constraint appears in whatever form the application dictates — a 16-hour wearable ESD monitor sized to cover a working day,[1567] a bike light delivering 12.5 hours at its 300-lumen setting,[gTTsvYbQOT4] a strap light drawing 1.37 A at full brightness for about 7 hours from 49.5 Wh,[1010] an electrostatic whiteboard at 1 mA giving around 9 months from a 3000 mAh pack,[1149] a wireless microphone whose processor is deliberately clocked well below its 72 MHz capability,[571] a shotgun microphone reaching 100 plus hours,[182] a dosimeter at about 12 months,[776] and a planetary rover carrying only about two hours of battery reserve, sufficient to drive 500 metres before solar power becomes mandatory.[896]
