# constant current mode

Constant current mode is the operating state in which a power supply or electronic load holds current at a set value and lets voltage go wherever the load dictates, rather than holding voltage fixed and letting current fall where it may.[1688][200] A bench supply with a current limit is really a dual-mode instrument: it runs as a voltage source until the load demands more than the set current, at which point it crosses over and becomes a current source, dropping its output voltage as far as necessary to keep the current at the programmed figure.[1688][1293] The maximum voltage it is willing to reach in that state is the compliance voltage.[1087][1676]

The crossover is what makes the mode useful. A supply set to 0.5 A stays in constant voltage while the load draws 100 mA, transitions once the load asks for a little over 0.5 A, and will hold 0.5 A even into a dead short.[1688] Applying a load to a supply set for 1.8 A pulls the terminals from 6 V down to 1.1 V, and the discrepancy between the 1.1 V at the load and the 1 V read back at the instrument is simply drop in the leads and connections when nothing is being remote sensed.[1293]

## What determines the operating point

Which mode a supply sits in is set by the load, not by the operator. With a current source feeding a string of nine LEDs whose combined forward drop is around 46 V, the current setting governs brightness and the 46 V is merely whatever the diodes happen to drop; the LEDs are current-driven devices and are not fed from a 46 V voltage source.[1688] Push the load resistance up until the required voltage exceeds what the supply can deliver — at 1 mA, a change from 8 kΩ to 9 kΩ against a 9 V setting — and the instrument falls out of constant current into constant voltage, after which raising the resistance further simply reduces the current.[1688]

The same handover happens on its own during switch-on transients. An incandescent lamp pulls maximum current from a cold filament, so the supply enters constant current at the instant of connection and then drops back into constant voltage once the filament has warmed.[gqzZHbEfWDU] A supply set to 2 A driving a 3 A load sits at 0.12 V out while delivering its 2 A.[1030]

## Output capacitance

The dominant design constraint on any supply with a current limit is output capacitance. Any bulk capacitance on the output terminals sits downstream of the current-sensing loop, so at the moment the supply transitions into constant current that stored energy dumps straight into the load, over and above the set current, before the loop has any chance to react.[511][224][1691] The design goal for a lab supply with constant current capability is therefore to minimise output capacitance while still keeping ripple and loop stability acceptable — not to fit the largest capacitor that improves transient response.[511][1691][1293] Fitting a 2200 µF capacitor for transient performance defeats the current limit exactly when the current limit is what is protecting the circuit under test.[222]

Instruments frequently violate this. A 1000 µF output capacitor is high for any supply with a constant current capability.[511] Large output capacitance can also be identified indirectly by how long the output terminals take to discharge after switch-off.[1265] The consequence is visible in interaction with an electronic load: a supply with large, low-ESR output capacitors slams current into the load at the changeover, the load's own overcurrent protection trips, the supply briefly re-enters constant current, and the two instruments oscillate against each other.[1298] A load in constant resistance mode can drive a supply into sustained oscillation between constant voltage and constant current.[828] Conversely, a well-behaved pairing shows the load's loop response holding 1 A without upsetting the supply's constant current mode at all, with no oscillation even at an 80 V setting.[1691]

## Implementation and indication

A linear supply implements the mode with a series pass transistor and a shunt sense resistor: the voltage developed across the sense resistor sets the current, and the same pass element reverts to acting as a linear voltage regulator when the supply falls back into constant voltage.[200] The approach scales — a 10 kW argon ion laser supply kicks into constant current after the high-voltage strike pulse and regulates it with a water-cooled linear pass stage.[1381] Digitally controlled electronic loads instead synthesise constant current, constant resistance and constant power in firmware around a fast DAC and ADC, which is also how they generate programmable transients and slew rates of up to 5 A/µs.[862][1550] Determining which mode the hardware is actually in can require a dedicated circuit; one design uses an LM321 as a comparator whose output tells the microcontroller whether the supply is in constant voltage or constant current.[1561]

Front-panel indication matters because the mode changes silently. Good practice is an unambiguous annunciator — an indicator that turns red in constant current to match the silkscreen, or a display that changes colour on crossover.[1691][1293] Changing only a small bullet dot is not obvious enough to read at a glance.[1298] Segments too dim to see and instruments that boot without showing which mode they are in are both faults of the same kind.[TQcV3ftPLgA][862]

## Bench applications

Constant current mode is the standard way to bias a device whose voltage is not the quantity of interest. Setting 5 V compliance at 10 mA drives a back-to-back red/blue LED assembly that a multimeter's diode range cannot light, since that range supplies only a couple of milliamps.[1087] Programming 1 A through a PCB via and measuring the resulting voltage across it with a 6½-digit meter turns the supply into the excitation half of a four-terminal resistance measurement.[543] Testing a fuse works the same way — 400 mA through a fuse producing 0.3 V of burden voltage, including cable drop in a two-wire connection — with the supply's compliance voltage appearing across the gap once the fuse blows.[1676] Constant current at half an amp into a flat battery pack is a way to see whether dead cells or their protection circuitry will come back.[Cn3DVQGmF9A]

## Range, resolution and behaviour under test

Useful low-end range is a real specification. A supply whose minimum constant current setting is 1 mA covers sleeping microcontrollers and idle RF products; one whose current limit will not go below 0.6 A cannot do the job at all.[1402][1558] Setting resolution can be finer than display resolution — increments of 0.5 mA on a display showing whole milliamps.[1293] Accuracy is checked by shorting the output and comparing readback to a reference: 2.995 to 2.996 A against a 3 A setting, and 10 mA settings holding well within a 0.1% plus 5 mA specification.[439]

Transition behaviour is checked with a single-shot capture triggered on the falling output voltage.[224][1265] Clean behaviour means entering constant current without overshoot or oscillation; a small undershoot on entry, recovering in around 50 ms, is normal.[1691][1265][1030] Defects show up here. A supply may take 7 to 8 ms to recognise the overcurrent, over-volting the load during that window.[1402] Adjusting the voltage setting while in constant current should not knock the supply out of the mode, but one instrument jumped out for roughly 12 ms at inconsistent intervals whenever the voltage knob was moved.[1402] Another dropped its output voltage unpredictably at a 4 A setting into a 50 W load where about 3.3 A at 15 V was expected, and a third refused to hold a 5 A constant current setting, sagging to 4 A.[315][439] Shorting the sense terminals ought to put a supply into constant current; disabling the output instead is a different behaviour entirely.[1691]

## Electronic loads

On the sinking side, constant current is one of four standard modes alongside constant voltage, constant resistance and constant power.[281][1023][Y2rcx4vKxlc][393] A load in constant current is the standard instrument for exercising a supply: set 1 A, or step 1 A for one second and 2 A for one second in a repeating list, and observe what the supply does.[320][1265][1023] Configuration typically includes range, high and low voltage thresholds, and slew rate.[862] Because a load in constant current forces the supply into its current limit, the pairing is also the most direct way to find the supply's weaknesses — which is why an electronic load has a reputation as a power supply killer.[320][828]
