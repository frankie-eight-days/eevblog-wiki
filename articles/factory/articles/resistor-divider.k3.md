# resistor divider

A resistor divider is a pair of resistors in series across an input voltage, with the output tapped from the junction between them; the output is always some voltage less than the input because the circuit drops voltage across the upper resistor and taps off the voltage across the lower one.[1399] There is no gain in the arrangement at all.[1399] Despite this simplicity, it is among the most frequently used structures in electronics, appearing in amplifiers, attenuators, every form of voltage regulation, and in many circuits that are not drawn recognizably as dividers.[1399] By drawing convention the input is placed on the left and the output on the right.[1399]

## Loading and measurement error

A divider's nominal ratio holds only when nothing else loads the tap. Any additional resistance across the lower leg — including the measuring instrument — forms a parallel combination that pulls the tap voltage down. A digital multimeter's nominal 10 MΩ input impedance measuring a half-rail divider built from 100 kΩ resistors introduces roughly a 1% error; with a 1 MΩ upper resistor the error is about 5%, and measuring a small fraction of the rail with much larger resistances produces errors around 9% — in the worst case approximately an order of magnitude of error attributable to that 10 MΩ instrument resistance.[286] A divider of two matched 200 kΩ resistors fed a precise 10 V reads about 4.98 V at the midpoint rather than 5 V on a 10 MΩ meter.[1067] Analog moving-coil meters load far more heavily: a 20 kΩ-per-volt movement draws 50 µA at full scale regardless of range, and that current comes from the circuit under test.[1067]

The same parallel-loading principle applies in design. In an LM3914 battery-gauge circuit, an input divider network sits directly in parallel with the reference-setting resistor R1, so the true value of R1 must be computed from the parallel combination rather than used directly; the adjust-pin current (75 µA typical, 120 µA maximum) must likewise be added to the divider current before calculating the lower resistor, yielding in one worked example 10.91 kΩ, realized exactly as 12 kΩ in parallel with 120 kΩ.[204]

## References, biasing, and level shifting

Dividers are the standard way to generate reference and bias voltages. Two 1 kΩ resistors from a 5 V rail produce a 2.5 V reference for an LM311 comparator.[941] A divider across a split supply furnishes symmetric ±1.25 V trip references for a window overload detector, and dividing the input signal itself (1 MΩ series with 270 kΩ to ground) brings the sensed voltage down inside a jellybean comparator's common-mode input range when sensing near the rail, avoiding the need for a rail-to-rail part.[471] Op-amps on a single 5 V supply, such as the LM324, require a divider to bias inputs up to a DC offset near half the supply, with filtering to keep that bias clean, since grounding the inputs would clip the waveform.[713]

Regulator and reference output voltages are set by feedback dividers: a DC-DC converter with a 1.245 V reference and a 51 kΩ over 10 kΩ divider yields (51/10 + 1) × 1.245 ≈ 7.6 V.[366] The LM4041 reference adds an adjustment pin over the fixed LM4040 specifically so a divider can set the output.[1438] Conversely, a fixed-voltage regulator can be recognized by the absence of any feedback divider.[1475] The 555 timer embeds a divider of 5 kΩ resistors to generate its threshold and trigger levels at 2/3 and 1/3 of the supply (6 V and 3 V on a 9 V supply), and this string is popularly — though inaccurately — cited as the origin of the part's name.[555] The TL431 2.5 V reference paired with dividers is a classic power-good monitoring arrangement in PC power supplies.[1364]

As a level shifter, a divider of 1 kΩ and 680 Ω drops a 5 V TTL output to under 2 V for low-voltage logic.[1531] A 220 Ω/47 Ω pair knocks a roughly 3.5 V digital RGB signal down below 1 V to match a monitor chip's 700 mV peak-to-peak input level.[1246] A divider with a diode clamp also conditions a mains tap into a 50 Hz clock for a digital clock's ripple counter.[801]

## Keypads and configuration encoding

A divider chain lets many switches share a single microcontroller ADC pin: each key switches a different resistance into the divider, producing a distinct voltage that firmware maps back to a key.[1361][1282] Membrane keypads can use per-key printed carbon resistors for this purpose,[1282] and equipment test points labeled "ADC key" indicate exactly this arrangement.[1602] The technique is fragile in practice: a tactile switch whose contact resistance wanders from a couple of ohms to hundreds of ohms corrupts the divider voltage enough to cause major misreads,[1361] and high-impedance ladder dividers of this kind are susceptible to noise pickup as well as dodgy contacts.[1360] The same encoding trick appears as configuration strapping, where an internal ADC reads set resistors as a divider so that changing the resistor values changes the instrument's configuration.[977]

## Instrument front ends

In multimeters, the input divider network sets the voltage ranges, and much of a meter's stability and accuracy comes down to it.[1447] Quality meters use laser-trimmed ceramic hybrid thin-film divider networks;[1447][372][417][1382][628] the resistors share a ceramic substrate so all taps are thermally matched, with slots routed between taps to prevent creepage.[1723] Bottom-end meters substitute discrete passives,[1574] while better divider resistors can eliminate calibration pots entirely.[1378] A common calibration strategy is to trim only the millivolt range and rely on the divider ratios for all higher ranges, which is why errors grow on the higher voltage ranges.[852] Because the divider is the highest-impedance part of the front end, contamination or leakage across it loads the network and drifts every range; manufacturers strip solder mask around the high-impedance node for this reason, and a sealed ceramic divider that drifts points to contamination elsewhere rather than a failed network.[1564] The divider is also the first thing an unprotected input jack hits, ahead of any PTC or series protection resistor.[W2o8otvVML4]

Oscilloscope vertical attenuators are tapped divider ladders, and the pulse response can differ with the volts-per-division setting because each tap sits at a different point in the ladder with its own high- and low-frequency compensation; vintage instruments exposed per-step trimmer capacitors for tweaking each divider step's response.[6XpyOGw6RFM] Compensation capacitors across divider resistors serve the same AC-flattening role in meter dividers.[613][932] A standard ×10 passive probe is itself a divider built around a 9 MΩ resistor working into the scope's 1 MΩ input.[655]

## High-voltage and differential probes

High-voltage probes are dividers built from series strings of high-voltage resistors: a homemade 5 kV probe uses five 10 MΩ resistors each rated at 3.5 kV for a 50 MΩ upper leg, against a roughly 50 kΩ lower leg formed by a 56 kΩ resistor in parallel with the oscilloscope's 1 MΩ input and another 1 MΩ resistor.[85] Dropping most of the voltage across high-value series resistors so that only low-voltage amplification is needed downstream is the standard technique inside high-voltage differential probes.[909] Such probes use two matched dividers — for example 4 MΩ input legs with compensation capacitors against a low-value lower leg — feeding a differential FET front end,[932][1368][1415] and the high leg resistance lets the probe connect anywhere in a circuit without disturbing it.[1368]

Divider matching dominates differential measurement quality. The op-amps in a differential probe may exceed 100 dB of common-mode rejection, but resistor divider mismatch drags the achieved CMRR down to 40 dB or less;[1521] matching of the divider string and the differential amplifier's gain together set the figure, and ordinary component tolerance can throw the result out.[1744] The Wheatstone bridge is the canonical ratio-matching application: it is two dividers excited from the same source, and when the ratios are equal the voltage difference between the two tap points is precisely zero.[1476]

## Precision divider construction

Thin-film divider networks achieve very high leg values — around 9.9 MΩ — through serpentine trace geometries, since that is the only practical way to get such values on a substrate.[730] Mixing geometries (ladders, serpentine sections, coarse and intermediate trims) reduces the sensitivity of the final value to how much material the laser removes, letting trimming converge progressively on the target, and trimming can be done both per-resistor and on a ratio basis.[730]
