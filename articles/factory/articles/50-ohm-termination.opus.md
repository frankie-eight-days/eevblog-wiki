# 50 ohm termination

50 ohm termination is the practice of loading a coaxial signal path with a resistance equal to the cable's characteristic impedance, either by switching a scope's input from 1 megohm to 50 ohms or by fitting an external in-line terminator at the instrument end.[652][1566][311] Without it, a length of coax carrying anything but low frequencies behaves as an unterminated transmission line, and the resulting reflections corrupt amplitude and shape badly enough that the measurement means nothing.[652][1367] Getting the termination right — and accounting for what it does to amplitude and to the load seen by the source — is the difference between measuring a signal and measuring the cable.[652]

## Why an unterminated coax misleads

Coax is a transmission line, and at high frequency an unterminated one produces phase and amplitude effects, standing waves, and outright signal disappearance.[652] The behaviour is not subtle: with two unterminated channels connected, disconnecting one channel changes the amplitude on the other, and doubling the length of one cable changes the display again.[652] With the scope correctly terminated in 50 ohms a 1 V peak-to-peak signal appears as expected, but hanging an extra open-ended piece of coax onto the connection makes the signal vanish.[652] The correct approaches are a properly terminated 50 ohm coax or a high-frequency times-10 probe plugged straight in, either of which avoids transmission line reflection problems and lets the true output amplitude of a function generator be measured over frequency.[652]

Termination is also what makes a driven load defined. Feeding a signal generator output into a 50 ohm terminated load removes transmission line issues and reflections entirely, which is why it is the standard front end for common mode rejection ratio measurements.[1521][1631][1744] The same reasoning applies to devices that specify a matched load: a photon-counting sensor module with an output transformer expects 50 ohm termination, and a 50 ohm terminator has to be fitted to read it correctly.[869] An avalanche pulse generator develops its fast-rising pulse across a 50 ohm output, so the oscilloscope must terminate it in 50 ohms.[306]

## Internal versus in-line

Many scopes provide a switchable internal 50 ohm input, selected alongside 1 megohm and ground in the coupling menu.[1501][1638] Scopes without it — commonly the cheaper models — can be terminated with an external in-line terminator, or with a T-piece carrying a 50 ohm terminator hung off it.[652][1566][311] A missing internal termination is a visible cost saving in the front end: one 12-bit scope is identical in chipset to its more expensive siblings but omits the extra relay that would switch in the 50 ohm load.[1563] The same omission appears across an entire model line where the higher-tier scope has the extra relay and the 50 ohm termination and the sub-$1000 model does not.[1146]

Where the internal termination exists, it is not necessarily a single 50 ohm resistor. A 1 GHz front end uses a network — a 45R part plus smaller values with compensation — to hold the impedance flat to the full bandwidth, and the 50 ohm path is a separate switched route alongside the high-impedance 1 megohm path.[1717] On the generator side the arrangement mirrors it: an output buffer followed by a relay-switched 50 ohm output terminator.[587]

External terminators are preferred where power handling matters. A scope's built-in termination may be rated at only around half a watt, so an external 2 watt series termination is used instead when the applied voltage needs to be as high as possible.[1521] External terminators are generally rated for more power than the internal ones for exactly this reason.[1744] Practical in-line parts include 1 watt units and 2 GHz 2 watt series terminators supplied with active probes.[1266][Y7t6BIhBZhc][1368] Where a signal's amplitude demands it, at least a half watt 50 ohm load is the minimum.[1266]

There is a second reason to prefer an external terminator: comparability. Using an identical matched external terminator on two scopes rather than each scope's internal one keeps the termination the same across instruments being compared,[797][1220] and at high frequencies the difference between an internal terminator and an in-line one can itself be visible in the pulse response.[6XpyOGw6RFM]

## Amplitude bookkeeping

Terminating changes the level, and the change has to be accounted for. If a generator's 50 ohm output impedance setting is on and the scope is also terminated in 50 ohms, the path is double terminated and the displayed amplitude is half what the generator claims until the output impedance setting corrects for it.[652] The same double termination occurs when an in-line 50 ohm terminator is added to a scope whose input is already switched to 50 ohms.[1220]

Attenuators can be built from terminators directly: four 50 ohm terminators wired in parallel on the coax, with the generator set to a 50 ohm output, produce a low-level signal small enough to exercise a 500 microvolt per division range that the generator alone cannot reach.[196] Unintended dividers form the same way. Lossy coax with roughly 330 ohms of centre conductor resistance, feeding a scope terminated in 50 ohms, forms a voltage divider that attenuates the signal by about seven and a half times.[594] Adding 50 ohm terminators on both channels is nevertheless the fix for common mode garbage swamping a differential ripple measurement.[594]

A resistive divider probe works on the same principle deliberately: a 1K resistor in series requires a 50 ohm termination at the scope, internal or in-line, because the probe body is a transmission line that would otherwise reflect, and the 1K against the 50 ohms yields roughly a 21-to-1 division with a 1K DC input impedance.[1367]

## Active probes

Active FET probes generally require 50 ohm termination at the scope.[Y7t6BIhBZhc][1368][309] Some are supplied with their own in-line terminator, which allows them to be used with a 1 megohm input scope at a fixed extra attenuation factor.[Y7t6BIhBZhc][1368] A probe that outputs into a standard 50 ohm oscilloscope input, rather than through a proprietary vendor interface, will work on any scope with a 50 ohm input — and any scope at gigahertz bandwidth should have one — or with an in-line terminator otherwise.[1715] Homebuilt and commissioned high-frequency probes follow the same convention, feeding a 50 ohm terminated scope.[1525]

## Measurement work that depends on it

Bandwidth verification is done into a defined 50 ohm load so the cabling is removed from the result: a signal fed directly via coax with 50 ohm termination is stable across frequency, exposing any peaking or dropout as a fault of the instrument rather than the setup.[1260][1317] Comparisons of the −3 dB point between scopes are made with both inputs 50 ohm terminated,[1309][311] as are the amplitude checks that confirm a 1 V peak-to-peak signal into 50 ohms arrives as 1 V.[1260]

Noise floor measurements are made with a 50 ohm terminator on the input, which both defines the source impedance and shields the input from pickup — a well-shielded analyser with a 50 ohm terminator on the front shows no 50 Hz mains pickup at all.[528][529] Scope noise specifications are quoted this way: 50 ohm terminated full bandwidth figures of 64 microvolts standard deviation against 117 microvolts RMS on a 1.5 GHz front end,[1529] around 40 microvolts AC RMS on a 12-bit scope,[1566] and a figure that jumps by roughly a factor of four when the 50 ohm terminated input is removed.[1501] Termination also reveals when a range is genuine: identical noise on the 500 microvolt and 1 mV ranges with a 50 ohm terminated input confirms the lower range is only a software expansion.[1501] Comparing two scopes fairly means the same bandwidth and the same 50 ohm terminated signal on both.[1501][YkgCcYG29nk] Switching from a 1 megohm to a 50 ohm terminated input roughly halves the observed noise on some instruments.[199] For frequency response analysis, forgetting the 50 ohm terminator shows up immediately as a magnitude that shoots up past 1 MHz instead of rolling off.[1103]

## When not to terminate

50 ohm termination is not always the right choice. For measurements of oscilloscope ADC dynamic range at low frequency, the generator is better used in high impedance mode, because a 50 ohm termination brings the accuracy of the 50 ohm load, the divider, and the generator's 50 ohm output into the result — complications not worth carrying when signal integrity is not at stake.[6qjqhnQiQXQ] Ordinary oscilloscope probes work over unterminated coaxial cable by design, with no 50 ohm termination at either end, because of the specific construction of the probe cable itself.[1266] And testing a scope's pulse response thoroughly means doing it both with and without the 50 ohm termination.[6XpyOGw6RFM]

Termination also has to actually exist to matter: on a 1 gigohm input front end, fitting a 50 ohm shorting plug does nothing at all.[lXvXBdTP7Tg]

## On the bench

50 ohm terminators sit alongside banana-to-BNC adapters, BNC-to-RCA adapters and T-pieces as standard bench interconnect stock.[168] Signal generators expose the choice as an output load setting of high impedance or 50 ohms, which scales the displayed amplitude accordingly,[fM4bbKVw2og][1552] and instruments lacking a 50 ohm output termination offer only high impedance.[1701] A generator's stated output voltage range does not necessarily hold when driving 50 ohms.[876] A quick 50 ohm terminator can even be pressed into service to see what difference a defined load makes to a homemade EMC probe feeding a scope FFT.[1188]
