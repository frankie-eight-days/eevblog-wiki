# single sided pcb

A single-sided PCB carries copper on one face only, with the components mounted on the bare side and their leads passing through to be soldered on the copper side. It is the cheapest form of printed circuit board there is, and it remains cheaper to manufacture than a double-sided board even now that double-sided fabrication has become inexpensive.[1108][LAbWsqS51Tk] That cost gap is the entire reason the technology survives: it is still the default in high-volume consumer goods, in white goods, and above all in switch-mode power supplies, where a saving of a few cents per unit outweighs every inconvenience it creates for the layout engineer.[1111][1108][187][770]

## Why it persists

The economics are brutally simple. A board with copper on one side needs one imaging and etching pass and no through-hole plating, so it undercuts a double-sided board on price at any volume where the difference matters.[1108][187] Appliance manufacture is the clearest case: even inside expensive equipment, the power supply board will typically be single-sided, because it was built down to the last cent.[1ngqB4mxZOI][289] The pattern shows up across televisions, microwave ovens, washing machines and similar goods,[1108][1111] in plasma TV supplies,[52][725] in PC and instrument supplies,[462][630][607][1265] in the supply of a bench oscilloscope,[187] and in the cheapest toys and novelties, where the whole product has to reach the absolute lowest possible cost.[292][afErPINq8qc]

The material usually goes with the process. Single-sided boards are frequently phenolic-based rather than fiberglass, a substrate summarised as "none of that FR4 rubbish".[1ngqB4mxZOI][1562] Phenolic single-sided construction appears in 1970s and 1980s consumer gear as a matter of course,[1111][443][936][991] and it was still being used in low-cost boards subcontracted out for plasma TV drivers.[446] The combination has real electrical consequences: a cheap phenolic single-sided board offers no ground plane and no controlled-impedance routing, which is disqualifying for high-speed work such as USB 2.0 signalling.[12]

## Jumper links

Routing a circuit with one copper layer eventually runs out of room, and the standard escape is a jumper link — a wire or a zero-ohm resistor that hops one trace over another on the component side. Links are the visible signature of the technique, and vintage consumer boards carry them in large numbers.[256][378][303][142] The trade is made deliberately: one speaker board used what amounted to an entire reel of zero-ohm jumpers rather than pay for a double-sided PCB, because the manufacturer still judged the links cheaper.[LAbWsqS51Tk] The same reasoning scales up to heavy current, where a single-sided power board may carry bus-bar-sized links.[378]

Some designs avoid discrete links entirely by other means. A keyboard controller used screen-printed carbon overlaid on the copper, so the carbon runs served both as membrane contact pads and as the jumpers, which was cheaper than adding gold flash and individual link components — and cheaper still than moving to a double-sided board for layout reasons alone.[1108] An infrared remote used the same construction: ordinary copper traces under solder mask, with carbon printed on top making contact with exposed copper pads.[505]

## The craft of one-layer layout

Getting a design onto one layer with no links is treated as the "holy grail of PCB design".[353] It is a genuine skill rather than a formality: fitting an LED display, op-amps, trimmers and through-hole resistors onto one side is not a trivial task,[1171] and boards that achieve it with only a couple of links, or none, are noted as good work.[964][928][606] The difficulty is compounded when the mechanical designers dictate the outline, leaving too little board area to route what the cost engineer has insisted must be a single layer.[1171] Tilting a chip a few degrees to free up two more escape routes is one of the moves that results.[1171]

The constraint reaches into other details. Silkscreen designators are placed outside the component outlines so they remain readable once parts are fitted, which matters especially for a kit.[244] Some manufacturers silkscreen the entire copper pattern onto the component side, so the traces on the far face can be followed without turning the board over.[1562][g2kCBfUa9H0][502] Surface-mount parts can still be used: they are glued to the copper side, run through a wave soldering machine, and appear as wave-soldered SMD on what is otherwise a through-hole single-sided board.[1ngqB4mxZOI][443][1278][1243]

Modern multilayer practice inherits the discipline. Routing an SMD board is essentially a single-sided design problem, because surface-mount parts do not provide a via at every pin the way through-hole parts do; the goal is to get as many signals as possible onto the top layer and fill in power and the remainder on the other layers.[1323]

## Servicing and reverse engineering

A single-sided board is markedly easier to work on. All the copper and all the joints are visible from one face, so solder joints can be inspected directly[1301] and a circuit can be traced out without flipping between sides.[378][502] Reverse engineering is easier for the same reason: there are no traces running under components on the top side that would have to be exposed by desoldering parts.[1313] Repair of a consumer amplifier is simplified when the board turns out to be single-sided rather than double-sided.[1072]

It also introduces a characteristic fault mode. Because every ground crossing has to jump over other traces through a link, a single bad link can break a ground connection in a way that presents as a dry joint or a missing net, and tracing an unbuzzable ground pin back through the links is a recognised diagnostic path.[XUyjRm1Upjs][1394] The absence of plated-through holes also blocks a common modification technique: the vias do not go through to the other side, so a wire cannot simply be soldered to the back of a via, and hacking into a trace means drilling or scraping solder mask.[505]

## Single-sided load

A related but distinct property is single-sided *load* — all components populated on one face, whatever the number of copper layers. This is chosen for manufacturing cost, since only one placement and soldering pass is needed,[189][1447] and in professional equipment it is also chosen for access and serviceability, keeping the assembly reachable rather than sandwiched.[598] A comparison of two multimeters found the cheaper-to-construct arrangement was the single-sided load, with the more feature-packed instrument requiring double-sided load.[1447] Populating both faces also costs height: moving a phone design from two-sided to one-sided population removed roughly 30 to 40 percent of the board thickness, before counting the shielding that had to be doubled up as well.[243] Partial exceptions are common, such as an otherwise single-sided load with only the MOSFETs mounted on the reverse.[397]

## Historical and hobbyist significance

PCBs were expensive enough in the era of electronics magazines that essentially everything published was single-sided.[142] A double-sided project was hard to publish, because magazines printed PCB artwork for readers to photocopy and etch their own boards, which only works for one layer.[1619] Fitting a design into that constraint drove architecture: a RAM stack was the classic workaround for the difficulty of laying out memory on one layer.[142]

Dave Jones set single-sided construction as a hard design goal for a 32-channel PC-based logic analyser precisely so readers could build it themselves, and that constraint forced the choice of Lattice in-system-programmable devices to cut the pin count enough to fit; the finished board needed only a few jumper links.[747] A contemporaneous competing design used double-sided plated-through boards, a difference driven by cost — a tooling fee for a plated-through double-sided board ran to hundreds of dollars, against the five dollars such a board costs now.[1326] Kits still use single-sided boards to hold the price down.[464][353]

For one-off and low-volume work that calculus has now inverted. Unless a design is being built in the millions, there is no longer a large cost advantage in going single-sided, which frees the designer to use the bottom layer as a ground plane instead — even on a layout that could almost have been squeezed onto one side with about a dozen jumper links.[244] In production, though, the old rule holds: "single-sided is still cheaper in production".[LAbWsqS51Tk]
