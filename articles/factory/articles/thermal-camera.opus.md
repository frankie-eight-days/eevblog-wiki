# thermal camera

A thermal camera is an infrared imaging instrument that builds a temperature map of a scene rather than reporting a single number: functionally it is a large array of point thermometers, each pixel behaving like an individual infrared spot measurement, assembled into a picture.[403] For electronics work this converts thermal measurement from a serial, contact-based chore into a single glance — an overall thermal map of a product plus individual component temperatures, without attaching a thermocouple to anything.[622] It is a general-purpose diagnostic instrument, useful for locating faults, characterising thermal design, and verifying that a product behaves as advertised.[593][773]

## Resolution, sensor arrays and instrument classes

Detector array size is the dominant specification. A FLIR E60 with a 320 by 240 detector represented roughly $12,000 of handheld instrument and the top of that manufacturer's E-series at the time.[401][403] The E8 offers a 320 by 200 array.[622] The bench-mounted ETS320 combines a 320 by 240 screen with a stand that fixes the camera above a board, and can measure up to 260 °C.[989][1064] At the low end, the Lepton core used in cheaper products is 80 by 60.[669]

Resolution is not merely a matter of image quality; it determines whether a fault can be found at all. A single-spot infrared thermometer is inadequate for tracing a fault distributed across a plane, where the temperature is fairly evenly spread and detecting the anomaly is close to a needle in a haystack.[401] Conversely, resolving individual SMD components for design, manufacture, repair and rework is the specific capability that makes a high-resolution imager useful in electronics.[207]

A larger array does not automatically produce a visibly better image — a comparison between a high-end camera and a low-cost unit with a much smaller detector produced images that were not that different — so the array size alone is not the whole story.[403] Optics matter too: getting a usable reading on something as small as a via requires a good close-up macro lens, and without one a measurement of a 2.2 °C rise is not the most accurate.[543]

## Visual thermometers versus true thermal cameras

A distinct product class sits between the infrared spot thermometer and the true imager. The FLIR TG165 contains the same 80 by 60 Lepton core used in the smartphone-attachment cameras, but the temperature it reports does not come from that core at all — it is taken from a pyroelectric spot sensor behind the front window, laser-targeted, and the Lepton image serves only as a visual indication of where the spot is.[669] Because the reading is not derived from the array, there is no scale on the image and the core needs no calibration; the device looks like a thermal camera but functions as a spot thermometer.[669] Its advantage over a full camera is simplicity for users who only want to point and measure a temperature.[668]

The same distinction shows in the calibration hardware. A proper thermal camera carries a large internal shutter — an aluminium plate painted matte black with a temperature sensor embedded directly on it, so its uniform temperature is known precisely — whereas a cheaper instrument's shutter may be plastic with no sensor on it, which limits how good the calibration can be.[670]

Sensor and lens quality separates products more sharply than marketing does. In a side-by-side test against a FLIR i3 using a half-matte-black can of hot water, the Fluke VT02 resolved a human figure only as a blob, its performance judged the worst seen in a thermal camera.[O3LP2entyNQ][668] A secondary ergonomic complaint applies to that design: the screen sits at one angle and the sensor at another, forcing the user to mentally offset the aim point, whereas keeping the screen parallel to the sensor avoids the problem.[O3LP2entyNQ]

## Emissivity and reflection — the principal measurement traps

Thermal cameras measure emitted infrared, so the surface finish of the target governs the reading. Matte black gives an emissivity of about 0.95 or thereabouts, and the camera itself has to be configured with an emissivity figure to convert radiance to temperature; a typical working setting is 0.95.[O3LP2entyNQ][fpDnVSKfhew][1122] Bright shiny metal cans, clear anodised aluminium heat sinks and shiny solder pads all violate that assumption and produce readings that are simply wrong.[fpDnVSKfhew][1023][401] The standard workaround is a piece of black electrical tape on the surface of interest — not the best heat transfer, but it establishes a known emissivity.[fpDnVSKfhew][917] Where that is impractical, a thermocouple on the part is the better instrument.[1023]

Reflection is the second trap and the more deceptive one, because a reflected source appears as a genuine temperature difference. Shiny solder pads showing as a different temperature are reflecting, not running hot; a hand brought near shiny BNC connectors changes the apparent temperature of the metal; and overhead lighting can produce a reflection that has to be worked around by moving the camera.[401][3zAWgNjx5Ew] Anything nearby can reflect off a target, though cameras provide a reflected-temperature setting to compensate.[989] Knowing the reflective properties of what is being viewed is a prerequisite to trusting the image — it is described as "a real trap for young players".[401]

Absolute accuracy should be treated with suspicion in the absence of a calibration check, and the camera itself is generally not calibrated by its user.[403] Where a survey across a heated plate read 55.2, 55.3, 55.8 and 56.4 °C at different points, the spread was real and located the heater in one corner, but the underlying calibration remained unverified.[403] Neglecting emissivity means the number is indicative rather than exact.[811]

## Fault finding

The core diagnostic use is locating a component that is dissipating power it should not. On boards where a rail is drawing excessive current, a failed part with a low-impedance or latch-up fault shows a thermal difference against its neighbours, and the whole board can be surveyed before a probe is applied.[593] This replaces the traditional method of wetting the back of a finger and touching each device in turn — a method still used in parallel, and one that has the merit of working where the camera has no line of sight.[593][1527] Access is the practical constraint: probing a board in a chassis may require extender cards before either method is possible.[593]

The camera is equally valuable for negative results. On a LeCroy 9384C drawing roughly 11 A where 6 A was the maximum expected, a $12,000 imager found no hot spot anywhere on the board; the power was dissipated uniformly across four ASICs, and since a specific short could not be localised the board was judged unrepairable.[401] The same survey ruled out a suspected mounting hole and showed the connector wiring heating from current flow with no hot spot at the hole, contradicting a widely held theory about the fault.[401] On an HP 35670A, the survey found the op amps at data-sheet quiescent currents and the ASIC at about 55 °C — nothing scorching or out of the ordinary — which cleared them as suspects.[593]

Some faults are too small to resolve. A shorted SOT-23 hidden under a component produced nothing obvious on the image, even though significant current through it should have shown as a hot spot.[565] Conversely, gross failures are unmistakable: on a Raspberry Pi Compute Module CM4 board, the Ethernet chip sat at 70 °C and the DC-DC converter at over 80 °C, both too hot to touch, with the chip staying hot after power-off — while the processor drawing six watts barely got warm.[fpDnVSKfhew]

## Thermal characterisation and design verification

Because heat sinks are vastly more efficient with air flowing over them and are inefficient in still air, meaningful thermal performance must be measured with the lid on and all ducting in place.[622] The obstacle is that a closed case is opaque to infrared. Cling wrap solves this: it is both visually and thermally transparent, so a product can be sealed with it and imaged through the covering while retaining essentially correct airflow.[622][623] The penalty is a 4 to 5 degree drop in indicated temperature, which can be compensated; the heat map and its shape are not distorted, individual chips and energised relay coils remain visible, and with luck the airflow itself can be seen.[622] Not all visually transparent materials behave this way — transparency to visible light does not imply transparency in the infrared.[623]

Typical characterisation results include an 80 W LED light settling at 45 °C in the centre with the case bottom not much cooler, matching an advertised 20 °C rise above a roughly 24 °C ambient;[773] an LED strap light whose hottest parts were the metal strips between sections at 45 to 47 °C, with the anodised heat sink backing in the 40s;[1010] a bicycle light reaching 57 °C on the bench at 1500 lumens, acceptable because riding provides airflow;[gTTsvYbQOT4] a fast battery charger at 55 °C after a couple of minutes with the diodes hottest;[811] a PoE hat whose secondary-side output diode exceeded 110 °C at rated 12.5 W output;[1122] a boost converter whose inductor reached 100 °C dissipating 7.6 W;[1710] and a mains LED driver whose hot spot was its rectifier diode at 74 °C and still rising.[1253]

Component derating work uses the same technique. Ripple-current heating in electrolytic capacitors can be calculated, but empirical measurement on prototypes with a thermocouple and a thermal camera is the usual practice, and the comparison between one capacitor and ten paralleled units of the same total capacitance — the latter having far greater surface area — is directly visible as a temperature difference.[742] The result feeds a decision about whether the capacitors will be the reliability weak point.[742]

Where a device barely warms, the camera can be skipped; where a load test cannot reach the rated point because the available electronic loads top out below the required voltage, the thermal test cannot be performed at all.[1460][1263][1606]

## Interpretation thresholds

Human touch supplies the calibration for what matters. Around 60 °C and above the reflex is to pull the finger away, so anything that cannot be held is in that region; roughly 50 °C is the practical too-hot-to-touch boundary used as a design ceiling.[3zAWgNjx5Ew][1010] A differential is often more informative than an absolute: one heat sink running 3 or 4 degrees hotter than four identical ones alongside it points at the device on it having lower resistance than the others.[401] Similarly, cable runs sitting about 10 degrees above ambient under load, with the bends measurably warmer, is a normal and interpretable pattern rather than a fault.[31gkF0q9VfE]

## Applications beyond board-level debugging

Electrical installations are surveyed for hot joints — every junction is a potential failure point, and a scan every six months is a reasonable maintenance interval.[1651] Fixed wiring for high-current loads such as EV charging can be checked in situ at full charge rate.[EYx46kRv2Bw][31gkF0q9VfE] Photovoltaic arrays develop hot spots where a shaded cell, still carrying the series string current, is forced into reverse and dissipates power instead of generating it; these are detectable with a thermal camera on the panels and degrade individual cell life, with a broken internal connection taking the whole string out.[1426] Panel and connector inspection is limited in practice by physical access to the array.[I7wMkLJgnko]

Electronic keypad locks leak information thermally: pressing the buttons leaves a thermal signature that persists for a considerable time, and the order of pressing can be recovered from how quickly each mark fades, narrowing the code space substantially.[762][1509] The availability of phone-attachment thermal cameras makes this a practical attack in public environments.[1509]
