# current sensing

Current sensing is the measurement of current flowing in a circuit, almost always by converting it into a voltage that ordinary analogue circuitry can handle. The dominant technique passes the current through a low-value resistor — a shunt — and measures the voltage developed across it, which is then amplified and fed to a comparator, a control loop, or an ADC.[87][409][552] The alternative family of techniques senses the magnetic field the current produces, using a current transformer or a Hall effect device, and buys galvanic isolation at the cost of sensitivity and DC response.[769][1164] Current sensing is the foundation of constant-current regulation, current limiting, overload protection, energy metering and power profiling, and it appears in some form in nearly every piece of powered equipment.[200][273][354][409]

## The shunt resistor

The shunt is placed in series with the current path and the differential voltage across it is tapped off by two traces running to an amplifier.[861][946] The signal is inherently differential and floating at whatever potential the current path happens to sit at, so a differential amplifier across the sense resistor is the natural front end.[87] Shunt values in practice run from a few milliohms in high-current paths — 8 mΩ on a 100 W USB-C supply, 10 mΩ in a hot air station and in an audio amplifier's supply rail — up to 1 ohm or more where the current is small and the volt drop can be afforded.[1606][OvGdE5hC1Ro][1672][530]

Two constraints pull against each other. A larger resistance gives a larger, easier signal, but the shunt dissipates I²R and inserts a voltage drop into the very path being measured. Sizing therefore starts from the dissipation budget: for a 100 A metering application with a 2 W ceiling, rearranging P = I²R gives the shunt resistance as the allowable power divided by the square of the maximum current.[409] Physical package size follows the same logic — a motor current sense resistor is built in an older, larger style of surface-mount package rather than an 0805 or 1206 precisely because it must tolerate more dissipation.[330]

Where a single part cannot carry the power, resistors are paralleled. A 40 A switchmode supply uses twelve 0.1 ohm resistors in parallel as its output shunt, tapped off to the front panel current meter and to further circuitry.[272] A source measure unit parallels four 10 ohm power resistors to make a 2.5 ohm shunt.[607] An amplifier board uses two 20 mΩ resistors in parallel to form a 10 mΩ shunt.[1672] Battery packs commonly parallel two 10 milliohm resistors to reach 5 milliohms.[RasOXxxEhCk]

## Kelvin connection and sense taps

At low shunt values the resistance of the copper reaching the shunt becomes comparable to the shunt itself, so the sense connection must be separated from the current-carrying connection. This is a four-terminal, or Kelvin, arrangement: heavy conductors carry the current in and out, and separate light sense conductors tap the voltage at the resistor body.[543][731] A four-terminal shunt in a 7.5-digit multimeter takes the main current on heavy heat-shrunk wires and brings one sense wire off separately, the second sense connection being stitched down to the ground plane so the measurement is referred to ground.[731] The sense terminals are deliberately isolated from the surrounding ground pour on the top layer.[731]

The same discipline applies at the board level. On a multimeter's current path, the sense points are taken directly at each end of the 1 ohm resistor so that the resistor alone, and not the trace, forms the sense element; on the 100 ohm range the trace resistance is a negligible fraction of the total and the precaution is unnecessary.[929] Where PCB copper itself must carry heavy current, the traces are flooded with solder to lower their resistance, though the improvement is modest once the copper is already wide.[Lk2KRc6Bm4I] Power supplies with remote sensing run the sense wires alongside the main drive for both positive and negative outputs, snaking them the length of the chassis.[1402]

## High side and low side

A low-side shunt sits between the load and ground and is easy to amplify because the signal sits near ground potential, but it lifts the load's local ground above system ground by the drop across the shunt — around 0.1 V for a 1 amp design — which can make sharing grounds with an ADC awkward and may force a deliberate split between analogue and power grounds.[87] Low-side sensing is nonetheless widespread: a large portable battery system uses it for the main pack, and a USB-C supply places one shunt on the low side to ground alongside the positive-side connection.[1499][1606]

High-side sensing avoids disturbing the load's ground reference but requires an amplifier whose common-mode input range extends to the supply rail, typically a small dedicated part in a SOT-23 package sitting next to the shunt.[607][87] For low-side work, a general-purpose op amp whose common-mode input range includes ground is adequate for non-critical shunt measurement to a modest accuracy.[1436]

## Amplification, gain and ranging

The shunt voltage is small and almost always needs gain. Where a discrete instrumentation stage is built from op amps, the gain-setting resistors must be matched: matching a pair of 10k resistors by hand on a multimeter to within a couple of least significant digits gives far better common-mode rejection than the 1% tolerance they are marked at.[224] Gain also sets resolution — raising a microcurrent amplifier's gain from 200 to 500 makes one least significant bit of a 12-bit converter equal exactly one microamp, a more usable figure than 2.5 microamps.[238][259]

Dedicated current sense amplifiers remove much of this work. A zero-drift precision current sense IC such as the LTC6102 is used for MCU power measurement, with two devices fitted so that two current ranges can be covered.[269] Switching regulator controllers frequently integrate the function, bringing the shunt's differential voltage straight to dedicated current sense pins; a single-chip constant-current constant-voltage buck controller works this way.[1606] Power factor correction controllers carry both a current sense input and a separate zero current detect input, since the control scheme must know when current reaches zero.[273] Some microcontrollers provide the front end directly, with differential ADC input pairs and a programmable gain amplifier, which suits high-side or low-side current monitoring without external amplification.[87] Ranging can also be done by varying amplifier gain under digital control, as in a three-bit R-2R ladder DAC whose output sets the gain of an op amp in a combined current sense and temperature sense circuit.[501]

Resolution differences between product variants often come from the sensing chain rather than the shunt: an electronic load's A and non-A models, differing by a factor of ten in current resolution, appear to share the same current sense resistor, with the difference lying in the sense network or in software.[1023]

## Magnetic and isolated sensing

Where the current path cannot be broken or must stay isolated, the field around the conductor is sensed instead. A Hall effect current sensor sits over a wide trace and drives an op amp for gain; at 100 mV per amp it is intended for high-current applications and cannot resolve milliamps or microamps.[769] A clip-on toroidal probe for an oscilloscope contains the sensor and its amplifier and delivers a nominal 1 V per amp, effectively turning the scope into a clamp meter for waveform work.[1059]

Current transformers are the standard approach for mains metering. Direction and routing matter: if both the active and neutral conductors pass through the same core, their fields cancel and no useful signal results, so a transformer intended to measure a load's consumption must enclose only one of them.[1164] Equipment sometimes fits more than one transformer to obtain more than one measurement — one enclosing a single conductor for consumption measurement, feeding circuitry that routes back to the control board.[1164] Cost reduction shows up as omitted sensing: an EV charger measures current only on the neutral side and omits the core balance relay found in more complete designs.[1643] Because a transformer measures whatever net current passes through its window, redirecting a second generation source through a consumption transformer changes what the meter reports without touching the production measurements.[k2_mJtAeaog]

Fully isolated shunt modules occupy the ground between the two families. A part specified at 0.1% nominal accuracy and 300 kHz bandwidth, costing around 69 dollars, provides a differential output that can feed any sensor system and eliminates the design work associated with remote current sensing — expensive for a million-unit product, cheap for a one-off system build.[1410]

## Regulation, limiting and protection

Constant-current operation is built directly on a shunt. With a series pass transistor, the voltage drop across the shunt sense resistor is used to hold the current at a fixed value; when the control circuitry switches modes, the same pass element operates as a linear voltage regulator, which is why lithium-ion charger ICs are typically linear types.[200][176] Bench supplies use the same structure, with the output current sense resistor tapping off to an amplifier and thence to the series pass element.[861] Older regulator chips include current limit sensing internally, working against an external current sense resistor with the pass transistor mounted off-board.[803]

The topology of the output stage has consequences. Placing the sense resistor in the output path for constant-current control while also fitting a large output capacitance is a poor combination: the capacitor charges quickly on a voltage step, and when the current limit engages the charge stored in it can be dumped into the load in excess of the set limit. Output capacitance should be minimised in a constant-current supply for both reasons.[549]

Short-circuit protection in linear amplifiers uses the same principle without any amplifier at all: output current flows through 1 ohm emitter resistors, and when the voltage sensed across them rises far enough it turns on protection transistors that steal drive away from the pre-drivers.[354] Switching designs monitor current for overload and respond by reducing the commanded output to limit transient currents, alongside the protection inherent in the analogue design of the bridge.[1170] High-voltage supplies typically tap a 1 ohm resistor in series with the main switching element for the same purpose.[530]

Motor and stepper drives sense current to control it rather than merely to limit it. A stepper driver's current setting is checked by measuring a reference voltage at test points on the board — 0.6 V for one motor type, a different value for another — and an incorrect setting shows up as a stepped pattern in printed output.[274] A pump motor's shunt lets the firmware monitor how much current the motor is drawing.[330]

## Instrument current inputs

In multimeters the current shunt is the range element. Typical implementations use 100 ohms, 1 ohm and 10 milliohms for successively higher ranges, and these values set the burden voltage the meter inserts into the circuit under test.[929] The 10 A input has its own dedicated shunt.[1096] Input jack construction can itself be part of the sensing scheme: split current jacks are used to detect which terminal a lead has been inserted into and switch ranges automatically.[1096][832] The current sense jack circuit also carries its own protection and input resistor network — 5 megohm input resistors in one design — sitting behind MOVs and PTC devices.[1667]

Source measure units separate the two measurement paths physically, with a voltage sense channel on one part of the board and a current sense channel on another, each terminating in its own high-end ADC.[607] Beyond the measurement path itself, such instruments carry additional current sensing throughout for self-test purposes rather than for the specified output current.[607]

## Diagnostic and analytical uses

A shunt plus an oscilloscope is a general-purpose window into a device's behaviour. A resistor in series with the power line, with a scope across it, is the most basic tool for power line analysis; a 10 ohm resistor in series with a battery serves for probing an electronic lock.[762] Inserting a resistor into a USB power rail similarly yields a usable signal — at 100 mV per division a device drawing about 40 milliamps produces a clear trace, and many devices tolerate the added drop well enough to keep working while being profiled.[1006]

Sensing current also serves as simple presence detection: a resistor in a probe's supply line acts as a shunt, and the resulting voltage change indicates that a probe has been plugged in and is drawing current, with different probe types drawing different amounts.[966] Automated test fixtures fit a current sense resistor and a small current sense amplifier so that board current becomes one of the measured parameters of the test.[552]

## Identifying current sensing in hardware

A low-value power resistor with two fine traces leaving it toward a small amplifier is the standard visual signature of a shunt, and the arrangement recurs across power supplies, single-board computer expansion rails, battery packs and monitors.[861][946][RasOXxxEhCk][1215] Large power resistors on heat sinks alongside pass transistors indicate current measurement rather than dissipation duty in an electronic load, since a genuine load would not need such resistors for measuring current.[1434] Even a fitted zero ohm jumper in a footprint sized for a large power resistor suggests a current sense provision left unpopulated.[735] In schematics the same elements appear as a resistor with a tap into the control loop — a potentiometer forming part of the current sense circuit, for instance.[649]
