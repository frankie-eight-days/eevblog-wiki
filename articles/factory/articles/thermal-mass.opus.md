# thermal mass

Thermal mass is the quantity of heat a physical object stores for a given rise in temperature, and it governs almost every thermal transient encountered on the bench. A large mass resists being heated and, once hot, resists cooling; a small one changes temperature almost as fast as its surroundings.[972][VVOi2dbtxC0] In electronics work it matters most in soldering and rework, where the mass of the pad, the copper attached to it, the component body and the board itself sets how much heat must be delivered and how fast.[415][542][1322]

## Where the mass lives on a board

The heat sunk out of a joint comes from the whole thermally connected structure, not just the pad. Large ground planes, heavy copper pours and big wire-termination pads pull heat away from the tip and slow the joint to melting point.[1064][711] A power package with a thermal pad on its underside is a worse case than a plain small-outline part: the ground plane extends across the top of the board and continues on the bottom, so the pad and the leads have to be treated as two separate thermal problems, sometimes by cutting the leads and dealing with the thermal pad on its own.[1322] Multilayer boards are worse still, because the pad ties into internal copper as well.[1322] A two-layer board with only a pin or two touching a small area of plane presents little mass and can be reworked with hot air without a preheater.[688]

Board mass also shows up in processes other than hand soldering. In reflow, the profile depends on the number of thermal reliefs, the amount of copper and the mass of the parts themselves, and varies enough between boards and ovens that it has to be found by experiment.[415] With a hot air gun, the board underneath has to come up to temperature before the joints will flow, which takes time.[415][808] Assembly lines carry the same spread: boards leaving an SMT process may mix small parts with large thermal mass items such as heavy connectors.[684]

Very large assemblies are the limiting case. A thick multilayer substrate carrying a ceramic module is about as much mass as a printed circuit board ever presents, and it heats slowly enough that sitting it directly on a hot plate rather than above it changes only the time taken, not the temperature reached.[OZb-mB3X-30] This is what bottom-side preheaters exist for: bringing large-thermal-mass boards and BGAs up to an intermediate temperature, for instance 200 °C, so the hot air gun only has to supply the remainder.[OZb-mB3X-30]

## Tip mass and soldering iron performance

The tip of a soldering iron is a thermal reservoir. The initial surge of heat into a joint comes from heat already stored in the tip, not from the element; only once the tip has cooled does the sensor register the drop and call for power.[1065] A thicker, larger tip stores more and is the better choice for anything but fine work.[1065] Manufacturers sell this difference directly: high-performance and ultra-performance tips differ from standard ones only by extra metal inside, visible as a physical bulge, and they retain heat so the temperature dips less when the tip is applied to a joint.[1106][1646] Tip mass is not the same thing as element power, and the two should not be conflated.[1106]

Construction affects how quickly stored heat is replenished. Where the element is a separate part making imperfect contact with the tip, the extra thermal resistance introduces lag between the element heating and the tip getting hot — unhelpful when a large ground plane or similar mass is drawing heat away.[1064]

Every iron dips in temperature under load; the question is how far and for how long.[1063][1064] An iron with very little tip mass drops severely, and takes a long time to recover.[1063] At the extreme, a very thin fine-point tip has effectively no thermal mass, which combined with a low power budget of a few watts rules out any substantial component and forces a set temperature as high as 450 °C simply to overcome the drop that will certainly occur.[1113] A calibrator touched by a tip in free air reads the no-load, zero-thermal-mass temperature; the reading falls as soon as the tip meets a joint.[znYVJGfHj9Q] The same absence of load explains slow cooling in setback mode, where there is nothing for the tip to dump heat into.[1646]

The practical consequence is that iron selection is a match to the work. A low-capacity portable unit can be entirely adequate for small, low-thermal-mass work such as mobile phone boards while being unsuitable for heavy gauge joints.[5vbg8QEZXfY][1319] Soldering to a heat sink clip is a mild case; soldering to a massive copper wire or block is a different problem entirely, and a low-thermal-mass junction masks the difference between stations that would otherwise be obvious.[596] The usual response to a known high-mass joint is to raise the set temperature somewhat — moving from around 310 °C to 330 °C for a part with a large thermal pad underneath, for example — rather than to wait longer at a temperature that will never get there.[434] Where the tip is simply too small for the mass involved, no temperature setting fixes it.[1306]

## Hot air, desoldering and low-melt alloys

Hot air stations are sized to the mass they must heat. A low-capacity hobbyist unit suits small boards and simple low-thermal-mass components, while a high-airflow unit is needed for larger work; the difference only becomes visible on genuinely massive parts, since on a moderate chip two stations at the same temperature and indicated airflow can take a similar time.[1058][1659] Because temperature and airflow requirements track the mass of the specific part, production rework benefits from stored presets chosen per component.[1058]

For desoldering, the correct temperature depends on the mass of the pads, the mass of the components — transformers, large inductors, relays — and the thermal recovery of the gun and nozzle together.[542] BGA memory devices with little mass attached can be reflowed in place without a preheater underneath.[781]

Low-melting-point alloys work by keeping a joint molten long enough to lift a part. The amount of mass held molten determines how long the working window lasts and how hard it is to sustain: a small quad flat pack is much easier than a large one, and on a large device the solder can remain liquid for a noticeable time after heating stops.[437]

## Thermal mass used deliberately

Mass is often added on purpose. Thermocouple cold-junction compensation depends on holding both junctions at one temperature, and a design can achieve this isothermal block with large terminal studs and large pads that raise the thermal mass of the whole junction assembly.[419] Thermoelectric generators require one side of the module to be anchored: without a heat sink providing mass on one face, both plates equalise and the output collapses, so the heat sink effectively pins one plate to ambient because it takes considerable energy to warm it.[664] A diecast alloy shell can be chosen for thermal mass reasons in an instrument.[989] Extra solid metal is sometimes added inside a module purely to supply mass alongside a heat sink path.[745] Solder deliberately left on an exposed pad increases the mass and lowers the thermal resistance through to the pad on the opposite side of the board.[463]

Mass is also exploited for measurement fidelity. Instrumenting a reflow oven with a reference board of the same thermal mass as the work makes an independent meter track the controller's reading closely; a thermocouple left in free air, with no mass attached to it, reads differently from one bonded to the board.[558]

## Consequences outside soldering

Thermal mass sets the dynamics of any temperature control loop. An oven takes a long time to heat because of its mass, and that delay lets the integral term of a PID controller accumulate a large value and drive the system past its set point.[VVOi2dbtxC0] Conversely, a bare chip in a moving airstream has so little mass that its temperature tracks the chamber essentially in real time.[972] Buildings behave like the oven: concrete and steel retain heat, so a lab that peaks at 28–29 °C on a hot day only falls to about 25 °C overnight.[k7FlF0ON1VM]

Stored heat can be the point of a system rather than a nuisance. A hot water tank holds usable energy in the thermal mass of the water, which survives a power failure for potentially days and allows the heating element to run at low peak power — a heat pump unit drawing 1 kW for a few hours to charge a 315 L tank, instead of the large instantaneous draw of an instantaneous heater.[pXtSybs9QRs]

## Manufacturing artefacts

Differences in thermal mass leave visible marks on finished boards. Hot-air-levelled tin builds up unevenly over large copper areas, so large thermal masses show globs and an uneven surface while narrow traces come out comparatively smooth.[557] Wave or flow soldering can fail selectively on the higher-mass joints: on a board where most parts wetted correctly, the heavier items showed no flow-through at all, a latent defect that sat in the instrument for decades.[804] The same principle applies to how much copper a joint has behind it — a four-terminal current shunt with heavy traces and vias attached carries substantial mass at the joint.[472]

Component packaging carries the same logic. A regulator supplied with a thin tab has less thermal mass than a properly built device with a normal-thickness tab, and thinning the tab is a real reduction in the part's ability to handle heat.[512]
