# clock signal

A clock signal is a periodic digital waveform that gives a system a shared time reference, against which data is launched, sampled and latched.[1249][9hMsNOwY5AQ] It swings between the normal logic levels of the family in use — 5 V and 0 V for a 5 V TTL system, 3.3 V and 0 V for a 3.3 V one[1249] — and its defining characteristic on an instrument is that it is always transitioning, which is what makes it immediately identifiable among the other lines on a board.[1249][9hMsNOwY5AQ] Because almost every synchronous digital device depends on it absolutely, a missing or degraded clock is one of the most productive things to look for first when a board is dead.[1205][240][1322]

## Role in synchronous systems

In a synchronous interface the clock is the reference to which everything else is correlated; a data line has no meaning without it.[1249][9hMsNOwY5AQ] A bit-banged transmission is typically a tight loop of bit sets and bit clears with a clock edge between each bit, and without that clock the slave device receives no commands at all.[9hMsNOwY5AQ] Which edge acts is a property of the part: a counter may advance only when the clock goes negative and do nothing at all on the positive transition[1208], while a 74HC164 eight-bit serial shift register moves the data present at its input into Q0 on the positive-going edge and cascades each stage onward on the next.[1531]

The clock also defines the timing budget that the receiving device imposes. Data sheet switching waveforms specify a setup time, tSU, that data must be stable before the clock transition; violating it can drive the chip metastable, leave data unlatched and produce arbitrary misbehaviour throughout a design.[1249] Timing diagrams conventionally carry a clock along the top precisely so that the rest of the drawing is anchored in real time — drawn properly, the presence of the clock implies that the horizontal axis is to scale, and a diagram that breaks the clock with dashed lines or an S-shaped break is signalling that some interval has been elided.[1249]

## Buses that carry a clock

Serial buses are normally described by their clock alongside data: I2C carries clock and data lines[8P8Af5SR57U], I2S carries data and clock lines typically fed through series resistors[1322], and SPI adds a chip select to clock and data.[290][879] Some decoders offer a timeout mode that infers framing from idle time, so clock and data alone can be captured on two channels without the chip select.[CMoBGGqojqs] Debug and programming interfaces follow the same pattern, with a clock pin and a data pin into the microcontroller.[1522]

LED strips and display drivers often add a latch to the clock and data pair, the latch defining when a shifted-in packet is transferred.[227][1228] With a scope triggered on the latch rather than the clock, several packets of clock and data can be seen going past while only the one framed by the latch going low is accepted by a given chip.[1228] Where the latch is absent the image marches down the strip instead of appearing at once.[227] A shift register chain of this kind may present a register clock, a shift clock and a clear; the clear is optional, since zeros can simply be shifted in.[1365]

## Where clocks come from

Most microcontrollers derive their clock from a crystal across X1 and X2, with an internal inverter between the two pins. This makes X1 a digital input, so an external oscillator or any other suitable signal generated elsewhere in the circuit can be driven straight into it; X2 typically feeds the internal circuitry and can also be used to drive external logic.[1614] The resulting system clock is often a division of the crystal: a machine running from a 9 MHz crystal was measured at 3.266 MHz, a divide by three.[382]

Dedicated timing chips also exist for this purpose. The Tandy 1000 used an 8245A timing chip designed specifically to produce the clock signals an 8088 processor requires, and it contained both an unused internal oscillator and a switch to select between that and an external clock.[32] Programmable logic supplies clocks too — an FPGA on a spectrum analyser generates the clocks and control signals that drive the surrounding hardware[391], and an FPGA's flip-flop-and-clock architecture is a large part of what distinguishes it from a CPLD.[111] A microcontroller can be used as a clock source in its own right, generating clocks and PWM signals — for example, driving a charge-pump voltage doubler that turns a 3 V input into 6 V out.[473] Instruments and lab boards frequently provide clocks as well: a mixed-signal instrument can synthesise a digital pattern with defined duty cycle, frequency and idle time[692], a logic trainer offers a clock switchable from 1 Hz through 1 kHz to 100 kHz, plus a negative-going clock for dual polarity work[1463], and manual single-stepping through a debounced pushbutton lets a counter be advanced by hand.[1720] A pattern generator loaded with 10101010 is functionally a clock output.[qE-SwtYQM6E]

## Finding and identifying a clock on the bench

A clock is recognised on a scope by continuous periodic transitions where other lines are static or data-like.[1249][9hMsNOwY5AQ][1144] The practical procedure on an unfamiliar board is to probe the accessible test points or header pins and confirm the expected activity: a clock line, and clocked data alongside it.[593][1205] Where a signal is present but not identifiable, adjacent behaviour settles it — one side of a series resistor showing a clean periodic waveform at 3.072 MHz next to a neighbour whose content changes on every acquisition identifies the pair as clock and data.[1322][1394] On a mixed-signal instrument it is worth spending a few minutes labelling channels, including labelling the clock as such, so that captures remain interpretable.[143]

Whether a clock is running at all is diagnostic on its own. A calculator board showing 10.1 kHz and roughly 203 kHz of clock activity is at least powered and attempting to work.[663] Conversely, an absent clock localises a fault immediately: a DAC producing no output was traced to pin four, the clock line, having nothing on it.[240] Clock behaviour can also be state-dependent — an idle machine issued a 400 microsecond burst of clock every 10 milliseconds and only ran a continuous clock once a program was executing, with a further deliberate 24 microsecond dead period inside the continuous run.[382] A clock that cannot be measured is not always a clock fault: a microcontroller with no supply voltage reaching it will show no clock, and the correct conclusion is a power problem rather than a dead micro.[1520]

## Faults on the clock line

Because the clock is the one line whose absence stops everything, it warrants direct confirmation at the chip pin rather than at a convenient nearby point. In a case where data was demonstrably entering a serial chip and nothing was emerging, the clock proved to be reaching the pad but not the chip itself: pin three of a reworked part had a solder joint that made contact under probe pressure but not otherwise, and the signal disappeared when the probe touched lightly without added force.[689] The lesson is that the clock pin is worth double- and triple-checking before diving into protocol analysis, which in that case would have cost considerable time and found nothing wrong.[689]

Clock quality is a separate question from clock presence. A 25 MHz clock on a repaired NAS was captured with a low-inductance probe and shown to be badly degraded — attributed to deterioration of the output driver over time — yet the system still worked.[1288] The same tension appeared with an oscilloscope reference clock whose spectrum, viewed on a spectrum analyser with a probe held near the single-ended clock output pin, showed spurious components at a 67.5 kHz offset that a proper clock would not have; the considered position is that this is "a pretty awful clock" that ought to be fixed on principle, while conceding that if no measurable difference appears in the instrument's performance the practical impact is academic.[699]

Probing the clock is itself a source of artefacts. Long ground leads act as antennas and produce overshoot and ringing that is not in the circuit[1205][1322], while genuine ringing, undershoot and ground-bounce glitches on a clock edge only become believable when high-fidelity probing technique is used.[1081][689] Coupling from the clock is also a common explanation for glitches seen on neighbouring lines during a poorly-grounded capture.[689]

## Layout and emissions

Clock routing is one of the constraint-driven parts of PCB layout. On a bus, all the clock lines must reach every chip in the chain, which in practice means planning a clear routing channel and dropping vias rather than letting other traces consume the space.[974] Length matching is applied to a clock together with the data lines it accompanies — for example nine matched traces for eight data lines plus the clock — and excessive via-hopping between layers within such a group is poor practice.[1323] Routing a clock too far from the registers it drives, or through additional gates and indirect PCB paths, is where propagation delay begins to violate timing constraints.[1247] Where a single clock line has to drive a heavy load, adding a buffer stage — a buffer chip, or two inverters in series — offloads the driving source.[1365]

For emissions, the fundamental clock rate is not the figure that matters. A computer running a 6 MHz main clock is not fast by modern standards, but radiated emissions are governed by the edge rate of the signal rather than its repetition frequency; even a 1 Hz clock can radiate strongly if its edges are fast enough.[1176] Near-field magnetic probes pick up the loop currents from clocks and other signals circulating across the whole board and back to the supply, not merely the field around the crystal.[694] Bypass capacitor removal elsewhere on a board can visibly upset the clock at the point being probed, which may be exactly the effect of interest or may be unwanted contamination depending on the measurement.[1081]

## Schematic and symbol practice

Clock pins benefit from deliberate placement in schematic symbols. Differential clock pins should be repositioned so that clock positive can run off to a net and clock negative straight down to ground without a wire crossing the whole symbol.[1129] More generally, arranging a serial part's pins as data in, data clock and data out produces a compact symbol from which clock and data wires flow naturally to the neighbouring components, saving effort later and producing a more presentable drawing.[DNlA4X5_S30]
