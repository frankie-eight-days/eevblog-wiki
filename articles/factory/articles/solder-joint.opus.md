# solder joint

A solder joint is the fused metal connection between a component lead or termination and a printed circuit board pad, and it is simultaneously an electrical path and a structural one. Solder's primary job is to be a mechanical connection — a solid mechanical connection for components — and construction techniques such as right-angled board-to-board assemblies rely on the rigidity a joint provides rather than on any separate fastener.[1263][397] Because the same small volume of alloy carries both the current and the load, a joint that is mechanically compromised is an electrical fault, and the mechanical failure mode is the more insidious of the two: it is intermittent, temperature-dependent, and can lie dormant for decades before it surfaces.[555][804]

## Anatomy of a good joint

A good-quality joint made with standard leaded solder is bright, shiny, smooth, and drawn into a clean concave fillet where the lead meets the pad.[183][186] The same criterion applies regardless of package: through-hole pins, chip resistors, SO and QFP leads, and a wire seated into a solder cup are all judged by the same shiny surface and even fillet.[183][186] A joint that has been correctly executed uses a surprisingly small quantity of solder — close to the minimum needed to form the fillet — and well-made assembly work shows a perfect amount of solder and perfect fillets across every passive on the board.[183][472]

The failure of that standard is the cold joint. It appears as a ball or a blob rather than a fillet, with a craggy, messy, dull surface, and typically has not wetted or flowed through to the far side of the board.[183][aQ2AVLs8_7k] Excess solder produces the same visual signature — a big fat blob with no visible fillet — and is "a dead giveaway for amateurs".[183] On a plated-through hole, an absent fillet on the underside means current must travel through the thin plated walls and around the edge of the pad rather than through a proper connection, which is a defect even when the joint appears to work.[U_QoWa6qXeo]

The shiny-joint criterion is specific to leaded alloys. In a lead-free process, every joint looks frosted under the same inspection conditions that would demand a perfectly mirror finish from leaded work, so the traditional visual standard cannot be carried across; lead-free and leaded work are different types of soldering, and tools are kept segregated between the two stations to avoid cross-contamination.[1041]

## Making the joint

The iron tip must contact the pad and the lead at the same time, with the chisel face flat against the board for maximum contact; a joint that refuses to heat is usually a joint the tip is not sitting flat on.[183][aQ2AVLs8_7k] Solder is fed onto the far side of the joint, not onto the tip: solder placed on the tip and carried across burns off its flux on the way, producing a cold joint.[183][1205] Feeding a consistent amount onto each joint, and leaving the iron on the joint for a moment before starting to feed, gives repeatable fillets.[1205]

Solder diameter is the dominant control variable. The biggest mistake beginners make is feeding too much solder onto the joint, and thick 1 mm wire makes the quantity very difficult to control; 0.46 mm is a working default, with anything at or below 0.5 mm (0.02 in) recommended, and heavier wire kept on hand only for large joints.[180][997] Fine solder is what makes controlled surface-mount work possible at all, and it is a large part of why some people's joints look better than others'.[997][186] Flux is the other half — a flux pen applied before the iron — and additional liquid or gel flux helps on stubborn or large-mass joints.[997][183][433]

Thermal mass, not nominal iron temperature, sets the difficulty. A large component or a pad tied directly into a ground plane sinks heat away and takes several seconds to come up, sometimes requiring solder fed toward the iron to establish thermal contact.[183] Iron capability shows up directly in the dial setting needed: a low-capacity station has to be run at 400 °C to flow a heavy joint properly and quickly, where a high-capacity unit does the same joint at 270 °C.[596] Joints on high-thermal-mass parts stay hot well after the iron is removed.[183]

For surface mount, the dab method — solder carried on the tip and touched to each lead in turn — applies no lateral force to the pins and deposits close to the right amount for a fillet on each one.[997] Drag soldering across a row of fine-pitch leads works on packages down to quad flat packs, sometimes requiring two passes.[186] At 0.4 mm pitch, inspection gear is not optional: the work cannot be done reliably without viewing it afterwards.[408] A jeweller's loupe or inspection microscope is used to confirm the fillets and to check for shorts and solder splash between pads.[186] Where joints are uncertain, systematically reheating them will pull solder further up the lead.[1306]

## Through-hole wetting and lead trimming

On through-hole work, the solder must wet all the way around the lead and, with an adequately heated joint, will wick through to the top side of the board even from a small feed of 0.46 mm wire.[183] Checking the opposite side is part of the process, because heat applied from one side may wet only that side.[183] Absent flow-through is a recognised long-term defect: joints on aged equipment that never had adequate solder flow-through can crack completely decades later, and reheating such a joint may reveal that the original solder never flowed through the via at all.[804][1572]

Trimming leads is where an otherwise sound joint is destroyed. Cutters should be brought in just above the surface of the joint rather than down onto it, angled slightly, and never used to shear several leads at once, because the shock puts stress on the joint and cracks it.[183][546] Cutting into the fillet works in practice and is sometimes unavoidable — boards are occasionally ground flat for enclosure clearance — but it is not best practice, because it introduces stress fractures that cannot be counted on to stay benign.[555] Non-flush cutters exist for exactly this reason, though flush cutters plus a deliberate standoff distance achieve the same end.[CLD5NsCJdpQ] Similarly, through-hole pins left protruding under a part that must sit flat will crack their joints when the part is forced down.[1241]

## Mechanical and thermal failure

Solder is brittle enough that some ultra-high-reliability industries avoid it entirely, using press-fit connectors instead, precisely because solder joints can crack under stress and vibration.[517] On an electrodynamic shaker a TO-220 regulator can be sheared completely off its solder joints — an accelerated version of a failure that otherwise plays out over months.[1442] Connectors are the usual stress concentrators: repeated insertion loads USB ports' joints heavily, a spring-loaded socket puts continuing stress on its joints, and a mechanically loaded connector that can be felt wobbling is a strong indication of a failed joint.[1137][234][1727] Good practice removes the load before it reaches the metal — anchoring wires with tape so no flexion or vibration reaches the joint and causes fatigue.[395] Board handling matters too: a board should be flat against the bench when chips are reseated so it is not flexed and its joints not cracked.[536]

Thermal cycling is the other major mechanism. A classic thermal fault is a dry joint that opens as the component heats and expands, most often on large through-hole power components; over thirty-five years of powering up and down, expansion and contraction produce a micro-crack in the joint.[1452][1301] Cracked joints around parts that get hot are a common enough failure mode that visual inspection of the joints is a standard early step on any heat-cycled supply, and equally a reason to suspect microcracks on thermally active parts even when the rails all measure correctly.[1726][1322] Failed joints of this kind have been found on power-amplifier pins that cracked over years of heating and cooling, on an output choke, and on an AC mains connection where the crack produced audible arcing to the board.[1243][1091][1164] Sufficient overheating simply melts joints outright, at which point components such as diodes fall off under gravity.[1511]

## Diagnosis

Visual inspection is the first tool and the least trustworthy. Light and shadow at particular angles will make a perfectly sound joint look dry, and a joint that looks poor on a high-definition image is very often fine; conversely, some joints cannot be inspected at all until the board is removed, so the initial evidence has to come from measurement.[e98cYNPUxcg][804] A 3D stereo microscope, which allows the head to move and view the joint from varying angles, resolves far more about a joint than a flat digital image at high magnification.[e98cYNPUxcg][9s9LXOBknck] Where doubt remains after inspection, reflowing the suspect joint as a matter of course is the cheap resolution.[e98cYNPUxcg][1301]

Physical provocation supplements inspection: wiggling suspect joints, flexing the board, and applying local pressure while watching a measurement can expose an intermittent, and freezer spray or deliberate heating is used to force thermally-dependent joints to show themselves.[1091][398][565] A fault that reproduces on touch but resets identically on every power cycle is electronic rather than a mechanical joint fault — capacitive coupling from a finger, not a cracked connection.[689]

Probing technique can itself hide a bad joint. Where a lead is not properly soldered, probing the pad shows the signal and probing the top of the pin does not; pressing hard enough on the pin makes contact and restores the signal, disguising the defect.[689] Probe pressure must be enough to penetrate the oxide coating on the joint, which is why sharp probes matter for low-resistance measurements and why sprung probe holders are weighted to sink slightly into the joint.[230][GS0WqUKZ-3c] Thermal imaging has its own trap: shiny joints and metal cans reflect heat, producing hot spots that are reflections rather than dissipation.[401]

## Production processes

In volume manufacturing, joint quality is a yield metric. Moisture-sensitive parts that are not baked before placement cause a range of solder joint problems that ruin yield.[346] The reflow profile matters on both slopes — cooling too slowly lets the solder sit in its plastic region and produces dry joints — and heavy copper aggravates it further.[1216] Individual joints that fail to reflow are traceable to a large thermal mass on the pad reaching insufficient temperature, or to the flux in the paste failing to clean the joint properly.[588] Automated optical inspection examines the individual solder joints alongside component identification and lead-bank short detection.[684]

Process leaves signatures. Solder dragged up at the end of a pad indicates a hand drag-soldered job rather than reflow, and thieving pads on the larger terminations indicate reflow soldering.[582][1452] Boards frequently show a mixture, with hand rework after wave soldering visible in the finish.[574][1702] Alloy composition also determines long-term robustness: in one oil-filled system the composition of the solder on the joints made them brittle enough that chips could be pushed off by hand without heat.[2G7Z9IIQoIs]

Bulk reflow is sometimes attempted as a repair on suspected cracked BGA joints, using a toaster oven at around 230 °C or hot air over a specific device, on the reasoning that any bad joints will reflow. The technique sometimes changes the symptoms but is unreliable as a fix.[781][1154]

## Electrical properties

A solder joint is not a zero-resistance connection. Joints have resistance, as do PCB traces and component leads, and all of them dissipate I²R losses when current passes through.[1401] In high-power applications those connection losses — lead resistance plus joint resistance — become a real heating contribution alongside a capacitor's internal ESR, which is why very large capacitors use bolted lugs instead of leads.[742] The joint is also a defined point on the thermal path: LED thermal calculations use the thermal resistance from die to solder junction point, taken from the datasheet, as the first leg between the die and the heatsink fins.[361]
