# solder mask

Solder mask is the coloured polymer coating applied over the copper of a printed circuit board, covering everything except the pads, vias and features that are deliberately left exposed. Its two jobs are to stop the copper oxidising and, more importantly in modern assembly, to stop solder bridging between adjacent pins.[1353] Solder does not wet it: solder applied to a masked area balls up and falls off, held only weakly by flux residue, and repeated attempts merely blacken the surface.[1353][186] That single property is what makes fine-pitch surface-mount assembly practical at all.[186]

## Function during soldering

Because solder will only flow onto the exposed pad metal, a sliver of mask running between the pins of an IC confines each joint to its own pad. This is what allows a 0.5 mm pitch part to be drag-soldered with a chisel tip several millimetres wide: surface tension keeps the solder on the iron or on the pad, and it has no interest in the material in between.[186] The same mechanism cleans up excess solder paste — paste that appears to be smeared across the gap between pads pulls back into the pad wells during reflow rather than shorting.[415][346] In a reflow run the result is visible directly: no bridges anywhere along a fine-pitch package, with every pin individually wetted.[415] Where the mask is present, wicking the excess off a drag-soldered row is largely automatic.[510][437]

Where it is absent, the protection is absent with it. Boards laid out with traces routed between pins so tightly that no mask can fit will short during hand or wave soldering, and getting the paste volume wrong on such a footprint shorts the pins outright.[832][127] On very fine pitch work — a 0.4 mm QFN, for example — the absence of mask between pins is the difference between a routine job and a ruined one.[408]

## Solder mask expansion and minimum sliver width

Solder mask expansion is the amount by which the mask opening is pulled back from the copper pad, and it is the parameter most worth checking before a design is released. The failure it guards against is a sliver of mask so thin that it simply does not survive manufacture and breaks away, leaving bare gaps between pads.[127][193] The practical floor is around four or five thou of mask between adjacent pads; below that, down at a couple of thou, a fabricator will not produce it reliably — the same limitation that applies to minimum trace width on the copper layers.[244][127][1327] An expansion of 0.05 mm, or two thou, is already very small.[193] On a dense board the mask rules can collide with the copper rules: minimum pad-to-pad clearances of 0.127 mm on BGA fanouts leave no room for a manufacturable mask sliver, and the resulting conflict is not obvious from the copper geometry alone.[1327]

Registration matters as much as width. On a received prototype the mask openings should sit centred on the pads without overhanging them, and checking that alignment, along with via tenting, is a standard incoming inspection step.[1306][990] Dave Jones treats the 3D render of the bare board — mask, pad expansion, copper and silkscreen, with component models switched off — as the final visual check before Gerbers go out, and rates it above 3D component viewing as a reason to have the feature at all.[111][254][990][754] A footprint whose pad geometry would have broken the mask sliver is worth narrowing before release rather than after.[244]

## Deliberate shorts and deliberate removal

Pins that appear shorted on a finished board are frequently supposed to be. The diagnostic signature is the absence of mask between the pads combined with a solder bridge sitting almost exactly at the centre of the pad pair — a designer who intended a connection removed the mask and let the wave or the reflow join them.[1353][1460][1322][832] Mask removed on a pad-by-pad basis leaves characteristic half-moon shapes following the pad outline; a hand-drawn rectangle of removed mask across several pads is the other common appearance.[1353] Consistent with this, the deliberate removal is done because the alternative — solder mask between pads — is what a designer should normally use wherever pitch allows.[1353]

Mask is removed intentionally for a long list of reasons:

- **Current handling.** Leaving the mask off a power trace lets the wave solder or hot-air levelling process deposit solder onto the copper, lowering its resistance and raising its current capacity.[317][1559][811][1481][1393] This is effectively free, which is why it has been used for decades, and measured reductions in trace resistance are real but spread over a wide range; a thin coating achieves very little, so the technique cannot be relied on quantitatively.[317] It is the cheap alternative to specifying 2 oz copper.[811] The exposure must be designed in, because a standard board is solder mask over bare copper — SMOBC — with no plating under the mask at all, so scraping the mask off a finished trace reveals raw copper rather than a coating.[1216][1559][1710]
- **Heat transfer.** An exposed copper area on the reverse of a thermal pad, fed by thermal vias, allows a large chisel tip to heat the part from underneath so solder flows through the vias and bonds the thermal pad.[434][1322] The same exposure lets the board's own ground plane act as a heatsink, or lets a bottom-side pad contact an external metal bar or heatsink.[1347][744][533] Removing the mask in stripes rather than over the whole pad is a compromise that mostly defeats the purpose.[463]
- **Guarding and leakage control.** Around high-impedance nodes the mask is removed from the guard trace so that surface contamination leaks into the guard reference rather than into the sensitive node.[478][607][1564][1334][1576] The guard is not necessarily at ground potential — it sits at the reference point of the circuit being protected.[478]
- **Spark gaps.** A PCB spark gap only works if the mask is opened over both electrodes, because the mask is a good enough insulator to prevent the arc entirely; a gap left covered will break down somewhere else instead.[678][1695][1726]
- **RF shielding contact.** Bare, often gold-plated strips along board edges or between shielded blocks make contact with a metal can or the chassis.[673][810][913][1101]
- **Test and marking.** Untinted, mask-free vias serve as bed-of-nails test points and can be labelled on the silkscreen.[326] A small mask-free patch next to each board in a test panel gives the operator somewhere to mark a failure with a pen.[552]
- **Fiducials.** A fiducial is copper with the mask pulled back, and the pull-back is what provides the contrast the placement machine's camera needs against the surrounding mask.[127]
- **Appearance.** Removing mask in decorative patterns — flowers, logos, exposed traces for effect — costs nothing extra at the fabricator, because the mask is applied as one process regardless of the pattern it carries.[1229][425]

## Insulation and creepage

Solder mask is an excellent insulator and permits closer conductor spacing than bare board would.[1222] Where a bare-board separation would demand much more, the required clearance can fall to 0.13 mm once mask is added — but the exception is any exposed metal, such as connector pads, where the mask offers nothing and the original clearance applies.[1222] For a 150 V design on a masked board, roughly 15 thou of clearance is typical.[974] The insulation is not unlimited: on a high-voltage meter input lacking an isolation slot, an arc punched straight through a thin mask layer and tracked across the surface.[1734] Mask over a trace is also thermally insulating, which slightly raises the temperature a current-carrying trace reaches — one of several second-order terms usually left out of trace-width calculators.[1559]

## Manufacture and options

The mask is applied over the full copper layer and then imaged. In the photoimageable process the coated panel is UV-exposed through a film and developed, removing mask from the pads and other openings; the result is sharp and accurate.[939] The cheaper alternative used by low-cost prototype services is dot-matrix printed and visible as individual dots under magnification, whereas photoimageable mask shows clean outlines.[997][939] On a multilayer board the inner layers are fabricated without mask and laminated, with mask and silkscreen applied only to the outside faces at the end.[939]

Quoted board thickness is the finished thickness including the mask, so ordering a board without mask can yield a thinner board than expected, since a prototype panel shared with other customers will still be processed for mask.[1259] Mask can be taken right to the board edge without the burring that exposed copper produces there.[1193] V-scoring cuts through the mask along the groove, so traces near a scored edge are at risk.[239] Vias are tented when the mask bridges over the hole; below roughly 0.3 mm the mask will generally close the hole on its own, while larger holes may be plugged with a filler and then tented.[500][566] Peelable mask is a separate option that temporarily protects selected areas through wave and reflow and is then removed, at a noticeable cost adder per side.[1259] For repair work, liquid UV-curable solder mask exists and can be brushed on and cured with a UV torch, with conformal coating as the substitute when it is not to hand.[1497][qmcSEJehqZM]

## Colours and legibility

Green was once the only choice; red, blue, black, yellow, white, purple and pink are all now standard offerings, and the mask colour counts as one of the five apparent colours obtainable on an ordinary board, alongside the silkscreen overlay (itself available in multiple colours through multi-pass printing), bare copper, and gold flash.[186][zHpsA1RtQhw] Mask can also be near-clear, leaving the copper visible with a gold or brownish cast, which gives excellent trace contrast for debugging.[438][1710] Bare copper under a clear mask corrodes quickly if left unfinished.[zHpsA1RtQhw]

Colour choice is a working-visibility decision as much as an aesthetic one. Black mask, and gloss black in particular, makes traces nearly impossible to follow and causes camera auto-exposure to fail badly under bench lighting.[510][606][930][1613][1069][1159] Dave Jones states the position flatly — "I hate black solder mask" — attributing the aversion to years of gloss-black boards at Altium, while allowing that matte black with a white silkscreen looks good and is far more workable than gloss.[510][606][555][1069] Some black masks do let the pattern show through at the right angle, which reads as a deliberate high-tech effect, but the light has to be right.[130][aQ2AVLs8_7k] White mask draws the same objection for the underside of a through-hole board, where the joints and pads become hard to pick out.[1483][928] Opacity varies between mask formulations even for the same colour on the same board revision, changing how far the tracks show through.[185] Gloss mask can be mistaken for conformal coating until inspected closely.[1692]

## Damage, wear and failure modes

The mask is a thin film and its integrity is not absolute. Pinholes are commonplace, and a single one is enough to admit liquid to the copper underneath — the mechanism behind localised electrolytic attack on one trace while its neighbours are untouched.[1673] Corrosion, once started, creeps underneath the mask and attacks the copper along the trace, which is why battery leakage and socket corrosion cases have poor long-term prospects even after visible cleanup: a repaired board can die again months later from rot continuing under intact-looking mask.[507][1527][1668][1222] Alkaline leakage has been observed eating through the mask entirely and lifting it off the board as loose material.[1497] Mechanical wear removes it too: rotary range switches wipe the mask off the traces between contacts within tens of thousands of cycles, though contact resistance can remain fine and the debris generated is the greater concern.[u2-ot2vWLxI][QSmiMlWEpy0][Bh3gcBr0DKs] Older masks are a separate case — crinkly-surfaced green mask of a certain era could flake off with age.[265]

Mask is also removed by hand as part of repair and probing: scraping a small window in it to expose a track for a bodge wire, a mod wire or a scope probe is routine, and doing so on a fine-pitch package is often the only alternative to more invasive butchery of the board.[505][1376][1727][1362][GS0WqUKZ-3c][798]

## Historical practice

Solder mask was not always standard. Boards of the 1970s and early 1980s were commonly bare tin-plated copper with no mask at all, sometimes with the silkscreen printed directly over the tracks.[557][481][1019][1169][1463] A common older construction tin roll-plated the traces before the mask went on, producing the raised, crinkly track profile characteristic of the period.[645][651] Where no mask existed, shorting had to be prevented geometrically: chips were mounted at 45° to the direction of wave travel, with solder traps added, specifically because there was nothing between the pins to stop bridging.[1243] Some 1980s assemblies simply gave up on mask between the pins of a fine-pitch part and left the whole area open, relying on hand rework and leaving flux residue behind.[375][582] Putting mask on hobbyist and magazine-project boards was a deliberate innovation in the Australian kit market of that era, alongside tinning and a printed legend, at a time when the boards of the day were bare copper.[280][705]
