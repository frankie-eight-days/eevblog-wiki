# signal generator

A signal generator is a bench instrument that produces a waveform of known shape, amplitude and frequency for injection into a circuit or another instrument. It is one of the four or five items that define a general-purpose electronics bench, alongside a power supply, an oscilloscope, an SMU and a computer.[1565] Its value lies in being the known quantity in a measurement: when the stimulus is trusted, any discrepancy at the other end belongs to the device under test.[1492][701]

## Output termination

A generator output driven into anything other than its design impedance misbehaves in ways that are easy to mistake for a fault in the circuit. The standard practice is to feed the output into a 50-ohm terminated load so that there are no transmission-line issues and no reflections.[1521][1631] An in-line 50-ohm terminator on the cable serves this purpose.[1525][1744] A scope's internal 50-ohm termination will do the same job, but external terminators are generally rated for higher power, which matters when the point of the test is to drive as high a voltage as possible.[1744] Where a scope has no internal 50-ohm termination and no in-line terminator is fitted, the mismatch shows up directly in the pulse response.[6XpyOGw6RFM] An unterminated stub of coax hanging off the load produces reflections back down the line, and a square wave — which contains many frequencies — makes them visible.[652]

The consequence of ignoring termination is a simple amplitude error. An RF signal generator assumes a 50-ohm load; presented instead with a 1-megohm input, a setting of 100 mV RMS reads as 192 mV.[1709]

## Measuring common-mode rejection

Common-mode rejection ratio measurement is one of the tasks where generator amplitude is the limiting factor. The signal being measured — the residual common-mode output — is very small, so the input signal should be as large as the generator can produce; a maximum of 5 V peak-to-peak at 10 MHz is a typical working point, 10 MHz being the figure quoted in the datasheet under verification.[1521][1631] The generator output is shorted onto both inputs of the differential probe through a banana breakout, so that both wires carry the same signal relative to ground.[1631][1744] Repeating the measurement at 1 MHz should improve the result by roughly 10 dB, and the scope's input bandwidth limit is dropped in step with the test frequency — to 20 MHz for a 1 MHz measurement — so that the noise floor does not swamp the reading.[1521] A generator reaching 100 MHz allows the probe to be characterised across its full rated bandwidth.[1521][1631]

## Verifying an oscilloscope

A quick functional test of an unknown scope is to confirm that both channels display a waveform, that they trigger, and that a high volts-per-division range and a low one both spot-check correctly. If the displayed amplitude also matches the generator, the scope is very nearly certain to be fully functional, because a generator failing by 50 per cent and a channel gain failing by the same amount in the opposite direction is not a plausible coincidence.[1492] Feeding in 500 mV peak-to-peak and reading 508 mV is a reasonable calibration result for a scope.[1418]

The same method exposes what a scope actually does rather than what it claims. Feeding in a signal of known amplitude and sweeping frequency establishes the real analog bandwidth regardless of what the configuration menu reports.[978] Winding up the frequency until the displayed amplitude falls reveals bandwidth that varies with the volts-per-division setting — a limitation of the scope, confirmed as such because the stimulus came from a known-good Marconi generator.[701] A 10 MHz generator output, watched on a trigger-output line, gives a waveform update rate figure of 27,000 to 28,000 waveforms per second.[1566] A 200 MHz sine from an RF signal generator fed into a 200 MHz scope sampling at 1 GSa/s renders as a jagged shape, which is what motivates sine-x-on-x interpolation.[1213] Probe behaviour is characterised the same way, with a 1 V peak-to-peak sine at 1 MHz as the reference against which attenuation is judged.[453]

## Accuracy and drive limits

Generator amplitude accuracy sets a floor on what can be concluded from a measurement. A 4 V peak-to-peak setting can read 4.12 V while the frequency is exactly right.[1260] At the other extreme, a nominal 100 microvolt signal measured as 71 microvolts may say more about the generator's accuracy at that level than about the scope.[1501] Low-amplitude generation is generally where cheap hardware falls down: producing a 10 mV signal accurately is difficult, and the deficiency matters when the device under test has a gain of 100 and needs a small input.[2]

Current drive is the other limit. A generator channel set to a 5 V DC level can power a small board directly, but the rail may dip under load, and a generator that cannot supply enough current produces amplitude errors downstream that look like a fault in the board.[1525] A two-channel instrument is useful here precisely because one channel can supply the DC rail while the other supplies the test signal — 500 mV peak-to-peak into 50 ohms through an in-line terminator, up to the instrument's 100 MHz limit.[1525]

## Injecting into a live circuit

Connecting a generator to a node that carries DC pushes that DC back onto the coax and into the generator's output stage. Where a front end is AC-coupled and the injection point sits at 6 volts, the options are to AC-couple the generator with a series capacitor or to lift one end of the offending component so the voltage cannot be fed back.[966] Shorting the output is likewise not something a generator will tolerate happily.[n7XX75S98e8] Manufacturers acknowledge the risk from the other direction: a scope's built-in generator output may carry a fuse, placed there less against a short than against something being fed back into it.[1477] A generator's ground reference can also be the problem rather than the solution — an audio source with no galvanic connection to the bench avoids ground issues that a mains-referenced generator would introduce.[1072]

## Applications beyond displaying a waveform

Square-wave stimulus is a standard technique for compensating any closed-loop system: connect a generator, look at the output step response, and adjust until it shows only a small amount of overshoot before rounding off.[629] Current probes are checked by shorting the generator's 50-ohm output through the probe at 1 kHz, giving about 25 mA — below the probe's nominal 50 mA measurement floor.[1413] Multimeter input impedance effects are demonstrated by driving the meter from a generator, whose low source impedance makes 10 megohm and high-impedance modes indistinguishable.[584] In audio repair, a 1 kHz tone injected at the input and switched on and off shows whether the signal is reaching the ADC and whether the ADC output data changes in response.[1322]

A generator also substitutes for a misbehaving clock source: where an open-collector optocoupler and pull-up produce an edge too slow for a counter input, feeding the same node from a generator confirms the diagnosis immediately.[1208] Half-bridge gate-drive boards accept a generator into a 50-ohm input, with the board inverting the signal and adding dead time so both transistors cannot conduct together.[1557] Driven through a power amplifier, a generator running at 8.5 Hz can shake a vibration motor to hunt for mechanical resonances.[1607] At the high-frequency end, calculating the sensitivity of an RF crystal detector requires a generator capable of delivering −20 dBm at 10 GHz, with a power meter to verify the level at the measurement point.[EVcxrYnY1ok] EMC radiated-immunity testing to IEC 61000-4-3 is built around a computer-controlled signal generator and amplifier outside the chamber driving a transmitting antenna at the product.[202][546] Filter response can be measured with a generator and an oscilloscope, though it is considerably harder than using a spectrum analyser's tracking generator.[343]

## Generators built into other instruments

Many oscilloscopes carry a generator as a paid option, and the hardware distinction is real rather than a licence key: the base model of one 1000-series scope does not have the signal generator, and is understood not to have it physically populated even though the board layout is otherwise identical.[976] Similarly, a 1054Z lacks the source outputs of higher models in its family and cannot be ordered with the source option at all — there is not even a connector where a source board would attach.[674] Option pricing is substantial relative to the instrument: on a 999-dollar 350 MHz scope, a dual-channel 100 MHz generator is a 700-dollar option and a 50 MHz version 419.[1717] That generator warrants its own dedicated board, identifiable by its relays, carrying dual 100 MHz arbitrary generation.[1717] Option-slot scarcity can force a choice — some scopes cannot take both the generator and the logic analyser.[474]

Two-channel implementations raise the question of phase relationship between the outputs, which is set through a start-phase parameter rather than a direct alignment control.[CMoBGGqojqs] Feature sets on higher-end scope generators extend to arbitrary, sine, square, ramp, pulse, DC, noise, sinc and exponential-rise waveforms with AM and FM modulation.[1638] Modulation options elsewhere include AM, FM and FSK with sine, square and ramp modulating waveforms.[1529] One scope offers to cut out part or all of a captured waveform and save it as an output on the signal generator.[1582]

The quality of these built-in generators varies enough that some are not worth using. A generator whose output is a bare pin, with no way to adjust the output signal level, is best treated as though the instrument did not have one.[1582] Another handheld combination tops out at 1 MHz with sine, square, triangle and sawtooth.[1723] Where the generator and scope share reconfigurable hardware, switching applications can silently stop the generator — running a generator in one app and then moving to a separate oscilloscope app loses the signal, which forces the use of a combined app instead.[858]

Generators appear inside instruments other than scopes as well. Dynamic signal analysers covering DC to about 100 kHz include a source that generates sine and noise alongside their dual wide-dynamic-range inputs.[523] A frequency response analyser provides a source spanning 0 to 50 MHz.[1103] An isolated oscilloscope intended for gain-phase work carries a fully isolated signal generator to match its isolated channels.[1119] An optical-fibre probe contains a test generator used during its calibration routine, generating a signal the probe then measures to null out offsets.[1557] LCR tweezers may include noise generation at 0.1 MHz, a capability of marginal usefulness.[1335] A scopemeter could be fitted with a generator output as an option.[430]

## Standalone instruments

Dedicated generators span an enormous range of capability and price. A two-channel arbitrary waveform generator offering 160 MHz and 500 MSa/s with independent channels and separate sync outputs sits at the value end of professional gear.[323] A DG1022-class instrument at around 400 dollars is a reasonable first generator, in single- or dual-channel form.[400] At the other extreme are portable 20 GHz signal generators[1468] and 6 GHz synthesisers; the latter, when fitted with a small LCD screen, get reached for far more often than equivalent screenless units that require a computer and a GUI to drive.[MecbPOPeovs] Rohde & Schwarz built its reputation on RF signal generators and related high-frequency test gear long before entering the oscilloscope market.[104] Marconi Instruments occupied the high-end signal generator space in the same way.[416]

Older field instruments combined generation and measurement in one portable box: a 20 Hz to 64 or 110 kHz unit designed as both generator and receiver, supplied with a padded case and shoulder strap, was carried out to check telecommunication lines.[1258]

Signal generators sit at the expensive end of test equipment, and this shapes how labs provision them. Where a spare handheld multimeter or general-purpose scope can simply be bought and shelved, that logic does not extend upward: "You're not going to have a spare signal generator."[424] Timebase options — the same high-stability references offered on other instruments — are available in most signal generators, either as standard or as an option.[424] Against that, second-hand and low-cost new test gear has made generators more available than at any earlier point.[818] Failures do occur: a dead generator that will not come up at all is a total loss of the instrument,[CK5nbC_dBWk] and in a repair context a source output that produces nothing regardless of waveform selection, while the analog overload and half-rail detection circuitry flags faults, points at damage well beyond the source section.[538]
