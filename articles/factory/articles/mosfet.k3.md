# mosfet

The MOSFET (metal-oxide-semiconductor field-effect transistor) is a voltage-driven transistor in which a gate voltage, rather than a continuous base current as in a BJT, switches the device on and off.[1736] MOSFETs dominate switching applications — relay and LED loads, PWM dimming, and the switching elements of DC-to-DC converters — because their on-resistance (RDS on) is far lower than the equivalent VCE saturation behaviour of a BJT, giving lower voltage drop and more current and power handling in a given package size.[1736][1461]

## History

The MOSFET was invented in 1959 by Mohamed Atalla and Dawon Kahng at Bell Labs and demonstrated in 1960.[Z_c4wjBpSzE] The practical power MOSFET followed from work by Alex Lidow, whose key idea was to inject extra charge into a too-narrow region of the device so that it appeared wider to electrons; the device worked on the second attempt and immediately dropped in resistance.[1737] Lidow's team launched the HEXFET in 1979, roughly eight months after the first product, using a cell structure about three times denser than the earlier racetrack layout; the first part was the IRF100, followed by devices such as the IRF130, and the density advantage made the power MOSFET commercially compelling against the bipolar transistor.[1737]

## MOSFET versus BJT

A MOSFET is voltage-driven: applying a gate voltage turns the transistor on, with no constant drive current required, whereas a BJT needs continuous base current.[1736] MOSFETs are faster and better suited to high-frequency switching, including DC-to-DC converters operating into the megahertz region, which is why resonant-mode converter topologies such as LLC effectively cannot be built with bipolar transistors — their drive requirements are too high.[1736][1294] BJTs retain advantages: they are more robust, are not easily damaged by static discharge, may have better overcurrent capability, can work at ultra-low voltages, and are simpler to drive and bias.[1599] With a BJT there is no gate oxide to blow via ESD.[1736] Consequently, when a transistor has failed in circuit and the choice is between a BJT and a MOSFET, the MOSFET is the more likely suspect.[1520]

## Key parameters

The headline specifications are drain-source voltage rating (VDSS), continuous and pulsed drain current, and RDS on.[1387][1461] For a pure switching application, voltage, current, and RDS on are essentially the only parameters that matter; other datasheet content can largely be ignored.[1461] Banner RDS on figures can mislead: a headline value may be specified at, for example, 9 A and VGS 10 V rather than at the full 18 A rating, so the conditions of the specification must be checked.[1387]

Gate threshold voltage (VGS) matters greatly for drive. Characteristic output curves show drain current for different gate voltages — for one small SOT-23 device, 100–150 mA at 3.3–3.5 V gate drive versus over 400 mA at 5 V — and the transfer characteristic (drain current against VGS) is the other standard graph, plotted at several temperatures.[1736]

Input (gate) capacitance rises with die size: a small-signal part may be around 20 pF while a power device in a large package can be 630 pF, because higher-current MOSFETs need greater silicon area and capacitance scales with it.[1736]

Every MOSFET contains an intrinsic body diode (substrate diode) across drain and source, an unavoidable consequence of the silicon construction.[1736][504][326] This diode is often adequate as a catch diode but must be checked for suitability; it can also be deliberately exploited, for example as free input-protection clamping in a multimeter front end, or it can feed energy back through an H-bridge into the supply capacitance.[326][931][504]

## Gate drive practice

A 3.3 V microcontroller cannot directly drive an ordinary 12 V MOSFET; the recommended solution is a logic-level MOSFET, otherwise a second transistor with a pull-up resistor is needed, taking care that the pull-up against gate capacitance does not produce an excessive rise time.[107] Distributors parametrically list logic-level parts, and a device specified to work at around 2.5 V is generally safe for logic-level use; "logic level" now typically means 3.3 V compatibility rather than 5 V.[400] Where a higher gate-source voltage is needed for low RDS on — for instance where 3.3 V drive is inadequate — the gate can be driven from a higher rail rather than directly from the microcontroller.[232] Gates must never be left floating.[262]

## Applications

Switching is the dominant use. Synchronous rectifiers in DC-to-DC converters use external N-channel MOSFET pairs driven by multiphase controllers, with switching frequency sometimes locked to a sampling clock to avoid interference.[391] A synchronous rectifier can replace a diode bridge entirely: four MOSFETs under smart control eliminate the roughly 0.6 V per-diode drop of a conventional bridge.[711] H-bridges use four MOSFETs (with multiple paralleled devices per leg for high current) arranged around the load in an H shape for motor drive or inverter output, typically with P-channel devices on the high side.[326][504] H-bridge stages feeding a transformer need DC blocking so that a shorted MOSFET does not place a dead short across the battery.[1620]

Battery systems make heavy use of MOSFETs. Lithium cell protection boards place MOSFETs (commonly a back-to-back N-channel/P-channel pair or dual devices) in series with the pack to disconnect on over-voltage or under-voltage, monitoring cell voltage through the same pin that powers the protection IC.[412][331][1541] Multi-cell chargers use a pair of MOSFETs per cell to switch each battery between charge and bypass paths, with charge and bypass never on simultaneously since that would short the cell; body-diode orientation determines which paths conduct.[812] A battery emulator is essentially a power supply with a series MOSFET or bank of MOSFETs driven to simulate a battery's equivalent series resistance.[1550]

In instrumentation, MOSFETs switch current-shunt resistors for range selection. Because a MOSFET's gate draws no current, the switch can sit in the low side or in series with a shunt, and devices in series can be cascaded across decade-spaced shunts (10 mΩ, 100 mΩ, 1 Ω, 10 Ω, 100 Ω) so that burden voltage stays constant per range.[931][232] High-current ranges demand genuinely beefy parts — keeping error to one 12-bit ADC count (2.5 mV at 2 A) requires RDS on below 1.25 mΩ — while milliamp-range switches can be any jellybean N-channel device whose VGS requirement is met.[232][931] High-end multimeters MOSFET-switch the 10 mΩ shunt of the amps range for the same reason.[929] Where a switch's on-resistance would corrupt a low-impedance feedback network, the MOSFET is moved into the high-impedance op-amp input path instead, where no current flows and RDS on is irrelevant.[931]

Other uses include resetting a peak-detector capacitor by shorting it with a MOSFET gate signal[490], soft-latching power switches built around a P-channel pass MOSFET[262], active ripple filters in which the absence of gate current permits very large gate resistors and therefore very small filter capacitors[1116], and analog multiplexers such as the 4066, which are simply four independent MOSFET pass switches with some on-resistance.[1611] A microcontroller's open-drain output is itself a MOSFET pulling a load low, and probing such a pin shows nothing switching unless a current path exists through the load.[890]

## Paralleling

MOSFETs may be paralleled for current, but current sharing cannot be left to the spread of internal resistances: a low-value ballast resistor in series with each device is required to even out the current — for example 0.3 Ω, 5 W resistors on each transistor in one parallel bank — because even same-wafer parts are not identical.[1698][1110][607]

## CMOS and digital inputs

A CMOS inverter is a complementary N-channel/P-channel MOSFET pair with gates tied together; with a slow-slewing input there is an interval where both transistors are partially on simultaneously, causing shoot-through current, which is why standard CMOS inputs demand fast edges and Schmitt-trigger inputs exist.[941] Schmitt action inside a logic gate is implemented with stacked MOSFETs — four on the input plus source-follower feedback devices — rather than resistors, which cannot be fabricated economically in the process.[941]

## Identification and repair

On a board, a MOSFET in an SO-8-style package is recognisable by its pinout: multiple pins shorted together form the drain on one side and the source on the other, with a single separate gate pin.[472][1190][1216][1672][1461] The reference designator Q marks a transistor generically — BJT, MOSFET, or JFET.[1461] Part-number conventions often encode the ratings directly: an 18N50 is an 18 A, 500 V device, and a 20N60 a 20 A, 600 V device.[1388][1387] Typical markings such as "4303" or "4407" on small parts correspond to common MOSFET families and can be found by searching the number with "MOSFET" and "PDF" — though identical numbers from different makers are not equivalents: one 4303 carried 8 kV gate ESD protection while another had none, a difference that could cause field failures if substituted blindly.[1461][1460]

Common failure modes are a drain-source short — detectable as near-zero or one-ohm resistance in-circuit — and a shorted gate, which should measure open; a shorted gate is not normal for a MOSFET.[1388][861][1460] Battery-protection MOSFETs are somewhat notorious for failing open, leaving good cells disconnected.[V0RWwSw96Sw][RasOXxxEhCk] Replacements for simple switching duty are forgiving: virtually any MOSFET with adequate maximum voltage and current ratings will work even if its RDS on and package differ, and serviceable parts are routinely salvaged from scrapped laptop power sections.[1460][1461] Parametric search by voltage, current, and RDS on is the reliable way to find equivalents; distributor "equivalent part" advertisements are not to be trusted, as they may point to a different package, polarity, or voltage class.[1387][1461] Metal-tab versus insulated-tab packages are interchangeable if an insulating washer and sleeve are added where the tab would otherwise be live on the heatsink.[1387]

## Related devices and beyond silicon

The IGBT (insulated-gate bipolar transistor) combines desirable aspects of the MOSFET with the ruggedness of the bipolar transistor, and high-voltage parts (for example 650 V field-stop devices) have historically been easier to obtain as IGBTs than as MOSFETs.[1620] Silicon-carbide FETs extend blocking voltage to around 1200 V, beyond the roughly 500–600 V ceiling of ordinary silicon MOSFET solutions.[1119] Gallium-nitride devices exploit a two-dimensional electron gas to achieve efficiencies theoretically thousands of times beyond what a silicon MOSFET can reach.[1737] At the other extreme of scale, modified MOSFETs with channels of order 50 by 100 nm serve as charge readout devices in silicon quantum computers, sensing currents of about half a nanoamp.[1316][1594]
