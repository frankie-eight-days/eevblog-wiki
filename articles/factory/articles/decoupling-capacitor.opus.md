# decoupling capacitor

A decoupling capacitor is a capacitor placed between a device's power pin and ground to supply the short, sharp current demands the chip makes as it switches, so that those demands are met locally instead of being drawn down the power distribution network.[1383][1365] The power source supplies the average current; the decoupling capacitors supply the peaks.[1383] Where that local reservoir is absent or too far away, the transient current has to travel through trace and cable impedance, and the resulting rail collapse and ringing produce real logic failures — spurious clocking of a shift register, for example.[1365] The terms decoupling and bypassing are used interchangeably for the same components.[1522][1081]

## Values and why several are used in parallel

Datasheets typically recommend 0.1 µF — 100 nF — with multiple capacitors, one per power pin, and it is standard practice to run several in parallel.[1129] It is not unusual to see a 10 µF, a 1 µF and a 0.1 µF grouped on a single supply pin, the point of the combination being that each part presents its low impedance over a different band of frequencies.[1129] A pairing of a 47 µF and a 10 nF divides the work explicitly: the large part absorbs heavy current bursts, the small part handles the high-frequency switching, and together they hold a reasonably smooth impedance from around 100 kHz up to a few hundred megahertz.[859]

Below the ceramics, the board itself contributes. Power and ground on adjacent inner layers form a plate capacitor spanning the whole board, and that distributed capacitance is a real, measurable decoupling element rather than a theoretical one.[1117]

Manufacturer part numbers set a floor rather than a rule. The 74HC595 datasheet calls for a 10 nF capacitor on each chip, while the ULN2803 specifies no capacitors at all.[1365] Around a single 5 V logic supply the difference between 10 nF, 100 nF, 1 µF and 10 µF is often immaterial to whether the circuit works.[1365]

## Bulk decoupling and current gulps

Small ceramics do not cover loads that draw hundreds of milliamps in a step. A board switching relays or driving similar loads warrants local bulk decoupling of a couple of hundred microfarads — either one capacitor per chip or a single bulk part for the board — so the charge is gulped locally.[1365] Forcing that current to come from an upstream board is poor practice, and it becomes acutely bad when the two are joined by a long thin ribbon cable, whose impedance the transient must cross.[1365] The same reasoning drives star power distribution, in which digital, analog and high-power branches radiate from one central point that carries its own bulk decoupling capacitor.[1365]

Large programmable logic shows the effect at scale. Big FPGAs take substantial gulps of current at power-up and demand massive, layout-critical bypassing.[1323] Xilinx devices on telecom switch-fabric cards were ringed with bulk decoupling in the form of 47 µF 16 V parts.[1019] A Spartan 6 carried a dense field of 0402 capacitors on the underside of its footprint, packed that small purely to fit them in.[475] The requirement is not uniform even between devices on one board: on an oscilloscope's main board a secondary ASIC ran heavy-duty ceramic bypass capacitors while the larger acquisition ASIC beside it used ordinary 100 nF parts distributed around its perimeter.[148] Conversely a small FPGA with only two VCC pins plus VCCIO does not need much decoupling at all.[1323] Vendors publish device-specific decoupling and bypass filtering guidance for their programmable logic, and it is worth following.[1323]

## Package parasitics and placement

The inductance of the capacitor and its connection sets the useful upper frequency, which is why unusually wide, short surface-mount packages exist — these are low-inductance capacitors built specifically for decoupling duty.[859]

Physical proximity is the whole point of the component, and this discipline carries into the schematic as well as the layout: decoupling capacitors should be drawn physically adjacent to the pins they serve, with a short local net rather than a long line running across the sheet.[1129] Whether the ground symbol sits between two capacitors or off to one side is a matter of taste and changes nothing.[1129] Decoupling capacitors are not to be scattered across a board the way AC coupling capacitors legitimately can be.[446] Occasionally the placement is so critical that the capacitor must sit directly on the pins of the device.[208] Older through-hole boards took a distinctive approach: bulk decoupling capacitors made as long bars that lay underneath a row of DIP chips, with leads entering the same holes as the chips' own power and ground pins, custom-built for the pitch of that particular row.[668] Generic versions sized to fit under standard DIP packages were once readily available.[668] On boards from before controlled-impedance practice, decoupling capacitors were placed one per chip and laid out in neat repeating lines alongside a single large ground plane.[410]

## Regulators, converters and analog supplies

Decoupling on a regulator is a stability requirement, not merely noise hygiene. A linear or low-dropout regulator — and the charging ICs built around similar internal circuitry — will oscillate without the capacitance the datasheet specifies; the control loop is simply unstable until the recommended output capacitance is present.[176] Many parts are correspondingly trivial to apply: capacitance in, capacitance out, and little else.[643][158] Modern regulators tolerate a wide ESR window, in one case roughly 0.1 Ω to 5 Ω, needing only a 1 µF capacitor on the input and another on the output.[6]

Where power comes from a source with limited peak capability — a tiny boost converter, or an energy-harvesting element that can supply only the average power — the product's internal decoupling is what actually services the pulse loads, and its adequacy determines whether the rail sags when a motor or transmitter switches on.[751][1383] Decoupling also shapes what a supply-current measurement looks like: the slow charging ramp visible at the start of a duty cycle is the decoupling capacitance filling before the circuit begins its operation.[662]

## Diagnosis and failure

Decoupling capacitors are useful landmarks when reverse-engineering an unfamiliar board: wide traces studded with capacitors and large numbers of vias are almost certainly power rails, which gives a starting point when probing blind.[1520] They are also a routine early suspect in a dead board, since a shorted ceramic pulls a rail down, and checking every decoupling capacitor for a short is a standard first sweep.[1522] The failure mode can be more dramatic than a short circuit alone: a decoupling capacitor that failed short has set a board alight, which is precisely the scenario FR-4's flammability rating exists to contain.[993] Odd, marginal behaviour in working equipment — logic that half-responds rather than failing outright — can also point back to decoupling on the affected board.[1203]

## How much is actually necessary

Specifying one bypass capacitor per chip is time-honoured engineering practice, followed regardless of whether the trace impedances and loop paths of a given layout genuinely demand it.[1081] Whether every one of them is required is a separate question from whether fitting them is correct, and manufacturers frequently leave decoupling footprints on a board unpopulated.[219] In circuits where the load is trivial and the supply local, a decoupling capacitor may be removable without consequence.[1681][1111] The prevailing engineering judgment nonetheless favours fitting them by default, because the faults that follow from omitting adequate local decoupling are intermittent, localised and difficult to trace back to their cause.[1365]
