# current transformer

A current transformer is a transformer whose primary is the conductor carrying the current to be measured and whose secondary is a multi-turn winding on a core, so that a large primary current appears as a small, scaled, galvanically isolated secondary current.[1390] The core is a ring with a bunch of turns wound on it, and the wire passing through the middle counts as a single primary turn; with a 1,000:1 ratio, one amp through the hole produces one milliamp out of the winding.[1390][1505] Because it needs no shunt in the current path, no supply rail, and no direct connection to the mains, it is the standard current sensor in energy meters, solar and household monitoring systems, inverters, EV charging equipment and AC clamp meters.[409][877][1620][1731]

## Ratio, burden and output

A current transformer is fundamentally a current-output device: the secondary delivers a current proportional to the primary current, and it is converted to a voltage by putting a resistor across it.[yOJ7xPugsdc][1505] In a meter front end the chain is simply the primary conductor, the transformer, a burden resistor developing a voltage drop, a low-pass filter, and the ADC.[409] Selecting a suitable resistor across the secondary is the whole of the current-to-voltage conversion.[yOJ7xPugsdc]

Ratios cluster around a few conventional values. Home energy monitoring hardware — solar monitoring systems, EV chargers such as the Zappi — generally uses 1,000:1 units.[1505] A 100:1 turns ratio is common in solar current transformers.[yOJ7xPugsdc] Instrumentation that accepts an external clamp may offer switchable scaling of 1,000:1, 100:1, 10:1 or even 1:1.[1505] The high ratios are a poor match for low-power work: at 1,000:1 a device drawing a watt or two produces a secondary current far too small to resolve usefully, so a 100:1 or 10:1 unit is preferable, and 1:1 better still — but low-ratio transformers are difficult to buy off the shelf.[1505]

Instruments that accept current clamps often support both kinds of accessory: a current-output clamp, which is a current transformer, and a voltage-output clamp giving a voltage proportional to input current.[1505] Where the instrument must compute power rather than merely display current, the current-transformer input is the one that has to be used.[1505]

## AC only

A current transformer works by transformer action and therefore responds only to changing current. An AC-only clamp meter contains a current transformer rather than a Hall effect sensor, which is why such a clamp can be entirely passive and needs no battery.[417][1731] DC current sensing requires a Hall effect device, and that in turn requires a battery to power the sensor and its amplifier — so a battery-less clamp accessory cannot measure DC no matter what ranges the host multimeter offers.[1731]

## Open-circuit hazard

An unloaded current transformer is dangerous. With the secondary open circuit the induced voltage rises without the burden to limit it — in theory without bound, in practice until something breaks down.[yOJ7xPugsdc] Clamping a current transformer onto a live conductor while leaving its output leads unterminated can therefore generate a very high voltage: "You cannot beat the laws of physics, Captain."[yOJ7xPugsdc] A current transformer that is simply left disconnected with no conductor threaded through its core is harmless, however, since with no primary current there is nothing to induce.[k2_mJtAeaog]

## Accuracy and error sources

Metering-grade current transformers are specified tightly. A Vacuumschmelze E4626X501, made specifically for electronic watt-hour meters, has a 100 A primary rating and a 2,500:1 current ratio, giving 40 mA maximum secondary current.[409] Its soft magnetic core yields an essentially fixed phase error, a negligibly small amplitude error and an extremely low, linear temperature dependence; it is also DC-tolerant.[409] The low permeability of the core material leaves a phase error of typically 4 to 5°, specified as 4.73°, which is calibrated out either in software or in the RC low-pass filter — software being the practical choice, since each unit can be individually calibrated once assembled.[409] Amplitude error over the full range from 1 mA to beyond 100 A stays within roughly ±0.25%.[409]

Consumer-grade monitoring hardware is looser. A 60 A residential monitoring transformer produces 80 mA out at its rated 60 A and remains linear to around 75 A, with system accuracy of about 1%.[877] Residual error at the bottom of the range shows up directly in the data: a monitored solar system reporting −10 W of production is reading current-transformer error rather than real power flow.[1626]

## Use in energy meters

Electronic watt-hour meters use current transformers in place of a traditional current shunt resistor, one large unit per phase.[409] Two considerations drive the choice. A shunt sized for hundreds of amps dissipates up to a couple of watts inside the meter, and it cannot usefully resolve the tens or hundreds of milliamps at the bottom of the measurement range demanded by IEC 62053-21 and its related standards, where the specified curves run from a couple of hundred milliamps up to a couple of hundred amps.[409] With the transformer's dynamic range in hand, a 12-bit ADC using its internal reference is adequate.[409]

## Use in solar and household monitoring

Household energy monitoring systems ship with strap-on current transformers that clip over an existing conductor rather than being wired in series, making installation simple and comparatively safe.[877] Channels are not fixed to particular transformers; assignment is done in software setup.[877] Physical aperture, not rating, is often the limiting constraint — two 20 A circuits in 2.5 mm cable can be passed through a single transformer to obtain their combined consumption if they physically fit.[877]

Placement determines what can be inferred. A monitoring system computes household load by subtracting measured production from measured consumption and resolving the direction of current flow.[k2_mJtAeaog] The consumption transformer ideally belongs at the bottom of the switchboard, but the physical arrangement of a typical fuse box often forces it onto the main incoming feed instead.[k2_mJtAeaog][1390] When it sits on the main feed and a second, unknown generating system also feeds the board, the monitor sees export where there is actually consumption and reports negative values.[1390] Inverters expose related placement-dependent modes: a hybrid inverter's zero-export-to-CT mode depends on an installed current transformer and does nothing without one, while zero export to load diverts everything to the emergency load output instead.[1628][gAu8CvMjDrU]

### Paralleling

Where one input must cover two generating arrays, two current transformers can simply be wired in parallel on that input.[1390] Their secondary currents add: one amp through the first at 1,000:1 gives one milliamp, two amps through the second gives two milliamps, and the input sees three milliamps representing total production.[1390] This works even on systems with a single production input and no software provision for multiple arrays, and it is done purely in the wiring, not in software.[1390] The cost is that the two sources can no longer be measured independently — recovering separate figures would require physically relocating a transformer to a point the switchboard layout does not allow.[1390] The technique is a known remedy in the industry, which is why monitoring software includes an explicit setting for where the consumption transformer has been placed.[1390]

The same installation can be reworked without buying hardware: an existing air-conditioner monitoring transformer can be repurposed onto a new array and paralleled, sacrificing the separate air-conditioner reading, which then falls into the total.[1390]

### Subtractive threading

The direction a conductor passes through the core sets the sign of its contribution, and this can be exploited to subtract an unknown source. Routing the output of a second, unmonitored inverter up through the consumption transformer in the opposite direction adds that current to the measurement with reversed sign, so the monitor's normal production-minus-consumption arithmetic yields the correct household figure.[k2_mJtAeaog] With one amp entering the house, three amps from the monitored array and two amps from the unmonitored one, the consumption transformer would otherwise read −4 A; threading the second array's conductor through in reverse contributes −2 A, and subtracting the monitored 3 A from the resulting reading gives the correct 1 A of true consumption.[k2_mJtAeaog] Moving the transformer a short distance along the same conductor to make the extra wire physically reachable changes nothing electrically.[k2_mJtAeaog] Disabling a redundant production transformer requires nothing more than leaving it disconnected, since a paralleled transformer with no live conductor through it contributes nothing.[k2_mJtAeaog]

## Use in equipment

Current transformers appear throughout mains-connected equipment as the isolated current sense element.

In a games console power brick, two are used. The first has both the active and neutral conductors passing through it, which cancels for normal load current and therefore cannot measure consumption at all — it is an earth leakage detector, responding only to imbalance.[1164] The second passes the active only, and measures the current drawn by the load.[1164] The pair supports an electronic fuse tripping at 610 mA as well as leakage protection, and a test winding of a few turns wrapped through the primary transformer allows product current, electronic fuse trip current and the earth leakage function all to be exercised in production test.[1164] One is potted with coarse windings; the other is an unpotted PCB-mount part with much finer and more numerous turns, feeding a diode bridge rectifier.[1164]

Hybrid solar inverters carry several: one to measure battery current bidirectionally, one on the grid output, one on the generator input, and external interfaces for additional units.[1620][1610][kKfZgAyFu8Q] Portable battery systems and EV supply equipment use them on the AC input and on the switched active.[1499][1507] Microinverters use very small ones — Pulse Engineering parts rated to 20 A, usable from 20 kHz to 1 MHz — where the primary is the metal strap on top of the package, since that is the only path that can carry 20 A, and the secondary turns are inside.[yOJ7xPugsdc] A power-factor correction controller's main inductor is likewise a transformer, its secondary winding to ground providing the zero-current detection the controller needs.[273]

Test and laboratory instruments use them too. A bench power supply places one around a winding to measure the input current to a converter stage.[1293] An oscilloscope taps line-frequency triggering with a small current clamp around the mains cable rather than a direct connection.[1639] Custom power-measurement test fixtures are built around banks of current transformers with the conductor looped through each one, supporting eight channels of voltage and power measurement including three-phase.[yDfybZx02e0] Accelerator instrumentation uses direct-current-to-current-transformer measurement to monitor the beam current circulating in a storage ring.[836]
