# magnetic field

A magnetic field is produced whenever charge moves: pass current through any wire, any component, any PCB trace, and a field forms around it.[1406] It is one half of the pair of fields that electromagnetics is built on, the other being the electric field, and the two are inseparable in anything that radiates.[694][1176] For practical electronics the magnetic field matters in three distinct ways — it is the mechanism by which inductors, transformers, motors and clamp meters work; it is the storage medium in which an inductor holds energy; and it is one of the two things that escapes a badly laid-out board and shows up as radiated emissions at a compliance test.[1406][1176][1193] It is also a reliable source of confusion, being described within the corpus as "the magnetic fields is the magic black art of electricity".[UStV3zyhgnQ]

## Fields around conductors

Current through a straight wire generates a field around it, and its direction follows the right-hand rule.[1406] The field surrounding a trace on a PCB falls off roughly with the square of the distance, which is why a magnetic sensor head intended to read the current in that trace has to sit directly against the board rather than an inch above it.[296]

Winding a conductor into a coil makes the fields of the individual turns add and pass through the multiple turns together, which is why inductors are wound as coils rather than left as straight wire, and why multiple turns is the usual route to a more effective inductor.[1406] A ferrite bead threaded over a wire is essentially a one-turn inductor whose core helps contain the field and makes it more effective.[1406] Ferrite cores through a coil extend the same idea into transformers.[1406]

## Energy storage and inductive kickback

Capacitors store energy in an electric field across a dielectric; inductors store energy in a magnetic field.[1406] Because that energy takes time to build up and time to remove, inductors and capacitors are what make circuit transients possible in the first place.[1406] At the instant a switch closes, an inductor has no field in it and passes no current at all — the complete opposite of a capacitor, which behaves as a short circuit at time zero — and the inductance resists current flow until the field builds up.[1406]

Collapsing that field is a way of generating large voltages. The magnetic ballast of an older fluorescent fixture uses the collapsing field to produce the voltage that strikes the arc and starts the lamp, and an automotive ignition coil works on the same principle.[1406] The same effect is a hazard when a chip drives a relay coil: removing the current collapses the relay's field and generates a large negative voltage on the inductor.[1406] This is a property capacitors do not share.[1406]

In AC power terms the field is the reactive part of a component. An ideal inductor or capacitor in an AC circuit dissipates no power whatsoever; in a real inductor or transformer the magnetic field is the reactive component, and it is only the resistance of the windings that actually dissipates power.[1730]

## Relationship to the electric field

Changing electric and magnetic fields create one another, and this is what allows current to appear to flow through a capacitor. No electrons cross the dielectric; the changing electric field between the plates creates a changing magnetic field, and that is the displacement-current mechanism that gives the equivalent of current through the insulator.[486] On the conductor the description can stay in terms of electric current; once inside the dielectric it has to be recast in terms of changing fields and the energy stored and transferred in them.[486] The relationship runs inversely between the two component types: for a capacitor, the faster the electric field changes the more current flows, while for an inductor the greater the change in magnetic field the less current flows.[486]

Light is made of oscillating electric and magnetic fields, perpendicular to each other and in phase, so that when one is at maximum so is the other.[1439] Wherever electric and magnetic fields coincide there is a flow of energy, calculable with the Poynting vector.[1439] A battery on its own has an electric field but, with no charges moving, no magnetic field.[1439] Once connected, the charge on the conductor surfaces creates an electric field outside the wires and the current inside them creates a magnetic field outside them, and the combination in the surrounding space carries the energy.[1439] For steady-state DC in a current-carrying wire the electric field runs along the wire in the direction of current flow and the magnetic field points out of it, and the Poynting vector still points into the wire.[1439] With an AC source both fields reverse together each half cycle, so the Poynting vector at any instant still points from source to load and the DC analysis carries over unchanged.[1439] Whether magnetic fields are physically real or merely a relativistic shadow of electric fields is a question the corpus raises without settling.[UStV3zyhgnQ] Kirchhoff's voltage law failing in the presence of a magnetic field has been a related point of contention.[UStV3zyhgnQ]

## Near field, far field and EMC

Electromagnetic radiation has electric and magnetic components, conventionally drawn with the electric field along one axis and the H field at 90 degrees to it, propagating in different orientations.[1176][1273] In the near field the two are separable and behave very differently; at a certain wavelength distance they combine to produce the electromagnetic radiation that is measured in the far field, which is what a compliance test house actually measures.[1193]

The distinction that decides which field dominates is source impedance. The magnetic or H field has a very low source impedance in the near field, while the electric field has a very high one.[1176][1273] High-current, low-impedance sources therefore generate magnetic fields: heavy current switching, or very fast switching dumping energy into bypass capacitors and the interplane capacitance, produces a magnetic field because of the low impedance and high current.[1273] High-impedance sources that draw little current — a static 5 V supply, for example — generate electric fields.[1273] The practical shorthand is that high-voltage, low-current situations are E-field dominated and call for an E-field probe, while high-current situations with large loop areas are H-field dominated at a given distance.[694]

There is a terminology trap here: H is the magnetic field, while B is magnetic flux density, and the two are often used loosely as if interchangeable.[1176][1273]

On a board the governing quantity is loop area — the whole loop, from the supply out through the ground system, the power wires and any signal path.[694] A chip drawing large gulps of current around a big loop generates a correspondingly big magnetic field.[694] On a large board with a couple of dozen chips it is not possible to keep every trace's loop area short, so a majority of them will have large loop areas and radiate, mostly magnetically with some electric contribution, which is the origin of the increase in radiated emissions on a two-layer board relative to a four-layer one.[1176] Whether a given structure radiates predominantly electric, magnetic or combined field depends on source impedance, load impedance, frequency, the bypassing and the capacitive loading, and the interaction gets genuinely complex.[1176] None of which makes two-layer boards inherently unusable — a careful layout can approach four-layer performance.[1176]

An H-field probe is identifiable by the coil at its tip, as distinct from an E-field probe.[889] Because it responds to the whole current loop, it picks up not just the component being pointed at but everything looping around the board back to the power supply — a broader and dirtier picture than an E-field probe gives.[694]

## Measuring magnetic fields

Several instrument classes exist purely to convert a field back into a number.

A fluxgate sensor drives a split core into saturation alternately in each direction, and a secondary sense winding around the outside detects minute changes in an external field superimposed on that drive.[296] Many physical constructions exist, including multi-core types, to maximise sensitivity.[296] Because the field around a trace collapses so fast with distance, such a probe must be placed hard against the track.[296] Instruments of this type offer selectable bandwidths, down to a roughly 2 Hz setting for very low frequency or DC field measurement.[296]

A clamp meter closes a laminated core around the conductor and reads the field in the resulting loop with a Hall effect sensor, which is why the jaws must be closed to complete the loop before a reading is possible.[UJ6JG4eV0nY] Magnetoresistive bridges are used for the same purpose: one such part uses a push-pull Wheatstone bridge of four unshielded TMR elements, giving a differential output linearly proportional to the field applied perpendicular to the sensor package.[1413]

DC clamp measurement is vulnerable to the Earth's field, which is large enough relative to a low current range to dominate it — rotating a meter after zeroing changes the reading, and on a 2 amp range the variability can consume something like a tenth of a 2000-count span.[dv0B_WqL7w4] On that basis a low current range on a cheap DC clamp meter is of questionable value, since the range that is supposed to be its advantage is largely eaten by field-induced offset.[dv0B_WqL7w4] Prolonged exposure to a strong magnetic field can also leave a clamp meter with a permanent DC-only offset by magnetising the core, correctable in principle by degaussing.[UJ6JG4eV0nY][dv0B_WqL7w4]

An entirely optical approach exploits the Faraday effect: a polarised laser beam crossing an air gap has its polarisation rotated by an applied magnetic field, with the rotation set by the Verde constant of air, the field strength and the interaction length.[263] Field strength, and from it the current or voltage on the wire, can be recovered from the rotation.[263] The very rapid falloff of field strength with distance and the short interaction length limit such an instrument to a maximum working distance of a couple of feet, with an optimum distance rather than simply closer being better.[263]

## Shielding and stray fields

Fields escaping equipment interfere with sensitive measurements. A CRT oscilloscope is an electromagnetic device with large coils generating large fields, with a horizontal scan rate commonly around 25 kHz that appears directly in nearby measurements.[1329] Removing the lid of such an instrument makes the spikes easy to see.[1329]

Transformer geometry determines how much escapes. A toroidal transformer has effectively no air gap, the winding running around a single large core, and no air gap means far less stray magnetic field — the reason toroidals are favoured in high-end audio equipment.[511] Conversely a poorly designed oscillator coil can radiate a massively wideband field that swamps a small wanted signal from a nearby transmitter, so two such units placed close together can upset one another.[767]

Magnetic coupling can also be an attack surface. Some safes can be opened by swiping a magnet across the front to disturb the field of the door solenoid, making it behave as if it had been activated by the circuitry; a sufficiently thick door, on the order of 50 mm, defeats the attack.[8MC93KxPTHA]

## Data storage

Magnetic core memory stores one bit per ferrite ring in the field within that ring.[668][y3WlxxgZwcs] Writing flips the field inside an individual toroid; because the flux is confined to the ring rather than passing through the neighbouring holes, the field does not bleed appreciably into adjacent cores, which is what permits the very high packing densities achieved.[1713] Stored data is non-volatile enough that decades-old cores may still hold their contents if a way could be found to read them out.[668][y3WlxxgZwcs]

A crude inductive ferrite hard-disk head is a ferrite structure with a gap and a couple of turns of coil, generating a three-dimensional field at the gap.[395] The three-dimensionality matters: the design aims to put as much field as possible out in the y direction onto the platter and as little as possible in x, because the x component produces fringing effects across the track width.[395]

## Electromechanical devices

A brushless fan motor uses four coils with permanent magnets around them and a ring of permanent magnets inside, the drive alternating the field in a circular fashion to make the rotor turn.[1172] A moving-coil loudspeaker or tweeter has its coil moving within the field of a permanent magnet assembly.[1076] An electromagnetic buzzer is effectively a small electromagnetic speaker, and an external magnet can attenuate its output by acting on the internal coil.[rZqfp2XKvjE] A rotating coil inside a magnetic field is the classic voltage generator and the origin of the AC sine wave, with the phase angle of the rotor sweeping out the waveform.[1469][1009]

An analog watt-hour meter multiplies mechanically: a field produced by a voltage coil and a field produced by the current coil combine in the disc to make it rotate at a rate proportional to their product.[1446] A braking magnet supplies a controlled magnetic friction to stop the disc accelerating indefinitely, adjusted either by moving the magnet in and out relative to the disc or, in fixed-magnet designs, by a shunt plate.[1446] Because the magnet's field varies with temperature, and an outdoor meter box can see something like a 60 degree swing between summer and winter extremes, a strap of magnetic material with an inverse temperature profile is fitted to cancel the drift — otherwise the calibrated braking torque changes and the meter runs fast or slow.[1446]

A moving iron or iron vane ammeter passes the current through a single coil, and two iron vanes inside develop opposing fields that work against a spring.[1463] Such meters respond to the square of the current, so without compensation the scale is markedly non-linear at the low end.[1463]

An earth leakage circuit breaker passes both active and neutral through the same core, so that equal and opposite currents cancel and produce nothing; any imbalance generates a field which is sensed on a further winding and trips the device at a defined current.[1164]

Buried services can be traced magnetically. With no physical access to the pipe, an inductive-mode transmitter set on the ground generates a field that penetrates the soil — a claimed 20 feet of depth in practice — and couples the signal into the conductive pipe, which is then followed with a receiver, though the indirect coupling costs a lot of attenuation.[1338]

## Near-field coupling in RFID

Contactless credit cards are not really radio in the near field sense — the coupling at 13.56 MHz is a magnetic field acting as a conventional transformer, with the coil in the card as the secondary.[889] The card's chip draws its power from that coil and signals back by modulating the load on the secondary.[889] Passive cards work entirely from the field picked up from the reader, whereas active jamming cards carry their own battery.[890]

Read range is therefore a function of field strength. The field can be captured on a scope with an H-field probe and observed growing as the reader approaches; a phone-sized reader must get to within about an inch before it can synchronise and complete the protocol.[889] A signal remains detectable at greater distances even where it is too weak to establish a connection, so a more powerful reader generating a larger field extends the practical skimming distance.[889] Shielding wallets accordingly work only for cards placed in the shielded compartment; a card in an unshielded pocket reads straight through multiple layers of the same bag.[889]

## Large fields and physics instruments

In quantum computing based on electron spin, the spin is the fundamental microscopic magnetic dipole of elementary particles such as electrons, protons and neutrons, and placing an electron in a magnetic field splits it into two states, spin up and spin down, usable as a qubit.[1316] The field of a spin can be pictured as the familiar dipole field lines of the Earth, emerging from one pole, winding around and re-entering at the other.[1316] Making that splitting useful means the energy difference between the two states must exceed the thermal energy of the surroundings, which is achieved at around 0.01 kelvin in a field of about 1 tesla.[1594] One tesla is a large field by everyday standards: the Earth's field is around 50 microtesla, roughly 60 microtesla in Sydney.[1594][1316] Such fields come from a superconducting magnet — a niobium-titanium solenoid of around 10 henry inductance running at about 100 amps produces roughly 6 tesla — or, increasingly, from small arrays of permanent magnets.[1594][1316] Field also determines the charge-transition behaviour of the readout structure: at zero field, at a particular bias point, an electron has equal preference for being on or off the donor atom, giving a random telegraph signal.[1594]

In a synchrotron the field is ramped synchronously with the particle energy so the beam stays on a fixed radius, which is what removes the need for a long linear accelerator and allows the energy to be topped up on each revolution.[836] An insertion device such as a superconducting wiggler undulates the beam back and forth to produce waves of electromagnetic radiation at a chosen frequency, and a higher field produces higher energy because it bends the beam on a shorter radius.[836] Pickups inside the beamline detect the electric and magnetic fields produced by the electron beam itself.[836]

A caesium beam frequency standard uses a vacuum tube with a magnetic field associated with it, containing roughly 7.2 to 7.5 grams of caesium coated on the cathode and boiled off over the life of the tube.[423] A travelling-wave tube uses a quadrature magnet arrangement in the casing, alternating north and south, to keep the electron beam focused as it races down the tube alongside the RF signal on the helix.[XqakD0dXdjM]

Because neutrinos carry no electric charge they are not bent by magnetic fields, so their direction of arrival preserves information about where they came from.[1gfM_9EdLSs] Conversely, the absence of a planetary field elsewhere makes magnetometers useless on an off-world rover, forcing reliance on an inertial measurement unit instead.[896]

Wireless EV charging systems operating around 80 kHz raise field exposure questions, with flux density in microtesla compared against general public and occupational exposure limits across frequency, alongside the ordinary EMC concern of what other electronics in the vehicle are being exposed to.[1584]

## Magnetic fields in pseudoscience

The magnetic field is a favourite of technically vacuous product claims, largely because generating one is trivially easy and the claim that a field is being produced is therefore literally true while explaining nothing. A wearable device claiming to deliver unique magnetic fields to the body to induce calm, alertness or sleep does generate a field — at frequencies from 0 to 22 kHz — and the accompanying narrative about capturing molecular signatures with Helmholtz coils, gradiometers and SQUID outputs and then playing them back at ultra-low field strength has no mechanism behind it.[1290] Nothing about the field being non-ionizing and non-thermal supplies the missing mechanism.[1290]

A heater marketed on the claim that oxidation of a conventional element creates a galvanic effect which in turn creates magnetic fields of various frequencies, that these fields attract and burn dust, and that the absence of a magnetic field is why the product does not smell, is not a description of any real physics.[1186] Free-energy crowdfunding pitches follow a similar pattern, with over-unity magnet motors sometimes hedged by the claim that the magnetic field degrades over time and so no conservation law is violated.[HfqpuhYiR3o]
