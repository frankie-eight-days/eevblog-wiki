# arduino

Arduino is an open-source microcontroller development ecosystem built around the Atmel ATmega family, combining a standard board footprint, a bootloader, and a simplified C development environment into a single package.[45] The board itself is not the Arduino — it is the generic carrier for the ecosystem, and the same toolchain will program a bare ATmega soldered onto a board of one's own design.[45] Its significance is that it removes the setup work that normally stands between a beginner and a working microcontroller program, without giving up a real compiled language.[45][900]

## Architecture of the platform

Underneath, the environment uses the AVR GCC compiler, but the compiler is hidden from the user.[45] Programs are essentially standard C, with floats, double-precision floating point, for loops, and the rest of the usual C constructs available.[45] What the environment adds is a level of abstraction above raw AVR GCC or a PIC C compiler: header files, register configuration, oscillator options, and input-pin setup are handled by the framework rather than by the programmer.[45] Register-level initialisation is a substantial hurdle in conventional microcontroller development — a micro that has not had its peripherals configured simply does nothing — and eliminating it is the platform's main practical contribution.[900]

Because the programs are ordinary C compiled by AVR GCC, they are portable, which makes the platform a professional development tool rather than only a hobbyist one.[45] The trade-offs are specific: there is no low-power solution, any peripheral without an existing library requires writing one, and a project large enough to max out a conventional micro is not a good fit.[45]

The hardware side is a standard header layout that accepts stackable expansion boards, or shields.[45] Board designs, schematics included, are published, so derivatives and clones are common — Ethernet variants with power-over-Ethernet supply so that a single LAN cable both powers and connects the board,[203] kit-form versions intended to be assembled by hand,[203] and boards with expanded peripheral counts such as two USARTs, four SPI, two I²C, nine PWM channels and additional GPIO.[913] Compatibility has also been claimed by hardware with entirely different silicon: PIC32-based boards running an 80 MHz MIPS core,[711] and FPGA boards laid out to the R3 form factor.[795][636]

Programming a board or a bare chip requires either an in-system Atmel programmer or a pre-programmed bootloader, in which case a cheap USB-to-serial converter board is sufficient.[45] The FTDI USB-to-serial part is the standard choice for adding USB to a design whose microcontroller only offers a serial port.[676] On the host side an attached board simply enumerates as a generic serial port.[WXJP_CNYt3o]

## Code size and execution timing

Program memory is rarely the binding constraint. A ten- to twelve-line sketch driving a character LCD occupied about 2.3 K of the 30 K available on the board, and that figure already includes the LCD driver, string handling, and other library overhead.[45] Where a program does have to be squeezed byte by byte, the correct response is to choose a part with more memory rather than to optimise.[45] Wi-Fi-capable targets shift the baseline considerably: a plain LED flasher built for the ESP8266 consumes around 222 K, roughly 21 percent of program space, because the entire Wi-Fi stack is linked in.[998]

Execution timing matters where a sketch bit-bangs a protocol. The standard `digitalWrite` routine takes a couple of microseconds to execute, which is significant against a modulation bit time of 562 microseconds; the fix is to subtract the routine's overhead from the nominal delay, which in one infrared transmitter meant trimming a delay down to nine microseconds.[506] Faster pin-access methods exist, but `digitalWrite` is the framework's basic idiom.[506]

## Electrical interfacing

Outputs are 5 V TTL on the classic boards, which is a recurring source of interface problems. Driving logic that expects less than 2 V requires a resistor divider on each line — a 1 K and 680 ohm pair, for instance — when the target board has no series resistors of its own.[1531] A 3.3 V display module needs level converters in the other direction.[1699] High-voltage display drivers can be worse still: some require a logic high of VDD minus two volts, meaning a 10 V input signal, and are therefore not compatible with 5 V TTL at all, so a level translator chip or transistor pull-up arrangement is mandatory.[948] Mixed-supply projects that combine a 5 V board with 3.3 V radio hardware need a conversion board between them.[227]

An LCD segment can be driven directly from board pins by generating in-phase and out-of-phase waveforms, which produces twice the supply rail peak to peak across the segment from a single logic rail.[1045][1046] Driving a segment with a static logic one instead, against a grounded common pin, applies a long-term DC bias to the panel.[ZtOXfFkV2jw]

Onboard regulation is stable over temperature. Measured at room temperature, one board's rails sat at 5.015 V and 3.407 V; over a 12 °C excursion the 5 V rail moved by 1 mV while the 3.3 V rail moved by 5 mV.[101]

## Grounding and probing hazards

A board powered over USB from a desktop computer is not isolated. Its ground is connected to mains earth through the USB cable and the computer chassis, measuring around 9 ohms to earth.[279] Clipping an oscilloscope ground lead to the board's ground point is safe, but clipping it anywhere else — a power rail, or an arbitrary node on an attached shield — shorts that node to earth.[279] The board's own supply cannot deliver enough energy to be dangerous, so the result is a supply short and a shutdown rather than an explosion, but the circuit stops working and the fault is easy to misdiagnose.[279]

Probing fine-pitch signal pins directly carries its own risk of shorting adjacent pins and destroying the board; soldering up a female-to-male extender to bring the signals out is the safer approach.[689] When troubleshooting a display that is receiving clock and latch signals but showing nothing, confirming that the board is actually outputting valid 5 V logic on each line narrows the fault to the data path or to the sketch itself.[689]

## Typical project patterns

The platform's characteristic use is a task that needs to work within minutes rather than hours — setting a few logic outputs, or putting text on an LCD — where wiring a bare PIC onto a breadboard would be disproportionate effort.[89] Beyond that it appears as the control element in a wide range of builds: shorting out the contacts of a remote control by wiring an output pin across the switch,[505] transmitting a reverse-engineered infrared code directly from an LED on an output pin instead of hacking the remote at all,[506] persistence-of-vision displays,[EHLRQbCW5oM] a light-painting staff carrying a board plus an RF module,[227] motion control for milling machines and 3D printers via the RAMPS-style boards and G-code interpreters such as GRBL,[843][753] and a four-axis robot arm whose inverse kinematics run on the board so the arm can be commanded in XY coordinates.[586]

Where a bench project needs a serial-attached controller alongside a full PC, an Arduino-compatible section is sometimes integrated onto the same single-board computer, with the host application talking to it over a serial port.[917][BoL8gD8IcXg]

The instrument-design case is instructive. A lab power supply built around an ATmega168 or ATmega328 was made Arduino-compatible specifically because the firmware would be written in the Arduino environment.[232] That decision propagated into the hardware: a ceramic resonator and an external AVR ISP header for in-circuit programming,[232] an FTDI-based programming interface brought out to the rear panel so the case need not be opened to reflash the firmware,[259] I²C pull-ups dropped to 2.2 K because five devices on the bus were slewing the edges badly enough to lose bits,[259] and the constant-current setpoint driven from a hardware PWM output rather than a second DAC channel.[259] The current-sense front end used a programmable-gain amplifier with a 320 mV full-scale input across the shunt — 0 to 320 mA at 1 ohm — and autoranged under firmware control to hold 12-bit resolution down to 10 microamp steps.[259] Debugging that firmware turned up faults on both sides of the boundary: an I²C LCD library that misbehaved even when substituted with the manufacturer's own code,[240] and a DAC that produced nothing because only 14 of the required 16 bits were being clocked out.[240]

## Development-tool and ecosystem effects

The platform's hardware design was shaped by the free tools available to it — the board was laid out within the limitations of the free version of Eagle, and the wider open-source hardware movement grew up around those same free tools and their constraints.[527] Boards in the standard form factor are cheap to fabricate because they are small, and the classic board's edges are rough to the touch on all four sides, the telltale of a V-grooved panel snapped apart after assembly rather than individually routed.[127][ct9K-EutiIQ]

Third-party silicon vendors have treated the platform as a target to be matched or joined: supporting standard platforms is a way for a semiconductor manufacturer to get its parts into the hands of people who will later specify them professionally,[123] and a competing low-cost development board that stopped short of compatibility gave up part of its potential reach.[96] The ESP8266 in particular is programmed through the Arduino environment via an added board-support package, which installs support for the Adafruit Huzzah, NodeMCU and WeMos boards at once; the chip costs about $3 and includes Wi-Fi.[998][yabl87SD-3M] The WeMos D1 Mini's board package maps its silkscreen pin names into the framework, so an ordinary `digitalWrite` to D0 works as it would on any other board.[VTAwzrKPjeE][952] A large third-party library ecosystem covers web APIs, MQTT, addressable LED strings, and quadrature decoding, the last of which removes the need to write a direction-detecting algorithm by hand.[yabl87SD-3M][1573]

The platform is not accepted everywhere. In industrial embedded computing, proposing an Arduino or a Raspberry Pi in place of an established standard such as PC/104 is not taken seriously, and PC/104 has outlasted three decades of contenders.[1028]

## Design philosophy and criticism

The platform was originally aimed at artists rather than engineers.[818] The criticism levelled at the maker movement it fed is that dropping in a board and blinking lights can stop a learner from digging down to the layer where the current direction through an LED and the registers behind a library call actually live; the value appears when the learner takes that next step.[818] The counter-position is that the platform expands embedded design rather than diluting it, since it brings more people in and remains one tool among many rather than a replacement for conventional microcontroller work.[89]

Compared with the PICAXE, the Arduino is the less simple of the two — PICAXE programs can be entered as flowcharts with no programming language at all — but PICAXE is not a professional platform, whereas Arduino code is real C.[45] That last point is the basis for preferring it over niche teaching languages: a beginner who learns C on this platform, library-dependent as the code may be, transitions seamlessly to industry-standard C afterwards.[546] Programmers who insist that assembly is the only legitimate way to write embedded code, and that they can do it faster than any higher-level language, are simply wrong about the economics of the work.[45]

## Corporate ownership and licence terms

Arduino was acquired by Qualcomm.[1721] Prior to the acquisition the company had taken $53 million across two venture rounds from investors including Renesas, Bosch, CVP Capital and Andreessen Horowitz, with annual unit sales in the range of one to two million; the acquisition price was not disclosed, but a figure of at least $250 million is a reasonable estimate.[1721] Revenue came from hardware and from software services, with hardware the dominant share.[1721]

The revised terms and conditions issued afterwards reserve the right to change, suspend or discontinue any service, with paid premium services honoured only for their specified term and 30 days' notice on shutdown — standard terms, but consequential for any product design that depends on the company's continuing services.[1721] A newly added clause targets under-18 users, who must supply a date of birth at registration and receive a limited account, despite entry-level hobbyists being a large part of the platform's base.[1721] Because the platform is open source, it can be forked regardless of what happens to the company.[1721]
