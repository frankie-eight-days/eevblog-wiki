# 5 volt rail

The 5 volt rail is the nominal supply that powers digital logic in the great majority of electronic equipment built from the TTL era through to the present. In older instruments and computers it is typically the highest-current rail in the system and often the only one the digital section needs, which makes it both the busiest node on the board and the first thing worth measuring when a machine will not boot.[905][598][1060] Its conventional tolerance is plus or minus 5%, a window running from 4.75 V to 5.25 V.[905]

## Tolerance and where to sit inside it

The 5% window is the working definition of a healthy rail: 5.12 V is unremarkable, 4.97 V is fine, and 4.93 V on a running machine is enough to rule the supply out as a first-order suspect.[905][1203][Gbn_51IoJiM] Readings outside the window are not automatically fatal — 4.68 V measured on a working analogue oscilloscope is out of spec but not worth agonising over — though they mark the supply as degraded.[502] Values that sit inside the window but low, such as 4.79 V on a standby rail, are technically in spec yet suspicious enough to follow up.[378] The same suspicion applied to a receiver measuring 4.8 V eventually pointed at a specific failed 22 nF capacitor, C405.[379] A reading well above nominal is just as diagnostic: 5.68 V on what should be a regular 5 V supply is clearly not a normal rail, and 6 V where 5 V is expected suggests the internal supply itself has drifted out.[297][1724]

Because the tolerance is symmetrical but the losses in a system are not, the standard practice on high-current 5 V systems is to trim deliberately to the high side rather than to exactly 5.000 V.[905] Old boards populated with hundreds of chips would be set at 5.25 V at the regulator, so that circuitry near the connector sat at the top of the tolerance band and circuitry at the far end of the board — after the drop across cables, connectors and traces — still landed above 4.75 V.[1216][905] Equipment found trimmed to 5.25 V in the field is doing exactly this.[630] Trimming precisely to 5 V, by contrast, spends the entire margin on IR drop.

## Current, and the shape of the supply around it

The 5 V rail's current demand is what distinguishes it physically from the other rails. An Amiga 500 draws about 1 A idle and just over 2 A while reading a floppy, against 0.7 A on the plus 12 V rail and effectively nothing on minus 12 V.[438] A LeCroy 9384C oscilloscope supply is rated at 14 A on the 5 V rail, and — importantly for load testing — derives its 3.3 V rail from it, so both must be loaded together to verify capability.[398] By contrast a rubidium frequency standard draws only about 90 mA on 5 V against 0.77 A on its 15 V rail, since the 5 V there serves only control logic.[235]

That current shows up in construction. In a well-built supply the transistors and power diodes on one side of the heatsink belong to the main 5 V rail, while the smaller plus 12, minus 12 and minus 5 rails share the other side with less current capability.[790] On the board, the 5 V connection is given multiple parallel pins or wires — four wires on the highest-current supply of a scope acquisition board — shorted together at the connector and fanned into an internal plane.[398] Whole boards may carry nothing but 5 V: a six-layer digital board with one big 5 V digital plane and no other supply avoids the split-plane and routing-layer problems that a modern multi-rail FPGA design forces on the layout.[598] A dedicated ground plane and a dedicated 5 V plane on a six-layer board, with the remaining layers left for routing, is the classic arrangement.[1060]

## Regulation

The rail is generated by whatever suits the current: a 78L05 with series diode protection for a handful of milliamps of instrument logic, a switching regulator such as a TPS54302 rated 3 A where the rail feeds a whole product, or a discrete linear regulator with a series pass transistor and an output current-sense resistor in older instruments.[1272][1527][1672][804] At the bottom of the scale, some designs do not regulate at all: one vintage portable computer supply used a proper linear regulator for its 12 V rail but produced 5 V from nothing more than a 5 W zener diode, an approach that stands out as poor next to the effort spent elsewhere in the same supply.[1364]

Where the 5 V rail feeds most of the system, its integrity is a single point of failure for the product. When a 5 V regulator supplies the entire logic board, thermal shutdown of that one part resets the whole instrument.[512] Where 5 V additionally supports USB charging and most of the peripheral circuitry, verifying it is the first job of any repair.[1672]

## Ripple, noise and bypassing

Manufacturers specify ripple on the digital 5 V rail, and the specification is meaningful: a Tektronix 2465B allows 150 mV peak to peak, and a unit measuring 350 mV is far enough out to explain misbehaviour by itself.[1203] A Raspberry Pi PoE hat produced close to 400 mV peak to peak on its 5 V output at full power, and at very light load the same converter's regulation broke down entirely, creeping up to 5.8 V below a minimum load of roughly 0.1 W.[1122]

The worst-case demonstration of what bypassing does is a crystal oscillator driving a 50 ohm load with no bypass capacitor at all, where the 5 V rail swings up 2.5 V and down 2.5 V on every transition because there is no local reservoir to supply the gulp of high-frequency current the output stage demands.[1089] Removing the load leaves the rail still poor but without the large transitions.[1089] Ordinary bulk decoupling is simply a large electrolytic between ground and the 5 V rail, frequently present on the board but absent from the published circuit.[827]

Rail noise on this scale is not visible at normal vertical settings. Confirming that a marginal 5 V regulator was misbehaving required AC coupling and 100 mV per division; on a 5 V scale, and even inside a couple-hundred-millivolt trigger window, nothing could be captured.[512] The regulator was still regulating at 5 V; only the AC component had changed.[512] For serious rail measurement a dedicated power rail probe into a 50 ohm input is the appropriate instrument, and while conveniently adjacent ground and 5 V pins are good enough to probe, wiring directly to the point of interest is better.[1733]

## Diagnosis

Measuring the 5 V rail is the standard opening move on a dead or misbehaving machine, ahead of consulting the service manual for the unlabelled test points.[1301][536] A good 5 V rail with lit LEDs and a running self-test establishes that the digital section is alive and shifts suspicion elsewhere — to the CRT, the video output, or another rail.[695][1348][536] A rail at 1.47 V ends the investigation immediately and points at the power supply.[1301] The inverse case is equally informative: an instrument with plus 5, minus 5 and plus and minus 15 V all present but its 2 V rail dead should still have brought up its main processor, and did not.[405]

A shorted 5 V rail is diagnosed by resistance rather than voltage, and the technique is to hunt for the lowest reading across the rail while working across the board. On an ArduCopter board a resettable polyswitch fuse running hot flagged the short; probing gave 0.22 Ω at the chip pins but 0.08 Ω at one particular capacitor, with a suspect tantalum reading 0.11 Ω.[230] The schematic's three tantalums across the 5 V rail were lifted and proved innocent — a textbook case of being led up the garden path.[230] Sharp probes matter, since oxide on solder joints makes low-ohms readings fluctuate.[230] The same method on a programmer board found the 5 V rail flatly shorted, and a healthy rail afterwards measured 11 Ω in both directions.[1061] Measuring current instead of resistance also localises the fault: an intercom drawing 197 mA against a 200 mA bench limit showed the 5 V rail itself was fine and the problem lay elsewhere.[710]

Hiccup behaviour is its own signature. A 5 V rail captured on a scope starting up to 4.5 V, shutting back down and repeating every second or two indicates the supply is going into overcurrent protection, which in one repair appeared only after capacitors had been changed — the original fault having been large ripple on an otherwise accurate rail.[1203]

## Interdependence between rails

The 5 V rail rarely fails in isolation, because supplies are often referenced to each other rather than independently. In one instrument every rail's voltage reference was tied to the 15 V rail, including the 5 V regulator's, so a fault dragging the 15 V rail down dragged the 5 V and the negative 15 V down with it — with all rails dropping by exactly the same percentage, which is itself the tell.[804] The coupling ran the other way too: an overload on the 5 V rail could cause the other channels to lose regulation.[804] In a portable PC supply the minus 5 V rail was derived from the minus 12 V output, so a dead minus 12 V guaranteed a dead minus 5 V and both had to be traced back to the same cause.[1364] A failure that puts excessive current through a DC-DC converter can take out the 5 V output and, through it, everything downstream.[E1IqcGcZKHE]

## Use in circuit

Beyond bulk logic supply, the 5 V rail serves as the reference for ordinary interfacing. A pull-up resistor tied to the 5 V rail at one end can be pulled low through a series resistor to emulate a button press without any risk to the target.[liWWY5cSs4Q] A control panel read a button ladder as an analogue voltage between the 5 V rail and an auxiliary input through a built-in ADC, so a dicky contact produced voltages all over the place and inconsistent decoding.[1360] A digital input specified with a 0.5 V minimum low and a high threshold above 1 V can simply be tied to the 5 V rail when the fixed state is wanted.[1246] Development boards commonly expose both 3.3 V and 5 V for breadboard work.[888] When powering a prototype's 5 V rail from a bench supply, overvoltage protection set to around 5.1 V is the guard against the knob being moved or misset to 12 V and destroying expensive hardware.[1265]
