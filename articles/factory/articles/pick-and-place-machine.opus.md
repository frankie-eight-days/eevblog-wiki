# pick and place machine

A pick and place machine is the automated placement stage of a surface-mount assembly line: it takes components from reels, tubes and trays and sets them onto a solder-pasted board, one after another, with no manual handling.[127] It exists to remove labour from board assembly — the through-hole alternative is a line of workers installing parts by hand, which costs time, effort and money — and a modern placement machine churns out boards far faster with far less effort, set up once and then run at the push of a button.[127] The machine does not solder anything: it presses each part into the wet solder paste, which is tacky enough to hold the component in position until the board reaches the reflow oven, where the pre-applied paste actually forms the joints.[684]

Because the machine sits at the centre of the manufacturing cost model, its constraints propagate backwards into schematic capture, bill-of-materials selection, footprint choice and panelisation. Much of what is called design for manufacture is really design for the placement machine.[1307]

## Head, nozzles and placement speed

The head picks a part up with a vacuum nozzle, carries it across the board and sets it down; the component is held only by suction for the whole of that traverse.[1307][197] Cycle time is dominated by the XY return trip between the feeder bank and the board, so machines carry multiple nozzles to amortise that travel: a dual-nozzle head picks two components before moving to the board, eliminating one round trip, and a six-nozzle head can pick six passives at once.[264] Feeders arranged so that two parts can be picked in succession without returning to the start are a real time advantage.[264]

Part mass sets the speed limit. Lighter components can be accelerated harder without being flung off the nozzle, so small passives move quickly while a large power resistor forces the head to slow down for both the pick and the traverse.[1307] The very smallest parts also demand different hardware — an ultra-high-speed head capable of 0201 is a physically different head that has to be fitted or selected on the machine.[1307]

Two broad technology classes exist. Cheaper and older machines place 0603 and larger reliably; they may manage 0402 but with worse yield, so an assembler may charge more for 0402 placement because the job has to go on one of their more advanced machines.[186] Components mounted at 45 degrees are handled without difficulty and need no special accommodation.[1323]

Throughput is quoted in components per minute on production equipment,[1468] in components per hour on prototyping machines — a small-run machine of that class runs around 3,500 components an hour while still handling micro BGAs and 0201 packages.[207] A complete panel of ten small boards has been timed at just under one minute forty seconds from entering the head to leaving the far side.[264]

## Alignment: fiducials and vision

The machine locates the board optically. A fiducial is a reference mark read by the placement camera so the machine knows where to place components accurately.[1512] The typical form is a 1 mm diameter circle with the solder mask cleared around it, acting as a vision alignment point.[239] Fiducials belong on the copper layer, never on silkscreen: silkscreen registers imperfectly against copper, and any offset between the two would be transferred directly into placement error.[1512] A panel usually carries three fiducials, top and bottom, along with tooling holes in the corners.[239][264] Fiducials are normally placed outside a package, though a single mark centred under a large chip is also used, with the vision system finding that mark as the chip centre.[405]

Component-level vision closes the loop on the part itself: a fixed downward-looking camera images the picked component, finds its centre and computes an offset, so a part that came out of the tape at the wrong angle is autocorrected before placement.[795] Higher-end machines add laser alignment on each head alongside full camera alignment and a nozzle changer.[358] Adding vision to a home-built machine is now easy and cheap, which was not the case ten or fifteen years earlier.[740]

## Feeders and reel capacity

Every distinct part number on a board occupies its own feeder. Components arrive on reels — a thousand or three thousand parts to a reel is ordinary — and the reels mount into feeders carried on wheel-in carts, front and back of the machine.[1697] A large machine might carry sixty or a hundred feeders,[1697] a mid-range one twenty to fifty reels,[239] and a semi-automatic twin-head machine up to 120.[358] ICs can be supplied in tubes that slip into the machine and shoot the chips out,[127] and parts made in waffle trays are picked directly from the tray instead of from tape, at the cost of holding fewer parts and needing changing more often.[1132]

Feeder count is a hard ceiling on a design. A machine that supports only forty reels is already half consumed by a design carrying twenty-six distinct capacitor types.[1307] Wide tape occupies several slots at once.[1307] Once a design exceeds the feeder capacity of a single machine — around eighty line items on one particular machine — the board is not unmanufacturable, it simply has to go through multiple passes, or through two or more machines in series.[1307] Production lines are built this way deliberately: three physical machines in series can present something like 300 feeders, with one machine often loaded entirely with passives.[1697] A board too big for one machine's feeder complement is routed through the first, second and third machines in turn before reaching the reflow oven.[12] Even a modest board can require loading up several machines because the reels will not fit on one.[1679]

Parts must be supplied in a form the machine can accept. Cut tape with no reel and no leader tape is useless to an assembly house.[127] Loose tape can be handled by some machines, but many assemblers will have to re-reel it and will charge for doing so.[m9tza_c4sxc] Machines also lose parts during placement, so consigned stock should include a few percent overage — a rule of thumb of around 5% for cheap passives, with expensive precision parts worth flagging for extra care.[m9tza_c4sxc]

## Bill-of-materials consolidation

Because a feeder slot is a scarce resource, consolidating part values is a direct manufacturing saving. Seven different resistor values on one small board is worth questioning: each one eliminated is one less reel to put on the machine.[1306] Where a calculation calls for an odd value such as 4.99 k, two 10 k resistors in parallel reuse an existing line item instead of buying another reel and consuming another slot.[1307] Two identical MOSFETs placed back to back give a second body diode from the same bill-of-materials item, saving a reel.[931] Choosing a 1 A transistor already on the machine over a lower-rated part that saves two cents is normally the correct trade, once the extra reel, the sourcing effort and the machine slot are counted.[1307] The same logic favours standing jellybean support values: keeping a 240 ohm resistor permanently loaded for LM317 designs and reusing it elsewhere rather than adding a 220 ohm.[1438] Where several bulk capacitors of one value are used in parallel, the design gets bulk pricing and still occupies only one feeder.[742] Companies that manufacture in house apply this across their whole catalogue, since a new component means new stock to purchase, store, load onto the machines and change over.[1405][ag-MjKAfATw]

## Board handling and panelisation

The machine grips the board by a tooling strip along the top and bottom edges, about 10 mm wide, which lets it sit in rails and be moved through automatically.[127] Tooling holes of around 3.2 mm hold the board in place and move it along the conveyor into the machine and out to the reflow oven.[239][1158][552] The panel frame is otherwise dead area — a mounting frame carrying tooling holes and fiducials and nothing else — though it can be used to carry test or other functional circuitry.[552]

Panelising is what makes small boards economic. A single small PCB fed through on its own leaves the machine badly under-utilised, since it can only place one board's worth of parts before the conveyor moves on.[239] Stepping twelve boards into one panel lets all twelve be assembled in one loading; the parts still go down one at a time, but the process overhead is amortised.[127] With two boards on a panel the head, having already picked a part, can place it on both without a separate board pass — not quite double throughput, but close.[1299] The assembler, not the bare-board fabricator, defines the ceiling: the PCB house states its maximum panel size and the assembly house states the maximum panel its machine can take.[255]

Stiffness matters as much as size. A panel held only at the top and bottom rails will sag in the middle when the head comes down to place parts, and a 0.8 mm panel warps visibly under its own weight.[1158][127] The usual remedy is a custom tray from the assembler that the panel sits snugly inside, supported across its whole area.[1158] Boards that get too thin become a persistent nuisance, particularly for double-sided loads where the assembled first side prevents the board from simply being laid on a flat carrier.[761]

Length is rarely the binding constraint — height and width inside the machine are.[465] A board over a metre long can be assembled either in one pass on a machine big enough to take it, or in halves, moving the board along and realigning between passes.[533] Comparable limits govern LED tape, where the placement machine's working area sets the strip length and longer runs are joined at the factory.[1617] Fully routed boards with no breakaway tabs cannot ride the rails at all and are run in a custom jig instead, sometimes several at a time.[465][335]

## Design consequences

Single-sided loading is cheaper, so putting every component on one face is worth real effort — mounting power devices on the underside, or flipping the whole board, avoids a second placement operation.[744] A double-sided load costs more and takes longer because the board must go through the machine at least twice.[219] The same reasoning favours a light pipe through a hole in the board over a top-mounted LED that would force a second pass just for that part.[547]

Anything that cannot go through the machine is an extra production step with its own cost: once a design commits to surface mount, the aim is for as much of it as possible to be surface mount so the whole board comes out of the machine finished, with no bolts, washers or bolted-on heatsinks to add afterwards.[1347] Surface-mount versions of normally through-hole parts serve this end — pin headers and connectors given SMD pads are placed like any other component and reflowed with everything else,[1097] and surface-mount cells in an 1812 case can be placed with an ordinary machine.[1242] A cheap all-SMD display that eliminates hand soldering removes the hand-assembly labour from the price entirely.[298] Surface-mount switches are effectively machine-only parts: hand placement cannot achieve the alignment needed for keys to protrude cleanly through a front panel, and closely spaced switches leave no room for an iron.[11]

Placement constraints can also show up as apparently odd layout. Bypass capacitors routed around the outside of a chip rather than tucked against its pins, together with unusually large footprints, are consistent with an assembly house's own placement requirements rather than an electrical choice.[1512] Unpopulated alternative footprints let a product be re-spun quickly by changing only the placement program, so a different display or connector can be fitted to whatever is available that month.[295]

## The surrounding line

Placement sits between paste printing and reflow. Paste is applied through a stencil or dispensed automatically, inspected, then placed and reflowed with a thermal profile set individually for each board type.[12] Pasted boards must reach the machine quickly — beyond roughly an hour the paste dries out and is no good.[163] Fiducials serve the paste dispenser as well as the placement machine, giving it the same two-corner reference before it imports the paste file.[163] After reflow the board normally goes to optical inspection, which compares it against a golden reference board and flags components that fell off the nozzle, were misplaced, or tombstoned; those are then reworked by hand, which is why an otherwise machine-built board sometimes carries a handful of visibly hand-soldered parts.[1522]

The data handed to the assembler is a placement file listing each designator, its footprint and its XY location relative to a chosen reference point, usually the bottom-left corner, in CSV or a similar format; this is what gets fed into the machine.[127] It should be accompanied by the overlay diagram showing reference designators and by a physical sample built exactly as intended, because the assembler cannot infer intent.[127] Programming the machine is a real job with a real cost — an emergency assembly that requires bumping other customers' jobs to program the machine immediately can be quoted at several times the normal price.[VnrtneFP_6A]

## Machine cost and the small-volume question

Production equipment is expensive in a way that is not obvious from the machine price alone. A machine on its own may be around 100K, but each feeder costs about $2,000, so a fully equipped line comes to roughly half a million.[264] Prototyping-class equipment is cheaper but not cheap: an entry manual machine at about $19,000 Australian rises to roughly $22,000 with manual feeders and over $40,000 with a good number of automatic feeders, since automatic feeders are the expensive part.[795] A desktop production machine sits around 15,000 Australian fully loaded with 48 feeders, against a 10K base price before feeders, freight and clearances.[uR69Vmh7qnI] Do-it-yourself kits reach much lower — around $1,300 — but at 200 to 400 components per hour they are well short of professional throughput, and the kit in question shipped with no feeder support at all, requiring cut tape to be laid on the table for the camera to find.[740] Feeders, not motion, are what make a machine genuinely usable.[740]

The practical judgment is that owning one has a narrow window of usefulness: the boards, the part count and the volume all have to line up.[740] Below that window, setup time exceeds the time to place parts by hand, and above small batches the bottleneck moves to workflow — paste, oven, handling — rather than to the placer itself, since twenty panels cannot simply be pasted in advance and queued without the paste drying.[740] Reported experience with sub-$10,000 desktop machines centres on feeder reliability and absent vendor support rather than on placement accuracy.[5WxyCvXBoSY] For batches of around fifty boards, hand assembly remains the rational choice, and the deciding factor is not only the purchase price: as Ian Johnston puts it, "It's not just the cost of the pick and place, it's the learning."[a4Xpsenpd6E] The alternative is to hand the work to an assembler and pay per board.[a4Xpsenpd6E]

## Beyond PCBs

The same architecture — vacuum end effector, machine vision for orientation, computer-planned placement pattern — is applied outside electronics assembly. In flexible solar panel production a robotic pick and place picks up individual die-cut modules, checks their orientation and lays them onto a fabric substrate in a computer-determined pattern before the fabric is laser-cut to outline.[S-UqkM8iDKM] Vacuum-assisted manual lifting rigs used for handling boxes are described in the same terms, as the manufacturing equivalent of a placement machine.[1685]
