# crt

A cathode ray tube (CRT) is an evacuated glass tube in which a beam of electrons is accelerated toward a phosphor screen and steered across it to draw an image.[524][159] For most of the twentieth century it was the only practical display for televisions, oscilloscopes, computer terminals and instrument front panels, and it remains the defining component of vintage test gear: the part that sets the depth of the enclosure, carries the lethal voltages, and is usually the first thing to fail.[628][208][WHwf26JCUec] The underlying insight — that a picture can be built from rows of varying brightness rather than from independently addressed dots, with the beam steered by electromagnets and its intensity set by a voltage — is what turned the tube into television.[1577]

## Construction and operation

The tube is evacuated, with a screen at the front and a narrow neck at the rear carrying the electron gun and the deflection assembly.[524] Early oscilloscope tubes used a round faceplate; later ones adopted a rectangular front that still tapers back into a round neck.[524] Drive electronics sit on a small neck board pushed onto the base pins, with the filament driver and associated circuitry mounted directly on the back of the tube.[839][523] In a television the beam is steered by a deflection yoke and driven from a line output transformer, with the video path handled by a single integrated jungle chip feeding a three-transistor driver on the neck board.[1246][970][830]

The screen face carries a positive charge in order to attract the electrons bombarding it from the rear, which is also why the front of a CRT collects dust.[BUW6h88weXU][Uk5F3rQNUkU]

Deflection comes in two forms, and the choice is fundamental rather than cosmetic. Electromagnetic deflection uses coils around the neck; electrostatic deflection uses plates inside the envelope. The Sony WatchCam pocket monitor uses a full electromagnetic deflection tube, identifiable by its coil windings, in contrast to the electrostatic Sinclair flat tube.[1033] Small portable televisions and the flat Sinclair design differ not only in deflection but in acceleration technology as well.[830]

## High voltage

The anode supply is the dangerous part of any CRT-based instrument. A Tektronix 2225 oscilloscope runs 7 kV on the anode, generated by a voltage multiplier fed from the mains board and covered by a plastic shield and warning labels; the anode lead is routed across the chassis under a plastic retaining clip.[208] A Fluke PM3370B combiscope uses an EHT driver transformer feeding a tripler that takes roughly 5 kV to 14 kV for the tube.[1452] In televisions the high voltage comes off the line output transformer and reaches the tube through the anode cap.[830] Sinclair's flat pocket television is at the other extreme, with an EHT of 2.4 kV produced by a ZTX655 and a multiplier chain, and 1,440 V on the repeller electrode.[553][554]

These supplies remain charged after power is removed, so instruments must be treated as live even when switched off.[523] The rule applies to miniature tubes too: a camcorder viewfinder CRT does not store the energy a full-size monitor or television does, but the same high-voltage precautions still apply.[380]

## Oscilloscope tubes

In an analog oscilloscope the beam sweeps across the screen from left to right while the vertical amplifier deflects it in Y, so the trace is drawn continuously rather than stored.[159] A digital oscilloscope performs the same function with stored samples; in manual operation the two behave identically, and the claim that analog instruments are harder to use does not survive comparison with a well-laid-out analog front panel.[86] An old second-hand analog CRO remains the recommended first oscilloscope for a beginner, with a 20 MHz dual-channel instrument sufficient for hobby work.[86]

CRTs could also render text. The Tektronix 213 drives its readout through a custom CRT character generator ROM whose X, Y and Z axis outputs are switched to the tube only in multimeter mode and disabled in scope mode.[628] Later Tektronix instruments print power-on self-test results as text on the CRT, which makes diagnosis awkward when the tube itself is the fault.[1203] High-end analog scopes provide separate intensity controls for the waveform and the readout, along with waveform focus, beam find and scale illumination.[695] Some tubes carry a removable contrast filter on the front; a 2465B missing that filter still works normally.[695] Vectorscopes of the period exposed astigmatism and geometry adjustments on the rear panel as a matter of course.[756]

The tube also sets the size floor for a portable instrument. In the Tektronix 213 the surrounding circuitry could be shrunk with modern parts but the CRT could not, so a redesign would not be substantially smaller.[628]

## Geometry and image faults

Dark rounding in the corners of a CRT image — vignetting — is not caused by a circular mask over a rectangular screen.[524] It occurs when the deflection coil sits too far back along the neck, so the beam at extreme deflection angles clips the inside of the tube; sliding the coil forward to the base of the neck restores a full test pattern, though the coil's rotation must then be reset.[524] Apparent pulsing of screen brightness is an artifact of the scan update rate interacting with the camera, and shutter-priority exposure avoids it.[524] The mesh over the front panel of such instruments is a screening mesh that stops CRT scanning frequencies escaping.[524]

Geometry can also be trimmed magnetically. A small magnet fitted at the factory on the WatchCam board adjusts the image, but the effect is slight — it expands the right-hand side a little — which argues against the tubes being individually matched to their boards on the production line.[q1CaC_Ezt-c] External neodymium magnets warp the raster dramatically enough to animate the image.[Z6yLBqc5SCc]

CRTs need a warm-up period before reaching full brightness, so a vintage machine that appears dead on power-up may simply not have come up yet.[955]

## Miniature and flat tubes

Very small CRTs were produced in quantity for camcorder viewfinders and pocket televisions. A Sony electronic viewfinder tube is a conventional CRT in miniature, its assembly lifting straight out of the housing.[380] Broadcast-style shoulder cameras used a CRT viewfinder rather than an LCD, with a tally light on the front to show the subject that recording is under way.[1391] Portable televisions of the mid-1980s used some of the smallest monochrome tubes available, packaged with a UHF/VHF tuner and battery operation.[830][970] Small battery-powered oscilloscopes were built around miniature tubes as well.[813][416]

The Sinclair FTV1 flat-screen pocket television is the notable exception to the conventional geometry: a flat CRT rather than an LCD, and by most accounts the only flat-screen television built around a cathode ray tube.[554][553] Its deflection plates are labelled F and L for frame and line rather than X and Y, and it carries three control grids fed from an on-board video amplifier.[554] Because the beam strikes the screen obliquely, the tube cannot produce as sharp a spot as a conventional CRT, and a Fresnel lens on the front corrects the viewing geometry; the widescreen format reduces how far the beam must be deflected, trading physical size against drive power.[554] In some units the envelope is transparent, exposing the plates directly.[553]

The same beam-steering principle scales in the other direction: an electron beam lithography writer is functionally a CRT with a spot size of 2 nanometres, rastered across a chip.[1594]

## Service and repair

CRTs dominate the mechanical design of the instruments that contain them. In a Tektronix TDS540D the tube must come at least half way out before the main board can be lifted from the chassis.[WHwf26JCUec] In a Tektronix 2225 the vertical gain adjustment pots — R145 and its channel-two counterpart — are located on the main board underneath the CRT.[208] In an HP 9845B a board extends the full height of the machine to reach the tube.[1404] A Macintosh SE's tube is attached to the front bezel, so the rear case lifts away from it.[414]

Mounting arrangements vary in sophistication. The Osborne 1 rigidly mounts its 5-inch NEC tube to the inner plastic, leaving the outer shell to provide the shock absorption, which is cheap and effective without a dedicated spring mount.[955] An HP 85 uses a welded aluminium bracket running the full depth of the case.[903] Placing a tube very close to a board below it, as in the Stanford Research SR430, is poor practice.[452]

Faults cluster in a few places. A neck board can work loose from the tube base entirely, which is enough to kill the display and is repaired by pushing it back on.[523] CRT driver boards fail, sometimes with visible fire damage, while the power supply and digital sections continue to work normally.[WHwf26JCUec] A dead CRT can also be secondary: on a Compaq Portable the negative rails feeding the display do nothing until the power supply is repaired, so the tube cannot be tested at all beforehand.[1354][1364] A raster that is present but extremely faint and compressed indicates a tube or drive problem rather than a supply failure.[9viKKRFhQog] Residual dark fringing at the corners can persist after other faults are cleared and is not affected by the rotational position of the deflection components.[523]

## Replacement and obsolescence

Where an instrument provides a video output, replacing a failed tube with a small LCD panel is a practical alternative to repairing the CRT drive. A TDS540D with a dead CRT still works fully through its VGA output, making a 6- or 7-inch VGA-compatible panel a viable substitute that is lighter, lower power and more reliable.[1185][1420] Working purely from an external monitor is possible but awkward, because the softkey labels no longer line up with the front-panel buttons.[1185][1354] Video hardware of the period frequently supported both paths from one chipset, as with the Chips and Technologies 65545, which could drive either a CRT output or an LCD.[1028]

CRT televisions are no longer manufactured, and they make up a large share of current electronic waste.[qDWLTSfrq_E][xiXucGYuQ6M] Instruments built to service them have been obsoleted alongside them; a Philips PM5639 colour analyser, which uses red, green and blue filter lenses to take a spot measurement of a single point on the screen, has no remaining market even though the underlying technique still applies to calibrating modern LCD monitors.[967]
