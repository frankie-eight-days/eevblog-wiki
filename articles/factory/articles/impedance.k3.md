# impedance

Impedance, designated by the symbol Z and measured in ohms, is the AC equivalent of resistance: it is the ratio of AC voltage to AC current and can be thought of as "AC resistance," with the familiar Ohm's law relation Z = V/I still applying.[1728] Unlike DC resistance, impedance is a complex quantity combining a real (resistive) component and an imaginary (reactive) component, the imaginary part representing the phase relationship between voltage and current across the element.[1729][1470]

## Definition and complex form

Impedance is defined as Z = R + jX, where R is the real resistive component and X is the reactance, the imaginary reactive component.[1728][1730] Because reactance can be inductive (+jX_L) or capacitive (−jX_C), the sign of the imaginary part depends on whether the circuit is dominated by inductance or capacitance, and both X_L and X_C are expressed in ohms so they add directly to resistance.[1728][1730] Impedance can be written in rectangular form (real plus imaginary) or polar form (magnitude and phase angle), the two representations being interchangeable; polar form is preferred when multiplying or dividing, for instance when calculating current as I = V/Z.[1729][1661]

The AC form of Ohm's law replaces R with Z: V = I·Z, where V, I, and Z are all vectors carrying real and imaginary components.[1469][1729] Solving AC circuits is otherwise identical to DC analysis — Kirchhoff's laws, nodal and mesh analysis, Thevenin and Norton equivalents, and superposition all work unchanged once impedances replace resistances.[1729] Series impedances sum directly (Z_T = Z1 + Z2 + Z3), and parallel impedances combine reciprocally, exactly as resistors do in DC.[1728]

## Impedance versus reactance

Impedance and reactance are distinct terms even though both are measured in ohms. Reactance is purely the complex, frequency-dependent part — the inductive or capacitive opposition alone, with no resistive element — whereas impedance encompasses both the real resistance and the imaginary reactance of a complete circuit or component.[1728][1661][1660] For a physical capacitor, for example, the capacitive reactance X_C = 1/(2πfC) is the contribution of the capacitance itself, while the total impedance adds the equivalent series resistance (ESR) and the lead/package inductance at the frequency of interest.[1482][859] The terms are often used interchangeably in casual speech, but the distinction matters.[1482][1660]

Impedance should also not be confused with the characteristic impedance of a transmission line: both are expressed in ohms, but a component's impedance changes with every incremental change in frequency, whereas the distributed characteristic impedance of a transmission line is essentially constant with frequency.[1728]

## Related quantities

Admittance, symbol Y, is the reciprocal of impedance, Y = 1/Z, measured in siemens, and decomposes as Y = G + jB, where the real part G is conductance (the inverse of resistance) and the imaginary part B is susceptance (the inverse of reactance).[1728] Impedance is by far the quantity used in everyday practice; conductance finds use in fields such as electrochemistry, while susceptance and admittance appear mainly in more complex circuit analysis.[1728] In AC power calculations, apparent power uses impedance rather than resistance: S = I²Z and S = V²/Z, since Z includes both the real resistive and imaginary reactive components.[1730]

## Frequency behaviour of components

Capacitors and inductors have no fixed resistance; instead they exhibit a frequency-dependent reactance, with capacitive reactance falling as frequency rises and inductive reactance rising with frequency.[859] The total impedance of a real capacitor is the sum of its ESR, its capacitive reactance, and its inductive reactance at the frequency of interest, producing the characteristic V-shaped impedance-versus-frequency curve with a minimum at self-resonance.[859][1103] Electrolytic ESR is conventionally specified at 100 kHz because the capacitor is essentially resistive around that frequency; above resonance, package inductance dominates and impedance climbs again.[1103] Capacitors of different values and packages have different impedance characteristics, which is why bypass networks commonly place multiple values — for example 10 µF, 1 µF, and 0.1 µF — in parallel near a chip.[932][1129] Care is needed with many parallel values, since combinations can create a resonant impedance peak at some switching frequency and destabilise the circuit.[1117]

## Measurement

An LCR meter fundamentally measures impedance: it drives the device under test through a range resistor at a selectable test frequency (for example 100 Hz, 120 Hz, 1 kHz, 10 kHz, 100 kHz) and derives impedance from the voltage-to-current ratio; every other parameter — capacitance, inductance, quality factor, dissipation factor — is then calculated mathematically from the measured impedance, via relationships such as |Z| = √(R_S² + X_S²).[757][1473][81] Dedicated impedance measurement can also be integrated into a circuit: the Analog Devices AD5933 combines a DDS signal generator, a 12-bit 1 MS/s ADC, and DSP performing Fourier transforms to report impedance over an I²C interface.[922]

Measuring capacitors in circuit works surprisingly often, but surrounding circuitry with very low impedance swamps the measurement — readings become meaningless when the parallel impedance around the part falls to tens of ohms, and below roughly 10 ohms in-circuit measurement fails entirely.[1474]

## Practical consequences in circuits

Currents take the lowest-impedance path through a circuit and back to the source; at high frequencies this is the path of lowest impedance, not lowest DC resistance, which is why bypass capacitor placement and ground return routing govern where switching currents actually flow.[1085] A low-impedance, low-inductance ground plane — often implemented as a flood-filled plane — is the default good practice, with star-point grounding reserved for cases with a specific justification; a solid plane behaves as a distributed capacitor with very low impedance and is essential for fast, high-current logic such as ECL, which will not work on a two-layer board.[500][452] Power traces are likewise made fatter than signal traces specifically to lower their impedance.[244]

Termination matters once cables behave as transmission lines. An unterminated coax run can produce drastic amplitude and phase variations with frequency — at around 35 MHz the phase between two similar cables can even swap over — because reflections interact with the cable's impedance and length.[652] The standard RF system impedance is 50 ohms, as used in dummy loads and terminations.[1003] Instrument input impedance also loads the circuit under test: a 10 MΩ multimeter input can significantly disturb a precision voltage divider even when it is orders of magnitude larger than the circuit impedance being measured.[584] A rough rule of thumb for back-of-envelope work is that any parallel element an order of magnitude larger than the relevant impedance can be ignored.[453]

Output versus input impedance determines signal integrity in probing and coupling: a low-impedance output will not develop significant amplitude from stray capacitive coupling, whereas a high-impedance input node can show hundreds of millivolts of coupled signal from a nearby trace.[717] Conversely, a high-impedance node loaded by any significant impedance is swamped, while driving it from a low-impedance output — or shorting the input — suppresses the effect.[983] Fault-finding exploits the same principle: an unexpected 1 kΩ path to mains earth is classified as quite low impedance and a hazard when probing, since it is far from an open circuit.[279] Ferrite chip beads are characterised by their impedance at 100 MHz for filtering applications.[795] Outside electronics proper, transthoracic impedance — the human body's opposition to current flow, varying from about 25 to 180 ohms depending on body mass, skin resistance, and tissue — is a determining factor in successful defibrillation.[1008]
