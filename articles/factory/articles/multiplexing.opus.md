# multiplexing

Multiplexing is the practice of sharing one physical resource — a set of drive pins, a converter, a measurement front end, a transmitter — among several loads or signals by servicing them one at a time in rapid rotation. Its purpose is almost always to trade something cheap (switching speed, control complexity) for something expensive (pin count, silicon, connectors, discrete drivers).[1044][1491][948] The cost is never zero: a multiplexed resource is only available to each user for a fraction of the time, and that fraction shows up somewhere as reduced brightness, reduced sample rate, or reduced bandwidth.[948][1717][1090]

## Displays

Display driving is the classic case. A small seven-segment LCD can be driven statically, with one common pin and one pin per segment, and needs no multiplexing at all — a 3½-digit multimeter display might get away with forty pins.[1044] The pin count becomes untenable as segment count rises: an 8 by 14 segment LCD is given four common pins instead of one, and the segments are then multiplexed against those four commons purely to keep the driver pin count down.[1044] A very low pin count on an LCD data sheet is itself the tell that the commons must be multiplexed.[248] The same logic makes dense custom glass possible; the routing pattern on the back of a multiplexed panel is what lets a complex display escape with very few pins.[851]

Because the segments are lit sequentially rather than continuously, a multiplexed display is a flickering display observed at a low enough shutter speed. A vacuum fluorescent display driven this way shows on-off bursts at around 60 Hz, and brightness is set by adjusting the duty cycle and frequency of that burst.[717] The multiplexing on a bench instrument's display can be visible as flicker when the instrument is filmed or viewed on another screen.[576] In a working multiplexed digit driver each digit's drive appears staggered in time from the others when probed simultaneously, which is why all digits lighting up on power-on is evidence that the multiplexing chain — shift register, data latch, digit drivers — is functioning even before any data has been loaded.[689] A 74HC164 shift register cycling through the digits is a representative implementation, with the multiplex and drive circuitry mounted on the back of the display panel itself.[689] Segment refresh only needs to beat the eye: at least 40 times per second per segment is enough that the sequential switching is invisible.[1493]

Multiplexing also serves as a current-budget tool rather than a pin-count tool. Where a design cannot supply enough current to light every element at once — forty segments across five displays, for example — multiplexing one display at a time, blanking and shifting between displays, cuts the simultaneous load by a factor of five at the price of constant data shifting.[1491]

The counter-case is direct drive. For a Nixie tube display, multiplexing was rejected in favour of driving every element continuously: multiplexing raises peak currents, produces a dimmer display, and leaves the result uncertain, whereas direct drive gives a known constant brightness at the cost of many more high-voltage driver channels.[948] The judgement stands that multiplexing earns its place mainly when space or pin count is genuinely constrained.[948]

Very simple segment driving can sidestep multiplexing altogether. A single e-ink or similar segment can be placed between two microcontroller I/O pins and AC-driven by toggling the two pins against each other, then tri-stated when the segment should not be driven — a different and much simpler proposition than multiplexing.[1241]

## Switch and sensor matrices

The reciprocal technique reads many inputs through few wires. A keypad is normally built as a switch matrix in the microcontroller: digital lines for the commons, digital lines for the rows, multiplexed and scanned continuously.[1360] A properly scanned matrix is tolerant of degraded contacts — a switch a couple of hundred ohms out of spec still reads correctly and is not confused with other buttons on the array — so a keypad that fails on one button is evidence that a matrix scan was not what the designer implemented.[1360] A 68-key musical keyboard uses the same arrangement with diodes at the matrix, sampling every key and recovering per-key displacement over a small number of cables.[256]

## Shared converters and measurement front ends

Instruments multiplex their analogue front ends into a single converter to avoid duplicating expensive hardware. An LCR meter measures differential voltage across the device and current through a selectable range resistor from about 1 ohm up to 10 meg, then multiplexes both into one ADC, with the remaining work done in maths and filtering.[757] A multimeter chipset multiplexes its input network internally: the commons are shorted to ground for AC and DC volts, while in ohms and capacitance modes the ground connection is dropped and the lower end of the resistor network is activated instead.[853] A VNA built on spectrum analyser hardware reuses the existing measurement chain by alternating between a spectrum sweep and a reflected-power sweep, obtaining S11 with very little additional hardware.[1101] Even an older instrument without an ADC chip multiplexes: a slope-conversion design counts up in a decade counter, then multiplexes the counter output through a character generator ROM and cycles through the digits via digit transistors.[628]

Where converters are cheap enough relative to the specification, designers avoid sharing. A power meter with separate 16-bit SAR ADCs for voltage and current multiplexes nothing.[1693] A 16-channel data recorder gave each channel its own 16-bit converter, "none of that multiplexing rubbish", though bandwidth still scaled with channel count — 20 kHz analogue bandwidth in two-channel mode falling to 5 kHz with all sixteen channels sampling at once.[1090]

Front-panel behaviour can be multiplexed too, and not always desirably. Giving ohms and continuity separate rotary switch positions, rather than multiplexing them onto one position with a soft button, is preferable in use.[60]

## Interleaving in oscilloscopes

Digitising instruments multiplex ADCs to reach headline sample rates that a single converter cannot. The ADC08D1520 is a dual 8-bit converter rated 1.5 GSa/s per channel, or 3 GSa/s from a single input when the two halves are interleaved.[875][1477] Two such dual parts give four channels at full rate with no sharing; interleaving is what buys the doubled single-channel figure.[875]

The trade is visible directly in published specifications. A four-channel scope using two dual ADCs must multiplex them, so its 2.5 GSa/s single- or dual-channel figure halves to 1.25 GSa/s in four-channel configuration — and that 2.5 GSa/s is itself well under the 3 GSa/s the silicon can deliver, plausibly for noise reasons.[1477] Another design combines both ADC chips such that the individual channels cannot be separated at all, ruling out the channel-one-and-channel-three trick available on some scopes; 4 GSa/s on one channel becomes 2 GSa/s with a second channel enabled and 1 GSa/s with all four on.[1717] An ASIC that integrates two converters but exposes four inputs implies the same internal multiplexing, and is the natural basis for a cost-reduced variant once the non-recurring engineering cost of the ASIC has been absorbed.[1639][1717] Front-end analogue sections perform their own multiplexing alongside DC offsetting and level shifting before the converters.[37] Source measure units similarly carry muxing throughout the feedback and sensing paths.[718]

## Multiplexing power and beams

The principle extends beyond signals. A battery charger with four cells alternates them in pairs on a 500 ms period — cells one and three on while two and four are off — because charge and bypass paths cannot both be active on a cell without shorting it.[812] The multiplexed switching also interleaves brief measurement intervals of a few tens of milliseconds.[812] A single-emitter infrared power beaming system must track multiple receivers and alternate between them, multiplexing the delivered power across up to four devices from a claimed 5 W total.[1092] A phased array intended for wireless charging could in principle be multiplexed between transmit and receive to locate the target device.[1369] Frequency division multiplexing and related terminology are also common padding in wireless power marketing material with no supporting hardware behind it.[1408]

## Related uses

Where a bus cannot address the devices attached to it — several I²C peripherals sharing one address, or too few hardware serial ports — a multiplexer is one of the standard remedies, the alternatives being separate bit-banged lines or paying for an extra chip.[9hMsNOwY5AQ] Existing multiplexing infrastructure is also what makes display retrofits practical: a VFD-to-LED conversion can reuse the instrument's row and column drivers and multiplexing timing unchanged, substituting only the emissive technology.[1669] Dot-matrix LED modules likewise assume multiplexed driving as a matter of course.[960]
