# current limiting

Current limiting is the deliberate capping of the current a source will deliver, either by design inside a circuit or as a user-set threshold on a bench power supply. Its purpose is to bound the energy available to a fault: a supply that limits at a set current can have its output shorted indefinitely without damage, and a circuit fed from a limited source cannot draw enough current to destroy itself before the fault is noticed.[315][1688] Absent any limit, the only thing setting the current is the impedance of the load and the wiring, which in a fault condition is close to nothing.[1427]

## Constant current mode in a bench supply

A regulated supply with an adjustable limit behaves as a constant voltage source until the load demands the set current, then crosses over into constant current mode and lets the output voltage fall to whatever value sustains that current.[1688][1030] With 2 A set and a 3 A load connected, the output collapses to around 0.12 V while still delivering the full 2 A.[1030] Shorting the output of a supply limited to 0.5 A produces exactly 0.5 A and no more.[1688] The same behaviour appears as a diagnostic display: a 1 A limit into a faulty 3.3 V rail holds 1 A while the rail voltage drops far below nominal.[401]

In constant current mode the supply is functioning as a constant current generator, dialled to whatever value is wanted, even though it is normally thought of only as a protective ceiling.[1688] That protective framing is the usual one, because most of the time the circuit under test is being run in constant voltage mode and the limit exists only for when something goes wrong.[1688]

Limiting is not the only possible response to an overcurrent. Overcurrent protection, where implemented separately, switches the output off entirely instead of entering current limiting mode, which is the appropriate choice when continuous operation at the limit would itself be damaging.[439] Without it, a supply simply sits in current limit continuously as a normal supply does.[439] Some designs make the limiting behaviour selectable between several modes.[1606] The limit and the load state are usually indicated on the front panel, and a limiting supply may or may not disturb its own upstream source — a USB-powered instrument can current limit its outputs without collapsing the USB supply feeding it.[1552][337]

The crossover is not instantaneous. On a supply set to a 1 A limit driving a 20 V range, the output can ramp toward the full set voltage for several milliseconds before the control loop determines that limiting is needed and pulls the output back, producing visible overshoot on turn-on.[1402]

## Bringing up unfamiliar hardware

The standard bench procedure for powering an unknown or repaired board for the first time is to set a current limit well below what would cause damage and only then apply power. The margin is chosen against a rough estimate of expected consumption: 20 V at a 0.25 A limit is about 5 W into a board that should draw nothing like 5 W, which is an acceptable margin.[682] Typical working values are 100 mA for small logic and display boards[573][717][1531][qmcSEJehqZM] and around 1 A for a complete portable consumer device.[863] A first power-on of a repair candidate at 0.1 A rather than a more generous 1 A means that if the board is faulty there is not enough power behind the supply to do any harm.[717][1493] The same reasoning motivates substituting a current-limited bench supply for a board's mains supply during fault-finding, both for safety and because the exposed mains supply is itself hazardous to have on the bench.[1672] Where the required rail is high voltage, the ability to current limit is the main thing lost when a bench supply cannot reach the needed voltage.[1657]

A limit set too low creates its own failure mode. Power-on surge currents can exceed the steady-state draw by a wide margin, and if that surge trips the limit, the supply drops its voltage in response, which can in turn upset the converter or circuit being powered.[895] Choosing a limit for a finished product being tested is therefore a different judgement from choosing one for a design under development, where a tight limit such as 1.1 A per channel is a reasonable safety margin.[895][577]

## Current limiting inside circuits

The simplest implementation is a series resistor. Placing one in series with a chip's supply will not prevent CMOS SCR latchup, but it prevents the excess current of a latchup event from destroying the chip; the penalty is degraded high-frequency performance, which confines the technique to DC and low-frequency parts.[16] The same idea protects instrument inputs, where high-value resistors ahead of a chipset's pins limit the current during an overload without affecting accuracy, since the input impedance is high enough that 1% tolerance parts suffice.[853]

LEDs are the canonical case for series limiting, because their forward characteristic is steep enough that a small voltage error produces a large current error and there is nothing in the device itself to restrain the current.[1427] Driving LEDs directly from a chip output with no dropper resistor and no current limiter is a design fault, and one that would be expected to fail a design review.[1493] The consequence of omitting the limit entirely is destructive: 15 V across a small red LED lights the bonding wire itself, produces flame over the wire, and destroys the part.[857] Where an LED driver is only a MOSFET switch rather than a constant current stage, the limiting must be done externally in the LED strip, and provision on the board for a current limiting resistor — ideally with DIP-switch selection between two or four resistor values — is a worthwhile addition.[661][643]

Many integrated parts include limiting. A high-voltage op-amp intended for power supply service can accept 0 to 150 µA on a dedicated pin and translate that to a 0 to 700 mA adjustable output current limit, making it close to a complete supply in one package.[1701] Switching regulators offer an externally programmed limit set by a resistor[643], LED driver controllers implement cycle-by-cycle current limiting alongside short-circuit protection and undervoltage lockout[1252], energy-harvesting parts limit a switched auxiliary output to 300 mA internally[664], a nixie driver's totem-pole high-voltage output can be current limited through its bias input[948], and a regulator such as the LT3080 provides internal current limiting and thermal shutdown as standard.[222] A linear supply design can be simplified by using the regulator's own adjust pin to control current as well as voltage, rather than adding a separate limiting stage downstream, though a current sense resistor is still needed.[221] Host ports are commonly limited as well, with local regulation providing current-limited 500 mA to a front-panel USB connector.[360] Protection of this kind — current limiting together with overvoltage clamping — is what distinguishes industrial-grade I/O from ordinary I/O.[979]

Some products omit it. A supply may be sold with voltage and current displays but no current limiting at all[272], and a toothbrush charger may connect the rectified output of its coupling coil straight to the cell with nothing in series, relying instead on the maximum current the coil can deliver as the effective charging limit.[284]

## Interactions and side effects

Interrupting current to an inductive load causes the voltage to fly in one direction or the other, so when VI limiting engages in an amplifier driving a heavily inductive load, catch diodes to the positive rail and to ground are required to absorb it.[354]

Current limiting also confounds diagnosis. In a supply whose rails are of the current limiting type with series limit resistors, excessive loading anywhere causes the affected rail to read 20 to 30% low.[804] That signature can be misread: if one board on the 5 V rail were faulty, the limiting should pull down only that rail, so all rails sagging together points to a mechanism other than a single board fault — though limiting further downstream can also drag rails down.[803] Confirming that a supply is not entering current limit rules the supply out as the cause when a system fails to boot.[pKV_JiauAE4] A supply's own limit can intervene unexpectedly and slow an unrelated measurement, particularly with a modest wattage source and long leads.[1254]

Limiting works alongside, not instead of, other protection. An output fuse would not normally be expected to blow because the channel's current limiting stops it first, but it is worth having as backup.[440] When a supply rated to current limit at 3 A instead blows a pass transistor and its 5 A output fuse under a short applied through relay contacts, that is a defect rather than expected behaviour: a supply of that grade should current limit at 3 A and handle the fault properly, and a good supply should survive repeated dead shorts on its output, which is what the current limiting is for.[861][315]
