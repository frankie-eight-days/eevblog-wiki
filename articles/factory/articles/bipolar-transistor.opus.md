# bipolar transistor

The bipolar junction transistor (BJT) is a three-terminal semiconductor device whose collector current is set by the current forced into its base, making it the current-controlled counterpart to the voltage-controlled field effect transistor.[748][1736] Its terminals are named base, collector and emitter, in contrast to the gate, source and drain of a MOSFET.[Z_c4wjBpSzE][1394] Before the power MOSFET became commercially viable, bipolar transistors were the dominant power device, used for everything from driving motors to building power supplies, despite being limited in speed, fairly delicate, burdened with a safe operating area problem, and limited in gain.[1737] They remain in wide use where ruggedness, low-voltage operation and linear performance matter more than switching speed.[1599][1736]

## Operation and gain

A BJT and a MOSFET are physically different devices that operate by different mechanisms at the silicon level.[748] In a bipolar transistor the gain, or beta, is set by the physical construction inside the device — the doping of the lightly doped region, how thin it is, and the rest of the internal geometry — and that beta is what multiplies base current into collector current.[748]

Because the device is current driven, current must actually flow into the base for the transistor to turn on; there is no equivalent of simply raising a gate voltage.[708][1736] In switching service this makes the base current a permanent load on whatever drives it. A discrete BJT switch therefore needs a base resistor, and a design can shed that resistor entirely by substituting a MOSFET, which also removes the base-emitter drop from the circuit.[242]

The base current is small but never zero, and in high-impedance circuits it is a real error term. Hanging a bipolar transistor off an RC timing node loads that node with base current and corrupts the charge time, which is why a proper high-impedance buffer is used instead.[471] The same effect makes resistor ratios critical in circuits such as soft-latching power switches, where the base pull-up must be high — 100 k and above, with a 1 M and 22 µF timing pair — and the balance between values becomes delicate unless the design moves to MOSFETs.[262]

## Darlington and compound configurations

A single bipolar transistor does not necessarily have high current gain, and the classic remedy is the Darlington pair, which cascades two devices to multiply their betas.[1116][649] A 1964-vintage laboratory supply used a Darlington pass element precisely because the available bipolar transistor had low gain on its own, and the extra stage was needed to get usable performance.[649] The Sziklai pair is the alternative compound transistor for the same job.[1116] Darlington pairs also appear in trivial applications: a small continuity tester built around two bipolar transistors in Darlington configuration, three 1.5 V cells and an incandescent bulb has enough gain to conduct through the resistance of human skin.[Uk5F3rQNUkU]

The capacitance multiplier exploits the gain directly. An emitter follower fed from an RC filter passes the input voltage through to the output while drawing only a fraction of the load current through the filter resistor, so the effective capacitance seen by the load is the physical capacitance multiplied by the transistor's beta.[1116] Kilohm to tens-of-kilohm resistors with modest capacitor values are sufficient, and the pass element can be a single BJT, a Darlington, a Sziklai pair or a MOSFET — the principle is unchanged.[1116]

## Junction breakdown ratings

The base-emitter and base-collector junctions have reverse breakdown voltages that appear in the absolute maximum ratings of a data sheet rather than in the electrical characteristics table: VEBO for emitter-base and VCBO for base-collector, the emitter-base figure sometimes written as VBR.[1157][n4NBUruLyoo] Driven in reverse, the base-emitter junction breaks down and behaves as a zener at roughly 5 to 10 V; because of the construction of the device the base-collector junction breaks down far higher, typically in the 20 to 100 V region.[1157][n4NBUruLyoo]

VEBO is a poorly served specification. Typical parts sit around 6 to 12 V, anything past 20 V is rare, and Digi-Key offers no parametric search on it at all — Mouser and OctoPart do.[1157] Filtering for VEBO above 20 V returns on the order of 16 matches from roughly 50,000 transistors, many of them the same part, and some of the survivors are impractical packages such as TO-3.[1157] The 2SC3326 is one of the few usable devices, and the Rohm IMX25 dual general-purpose transistor happens to carry a 25 V VEBO without advertising it as a feature.[1157]

## The transistor as a bidirectional clamp

Two NPN transistors with their collectors joined and their bases joined, emitters facing outward — one device effectively flipped — form a bidirectional clamping zener usable against impulses, overloads and ESD.[1157][n4NBUruLyoo][1000] The circuit works by deliberately abusing the junction breakdowns just described: in each direction one base-emitter junction sits forward biased as an ordinary diode drop while the other reverse-breaks down as a zener.[1157][n4NBUruLyoo] It is a fast clamp, it appears in countless multimeters, and it is difficult to find documented in textbooks.[1157] Fluke used the arrangement extensively and it was widely copied.[1157] Its practical constraint is the scarcity of high-VEBO parts: an instrument with a 15 V diode test mode needs a reverse emitter-base breakdown around 20 V, which is exactly where the available selection collapses.[1157]

## Comparison with the MOSFET

MOSFETs are voltage driven, need no steady base current, and have lower on-resistance — RDS(on), against VCE(sat) in a bipolar — which translates into lower voltage drop and more current and power handling in a given package.[1736] They are also faster: the bipolar transistor exhibits a storage time, a delay of the order of microseconds between the drive being removed and the device actually turning off, and this is what limits the switching frequency of BJTs.[1409] Storage time is not always specified in the data sheet.[1409] For megahertz-region converters MOSFETs are the only practical choice, and an LLC resonant converter cannot reasonably be built with bipolars because their drive requirements are too heavy.[1736][1294]

Bipolars retain concrete advantages. They are more robust and hard to damage electrostatically, having no gate oxide to puncture; they tolerate overcurrent better; and they operate at ultra-low supply voltages where a MOSFET's gate threshold and biasing become awkward.[1599][1736][861] A 2N3055 in a power supply is close to unkillable, whereas an equivalent MOSFET such as a 150 V CEP60N15 is a plausible failure suspect in the same position.[861] BJTs also remain preferred in linear applications.[1736] Package habits differ too: a BJT is generally not available in SO8, so an SO8 device with several pins tied together is almost certainly a MOSFET.[1461]

The insulated gate bipolar transistor merges the two. Structurally it is a FET variant in which the low-resistance N+ substrate is replaced with a P substrate, after which the device behaves partly like a bipolar and partly like a MOSFET and achieves a lower effective on-resistance at high voltage — combining MOSFET drive with bipolar ruggedness.[1737][1620]

## Jellybean parts

A jellybean bipolar is one that has been available essentially forever — some date from the late 1950s — is stocked everywhere, and is cheap enough that the choice barely warrants thought.[1599] The 2N2222, 2N3904, BC547, BC549 and ST25C fall into this category for small-signal switching, with the 2N2222 the reflexive choice for an open-collector NPN where almost any NPN would serve.[242][1708][1423][1676] At the power end the 2N3055 and its complementary 2955 are the standing recommendation, though such parts move in lower volumes than medium- and low-power transistors.[1599] Higher-voltage jellybeans exist for specialised supply rails: a 2N5401-class 120 to 150 V device gives usable gain and low noise in a condenser microphone front end running from a 120 to 150 V rail.[609]

Selecting a non-jellybean part is a parametric search exercise. Searching Digi-Key's discrete BJT category for NPN devices still leaves around 9,000 candidates before a collector-emitter breakdown filter of 200 V and above narrows it for a high-voltage application such as Nixie tube driving.[948] The catalogue is large — roughly 22,500 BJTs at one distributor — but skewed by package and by which parameters are searchable.[1157]

## Applications

In a linear regulator the error amplifier compares the output divider against a reference and drives a pass element that may be a BJT or a MOSFET; the topology is indifferent to which.[861] The same op-amp-plus-transistor pattern builds a constant current source, with an NPN as the pass device on the low side or a bipolar on the high side, the op amp forcing its two inputs equal across a sense resistor.[1688]

Bipolar transistors also constitute the internals of many analogue ICs. Bipolar comparators draw supply currents of a few hundred microamps and are characterised by input bias and offset currents that follow directly from the input technology, unlike their CMOS equivalents.[1464] Low-noise audio op amps are often entirely bipolar, reaching figures such as 1.5 nV/√Hz.[1405] Conversely, a CMOS op amp built on a silicon-gate process substitutes MOSFETs for the traditional bipolar input devices in exchange for much lower power.[238] At high frequency, bipolar devices fabricated in silicon germanium are combined with CMOS on the same wafer in a SiGe BiCMOS process.[o2NxHu5Bsnk]

Where matching matters, discrete bipolars are inadequate and monolithic transistor arrays are used instead, because devices on the same die track one another; differential front ends typically use five or six transistors with their emitters tied together, sometimes with JFETs ahead of the bipolar stage.[932][879] The cascode — a common-emitter stage stacked with a common-base stage — is used for output stability in precision instrumentation, and can be built as a hybrid with a bipolar and a MOSFET.[718] As a follower the bipolar is more solid than a JFET, which manages only about 0.7 to 0.8 of unity gain in a phase splitter.[616]

The bipolar pair also appears as an equivalent circuit rather than as real silicon: a thyristor is modelled as a PNP and an NPN cross-connected, so that gate current entering the NPN base turns it on, which in turn conducts through the base of the PNP and latches the structure.[1172]

## Identification and failure

A functional NPN shows a diode drop from base to emitter, and the absence of that drop is direct evidence that a suspect part is either not a bipolar or is destroyed; comparing against a known-good unit settles which.[1520] Package alone is not diagnostic: the same SOT-23 footprint may hold a 2N2222 or an N-channel MOSFET such as an IRLML2803, and only tracing and analysing the surrounding circuit reveals which one the design intends.[675][284] Part numbering is equally treacherous, with the same numeric suffix appearing on both a high-voltage NPN and a MOSFET from different manufacturers, so the schematic symbol in the data sheet is the reliable discriminator.[1708] A cheap component tester will identify a surface-mount bipolar directly.[1020]

Power bipolars fail in the ways power devices generally do. Repeated thermal cycling works the solder joints of a heat-sinked device loose over time, producing intermittent faults in amplifiers and power stages.[1243] Undersized heatsinking on cheap output transistors such as an A1695 guarantees the same outcome sooner.[671] Bipolar power transistors are still found bolted to heatsinks in precision equipment such as AC voltage standards, and in unusually wide packages like the 2SC3858.[709][hVIo7vmIExw] A hot small-signal bipolar such as a BD136 is not necessarily itself faulty — it may simply be sinking current dumped into it by a failed pass transistor elsewhere.[861]
