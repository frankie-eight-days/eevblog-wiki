# hand soldering

Hand soldering is the manual formation of solder joints with an iron, as opposed to the automated wave and reflow processes that assemble most production boards.[256][239] It remains a core skill even for those who stencil and reflow their own work, because parts eventually need reworking and rework technique differs from first-pass assembly, particularly when removing components.[186] In production it is a distinct labour step with its own cost, and in teardowns it is a visible signature that separates machine-assembled boards from human intervention.[239][511]

## Technique

Surface mount is not the obstacle it is often assumed to be: the basic tooling is a chisel-tip iron, fine solder, and a well-designed board with well-designed pads, and useful results follow with little experience.[186] SO packages at 1.27 mm pin pitch are large enough to solder pin by pin with the iron and fine solder, one pin at a time.[186] Larger passives are trivial — 1206 parts are enormous by surface-mount standards and require nothing more than adequate eyesight to solder by hand.[558] Below that, 0603 begins to get annoying for both hand soldering and hand placement.[558]

The flux carried inside cored solder is generally sufficient for placing ordinary components; separate flux is not required for that stage.[1306] Gel flux is also used for hand soldering work.[433]

Access geometry matters as much as pitch. A part recessed among taller neighbours can force the iron to near-vertical to reach it, which is a layout consequence that should be anticipated at design time rather than discovered at the bench.[1306] A systematic order of attack prevents missed joints: having soldered one end of every part, the other end is known to be on the opposite side, so the board can be worked through in a single direction.[1306] On through-hole boards, components are fitted by height — flat parts such as resistors first, then ICs — so that the board sits level against the bench at each stage instead of rocking while it is being soldered.[555]

Too much solder is the characteristic hand-soldering error, and on fine-pitch parts it bridges pins directly.[1306][1353]

## Package limits

Some packages are simply outside the reach of an iron. Leadless packages such as LGA have no pads extending down the side of the case, so once the part is laid on the board the pads are not directly accessible to the iron at all; such chips are designed only for reflow.[181] The workaround is to invert the part and run individual bond wires out to a coarser prototyping footprint that can then be hand soldered.[181] A 0.5 mm leadless chip proved troublesome enough that the joints had to be reflowed before the circuit worked, raising the question of what would happen to the same part in volume production.[366] An 88-lead QFN at 0.4 mm pitch — about as fine as pin pitch gets in the industry — is challenging for anyone to solder by hand, and assumes solder mask can still be placed between the pads.[408]

Where miniaturisation is the whole point of a design and the smallest available packages are used to fit the smallest possible board, hand soldering ceases to be a viable option and reflow is mandatory.[866] Conversely, hand-solderability is a legitimate part-selection criterion alongside voltage range and accuracy: a three-pin SOT-23 is chosen partly because it is not too small to solder by hand.[87] Footprint size and difficulty of soldering are among the parameters weighed when selecting a converter IC.[139]

## Designing for hand soldering

Solder mask between pins is the single most important layout provision. Its absence — for example where mask has been removed as one large square over a fine-pitch footprint — permits solder to bridge between pins during hand, drag, or reflow soldering if too much solder is applied, and the problem intensifies as pitch drops below 0.5 mm.[1353] Getting mask between pins both prevents shorting during reflow or hand soldering and allows tighter conductor spacing.[1222] A practical target is a four- to five-thou gap of solder mask between pads; a couple of thou cannot be manufactured reliably.[244]

Vias made needlessly small — 0.3 to 0.4 mm — cost extra by forcing a finer drilling process and make it harder to solder wires to the board for hacking and modification.[1034] Choosing through-hole switches over surface mount allows a board to be hand-assembled to a professional finish with well-spaced keys, at the cost of losing usable surface area on both sides of the board.[11] For a one-off board that will be hand soldered rather than manufactured, single-sided component loading is unfussy and parts can simply be placed on the top side.[974]

Hand-soldered through-hole joints also differ thermally from wave soldering: hand soldering heats only the individual pins from the underside.[782] A joint formed by hand tends to get solder right over the top of the termination rather than only a fillet around the side, which is more robust than the reflowed result.[1137] Placing a multilayer ceramic capacitor directly across two pins that must be hand soldered is poor practice, since thermal stress adds to mechanical torsion as a cause of short-circuit failure.[1036][1035]

## Production economics

Hand soldering is the residual process for everything a machine cannot handle, and every hand-solder step carries cost. Parts supplied loose in bags rather than on reels, in tubes, or in trays must be hand placed and soldered, which drops assembly efficiency, raises the price charged, and lengthens the build.[127][239] Components cut from a reel and delivered in individual bags are good only for hand soldering, requiring an assembler to remove each one and place it with tweezers.[239] Through-hole switches, binding posts, and 4 mm banana plugs on an otherwise surface-mount board have to be hand soldered; selective wave soldering is available in principle but unlikely to be worth the effort, so once other parts already require hand work, the switches are done by hand too.[239] Even so, the surface-mount conversion can pay for the extra hand-solder labour, with the benefit beginning to appear at perhaps 100 to 200 boards.[239]

The corresponding design goal is to eliminate hand-solder steps entirely. Shield cans held down by an accurately dimensioned heat sink pressing on their tops need no hand soldering at all, and at volumes in the hundreds of thousands every step removed from assembly is money saved.[1717] A display technology costing 32 cents or less that involves no hand soldering, unlike LCD and seven-segment LED alternatives, is placed entirely by the pick-and-place machine and avoids paying for hand assembly labour.[298] For a board with roughly 30 parts, hand assembly is quicker than setting up a pick-and-place machine; the machine wins at 500 parts per board or panel, where hand placement risks the solder paste drying out.[740] Hand placement of paste-printed parts is acknowledged as inefficient for anything beyond one-off builds.[562]

Hand soldering also persists as a deliberate industrial process, not merely a fallback: final hand soldering will always exist for components that cannot go through pick-and-place or wave solder, whether because they are physically difficult to access or because they are temperature-sensitive parts such as supercapacitors.[207] It is the assembly route for substrates that cannot survive a reflow profile, alongside specialised leaded solders run at lower temperature for shorter duration.[aLcdm6gB6eE]

Hand soldering is also chosen on cost grounds where a connector would otherwise be needed: soldering ribbon cables directly to a board, possibly with a jig, is cheaper than fitting a connector, and appears in consumer keyboards where every cent counts.[1108][256]

## Joint quality and inspection

High-reliability hand soldering aims for a shiny joint, cleaned with flux remover and inspected for a perfectly mirror finish.[1041] That criterion does not transfer to lead-free work, where every joint inspected under the same conditions looks frosted.[1041] Poor joints are identifiable by eye: dull, cracked joints on an interface PCB warrant a minute of resoldering before any other diagnosis is attempted, and cheap instruments have been found with blow holes in the solder and joints that are dry throughout.[1394][6]

Uncleaned flux residue is the most reliable visual marker of hand work. On otherwise well-executed instruments, residue localises the hand-soldered items exactly — front-panel BNC connectors, board connectors, and power jacks — even where reflow quality elsewhere is very good.[360][511][521] The same signature distinguishes factory hand soldering from later repair: a row of capacitors carrying original wave soldering next to one that has been hand soldered and replaced indicates prior servicing, as do a few pins with flux residue scattered around an otherwise perfect connector.[1203][1237] Where every other joint on a board is wave soldered, isolated hand soldering suggests either a factory touch-up or an attempted repair.[1189][1571] Rework caused by parts falling off the pick-and-place heads and being detected at optical inspection is the ordinary explanation for isolated hand-soldered passives on a production board.[1522]

Hand soldering quality is treated as a diagnostic of overall manufacturing care. Manually folded and hand-soldered contacts in budget multimeters are readily distinguished from automated soldering.[99] Transistor-test sockets in cheap meters are consistently hand soldered and consistently poor.[91] Parts sitting at odd angles with heavy residue point to hand assembly on boards where SO-8 packages were done adequately.[440] The opposite outcome is equally possible: hand-soldered wiring can be executed beautifully, with each joint formed deliberately.[330]

## Repair, prototyping, and modification

Hand soldering is the enabling technique for board-level modification. Mod boards are attached as components via half-moon castellations and hand soldered as a retrofit, though the same board can be machine placed and reflowed if it is designed in from the start.[1158] Ad-hoc modification wires are hand soldered and look ugly, which is tolerable on prototypes but undesirable in production or as field rework by repair technicians.[1158]

Instrumentation access frequently depends on it: two wires soldered to transmit and receive pins expose a serial debug interface;[977] wires tacked to a point of interest and routed outside the enclosure allow probing after reassembly;[855] and wires soldered directly to front-panel traces substitute for spade lugs.[667] The counterpart is that hand soldering to a live product is intrusive — reaching a tiny quad flat pack pin or an 0402 component may require removing the board, and risks shorting something out or ruining the board, which is why non-invasive measurement methods are preferred where they suffice.[1324]

Destructive-repair work also relies on it. A moulded connector whose body was damaged can be salvaged by chipping away the plastic, extracting and reinserting each pin individually, and resoldering the assembly, though a dual-row 1 mm pitch part makes this awkward and the pins do not seat until all are aligned.[794] When a header is soldered back in, the mating connector's height must be checked first and a spacer used, or the assembly will sit too flush to mate.[794]

## Learning and access

Hand soldering has long been taught outside formal engineering education. Retail electronics chains ran extensive in-store soldering setups and advertised teaching customers to solder, an initiative that did not survive past the early 1990s.[8Oragx4JJrE] Some engineering programmes historically forbade students from soldering at all on burn-risk grounds.[818] Community workshops fill the gap, teaching soldering to newcomers including young hobbyists building projects of their own.[1759] University electronics labs now include dedicated hand-soldering stations for build projects.[1565] Kit documentation that includes assembly instructions covering how to solder for those who have not done it before is regarded as the standard to aim for.[872] Hand soldering also appears as a timed competition event at industry trade shows.[1686]

Building a circuit physically and soldering it together is a legitimate way to settle a theoretical question — the resistor cube problem was solved by measuring matched resistors, soldering them into the network, and verifying the answer directly.[1472]
