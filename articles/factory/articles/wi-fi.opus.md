# wi fi

Wi-Fi is the short-range wireless local area networking standard that, in practice, has become the default way a piece of consumer or laboratory equipment gets onto a network without a cable. It occupies the 2.4 GHz band and, on later hardware, the 5 GHz band as well, with channels packed closely enough together that out-of-band emission control is a genuine design difficulty.[55][1310][tjdae8oqYMQ] For an engineer it presents three distinct problems that recur across almost every product that carries it: how much power it costs, how much RF hygiene the board layout demands, and whether the added attack surface, regulatory burden and setup fragility are worth the convenience.

## Origin and patents

The core technology is an Australian invention, developed by the CSIRO and subsequently commercialised overseas.[OlO8pNWnOak] The CSIRO won a patent infringement lawsuit against Hewlett-Packard over Wi-Fi, after which roughly thirteen further companies settled rather than go to court.[7] Royalties from that patent flowed into the organisation's general funds rather than being ring-fenced for research.[455]

## Available power and energy harvesting

Radio energy falls off with the square of the distance, which sets a hard ceiling on anything that proposes to recover useful power from ambient Wi-Fi.[4vHx-UyIM9M] Harvesting from ambient 2.4 GHz signals yields microwatts; only a dedicated multi-watt transmitter aimed at the receiver produces low milliwatts, on the order of five to ten and at best tens of milliwatts.[1532] This has not stopped a recurring class of product built on the claim that Wi-Fi-band RF can charge a phone, whether framed as capturing the signals that are already all around us into a lithium-ion cell[55] or as a "wireless power network" that a device joins the way it joins a Wi-Fi access point.[MCyLO-1grEk][1408][1532] The rhetorical move is consistent: the familiarity of Wi-Fi is used to make the power claim sound plausible, while the actual delivered figures — a 2 W transmitter feeding receivers that produce single-digit milliwatts, or several receivers in parallel producing 2.5 mW — are left in the fine print.[1408]

## Power consumption in battery-operated products

Wi-Fi draws a lot of power relative to the low-rate wide-area radio alternatives, which is the reason technologies such as LoRaWAN exist alongside it: Wi-Fi requires a local hub and gives usable range only within proximity of it, while cellular gateways reach anywhere but at comparable or worse power cost.[1062] The effect on runtime is easily quantified. An e-reader rated at one month of battery life with the wireless turned off drops to about three weeks with Wi-Fi enabled, and to roughly a week if the GSM radio is used as well.[108] A tablet of the same era was rated at seven or eight hours of continuous use with Wi-Fi off.[220]

The duty cycle matters as much as the peak. A typical Wi-Fi product powers up, spends ten to fifteen seconds scanning for networks at hundreds of milliamps, then sleeps for a minute at a fraction of a milliamp — a profile that will defeat a current-measurement setup that cannot range-switch quickly or trigger on the wake event.[1331] That sleep-to-transmit swing is precisely why dynamic range is the dominant specification in current-measurement instrumentation: an instrument resolving 10 µA on a 2 A range can capture both the transmit burst and the sub-milliamp idle without changing range.[1402] In a current shunt amplifier the same requirement lands on the op-amp's supply range, since a 2.7 V to 5.5 V part caps the achievable dynamic range at 5.5 V.[1325]

## Adding Wi-Fi to a design

The economics changed with the arrival of cheap integrated Wi-Fi microcontrollers. The ESP8266 is a roughly $3 part that combines a microcontroller with built-in Wi-Fi and is programmable from the Arduino IDE, which puts network connectivity into projects for the price of a couple of dollars.[yabl87SD-3M][998] The supporting library ecosystem does the heavy lifting: connecting to a network takes only a few lines, with the installed core handling the stack.[998] The Wi-Fi Manager library solves the credential-provisioning problem without reflashing — the module attempts the last known network, and on failure brings up its own access point, so that a phone or laptop connecting to it is redirected to a configuration page where the target SSID and password are entered, after which the module restarts and joins.[yabl87SD-3M] The same pattern of first-boot hotspot provisioning turns up across unrelated commodity products.[1720]

Module-level integration is common: a WeMos D1 Mini carrying an ESP8266EX serves as the Wi-Fi front end of a multi-digit display, taking 5 V power while its I/O runs at 3.3 V from an on-board regulator.[952] Development platforms combine the PIC32 with built-in Wi-Fi.[711] Where connectivity is even a possibility for a future revision, the engineering judgment is to select a part that already includes the radio — an ESP32 for Wi-Fi, or an nRF52 for Bluetooth, both cheap enough at a dollar or two that the unused radio costs little.[1694] Whether the link should be Wi-Fi, Bluetooth or cellular is itself a systems-engineering question that belongs at the requirements stage, derived from the stakeholder need rather than picked late.[1627]

## Layout, shielding and interference

Adding a radio imposes real constraints on the PCB. The antenna feed is a controlled-impedance trace, the copper is cleared from the opposite side of the board beneath the antenna, and the antenna itself is laid out on the board surface, with ground planes deliberately pulled back around it.[370][1190] Where Wi-Fi shares a product with other radios, extensive shielding cans are used so that, for instance, a GSM antenna does not interfere with everything else.[109] At the transmitter end the difficulty is not merely making the radio work in band but keeping it from emitting outside its band, which is hardest at lower frequencies precisely because Wi-Fi channels sit so close together; at higher frequencies the wider channel spacing relaxes the requirement.[tjdae8oqYMQ]

A striking integration failure mode appears on the Raspberry Pi 3, whose Broadcom BCM43438 combined Wi-Fi and Bluetooth part is mounted as a bare die on the underside of the board. A camera xenon flash at close range disrupts it: the network connection dies while the CPU continues running normally, and the integrated Bluetooth is presumed to fail with it.[901] Because the die faces downward, a board sitting flat is less exposed than one held up to the light.[901]

## Wi-Fi in test and measurement equipment

Instrument makers have added Wi-Fi mainly as a remote-control and remote-display path. A scope with a tablet-oriented interface can be operated entirely from a remote PC or tablet over Wi-Fi, working the same way as it does on the front panel.[701] Benchtop combination instruments ship with Ethernet, USB and Wi-Fi together, the Wi-Fi antenna supplied in the box so the unit can be reached from a PC or tablet.[875][876] Board-level instruments have used it as the primary interface: a web-based instrument can be driven over Wi-Fi instead of Ethernet and programmed and operated remotely from anywhere in the world,[858][2] and one browser-based scope can serve its own application software to the browser over the Wi-Fi link rather than requiring an installed program.[1056] Programmable power supplies carry it as a factory or licensed option, sometimes behind an option key that must be enabled before the wireless LAN menu becomes usable.[1173][1402][1293] The same capability can be retrofitted to older instruments through a bridge that attaches to a legacy interface and exposes the instrument to a web browser over Wi-Fi.[1232]

The implementation quality varies. A scope advertised as having built-in Wi-Fi may in fact rely on a USB dongle hanging off the back, which is "all marketing wank" as a specification claim;[MguJvnyX4fc] on one product family the cheaper variant simply omits the rear USB port and therefore the dongle and therefore the Wi-Fi.[1355] Setup is frequently the weak point: connection attempts that see the network and report a LAN address yet refuse to establish a session, requiring manual re-enumeration or gateway configuration, are a recurring failure, and equipment that must be networked before it can be used is worse off than a bench instrument that is plugged in and works.[858][2][1056]

There are also sound engineering reasons not to build it in. Adding a radio requires substantial regulatory testing in a domain the instrument maker may not specialise in, delaying the product; and a scope is often used where RF measurements are being made, so putting a deliberate radiated source inside the enclosure is unwelcome. A hardwired Ethernet connection remains the baseline for that reason.[PcxEO3fA_Ls] The same logic is enforced absolutely at radio astronomy sites, where Wi-Fi and Bluetooth must be disabled on every device brought on site, not merely the phones.[pxBxDPx9cyE] SAR compliance testing likewise has to accommodate Wi-Fi bands separately, with the tissue-simulant liquids changed to suit the frequency under test.[201]

## Reception margin and reliability

Performance at the edge of coverage separates otherwise similar products. Devices tested at the extreme end of a lab's Wi-Fi range, with barely one bar of signal, can be perfectly usable and quick,[226][205] while others become effectively unusable at the same marginal signal level and require a strong signal for any acceptable performance — a design defect, not a user problem.[220]

Setup and long-term reliability are a persistent weak spot in Wi-Fi-connected infrastructure. Solar inverters and monitoring gateways commonly connect through a Wi-Fi dongle to the home network for remote access,[3hHG_WcJtQo][1385] but the Wi-Fi path is where installations fail — a gateway that reports nothing while the installer insists it is producing power,[1626] an inverter whose Bluetooth link works only at arm's length while the Wi-Fi setup refuses to complete,[1719] or a unit whose network connection state is unclear from its LED pattern alone.[uE0x8YR7nMg] Where Wi-Fi does connect, it enables genuinely useful maintenance: a receiver with no remote control could still be joined to a network by pressing the WPS button on the router and then updated by a remote firmware upgrade over that link.[1151][StMI8PEYMBg]

## Grounds for leaving it out

There is a consistent engineering position that Wi-Fi should not be added to a design merely because it is cheap. A device with no Wi-Fi, Bluetooth or any other wireless interface cannot be attacked remotely and does not upload to a cloud service; the only access to its data is physical, through the USB-C port or the microSD card.[1619] For a project whose function is entirely local, connectivity is added complexity with no corresponding benefit.[1491] Against that, the counterargument is the same one that produced the ESP32 recommendation above: the marginal cost of a part that already has the radio is small enough that leaving the option open is usually the cheaper decision.[1694]

The category of Wi-Fi-dependent consumer hardware also carries a specific ownership risk. Wireless speakers built around Wi-Fi and a cloud service can be deliberately disabled by the vendor, which is why one such speaker has been characterised as "the Juicero of Wi-Fi" smart-speaker products.[Ac1zZo7wLo8][1519][1514]

## Health claims

Wi-Fi is a standing target for radiation-fear marketing and campaigning. Products sold as protection against "electro-smog" from laptops, cordless phones, Wi-Fi and tablets, priced in the hundreds of pounds and described with terms like quantum oscillation and holographic nano layer catalyst technology, are straightforward fraud.[1310] The physics does not support the fear: transmitter power levels are small, the field falls with the square of distance, and even 5G small-cell installations run at levels below those of a typical Wi-Fi router.[4vHx-UyIM9M] People who report electro-sensitivity after removing a mobile phone are typically still sitting in the 5 GHz field of their own Wi-Fi.[1310] Every transmitter can in principle be a hazard at sufficient power, which is exactly why exposure standards and bodies such as the ICNIRP exist.[1310] Mitigations sold on the same premise are self-defeating — a metal cage around a router will indeed attenuate the signal, and will equally remove the Wi-Fi service; unplugging the router achieves the same result, and reducing the transmit power in the router's settings is the adjustable version of the same idea.[o2NxHu5Bsnk] Organised opposition has repeatedly targeted municipal deployments, including a campaign against citywide Wi-Fi in Santa Fe, without lasting effect on coverage.[4vHx-UyIM9M]
