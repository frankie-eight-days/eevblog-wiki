# output capacitor

The output capacitor is the capacitance placed between a power supply's output terminal and its return, and it does two largely separate jobs: it stores and smooths energy so the rail holds up between switching or rectification events, and it forms part of the feedback loop that determines whether the regulator is stable at all.[110][95] In a switching converter it is the reservoir the inductor dumps its stored energy into, and its value sets the output ripple directly.[139][110] In a linear or low-dropout regulator it is a compensation component, and its value, its equivalent series resistance, and even its physical distance from the regulator can decide between a clean rail and an oscillator.[1285][95]

## Role in switching and rectified supplies

A basic boost converter works by storing energy in an inductor through a switching FET and then letting the inductor dump that energy into the output capacitor.[139] The same capacitor is the filter element in a buck or flyback topology, and behind a mains transformer and bridge rectifier it is what turns rectified half-cycles into something resembling DC.[1116][110]

For a design-from-scratch switching regulator the required output capacitance follows from the ripple specification: nine times the output current, times the on-time, divided by the permitted ripple voltage.[110] For 100 mA output, a 7.3 µs on-time and a 100 mV ripple budget this gives 66 µF.[110] Where that is not good enough, an optional second-stage LC output filter can be added after the main output capacitor — in one such design a 33 µH inductor followed by a further 100 µF.[110] Behind a rectifier the same trade exists: ripple can be reduced by simply increasing capacitance, but post-regulation is often the better answer than an ever-larger capacitor.[1116]

In a switched-capacitor voltage inverter such as the 7660 the output capacitor, conventionally labelled C2, is what determines the residual ripple, though the result depends jointly on the switching frequency, the internal resistance of the switches, and the ESR of the capacitor itself.[1115] Because of that dependence, 7660 datasheets typically do not contain a ripple-versus-output-current graph at all.[1115]

Output capacitance is also one of the parameters that sets converter efficiency, alongside the MOSFET and the switching frequency, when a DC-DC design is optimised for a particular output current.[895]

## The ESR floor

Ripple cannot be reduced indefinitely by adding capacitance, because past a certain point the ESR of the capacitance takes over and sets a floor on the achievable ripple.[1115] The mechanism is a packaging constraint: for a given package size, the higher the capacitance the higher the ESR must go.[1115] The same limit shows up in practice at the other end of the frequency range — enlarging the capacitor across the output of a 2000 W supply reduced the low-frequency ripple noticeably, from roughly 200 mV peak to peak toward 70 mV, while leaving the higher-frequency switching content essentially untouched.[1298]

## Stability in linear and low-dropout regulators

Linear regulators are closed-loop systems, and the output capacitor sits inside that loop. Standard NPN-pass linear regulators traditionally require a minimum output capacitance to keep the loop stable; the LDO variant using a PNP pass transistor is more unstable still, requiring both a capacitance within a specified range and an ESR within a specified range.[95] The underlying analysis is conventional control theory — Bode plots, phase diagrams, poles and zeros.[95] Getting it wrong produces oscillation, and the error can be in the value, the type, or the placement distance from the regulator.[1285]

This is the principal hidden cost of the low-dropout family. The 1117 is widely treated as a jellybean part because it offers the advantages of the 78xx series and the LM317 with better dropout, but its output capacitance is critical, and LDOs in general become unstable if the output capacitance or output impedance requirements are not met.[1438] Where the divider resistors around a regulator are cheap, a 100 µF output capacitor that simultaneously satisfies the value and impedance requirements for stability often is not, and LDO datasheets frequently devote an entire section to output capacitor selection.[1438]

Not every regulator needs one. Contrary to common belief, the fixed 78xx series does not require an output capacitor for stability and is stable with no output capacitance at all; the customary 0.1 µF is optional, as is the input capacitor unless the regulator sits a significant distance from the input filter capacitor.[1438] The LM317 likewise does not strictly need an output capacitor for stability, though it is normally fitted as a matter of course.[1438] There is one interaction to respect: if the optional adjust-pin capacitor is used to improve ripple rejection, the output capacitor must be larger than it, or the regulator goes unstable.[1438] On a working LM317 design the 1 µF recommended value proved somewhat generous — 100 nF on the output is perfectly stable in practice.[158] Some regulators are remarkably tolerant: a one-cent part ran a 100 mA load with no input or output capacitance at all and no oscillation, and remained well behaved with 330 µF hung on the far end of the supply lead.[1147]

Precision voltage references occupy the opposite corner from LDOs: the better jellybean references need no output capacitor and tolerate a capacitive load, so existing bypass capacitors on the powered circuit are not a hazard.[1438]

The same stability requirement propagates into application circuits built around linear blocks. A lithium-ion charger IC has internal charging circuitry equivalent to a linear regulator, and its loop is unstable unless the recommended output capacitance is fitted.[176] In a precision 1 A current source the loop stability is set by the phase margin of the reference's internal output amplifier, and the datasheet specifies an output capacitor — 10 µF in that case — with an ESR below 0.1 Ω; both the ESR and the capacitance matter, and oscillation in such a circuit usually means insufficient output capacitance for loop stability.[577] Such circuits are worth building and verifying before committing to a PCB: if a breadboard prototype cannot be brought to even semi-stable behaviour while swapping input and output capacitor types, the design is in trouble regardless of how much tighter the PCB loops will be.[567]

Conversely, a capacitor placed directly on the output of an op-amp is a liability rather than a benefit — it can affect stability, and a 100 nF part added on an op-amp output in the hope of reducing noise is better left off entirely.[413]

Substituting a pin-compatible switching regulator for another carries the same caveat: even with identical output-voltage programming, the ESR of the output capacitors must still be correct, because some circuits misbehave without it, and the datasheet detail has to be checked.[1475] Loop-response measurement is the systematic way to settle these questions — injecting a signal into the feedback divider of a DC-DC converter and measuring magnitude and phase reveals the stability under various loads and allows the correct output capacitor and ESR to be chosen rather than guessed.[1103]

## Constant-current supplies and the case for minimum capacitance

In a bench supply with constant-current capability, output capacitance is actively harmful. The current limit is enforced somewhere back on the board, but the output capacitor sits downstream of it and can deliver a spike of energy straight into the load, over and above the set current, during the changeover into constant-current mode.[511] Charge stored on the output can be delivered before the control loop has time to respond at all.[1691] The design goal for any lab supply with constant-current mode is therefore to minimise the output capacitance.[511][828]

Real instruments frequently violate this. A 1,000 µF output capacitor is very high for a supply with constant-current capability.[511] Another bench supply carried more output capacitance than it should, distributed across several 80 V parts.[1265] In a 360 W supply an additional capacitor was placed literally on the output terminals in parallel with the main output bank, adding to an already significant total.[1691] In a 2000 W supply, large low-ESR output capacitors dumping current into the load on the transition into constant-current mode tripped the load's own input protection.[1298]

The problem is not confined to power supplies. A battery-profiling instrument with 1,000 µF on its output supplies short load pulses from that reservoir capacitor rather than through the current-sense resistors, corrupting the measurement of exactly the transients such an instrument exists to capture.[1331]

Removing output capacitance is not free either, and there is a genuine optimum. In a lab supply design, the transient response on the changeover into constant current was clean with 22 µF, noticeably worse with 100 nF, and worst of all with no capacitor at all.[224] The complementary problem is discharging what remains: the stored charge is bled off deliberately, either by a bleeder resistor permanently across the output rail discharging a couple of hundred microfarads on the output terminals, or by a transistor that dumps the capacitor through a series resistor when constant-current mode is entered — the resistor value being a direct trade against turn-off speed, taking tens of milliseconds at 1 kΩ and approaching a direct short as the resistance falls.[861][224]

## Diagnosis, failure and repair

Output capacitors are a routine suspect in supply faults, and ESR is the usual measurand. High ESR in the output capacitors is the statistically likely cause of a burnt supply, since it leads to excess ripple on the output even under modest load.[1726] In-circuit ESR probing distinguishes good parts from bad quickly: on one oscilloscope's regulator board the 5 V rail output capacitor read as healthy while the pre-regulator capacitor measured almost 3 Ω and was the fault.[1203] Capacitance is worth checking too — a failed negative rail from a 7660 inverter came down to an output capacitor measuring 600 nF where 22 µF was specified.[918]

Output capacitors can equally be exonerated. Multiple capacitors in parallel on the secondary side, all visually sound with no bulging, argue against the output stage as the cause of a repeating hiccup, which is characteristically a primary-side start-up problem instead.[1423] Parallel arrangements are also easy to misread on a board: capacitors that appear to be wired in series may in fact be in parallel.[1122]

Not every capacitor on an output is there for regulation. A 0.1 µF part across the output of a supply may exist purely for EMC and CE compliance, making no measurable difference to the output noise, so its omission is a compliance change rather than a performance one.[1036] Values in this role are small — a 0.047 µF part specified on a laboratory supply's output, with 0.1 µF an acceptable substitution.[667]

## Construction and layout practice

The output capacitors are physically the largest components on many secondary sides, and manufacturers commonly glue them down with silastic so they cannot flap around in service.[800][497] Their placement matters electrically: the return path from the output capacitors to ground must not be made through a single via, which presents high inductance and constitutes poor board layout.[1475] Where capacitors are singular rather than doubled up, there is no redundancy against the short-circuit failure mode of ceramic capacitors, which can in turn lead to fire.[1710]

In cost-reduced offline supplies the output capacitor's size directly determines a visible performance parameter. An LED driver using transformer feedback with no secondary-side regulator IC and no optocoupler will almost certainly flicker, and how much depends on the type and size of the output capacitor.[1252][dLlhoUHlnjE] Adding a 470 µF, 50 V capacitor alongside an existing 330 µF one measurably reduced the 100 % ripple on the LED current without disturbing the power factor.[1253]

Output capacitance appears in the smallest circuits too: a die with only a small amount of on-chip output capacitance will charge that capacitance up, briefly flash an LED, and drop back down, repeating — the flashing being purely an artefact of the charging cycle rather than of any signal being received.[729]
