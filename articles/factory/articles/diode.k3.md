# diode

A diode is a two-terminal semiconductor device that conducts current in one direction only, which is why its schematic symbol is drawn as an arrow pointing in the direction of conventional current flow.[908][1747] It is among the most fundamental components in electronics, serving in rectification, reverse-polarity protection, transient clamping, signal steering, logic, and detection.[908][373] Diodes are polarity-sensitive: the cathode end carries a band marking on through-hole and surface-mount packages, the opposite end being the anode, and schematic reference designators D or CR both denote a diode.[aQ2AVLs8_7k][1306][565][1708]

## Electrical characteristics

A silicon PN-junction diode conducts essentially nothing until the forward voltage exceeds a knee of roughly 0.6 V, the exact value depending on temperature, device, and current; above the knee it conducts freely.[908] The forward drop in practice runs 0.6 V to about a volt, rising toward a volt at higher currents.[908][711] Turn-on is not abrupt — the characteristic curve rises progressively rather than snapping on.[443] Reverse-biased, an idealized diode passes no current, but real devices exhibit reverse leakage, which is normally negligible yet can exceed the input bias current of a good FET-input op-amp and therefore matters in precision circuits.[908][490] A PN junction also exhibits capacitance, known as junction, transition, or depletion capacitance, distinct from the diffusion capacitance associated with charge storage.[1384]

Several other devices are built on, or behave as, diode junctions. The base-emitter junction of a bipolar transistor is a PN junction that functions exactly like a diode, and a transistor can be used as one; its roughly 0.7 V drop can have circuit consequences, such as clamping a node too low to drive a 2 V LED.[748][242] An SCR is essentially a diode with an added gate terminal.[1172] A silicon solar cell is effectively just a diode that produces voltage when photons strike it, about 0.6 V per cell.[1426] An LED is a diode whose light output is close to linear with current even though its voltage-drop behavior is non-linear.[1427]

## Device types

- **Standard silicon rectifiers and signal diodes.** Workhorse parts such as the 1N4007 handle power-line rectification, while small-signal types such as the 1N4148 leak only tens of nanoamps in reverse, good enough for most isolation duty.[1449][1242]
- **Schottky diodes.** These drop roughly 0.2–0.3 V instead of 0.6 V.[473][567] For reverse-polarity or clamping protection this is a decisive advantage, because a Schottky clamp holds the protected node below the ~0.6 V threshold at which the silicon PN junctions of downstream transistors and ICs would otherwise conduct.[1747]
- **Zener diodes.** A Zener behaves exactly like a regular diode in the forward direction — same 0.6 V to 1 V drop — but also conducts in reverse at its Zener breakdown voltage, making it useful as a voltage reference and clamp.[908] The breakdown voltage is not fixed: it shifts with current and with junction temperature, and since Zeners are typically used as power devices they self-heat, a genuine "trap for young players".[908] Ordinary and Schottky diodes also exhibit reverse breakdown, and a BJT base-emitter junction in reverse breaks down at roughly 6 V, usable as an impromptu Zener while behaving as a 0.6 V diode in the forward direction.[908][n4NBUruLyoo]
- **Germanium and point-contact diodes.** Vintage equipment contains germanium types, including tiny point-contact devices, alongside silicon ones.[riBwRC_CaAA][649]
- **LEDs used as ordinary diodes.** With forward drops around 1.6–1.85 V for traditional parts, LEDs occasionally appear doing plain diode duty, for instance in series with an indicator lamp and dropper resistor.[1209][1598]
- **Multiple-diode packages.** Dual diodes such as the BAV99 come in SOT-23, and bridge rectifier packages integrate four diodes; a nominal 1 A bridge such as the DF02 can nonetheless be rated for a 50 A single half-sine surge.[565][376]

## Applications

### Rectification and power conversion

A single diode with a transformer produces a half-wave rectified waveform: the positive half-cycle passes, the negative half is blocked because the diode is reverse-biased.[1417] A conventional bridge rectifier sacrifices at least 0.6 V per diode — up to a volt at high current — so a synchronous rectifier replaces the diodes with MOSFETs whose low on-resistance eliminates most of the loss.[711] In a buck or boost switch-mode converter the catch diode carries the inductor current during the switch-off interval.[90] A critical sizing rule follows: the diode and inductor must be rated for the peak current, not the output current — a converter delivering 100 mA output may see 0.74 A peaks, calling for a 2 A diode such as a B220 where a 100 mA part would fail.[110] Diode type also directly affects converter efficiency, and some converter ICs integrate the diode to cut parts count and cost.[110][139]

Diode-capacitor networks build voltage multipliers. In a Cockcroft-Walton multiplier each stage sees only the input-level voltage, so a chain producing 5–10 kV out needs only ~1 kV rated diodes and capacitors.[469] Microcontroller-driven charge pumps double or invert a supply by steering charge with diodes, though real Schottky drops of ~0.3 V each pull the output below the ideal calculated value.[473][483] Substituting a TVS device for the usual Schottky at a supply input yields both transient overvoltage and reverse-voltage protection in one part.[233]

### Protection and clamping

A reverse-biased diode across a relay coil or other inductive load is close to mandatory: when the drive is removed, the collapsing magnetic field keeps current flowing, and the diode conducts to clamp the spike to about 0.6 V below the rail instead of letting it reach tens of volts and destroying the driver.[1406][708] A series diode at a power input blocks reverse current if the supply is connected backwards, a use seen from USB-powered gadgets to vintage calculators.[1747][1571] Diode OR-ing selects between a main rail and a backup battery automatically, and a series diode isolates a supercapacitor or battery so it cannot back-feed the rest of a product at power-down.[942][1242] Some designs avoid diodes entirely for supply selection — a DC jack with a built-in changeover switch needs no active circuitry at all.[1015] In a defibrillator, where high-voltage capacitors sit in series, a diode across each capacitor prevents manufacturing tolerances from reverse-charging one capacitor during discharge, which could burst it.[909]

Diode bridges form the front-line overload protection of multimeters: stacked diodes across the current shunt clamp the burden voltage — six in series give roughly 3.6 V — forcing current through the diodes long enough for the fuse to blow rather than the shunt resistor, and extra series diodes can be added where a higher clamp voltage is tolerable.[373] The scheme depends on surge ratings far above the continuous rating: parts in this role hold around 50 A for 8.3 ms or ~10 A for a full second while the HRC fuse clears.[376] Device programmers use a similar idea with a programmable clamp rail, holding each pin within 0.6 V of a set voltage via diode arrays.[1060] Integrated circuits carry ESD protection diodes on their pins even though many datasheets never draw them, and applying a negative excursion to a de-energized board can forward-bias these junctions — a phenomenon exploited by curve-tracer-style fault finders and a confounder in other measurements.[831][-ON1AdmAMOY]

### Signal and measurement circuits

A diode and capacitor form the simplest peak detector, and closing an op-amp's feedback loop around the diode makes a precision rectifier in which the amplifier drives its output to cancel the diode drop in real time, compensating for temperature and even a poor diode; adding a second diode and feedback resistor holds zero volts across the rectifying diode at the peak, eliminating its reverse leakage from the measurement.[490] Diodes also build logic: diode NOR and OR gates steer forcing signals in discrete digital clock designs.[801] Placing a diode across a resistor gives asymmetric charge and discharge paths, a standard trick in switch-debounce timing networks.[961] Series diodes serve as crude level shifters — one or two 1N914s add a predictable ~0.6 V per junction of offset, for example to lift an op-amp input away from a rail it cannot quite reach or to raise a MOSFET's effective threshold.[567][579][260]

## Testing and measurement

The diode-test function on a multimeter measures forward voltage drop — typically 0.5–0.6 V for silicon — and generally works with the part in circuit, making it useful both for identifying orientation and for troubleshooting, including LEDs.[1636][1522][1423] Meter test voltage sets a ceiling: a 36 V Zener cannot be broken down by a meter whose diode range tops out at 15 V, though it can still be verified as conducting in one direction.[1395] In-circuit capacitance measurement has a related trap — an LCR meter's test signal can itself turn on parallel diodes, ESD structures, and other active junctions, corrupting the reading; meters offering a 0.1 V RMS test level instead of 0.5 V RMS exist precisely to keep those junctions off.[1474] On a component tester or curve tracer a diode traces a characteristic L-shaped curve, distinct from a resistor's straight line or a capacitor's ellipse, and a source-measure unit captures the full forward characteristic curve point by point.[1137][638][718] Component-testing tweezers display diode orientation directly, though units with a low test threshold cannot light even a standard 1.8 V green LED, let alone white ones; other tweezers can exercise LEDs in resistance or capacitance modes instead.[81][1335] A diode that measures as a similar drop in both directions is faulty or paralleled by another path — an in-circuit reading of ~0.5 V both ways is a red flag, not a pass.[1649]

## Failure modes and repair

Short circuit is a recognized diode failure mode, and a shorted rectifier or clamp can dump supply energy where it does not belong, taking out downstream circuitry.[1511][538] Diodes also fail open, crack physically, develop intermittent internal connections that produce current-dependent latching behavior, and — in severe overheating events — can desolder themselves entirely and fall off the board.[1452][1527][1087][1511][WFVfbu1Xz3A] Thermal failure is a real possibility in high-voltage and power stages, and as a rule of thumb diodes tend to fail before the regulators they feed.[1452][1364] Because they are cheap, common suspects and usually testable in circuit, checking diodes early is standard troubleshooting practice.[1423][663]

## Design considerations

Diode selection has thermal and economic dimensions beyond the headline ratings. Junction temperature limits of +150 °C must be de-rated through the whole thermal stack — junction-to-case, vias, and insulating pad — and a heatsink already running at 160 °C leaves no margin at all.[895] Surge and continuous ratings differ by orders of magnitude, which is exactly what protection schemes exploit.[376] For manufacturability, consolidating on one diode type — for example using the same 1 A power diode already on the bill of materials where a separate small signal diode would otherwise be specified — reduces line items, reels, and pick-and-place setup.[1307]
