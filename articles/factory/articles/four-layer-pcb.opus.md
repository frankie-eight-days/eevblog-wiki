# four layer pcb

A four-layer PCB is a board built from four copper layers: an outer top and bottom pair carrying signals, and two inner layers given over to solid ground and VCC planes.[Ep4r-wD7PPs][899] The arrangement exists because the inner planes give every signal a continuous, low-impedance return path directly beneath it, which collapses the loop area that a two-layer board is forced to leave open.[1176][1081] Layer counts of this kind are almost always even; a three-layer board is not made, so a board showing internal copper is at minimum four.[738][410][969]

## Stackup and the role of the inner layers

The conventional stackup places ground and VCC as two solid copper pours in the middle and reserves the outer copper for signal traces.[Ep4r-wD7PPs][899] On a two-layer board it is a de facto standard to flood-fill the top as the positive rail and the bottom as the negative, but that approach does not carry over: on four layers the planes move inside and the outer layers become the wiring.[899] Burying the planes rather than the signals is not the only possible order — the signal layers can instead be placed on the two inner layers with ground and power on the outside, and the two variants differ measurably in radiated emissions.[1193][1273]

The planes are not always unbroken. A supply design may deliberately split the internal plane so that an internal power-supply plane and an internal ground plane stay physically separated between the component side and the rest of the board.[1561] Splitting is a decision to be made carefully rather than by default: chopping up ground and power planes on a four-layer board causes its own problems, and a plane split straight through a return path is among the worst things that can be done to a layout.[1176] A four-layer stackup can also be built on flexible material, with the dielectrics and a polyimide bottom layer specified in the layer stack manager so the flex portion is sandwiched rather than centred.[1262]

## Why it improves EMC

The difference is measurable rather than theoretical. Compared on the identical design, a four-layer version of a board showed roughly a 15 dB reduction in near-field magnetic emissions against its two-layer counterpart, narrowing somewhat around the 150 to 200 MHz region but remaining significantly below it across the sweep — a margin large enough to decide a compliance result.[1176] Although near-field probing does not correlate directly with far-field compliance measurement, the correlation is good enough that the two-layer board would certainly perform worse in a full EMC compliance test over the whole spectrum.[1176]

The mechanism is return current. High-frequency return current follows the lowest-impedance path, which means it wants to flow directly under its own trace.[1176] On a two-layer board with a split or interrupted ground, the return has to find its way back by an indirect route, enclosing a large loop that radiates in proportion to the switching current and the loop area.[1176] With a full plane, the return path under a trace is both the same length and far lower in inductance, and the same solid copper extends continuously to the bypass capacitors and the power pins.[1176][1580] Flooding the region under a chip with low-inductance ground and power gives the bypass capacitors and the signal return the short loop they need, which is exactly what the two-layer version cannot offer.[1176] The same reasoning applies to switching artefacts on supply pins: stitching pins together on a four-layer board substantially reduces the excursions seen on a double-sided layout that cannot support a solid low-impedance plane.[1081]

This does not make two-layer boards inherently unacceptable. A carefully laid-out two-layer board with generous via stitching and flood fills can approach four-layer performance, and can outperform a badly laid-out four-layer board.[1176] The advantage of four layers is that having the ground and power planes makes good performance much easier to achieve and much harder to get wrong.[1176] For a dense design spread over many chips in an awkward arrangement, though, careful two-layer work will not close the gap.[1176]

## Plane capacitance

The two inner planes, separated by the epoxy-resin fibreglass dielectric, form a parallel-plate capacitor across the whole board area.[1117] This yields a distributed decoupling capacitance that is bulk rather than local, and is a genuine effect worth analysing rather than a rhetorical one — a large internal ground plane with a power plane adjacent to it does behave as a capacitive plane pair.[899][1081] It is a supplement to discrete bypass capacitors, not a replacement for placing them close to the pins.[1081][452]

## Routing practice

Routing on four layers reserves the top signal layer for signal traces, dropping only what must be dropped to the bottom layer, and typically pushing power traces to the bottom.[1323] On a simple board with only a handful of chips, around 90 percent of the layout should sit on the top layer with 5 to 10 percent at most on the bottom, and no signal traces at all on the inner layers.[1323] Internal traces are a maintenance liability because they cannot be followed by eye when repairing or debugging a board.[1323] The routing order matters too: signal layers are routed first and power dealt with afterwards, since committing to a power trace early can force an entire bus down to the bottom layer unnecessarily.[1323]

Where controlled impedance is needed, the top trace over the ground plane beneath it forms a microstrip, which is the structure a four-layer stackup makes available for free.[1323] A shielded near-field probe built on a four-layer board uses exactly this: a single 50-ohm controlled-impedance trace on a controlled-impedance dielectric, with the split shield stitched around it by vias.[1178]

The classic outer-layer discipline is orthogonal routing — all traces on one side running in one direction and all those on the other side running perpendicular to them — which is what four-layer routing looks like when done conventionally and appears consistently on boards from the 1980s onwards.[1580][1053][994][410] With power and ground handled by the planes and the placement done well, this style of digital board is a legitimate case for letting an auto-router run.[994][410] Good component placement remains the dominant factor: layout is largely placement, and good placement lets the routing take care of itself.[410]

One local subtlety: ground fill is sometimes removed from under relay or connector pads to shave perhaps half a picofarad of pin-to-ground capacitance, but on a four-layer board this achieves little unless the fill is also cleared from the internal ground plane in the same region.[497]

## When four layers become necessary

Routing density, not emissions, is often what forces the move. Fine-pitch parts with five-thou traces that must escape between pads may simply not be routable on two layers within a given manufacturer's process rules, which is a common reason to step up.[1323] At the extreme, a 0.5 mm pitch BGA can specify a six-layer minimum just to break out, putting it beyond four layers except by unusual tricks.[514] Even a four-layer board that does accommodate an FPGA in a BGA package forces compromises, such as placing bypass capacitors further from the pins than the ideal fan-out-and-bypass-underneath arrangement an eight-layer board would allow.[711]

The trade runs the other way as well. Choosing a driver chip that can sit directly beside the LCD pins and be routed out cleanly may allow a two-layer board where a four-layer would otherwise be needed, and those cents matter in cost-sensitive products.[1074] Cheap consumer goods historically went to considerable lengths — printing series resistors onto a flat flex as a resistive substrate, for instance — specifically to avoid a four-layer PCB.[1166] Conversely, for any thermally demanding design where the part cannot be electrically tied to a plane, a four-layer stackup is assumed present because it is what an advanced design will be using anyway, and it supplies large ground and power planes to spread heat.[1347]

## Manufacturing and cost

Four-layer prototypes are inexpensive enough that home fabrication makes no sense — producing a four-layer board oneself would require enormous effort for no benefit.[ct9K-EutiIQ] Five 6.25 by 9.25 inch four-layer boards, with solder mask and single-sided silkscreen, came to 72 US dollars plus courier shipping, delivered by DHL within a few days of ordering.[ct9K-EutiIQ][1117][Ep4r-wD7PPs] Options such as a non-default solder mask colour added roughly ten dollars.[ct9K-EutiIQ] Large boards consume a lot of panel space and the four-layer process itself adds cost, so the price is remarkable more for big boards than for small shield-sized prototypes.[ct9K-EutiIQ] Quality at that price is prototype-grade rather than the absolute best — minor solder mask alignment error is visible — but usable for production if desired.[ct9K-EutiIQ]

Turnaround scales with layer count. A domestic fabricator quoted 24 hours for a double-sided board and 48 hours for a four-layer, against six working days for eight layers, most of that six days being engineering and tooling rather than the physical manufacture.[358]

CAD support varies. The free tier of some packages is limited to two signal layers, which makes a four-layer design impossible in that tier, while other freeware versions have been found to allow a four-layer design with power and ground planes and even split grounds.[1374][255] Post-processing scripts may need to adjust pad sizes per layer depending on whether the two-layer or four-layer process is being used.[125] A design tool's fabrication output stage will report the detected layer count, though Gerber previews commonly show only the top and bottom.[1193] Converting an existing two-layer design to four layers can be done by changing the inner power planes to signal planes and moving traces onto them, since the pours are polygon pours in either case.[1193] For a simple four-layer board, checking amounts to making sure the ground planes are not compromised and that DRC passes; a commercial board warrants days of checking instead.[1193]

## Recognising four layers in an assembled board

An unpowered board can usually be identified as four-layer by eye. The internal copper shows through the substrate as a darker background or a dark outline around the plane edges, visible against the lighter areas where the plane has been relieved.[ct9K-EutiIQ][1415][436][452][738][1256] Holding a board up to a light or a light box reveals the inner structure on some boards, and equally the inability to trace where tracks go is itself a symptom of internal layers.[1415][164] Some layouts include deliberate layer markers — small squares left in the copper — precisely so the inner layers can be counted, and older boards sometimes carry an explicit layer-count designator.[969][1060] Where a plane is present over only part of the board, the dark region maps its extent: an ECL section covered by unbroken ground and power, with the plane carried straight across a comparator input rather than split at it, is a case of the plane being placed where the decoupling matters most.[452]

Four-layer construction is common enough across instruments and consumer equipment that its presence is unremarkable — logic analysers, oscilloscopes, function generators, gas detectors, PDAs, benchtop and test instruments, and vintage computers all use it, generally with the same internal ground and power arrangement on what is often a single 5 V rail.[436][475][497][603][334][875][414][1053][1404][955][1060] In older equipment it signalled reasonably advanced manufacturing for its day, and mobile handsets of the early era were at least four layers.[492][410] Some vintage designs avoided it entirely, using a well-executed double-sided layout with orthogonal traces and no jumper links where four layers were not available or not affordable.[955][645] It was also possible to sidestep a four-layer board by integrating heavily: splitting a processor across custom ASICs kept the density low enough that a fancier stackup was unnecessary.[663]
