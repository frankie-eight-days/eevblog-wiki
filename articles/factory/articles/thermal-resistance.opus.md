# thermal resistance

Thermal resistance is the measure of how strongly a material, joint, or package opposes the flow of heat through it, expressed in degrees Celsius per watt (equivalently kelvins per watt).[105][191] A component with a thermal resistance of 50 °C/W will sit 50 °C above whatever it is dumping heat into for every watt it passes.[744] The figure matters because it is what converts a power dissipation number into a temperature, and temperature is what destroys parts: a designer who knows only that a device wastes 10 W knows nothing useful until the thermal resistances between that device's die and the surrounding air are known.[105][744]

## The electrical analogy

Thermal design is done with the same circuit theory as electrical design, and the mapping is exact rather than a convenience.[744][105] Power in watts takes the place of current, temperature takes the place of voltage, and thermal resistance in degrees C per watt takes the place of resistance in ohms.[744] A device dissipating 10 W is modelled as a 10 W source driving that equivalent current through a chain of thermal resistors, and the temperature at any node is found the same way a voltage would be.[744][105] In a datasheet these resistances are written with the symbol theta and a pair of subscripts naming the two points they span.[744][1347]

The chain for a typical power part runs junction to case, case to heat sink, and heat sink to ambient.[744][105] Junction to case, theta-JC, is the resistance between the semiconductor junction inside the package and the mounting tab or top of the case.[744][1347] Where no external heat sink is fitted, the relevant figure is junction to ambient instead.[sr1DOHnJi8I][105] Because the resistances are in series, they simply add: 10 °C/W from die to solder point plus 5 °C/W from solder point to the fins gives 15 °C/W overall, so 1 W into a heat sink already sitting at 85 °C puts the chip at 100 °C.[361] Elements in parallel divide in the usual way.[744]

Each physical interface in a real assembly contributes its own term — the die-to-case resistance of the transistor, the vias carrying heat through the board, the insulating sil pad, the heat transfer block, and the case itself.[1347][895] The sil pad figure is a published datasheet value like any other.[895]

## Package and device figures

Junction-to-case resistance is strongly a function of package size and construction. Large TO-247 packages have a much lower junction-to-case thermal resistance than TO-220 parts, which is one reason a bank of ten IRFP250N transistors split five per side of a heat sink is regarded as sound practice for an electronic load.[862] A plastic DIP logic package sits at 45 °C/W junction to case, so a die dissipating 1 W inside a package whose lid reads 45 °C is actually running at about 90.1 °C.[sr1DOHnJi8I] An MTP3055 power MOSFET specifies 3.13 °C/W junction to case and 62.5 °C/W junction to ambient — the gap between the two numbers being the entire argument for bolting the thing to something.[105] A high-power LED array specifies 0.13 °C/W junction to case at 4.2 A, low enough that 80 W of dissipation lifts the junction only about 10 °C above the case.[773] A Cree LED quotes 6 °C/W from die to its solder junction.[50] An LM317 in a common package is specified around 24.4 °C/W.[512]

Where a device is barely dissipating anything, the thermal figures cease to be a selection criterion; junction-to-ambient resistance can be waved through for a part chosen for a switching application where the chips never get warm.[1461]

## Resistors

The same parameter appears on ordinary resistors under the name RTH, and it is routinely overlooked. A typical axial quarter-watt resistor is specified at 140 K/W, meaning a full watt would raise it 140 °C above ambient.[191] Run at its rated quarter watt it rises 35 °C above ambient and does so continuously by design — the rating is a permitted temperature rise, not a promise that the part stays cool.[191] Thermal resistance is essentially a property of the package rather than the resistance value, so any half-watt resistor of a given size behaves much like any other.[212] Manufacturers usually present this as a graph of power dissipated against temperature rise above ambient rather than a single number.[212][191]

Lead length changes the picture. The leads themselves have thermal resistance, and a resistor mounted close to the board can use the PCB copper as a heat sink, which is why the derating curve is most linear for short leads.[191] Stretch the leads to 15 mm and the temperature rise at the far end falls off; at 25 to 30 mm almost no heat reaches the board, because it is all trapped in the resistor core or shed along the leads.[191] The result is a series thermal resistance problem rather than a simple lookup.[191]

## Getting heat off a surface-mount device

A surface-mount power device has no tab to bolt down, so the heat must go through the board. Pressing the plastic body of a package against a metal case does not work — the plastic presents far too much thermal resistance, and metal-to-metal contact is required to move the heat.[744]

Thermal vias are the standard route, and individually they are poor. A 1 mm diameter via in a 1.6 mm double-sided board with 1 oz copper and 35 µm of barrel plating computes to roughly 49 °C/W, rounded to 50 °C/W for estimation.[744] One watt through one such via means a 50 °C rise.[744] The remedy is parallelism: two vias give 25 °C/W, and boards typically carry a matrix — nine in a three-by-three, or a 4-by-4 pattern of sixteen surrounding the pad.[744] Commercial designs push this hard; a DPAK on a source measure unit board had its underside completely populated, on the order of a hundred vias.[607] The Saturn PCB Toolkit is the usual means of getting these ballpark numbers.[744] Via calculators generally normalise their output to a 1 °C temperature rise, so the designer must first decide what rise is acceptable.[543]

Solder flowing through the via barrels is often treated as a manufacturing defect, but it fills the barrel and lowers the thermal resistance through to the far side; removing solder mask from a pad to encourage this is a deliberate tactic.[607][463] Voids and bubbles beneath a sil pad work the other way, leaving air gaps in the path.[607]

Heat spreading into a copper plane is not free either. A plane used as a heat sink suffers spreading losses, becoming progressively less effective with distance from the source, so a plane can only be made so thin before the outer region stops contributing.[1196] Surface area and the convection over it are what actually remove the heat, which is why extruded heat sinks carry large deep fins.[1196]

Surface-mount thermal jumpers are electrically insulating bridges that carry heat from a device pad into a via field and then into the ground plane, using the whole board as a heat sink for very little area.[1347] They are not zero resistance but are reasonably low: an 0603 part is about 14 °C/W, while an 0612 — twice as wide as it is long rather than half as wide — drops to 4 °C/W, comparable to roughly ten thermal vias.[1347] Some parts reach 3 °C/W, and versions in aluminium nitride or beryllium oxide are available, beryllium oxide being marginally the better of the two and worth the cost only where cost is not a constraint.[1347] Fitting a jumper to a larger pad alone pulled a device down to 95 °C, with further improvement expected from vias to a ground plane beneath.[1347]

## Heat sinks, airflow, and measurement

Heat sink datasheets give thermal resistance in °C/W plotted against airflow in linear feet per minute.[895] For a 625-45 style extrusion, a specified 5 mph airflow corresponds to about 440 LFM, reading back to roughly 8 °C/W.[895] Against 17.3 W of dissipation — the loss implied by 75% efficiency at that input voltage — 8 °C/W predicts a 138 °C rise, or about 158 °C from a 20 °C ambient.[895] Under natural convection the graphs must be worked through to obtain a resistance, but under forced air the figure can be read directly, because dissipation in watts no longer affects the thermal resistance once the air is moving.[105]

Measured values track the models closely. In a controlled example at 18 °C ambient with 10 W flowing, the heat sink reached 76 °C and the case 87 °C; 58 °C across the heat sink divided by 10 W gives 5.8 °C/W against a graph value of about 5.5 °C/W.[105] The same measurement yields the case-to-heat-sink figure that could not be looked up beforehand: 11 °C across the joint at 10 W is 1.1 °C/W, poor for a raw bolted joint with no thermal compound.[105] With 550 ft/min of forced air the heat sink fell to 36 °C, giving 1.8 °C/W against a graph estimate near 1.2 °C/W.[105] The case-to-heat-sink differential was unchanged by airflow, since that interface is just the air gap between transistor and metal.[105]

Datasheet values should be treated as starting points rather than verdicts. A heat sink measured at about 4 °C/W where the nearest datasheet part claimed roughly 15 °C/W differed mainly in fin area, and taking the published number at face value would have condemned a design that back-of-the-envelope testing showed to be perfectly reasonable.[895] The same reasoning runs in reverse for junction temperature: a probe on the case is not the junction, and the datasheet junction-to-case figure has to be added in to get the real die temperature.[158] Where the die is inaccessible, junction temperature can be inferred from a thermocouple on the fins plus the known input power and the datasheet die-to-solder-point resistance.[361] Working the arithmetic backwards from 12 W into an aluminium strip and a measured 13 °C differential gives about 1 °C/W for the strip.[50]

Large aluminium heat sinks have so little thermal resistance that a single temperature sensor clamped to one face adequately represents power transistors mounted on both.[862] Pressing a heat sink into a stamped feature gives lower thermal resistance than the alternatives used elsewhere on the same assembly.[1477]

## Interface materials

Thermal compound is not improved by generosity. Beyond a certain thickness more compound increases the thermal resistance of the joint rather than decreasing it, so the quantity has to be right.[1058] Compound also ages: paste that has dried out and gone crusty presents too high a thermal resistance and produces the progressive worsening and eventual thermal cutout characteristic of a failing CPU cooler.[yKflC-fmSX8] Immersion arrangements carry a similar penalty — mineral oil around a die stack conducts heat but still adds thermal resistance to the path, in a module where each die dissipates up to 10 W against a 600 W module maximum.[1341]

Mechanical looseness is a thermal resistance in its own right. In a soldering iron where the tip is physically separate from the heating element and the thermocouple, the reliance on contact plus whatever air sits in the gap adds resistance between element and tip, producing lag between the element heating and the tip getting hot — a real problem when a large ground plane sucks heat out of the joint.[1064][1065]

## Beyond electronics

Conduction of any kind is governed by the thermal resistance of the medium, which need not be a solid; gases and liquids conduct too, just less effectively, and different metals, gases, and fluids vary widely among themselves.[1187] Air is a poor conductor but its thermal resistance is not infinite, so every heater — convection, radiant, or infrared panel — delivers some conducted heat as well as radiated and convected heat.[1187]

Cell cooling is the same problem at larger scale. Heat inside a cylindrical cell moves axially, so extracting it through the sides presents a higher thermal resistance than taking it from the top and bottom.[1340] In older cell designs the small thin tab is a choke point of massively high thermal resistance: a large slab of copper inside is of no help if the exit path bottlenecks.[1340] Folding all the copper over into one large chunk at the base of a 4680 cell gives a very low thermal resistance by making the contact direct, and does so alongside an order-of-magnitude reduction in internal resistance that cuts loss at 10 A from 2 W to 0.2 W.[1340]
