# capacitor discharge

Capacitor discharge is the removal of stored charge from a capacitor, either as a deliberate bench safety step before working on a circuit or as the natural exponential decay that governs any RC network.[1406][1655] The two meanings sit close together in practice: the same exponential law that describes a timing circuit's fall curve also decides how long a mains filter capacitor stays lethal after the power is switched off.[1406][1655] In equipment with a rectifier and bulk filter capacitors, the stored energy remains at hundreds of volts long after the supply is disconnected, and discharging it deliberately is the difference between safe servicing and a serious shock.[1655][631]

## The exponential decay

In a basic RC circuit, a voltage source charges the capacitor through a series resistor; switching the source out and shorting the top of the resistor to ground discharges the capacitor through that same resistor.[1406] The discharge curve is the mirror of the charge curve: the instantaneous capacitor voltage starts at whatever value it was charged to and falls exponentially from there, reaching 36.8% of the starting value after one time constant.[1406] The current follows the same shape but starts at its maximum, set by the applied voltage divided by the series resistance, and decays toward zero.[1406] In theory the voltage never quite reaches zero; after roughly ten time constants — about an order of magnitude beyond the time constant — it can be treated as fully discharged for any practical purpose.[1406]

The same curve appears wherever a charged capacitor sees a resistive path. Driving a capacitor from a signal generator through a random resistor produces a clean linear ramp on charge and a recognisable capacitor discharge curve as it dumps into the resistive load.[1552] In a 555 timer circuit, the discharge pin's internal transistor is switched on at the 6 V threshold and drains the 1 µF timing capacitor through the 100 K resistor to ground, taking half the time the charge took because the charge path went through 200 K.[555] A peak detector shows the unwanted version of the same behaviour: leakage prevents the held value from staying put, so the stored voltage droops away toward zero until the next peak tops it back up.[490]

## Bleeder resistors

Well-designed equipment discharges its own capacitors. A mains capacitor-dropper supply places bleeder resistors across the 220 nF mains capacitors so that a unit pulled from the outlet does not present a charged capacitor at the exposed pins.[1482] The implementation detail matters: two 750 K resistors in series, 1.5 MΩ total, are used rather than one, because the SMD resistors in question are rated to only about 200 volts each.[1482] Large high-voltage electrolytics in consumer gear usually carry a bleed resistor as well, but the presence of one should never be assumed — the capacitor should be measured before it is touched.[365]

Many supplies simply omit them. A mains supply with a full-wave bridge and bulk filter capacitors sitting at 400 volts DC can hold that charge for a long time after the mains is switched off precisely because no discharge resistors were fitted.[1655] The same is true of large LCD television supplies, where the main capacitor retains a couple of hundred volts with no bleed resistor across it and can take tens of minutes to decay on its own.[631][630] Photographic flash capacitors are a compact version of the same hazard, typically charged to 300-something volts with substantial stored energy behind them.[816][1440]

## Discharging with a multimeter

The low-impedance or low-Z voltage mode found on many multimeters — nominally provided to kill ghost and phantom voltages — doubles as a controlled capacitor discharger.[631][630][1429] Selecting it places a low impedance directly across the input, so the meter both drains the capacitor and reads the voltage while it does so.[816] On one implementation, the mode switches in a 1 K series resistor together with a PTC resistor.[1655] The effect is fast: a camera flash capacitor at 265–267 volts drops through 70, 34, 19 and 12 volts within seconds on an auto-V mode that inserts a couple of kilohms of load,[1440] and a television main filter capacitor can go from an overload indication to zero volts almost immediately.[631] A 0.5 µF capacitor operating at 128 volts is emptied essentially at once.[663]

Meters without a low-impedance mode can still do the job. Switching to the ohms range for a couple of seconds and switching back discharges the capacitor safely, and this works with practically any multimeter of reasonable quality.[1655] The reason is visible in the input protection: the ohms range presents a clamping circuit and PTC resistors across the input, functionally similar to what the dedicated low-Z mode does.[1655] In the 121GW, two 1.5 K PTC resistors sit across the input in ohms mode — a value high enough to limit the current and low enough to discharge quickly, and safe against 240 volts mains because the PTCs increase their resistance sharply as current flow and internal power dissipation heat them.[1655] Ordinary 1.5 K resistors of the same value would burn up quickly under the same conditions.[1655]

The recommended procedure is to disconnect the circuit from the mains before switching the meter to the ohms range, rather than switching ranges with high DC voltage present on the leads.[1655] A separate approach for larger capacitors is a discharge lead with a resistor built into it, placed across the terminals,[1655] or a suitably rated dummy load resistor calculated for the job.[630]

## Dielectric absorption and recovery

A capacitor that has just been discharged does not necessarily stay at zero. The voltage creeps back up after the discharge path is removed, an effect caused by dielectric absorption in the capacitor.[1655] The recovery is routinely visible when a discharged capacitor is re-probed with a high-impedance voltage range,[1440][1429] and on supercapacitors it can be pronounced.[1511] The recovered voltage is not dangerous, but it means a single discharge should not be taken as permanent, and it is one more reason to measure rather than assume.[1655][365]

## Bench practice

The standing rules are to measure before touching, and to assume nothing about bleed resistors.[365][1726] Equipment with vacuum tubes deserves particular caution, since anode voltages are high and travelling wave tube supplies can leave a couple of thousand volts floating around inside; the capacitors must be confirmed discharged before poking around.[XqakD0dXdjM] Large capacitors in vintage computers and CRT-based gear hold enough energy that draining them is a prerequisite to getting hands inside the chassis, and a low-Z range makes that quick.[905] Discharging is also a precondition for some measurements: ESR meters warn to discharge the capacitor before measuring, since applying a charged capacitor to the instrument can destroy it.[365]

Discharge behaviour is itself diagnostic information. Watching a filter capacitor decay slowly at 10 seconds per division confirms both that it is holding charge and that it will take a long time to become safe.[1388] Conversely, a mains filter capacitor observed actively discharging on the meter confirms the discharge path is doing its job.[1726] Where circuits deliberately discharge a capacitor, the timing shows up directly — a constant-current mode that switches a transistor to drain a 22 µF capacitor through 1 K to ground takes roughly 50 milliseconds to complete, and lowering that resistance toward zero shortens it.[224]

Small capacitors matter too, at the opposite extreme. Bypass capacitors on a low-power microcontroller store enough energy to keep the circuit alive for several seconds after the supply is broken, and the device can appear dead and then come back.[269] The absence of a load is what preserves charge: in a Cockcroft-Walton multiplier with no load connected, the coupling capacitor stays charged indefinitely, and only when the input amplitude is reduced does the discharge on the capacitors become visible as they take some time to fall.[469]
