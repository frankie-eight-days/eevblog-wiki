# oscillation

Oscillation is the unintended self-sustaining ringing of a circuit that was meant to sit still, and it is one of the most common ways a nominally correct design fails on the bench. Any closed-loop system — an op-amp stage, a linear regulator, a current source, an electronic load, a bench power supply — carries enough gain and phase shift to become an oscillator if it is compensated badly or loaded outside the conditions it was designed for.[629][1147][1550][1609] Oscillation is also useful in diagnosis: it is a visible symptom that points at a loop, a load, or a marginal logic level, whereas a circuit that simply does nothing offers no clue at all.[500][1245]

## Closed-loop amplifier stability

A closed-loop amplifier needs dominant pole compensation; without it the stage oscillates like a banshee.[629] The practical method for checking a loop is to drive it with a square wave from a signal generator and look at the shape of the output response.[629] Excessive ringing or overshoot on that step is the precursor to outright oscillation, so the test is deliberately pushed to worst case — adding capacitive load until the overshoot looks awful — to see whether the stage will break into oscillation.[l2LBkXxN81Y]

Capacitive loading on an op-amp output is a standard trigger. Adding bypass capacitance directly to an op-amp output, even through a series resistor, loads it capacitively and is not a good idea as a general fix, even where it happens to cure the immediate problem.[n7XX75S98e8][l2LBkXxN81Y] The alternative — bypassing the input side of the divider that creates a virtual ground rather than the output — changes the response away from ideal in its own way, so neither solution is free.[n7XX75S98e8]

Composite amplifiers, which cascade two op-amps inside one feedback loop to combine the best properties of each, oscillate readily if implemented carelessly. The general rule is that the second-stage amplifier must have a greater gain-bandwidth product than the first, otherwise the pair tends to oscillate.[1609]

Not every stage is at risk. An amplifier fed directly from a low-impedance battery, with no switching supply anywhere in the signal path, is stable enough that supply decoupling can be omitted entirely.[528]

## Regulators and references

Low-dropout regulators are a classic stability problem. They use a PNP or P-channel pass element, which is inherently less stable than the alternative, and they will oscillate unless loaded with the correct amount of capacitance, of the correct type, with the correct ESR.[1147] Verifying that an assembled regulator is not oscillating is a routine part of characterising it: the output is examined AC-coupled on a scope — 20 mV per division is a useful setting — and if the output is clean and the part is not dropping out, the noise performance follows.[158][224] A well-behaved regulator will recover from a load step with no ringing and no oscillation even into hundreds of microfarads of output capacitance.[1147]

The loop of a discrete linear regulator depends strongly on the pass transistor. Substituting a 2N2222 as the output device changes the loop response enough to make the circuit oscillate at high frequency, and simulation reproduces this closely enough to match what is measured on the bench.[95] When a loop is genuinely unstable, output capacitance is not a cure: a discrete regulator built with an extra gain-of-four stage folded into the feedback loop oscillated no matter how much output capacitance was added, and stepping the simulated capacitance from 10 µF down to 1 µF and then removing it entirely made no difference.[95]

Because stability is a property of the specific silicon and not just of the schematic, a part carrying a new suffix must be re-qualified rather than dropped in: it may drop out differently, or oscillate with load and bypass combinations that the old part tolerated.[1752]

## Precision current sources

A precision current source built directly from a voltage-reference application note oscillated badly enough to be unusable, which is the standard argument for breadboarding an application note rather than trusting it.[567][577] The symptom on the sense output was gross AC noise at 20 mV per division.[567] Fixes proved narrowly conditional: a 10 µF ceramic output capacitor landed in exactly the wrong spot and put oscillation into the loop, while other values worked, and a 470 µF capacitor across the sense resistor could not settle the loop once a 12 kΩ resistor was shorted out.[577] The circuit could be made stable for one particular load, which is acceptable when the intended use is a single fixed operating point such as a 1 A generator.[577]

## Power supplies, loads and load interaction

Oscillation frequently belongs to neither instrument on the bench but to the combination of the two. A bench supply driving an electronic load in constant-power mode at 96 W oscillated because the supply's response loop and the load's response loop did not work together.[1298] Constant-resistance mode at 1 Ω, with the supply current-limited to 5 A, produced the same audible oscillation.[439] A supply whose output was switched off still went into constant current mode and oscillated audibly, with the front-panel LED blinking, when an electronic load was connected to it.[1691] All of the difficulty in an electronic load — otherwise little more than an op-amp, a resistor, a bank of MOSFETs and a heatsink — is in the loop stability that keeps it from oscillating.[1550] At very low terminal voltages, constant-power mode can break into oscillation with the readings jumping around.[774]

Current-limit behaviour in a supply is itself a controlled oscillation: as the output capacitance discharges the voltage falls, the load current falls with it, and the loop oscillates and servos around an average of the set current.[221] A clean transition from constant voltage into constant current shows neither overshoot nor oscillation.[1691]

## Digital circuits

Oscillation in logic is usually the result of an input that is neither a valid high nor a valid low. In a CMOS gate, a slowly ramping input turns both transistors partially on, drawing excess current, and if the input oscillates the excess current is drawn as well.[941] A Schmitt trigger input removes the oscillation and the problems that follow from it, though it does not remove the increased supply current caused by a slow input; the supply current of a Schmitt trigger gate still peaks at the two threshold voltages.[941] The slower the input transition, the more oscillation is produced, and even a fast transition leaves some.[941] The consequence is not merely wasted power: an oscillating edge feeding an interrupt input rather than an indicator can trigger repeatedly.[941]

The same failure appears at board level. A leaky axial tantalum produced a non-valid logic level, the following stage oscillated, and its output drove a reset line, giving intermittent operation and an anomalous 4 mA supply current — a fault that would have appeared to work if tested at the moment the level happened to be valid.[1245] Where a counter misbehaves, zooming in on the clock edge to look for multiple transitions distinguishes a genuine count error from an input oscillating and going metastable.[1208]

## Intentional oscillation

Where oscillation is the intended function, the same loop conditions must be satisfied deliberately. An oscillator that will not start is often a phase problem rather than a gain problem: amplifier stages measuring the correct gain of roughly 40 times each will still fail to oscillate if the feedback arrives out of phase with itself.[XRyDyOANQhc] Confirming that a board oscillates at roughly the right frequencies isolates the fault to the control circuitry elsewhere.[XRyDyOANQhc] Injecting an external oscillator signal into a running oscillator makes the result jump around as the two oscillations conflict, so the injected signal must be removed before the native oscillation can be judged.[XRyDyOANQhc]

A soft-latching power switch oscillates by design if the button is held down past the RC time constant, and the oscillation frequency is set by that time constant — reducing the capacitor from 47 µF to 10 µF makes it oscillate correspondingly faster.[262] A blinking LED circuit stops oscillating well above its rated minimum supply voltage when the discharge resistor is too low; at 130 Ω the current through the internal discharge transistor is high enough to stop the circuit even at supply voltages above the chips' 4.5 V operating minimum.[1653]

## Control loops and slow oscillation

Oscillation at the timescale of seconds to minutes is the same phenomenon in a mechanical or thermal loop. In PID tuning, increasing the integral and proportional terms substantially increases overshoot; the derivative term resists change and therefore reduces the oscillations, since both overshoot and oscillation are change.[VVOi2dbtxC0] A resistance standard held in a temperature-controlled room showed a visible oscillation in its reading traceable to the air conditioner cycling on a nominal five-minutes-on, five-minutes-off pattern, superimposed on a slow ramp as the room came down from 26 °C to 22 °C.[834] A heat-pump hot water system monitored by a dedicated current clamp, with no history of oscillation in the logged data, began oscillating on its power draw overnight — the sort of intermittent control-loop fault that a power cycle can clear.[Uq553ckJ0uQ]

## Finding the source

The first question is whether the oscillation belongs to the circuit or to the measurement. Disconnecting the meter and the probe leads establishes whether the instrument is contributing, and an unloaded output can oscillate on its own account: a power rail probe that behaved perfectly into 50 Ω oscillated at its output as soon as the scope input was switched to 1 MΩ, a fault attributable to the output driver disliking the absence of a 50 Ω termination.[1057][1733]

Beyond that, the technique is substitution and selective disconnection. Swapping between chips from different manufacturers isolated an oscillation to one particular part variant in one particular circuit configuration and load, and the culprit turned out to be the virtual-rail chip rather than the obvious suspect.[1057] The oscillation was high-frequency, at roughly 2.57 MHz, and increasing capacitive load from a few hundred picofarads through 2 µF to 100 µF changed its severity.[1057] In an amplifier repair, powering the unit with the preamp disconnected removed the oscillation, though that alone does not prove the preamp is the source — the disconnection also changes what else is switched in.[1072] Suspecting a capacitor is harder when the unit is run from a DC supply, since there is no ripple for a failed bypass capacitor to pass and the observed symptom is oscillation of the power amplifier module itself.[1243]

A multimeter on its ohms range whose reading will not settle indicates an active driver on the pin rather than a passive resistance, and probing this way is generally safe.[p-eLu1z7-cs] Where the oscillation is suspected to come from the construction rather than the circuit, the parasitics can be measured and simulated: the inter-contact capacitance of a solderless breadboard is 2 pF, consistent across four different types, and that figure can be dropped straight into a simulation to explain why a breadboarded circuit oscillates.[568]

Not everything that looks like oscillation is electrical. A ceramic capacitor exhibiting the piezoelectric effect produces a signal with a superimposed period that is not at a fixed frequency, the interval between successive peaks visibly varying.[855]
