# electromagnetic interference

Electromagnetic interference (EMI) is unwanted electromagnetic energy that couples from one circuit into another, either radiated through the air or conducted along cables and supply lines.[996][548] Practically every electronic product radiates some of it: unless a device is completely sealed in a metal can that is fully effective across the frequency range of interest, its switching frequencies and fast digital edges will escape, with PCB traces and internal wiring acting as small antennas.[548] The subject has two symmetrical halves — emission, meaning what a product puts out, and susceptibility or immunity, meaning what it tolerates coming in — and regulatory regimes address both.[996][1176]

## Loop area as the dominant emission mechanism

The single most important factor in generating and reducing electromagnetic interference is loop area: the total area enclosed by the outbound current path and its return.[1081] The larger that area, and the higher the frequency involved, the more effectively it behaves as an antenna and radiates.[1085] A design in which a clock sits at one end of a board and its load at the other, with high-frequency edges forced to travel across the board and all the way back, generates large amounts of interference and will not pass EMC compliance.[1081][1085]

This is the direct reason high-frequency bypass capacitors belong immediately next to the chip they serve: proximity minimises the loop the switching current has to traverse.[1085] Place the bypass capacitor well away from the chip and the return current has no choice but to follow a longer lowest-impedance path through the ground system, re-creating the large loop and putting compliance at risk.[1085] The loop area of concern includes not just the signal trace and its ground return but the power system and its bypass capacitors as well.[1176] The general layout rule follows: keep traces short and keep the loop area small and tight, which reduces both EMI generation and susceptibility to incoming interference.[1176]

Parallel combinations of decoupling capacitors of differing values extend the useful frequency range of the decoupling network, and the technique yields measurable benefit in both signal fidelity and EMI terms.[33][1085] The absence of a ground plane has the opposite effect; early double-sided computer boards running 5 V TTL relied on ferrite beads to take the edge off the resulting emissions.[1021]

Fast, high-current transients are a further source. Shorting out a charged bulk capacitor — for example a 330 µF cap charged to 3 V discharged through a switch — produces a very fast, very high current pulse that generates substantial EMI, worsened by long wiring that enlarges the loop.[961]

## What determines susceptibility

Impedance governs how easily external fields disturb a circuit. High-impedance nodes are excellent points of noise pickup and couple stray fields capacitively with ease; a high-voltage probe divider network with roughly 2 nF across its high-impedance section required encasing the whole assembly in a copper tube for this reason.[85] Low-impedance circuitry is the converse case: an arbitrary waveform generator output with 50 Ω drive is difficult to disturb from outside, because the interfering sources are comparatively high impedance, so external shielding is largely unnecessary for immunity — though it may still be wanted to stop the circuit radiating, which is a separate concern.[1679]

The same principle underlies the industrial 4–20 mA current loop. Industrial installations run long wiring harnesses, and a relatively high-current signal into a low-impedance input rejects noise pickup that would corrupt a high-impedance voltage signal over the same run.[705][706]

Differential measurement addresses coupling from a different direction. Long leads pick up interference both capacitively and by EMI coupling, and because it arrives on both conductors equally it appears as a common-mode signal; twisting the pair encourages that equality, and the amplifier's common-mode rejection removes it.[1521]

Immunity is a specified quantity, not an absolute. The Fluke 87V is rated to hold its accuracy up to around 3 V/m, and the meter was found to misbehave near a wireless router and could be locked up or bricked by a GSM mobile phone placed next to it.[112][178] Meters and instruments of all kinds are susceptible to electromagnetic interference.[112] Physically heavy, metal-rich construction correlates with strong immunity to external EMI.[372] The concern is not merely cosmetic: external noise interfering with a soldering iron's control electronics could lock it up or drive it to maximum temperature, so the design has to be foolproof.[472]

## Countermeasures in production hardware

Shielding cans, conductive foil, and gasketing are the standard structural remedies. A conductive RF sponge bonded with conductive adhesive maintains contact to a metal can;[964] RF fingers and metallic sponge around a lid establish a low-impedance RF path to the enclosure;[969] copper shielding tape appears under main boards for EMI reasons;[495] and shielding tape over a cooling fan aperture closes off a large opening through which energy could pass in either direction.[607] A sensor board may be extensively foil-wrapped to keep interference out of the analogue circuitry.[603] Not all shielding is magnetic — a plain shielding can addresses electric-field coupling, which is a different problem from magnetic shielding.[wWymcaiaQXM]

Ferrites are the common cable-level fix: cable wrapped a couple of turns through a ferrite takes the edge off conducted EMI,[641] and a large ferrite clamped across an LCD ribbon cable has the character of a late-stage remedy rather than an original design intent.[985] Similarly, a common-mode choke accompanied by a capacitor tacked on as an afterthought suggests EMI problems discovered during development.[418] RFI beads in a multimeter's signal path exist purely for high-frequency rejection to meet emissions standards; they are electrically bridgeable with a zero-ohm resistor if compliance is not a consideration.[884]

Topology choice matters at the power-supply level. Switching converters that dynamically vary their switching frequency across a wide range are difficult to filter, whereas a resonant-mode converter operates over a narrow frequency band by definition, making the EMC filter far easier to design.[1294] Peripheral controllers may include features such as late phase inversion in an LCD driver specifically to reduce power consumption and EMI.[1242]

Where components are placed relative to a power switch has compliance implications too: capacitors sitting on the battery side of the switch cannot serve any EMI requirement while the instrument is off.[1449]

## Compliance and testing

In the EU, the EMC Directive is one of the directives underlying the CE mark. It requires that a product neither emit electromagnetic radiation likely to interfere with other equipment — covering both radiated and conducted interference through connecting cables — nor be unduly disturbed by external electromagnetic interference from other products or the surrounding environment.[996]

Conducted emissions are measured by inserting a network between the mains and the device under test that passes power through while tapping off any RF present on the supply lines into a receiver or spectrum analyser.[993][548] Radiated emissions and immunity testing are performed in a shielded anechoic chamber, which functions as a Faraday cage in both directions: it excludes outside ambience such as broadcast signals so measurements are clean, and it contains the test facility's own emissions, which reach 200 V/m during immunity testing and would otherwise affect the surroundings — a real consideration under an approach path with an airport nearby.[202] Automotive-scale chambers add complications of their own, since a dynamometer running inside the chamber is itself an interference source whose spectrum must be characterised and worked around.[202] Instrument-level pre-compliance work is aided by scopes with an integrated spectrum analyser, EMI being a routine design problem rather than a specialist RF one.[199]

Compliance failures are historically common. Early personal computers built on double-sided boards without ground planes struggled against FCC requirements, and the TRS-80 was known to interfere with AM or FM radio reception when switched on.[955] Manufacturers with the resources took the problem seriously — shielding the underside of a subsidiary board, moving to four-layer construction — while cost-reduced consumer designs sometimes omitted a metal can entirely to save a few cents and radiated enough to cause interference as a result.[1053][955][767]

## Interference on the bench

Ambient interference routinely corrupts low-level measurements, and identifying its source is a normal part of instrument work. Pulse-width-modulated LED studio lighting couples a substantial amount of noise into test leads and measurement setups; switching it off produces a visible reduction in observed noise, and in unfavourable geometries the contribution can be far larger.[594][762] Computer monitors are another local source — moving a hand near a screen is enough to couple switching noise into a nearby circuit, and the effect persists with the circuit under test switched off, proving the noise is external rather than residual.[577][970]

Mains frequency dominates the ambient spectrum of a typical lab or office environment.[1379] Bench multimeters and handhelds exploit this by setting integration time to an integer number of power line cycles, so 50 Hz or 60 Hz interference is averaged out of the reading.[1379] Where that is not sufficient, enclosing the entire measurement — batteries included — inside a diecast shielded box and bringing the signal out on BNC eliminates external pickup.[1328] Touching or twisting the leads of a sensitive measurement visibly changes it, and the results vary from lab to lab.[1379]

Interference can also enter through the probe rather than the circuit. Electrostatic events can impulse into a probe or plain coaxial cable and produce glitches on screen that have nothing to do with the circuit under test, an effect that survives a lab coat and ESD bench at very low signal levels.[14] Unterminated inputs, probe movement, and proximity of a hand all show up as pickup when working at high sensitivity, so noise pickup becomes a first-order concern down at 500 µV/div.[196][1723] Instruments themselves can add noise as channels are enabled: enabling a third channel on a four-channel scope raised measured standard-deviation noise from 90 to 123 counts.[1355]

Suspected interference is not always interference, and the discipline is to test the hypothesis. A popping noise in a powered speaker, initially attributed to a Bluetooth-transmitting phone placed on top, did not survive repeated attempts to reproduce it by removing and replacing the phone, and the interference explanation was discarded.[1714]

## Other interference sources and phenomena

Mains ripple injection systems deliberately impose modulated high-frequency control signals on the distribution network to switch loads such as domestic hot water and street lighting. It is not uncommon for these control frequencies to upset products that are not well designed or well filtered, causing lights to flicker among other effects.[1283]

Broadband domestic sources degrade the radio environment. From an amateur radio perspective, "Plasma TVs are a killer" among nearby noise sources, which is why sites on the edge of the bush with distant neighbours remain usable within a large city.[218] Handheld near-field probes built around inductors and an audio amplifier make emissions audible, letting a user hear what equipment is radiating through headphones or an audio output.[827] Unexplained emissions can be observed disturbing other instruments directly, such as signals causing a frequency counter's backlight to flash.[uI62sAN5JzU]

Sensitive experimental apparatus faces the problem in acute form. A quantum computing cryostat is itself a Faraday cage with additional shielding throughout, yet still picks up interference because the measurements are so sensitive and signals must be brought in through the shield; fully shielded rooms with copper cable feed-throughs are the further step available for low-frequency work.[1594]

High-power wireless energy transfer raises interference concerns of its own. Dynamic wireless EV charging operating in the tens of kilohertz brings safety and EMC/EMI problems that have to be managed against vehicles containing hundreds of microcontrollers.[1584] For consumer wireless power, regulatory, safety, and interference constraints are engineering obstacles of the first order rather than details.[o2NxHu5Bsnk]

The topic also attracts pseudoscience. Electromagnetic interference appears alongside harmonic distortion, reactive power, and power quality in the marketing copy of energy-saving devices that do nothing, used as plausible-sounding technical vocabulary.[870] Shielding products sold to consumers — metal cages for Wi-Fi routers — do work in the literal sense, in that they also block the Wi-Fi, making the router pointless.[o2NxHu5Bsnk]
