# solar panel

A solar panel is a photovoltaic module: an array of silicon cells, laminated behind glass in a framed assembly, that converts incident sunlight directly into DC electricity. A silicon solar cell is effectively just a diode that produces a voltage when photons hit it, and a typical residential panel wires sixty of them in series in a 6-by-10 arrangement at roughly 0.6 volts per cell.[1426] What makes the technology worth discussing in engineering terms rather than promotional ones is that its output is bounded by a hard physical ceiling — the irradiance falling on the capture area — and almost every practical question about a panel reduces to how much of that ceiling a given installation actually reaches.[1633]

## Irradiance, capture area and the ratings that follow

The standardised industry figure for sunlight at the earth's surface is roughly 1,000 watts per square meter, and that is the number every panel is rated against.[1633][sJS2paVW-kc] Standard test conditions specify exactly that irradiance, so a panel sold as a 250 watt module is promising 250 watts at 1,000 W/m² with correct tilt and the correct load.[724][632] That figure is the theoretical maximum for a panel held perpendicular to the sun and tracking it, which makes it a best case rather than a typical one; real output falls below it according to geographical location, time of year and everything else.[632] A hypothetical 100% efficient panel of one square meter would therefore deliver 1,000 watts.[sJS2paVW-kc]

With solar it is all about capture area, and a conventional flat panel's capture area is essentially 100% of its face, less the small obstruction of interconnecting wires.[1633] This is why panel specifications collapse neatly into watts per square meter. A 230 watt SunPower E18 works out to 185 W/m²;[632] an LG Neon of the period sits around 210 W/m²;[1321] a 370 watt Neon 2 measuring about a meter by 1.7 meters gives about 217 W/m²;[1389] a modern commercial module runs about 200 W/m².[1047] Good terrestrial panels are around 250 W/m², against roughly 460 W/m² for space-qualified panels.[wTzQg1N-SLI] Expressed as conversion efficiency, an average modern module is around 23%, with better ones available — a 400 watt residential panel at 22.6% is near the top of the market — but the Shockley limit sets the ceiling that no amount of development gets past.[1633][1480]

Reflection is a minor loss mechanism, only about 5% of incident light, because almost all modern cells carry an anti-reflective coating to absorb as much light as possible.[1575] Bare silicon without that coating reflects over 30%, and removing the coating to change a panel's colour trades away efficiency.[1575] The dominant loss is thermalization: light is absorbed but not converted to electricity, and ends up as heat in the cell.[1575] Most of the energy a panel does not convert is absorbed as heat, not reflected away.[1575]

## Physical construction

A representative 250 watt monocrystalline module uses 156 by 156 millimeter cells with three busbars, weighs 17.3 kilograms, carries three bypass diodes, has an IP67-rated junction box on the back, and uses an anodised aluminium frame with a protective black coating.[484] Panels are mechanically tough: LG Mono X modules are rated for 5,400 pascals, equivalent to about 550 kilograms per square meter, and are designed to take golf-ball to tennis-ball sized hail without shattering.[846] A normal panel is just over a meter by 1.7 meters,[dlOtqDPCO1o][1389] which puts a 250 watt module at about 1.5 square meters,[881] and a 320 watt module of the same physical size at that same 1.5 square meters.[935]

Electrically, a 250 watt LG S1C-G3 gives 30.77 volts at peak power and 8.13 amps, with 8.62 amps short circuit, a 1,000 volt maximum system voltage and a 15 amp maximum series fuse.[484] A 370 watt LG 370N1CN5 sits at 35.8 volts maximum power point with 41.8 volts open circuit, 10.3 amps at maximum power and 10.9 amps short circuit, again with a 1,000 volt maximum system voltage that matters only when panels are strung in series.[1385] Larger commercial modules of 400, 415, 440 and 500 watts are readily available, and the physically larger 500 watt class is worth considering where roof area is the binding constraint.[1544][1628][puGooNKVE74][dlOtqDPCO1o]

## Temperature dependence

Panel efficiency decreases as cell temperature increases, at a rough rule of thumb of about 0.5% per degree Celsius for silicon.[1630] The practical consequence is that rising irradiance through the middle of the day heats the modules and pulls their output back down, so the theoretical peak implied by the nameplate rating is essentially never reached on a clear day; the curve overshoots briefly and then sags as the modules warm.[BtQUuD6QRMw] Conventional rooftop modules are helped by being lifted clear of the roof surface, which allows airflow underneath to cool them.[938] Integrated roofing products and panels sealed into a road surface lose that airflow, and the heat that builds up costs output.[938][743]

Actively cooling panels with water pipes bonded to their backs is offered commercially, with piping built into the panel so pool or supply water can be circulated through it.[1630] Cooling by 10 degrees Celsius recovers only the 0.5%-per-degree improvement, which is a poor return for plumbing an entire roof; where roof area is limited, the area is better spent on photovoltaic panels alone, whose output can be applied to any load rather than only to heating water.[1630]

## Shading, bypass diodes and series strings

Panels contain bypass diodes — three across a 60-cell module — so that current can route around a shaded section rather than being throttled by it.[484][1426] In a string installation, however, the modules themselves are in series: a twelve-panel string stacks 60 cells twelve times over and develops around 450 volts nominal DC.[1426] The failure of a single panel in such a string takes the entire string down.[1217] Those voltages are dangerous, and DC arcing inside the isolation switches can cause fires; roof-mounted DC isolators do fail in service.[1426][vlKQ3TzGrX8]

Shading losses are disproportionate and counterintuitive. The diffuse shadow of a coaxial antenna cable falling across a couple of cells of one panel in a twelve-panel array dropped output by 15 to 20% at 9:40 in the morning.[1426] Bypassing that one panel entirely accounts for only just over 8% of the array, so the diodes alone do not explain the loss; the remainder comes from the string inverter's maximum power point tracker being unable to cope with the disturbed operating curve.[1426] Panels also operate best with a uniform amount of light across their surface, which is why rooftop installs are laid out to avoid shading from nearby trees and other obstacles.[1603]

Microinverters address this directly by giving each panel its own maximum power point tracker, so a shaded, soiled or failed module degrades only itself instead of dragging down an entire series string.[1426][1386] A bird nesting on one panel will pull a string system down and will not pull a microinverter system down.[1386]

## Matching panels to inverters

Because a panel never reaches its nameplate rating in practice, it is normal and sensible to fit a panel rated above its inverter. A 295 watt microinverter on a 370 watt panel is a correctly specified pairing based on real irradiance data, at the cost of clipping on a nominal 38% of days per year.[1386] The practice is taken much further in regions with poor irradiance, where a 5 kW system may be built from 10 kW nominal of panels because the full nameplate output is never seen.[1386] Voltage compatibility is generally comfortable: a microinverter accepting 16 to 60 volts with a 60 volt absolute maximum is well clear of a panel whose open circuit voltage will not exceed 41 volts.[BtQUuD6QRMw]

Given a choice between equally sized panels, the higher-rated one is always the better buy: the physical area and the available irradiance are identical, so on a poor day at 500 W/m² the higher-rated panel still yields more, and the inverter absorbs the extra.[1386] The counterweight is price — the absolute bleeding edge of wattage carries a premium that ruins the bang per buck, so the sweet spot is a step below the top of the range.[1385] Inverter efficiency itself varies with input voltage, typically 94 to 97% and dropping only at the low end, so panel construction, cell count and technology all feed indirectly into system efficiency.[1386]

## Orientation, tilt, soiling and degradation

Rooftop panels are typically tilted, perhaps 20 degrees, to improve the angle of incidence; laying a panel flat costs around 20% relative to that.[632] Where a roof has more than one usable aspect, panels of different orientations should be split onto separate strings — an eastern array captures the morning load and a western array the afternoon, so an east-facing set reaches peak power much earlier in the day than a west-facing one.[1628][1385] Seasonal variation is large: in Sydney a rough rule of thumb is that winter output is about half of summer output.[I7wMkLJgnko][QkFioBbH5aM]

Dirt and bird droppings reduce output efficiency, though a lightly soiled array is hard to benchmark, because the sun moves during any before-and-after cleaning test and confounds the result.[724][0KX5F7Si-TY] Panels tend to end up dirtier along their lower edge, where run-off collects the grime as it drains.[0KX5F7Si-TY] Tilted rooftop panels are largely self-maintaining, since rain carries accumulated dirt off the surface.[1683]

Degradation is slow and warranted. A representative module is warranted at 97% in the first year, 0.7% annual degradation thereafter, and 80% at 25 years, with a 10 year product warranty.[484] Seven-year-old panels show almost no measurable production loss beyond accumulated dust, which cleaning restores.[1385] Panels removed from a roof after that long are still worth reselling and can be expected to last another 15 to 20 years,[vlKQ3TzGrX8] and reusing existing panels in a reinstall — or buying warehouse clearance stock — is a legitimate approach.[I7wMkLJgnko] Long service life also has a disposal consequence: solar panels are forecast to make up around 10% of Australian e-waste by 2035, up from less than 1% in 2015.[qDWLTSfrq_E][xiXucGYuQ6M]

## Cost, embodied energy and payback

Panels are not free of environmental cost. Silica must be mined at scale to produce the silicon and the glass, and the aluminium frames come from a heavily energy-intensive smelting process, so a significant amount of energy goes into manufacture — which is precisely why the industry speaks of a payback period.[sJS2paVW-kc][1591] Once produced, a panel keeps generating until it physically fails, on a typical life of 15 to 20 years.[sJS2paVW-kc]

The module itself has become the cheap part. Prices have fallen to the order of 36 cents per watt, and a 400 watt panel can be had for about 135 euros.[681][1544] This is a transformation from the early days of the industry, when a panel cost around $800 for 80 watts and the arrival of maximum power point tracking, worth 25 to 30% more harvest, materially changed the economics of PV.[eldXifbNm2A] Because the modules are now nearly a commodity, installation and maintenance costs dominate the system price and stay the same regardless of what the panels cost — so arguments that future panel price reductions will rescue a marginal installation miss the point.[681] The same logic applies to manufacturing innovations: a claimed 60% reduction in raw wafer cost is not a 60% reduction in the cost of solar on a roof, where installation and balance-of-system costs are what matter.[IbYtn420QBM]

## Flat mounting and road surfaces

Putting panels flat on the ground and driving on them combines every available loss mechanism at once, and the resulting installations consistently underperform rooftop solar by a wide margin. The losses are additive and predictable from a data sheet: a flat, non-tracking orientation costs about 20%; a thick, hardened protective glass or resin cover attenuates transmission; sealing the panel into the road removes the airflow that cools rooftop modules; and the surface then accumulates dirt, tyre grime and rotting leaves that destroy the uniform illumination panels need.[632][681][743][1603] Micro-cracks follow from the traffic loading itself.[1603]

The measured results bear this out. The Colas Wattway installation in Normandy was a one kilometer road covering 2,800 square meters, built from 2,800 panels of roughly 120 watts each for a 336 kW nominal system at a cost of 5 million euros — 14.88 euros per watt against about 1.57 euros per watt for a commercial solar farm.[1603][1047] At its peak the road produced half its expected energy, a section was replaced entirely, and it was removed after almost eight years.[1603] Equivalent rooftop systems within a couple of kilometers of a comparable installation averaged 146 kWh per square meter per year.[850] A Solar Roadways installation of 30 panels rated 50 watts each — a 1.5 kW system — produced less per day than a 3 kW rooftop array in winter, and its own SR4 modules are not full solar panels but a mixture of cells, heating elements and thick glass coating.[1269] Summarised against rooftop solar, the outcome is roughly a third of the power output for nine times the cost, before accounting for the physical disintegration.[1356] Motorway trials were abandoned because the panels were not resistant to heavy trucks and cars driving over them,[1556] and the failure mode where the panel and top layer detach from the underlying concrete produces distortion which causes further damage.[1234]

The engineering alternative is not exotic: mount the same panels above the road, cycleway or car park instead of in it. Doing so gives optimum output per unit cost, provides shade underneath, avoids the tough surface coating and the tyre tread entirely, keeps the panels easy to clean, and yields a 20 year service life on existing technology.[743][1234][1591] The same argument applies to rail corridors, where overhead wires and poles impose inherent shading for some hours a day, and where roofs and facades of stations and platform shelters are the obviously suitable surfaces.[1534][1683] Barcelona's roads and sidewalks together amount to a greater surface area than the city's roofs, but panels on those roofs cost about a tenth as much, deliver two to three times the output and last five to ten times as long.[1389] The one universal principle is that nothing should be placed on top of a solar panel.[850]

## Panels on vehicles

Vehicle-mounted solar is constrained by area before anything else. A solar-powered production car offers five square meters of double-curved array, and a lighter competitor three square meters for 700 watts — less than two residential panels.[1480][QkFioBbH5aM] Against a 40 square meter rooftop array, that is roughly an eighth of the capture area at best.[1480] The body shape makes it worse: with panels on the bonnet, roof and boot, whichever surface faces the sun leaves the others shadowed by the car itself, and a reasonable engineering assumption knocks off at least 25% for shape alone.[1480] The shading behaviour of these arrays is undocumented, and a single lamppost shadow crossing a parked car can cost a large fraction of the day's harvest.[1480] Working the numbers through with generous assumptions yields around 24 kilometers of extra winter range per day against a claimed 70.[1480] Even 700 watts of panel is only about 350 watts in winter.[QkFioBbH5aM] There is also a second-order penalty: maximum panel output coincides with the car sitting in hot sun, so much of the harvested energy goes back out through the air conditioning needed to cool the cabin.[TL4bLqcikbI]

The correct configuration for charging an electric car from the sun is a rooftop array plus a solar-aware charger, or panels over a workplace car park.[1480][QkFioBbH5aM] Onboard solar is supplemental technology, and worth integrating on that basis, but no amount of cell efficiency improvement changes the area argument.[1480]

Purpose-built solar racers use the same physics more aggressively. A competition solar electric car carries about one kilowatt of plain monocrystalline silicon, split into three strings across the top surface specifically because the awkward curvature would otherwise cost efficiency.[923] Spacecraft and rovers make the tilt explicit — a lunar rover design added a tiltable panel so it could be aimed at the sun — and satellite arrays are sized in square meters, at roughly 33 square meters per Starlink satellite.[886][wTzQg1N-SLI] At the small end, the same technology forms the mother-of-pearl face of a solar-powered wristwatch.[0018phUFjuQ]

## Systems, monitoring and measurement pitfalls

Household installations are commonly monitored with current clamps fitted around the conductors, measuring production from the panels, house consumption and grid export separately; the clamps are directional and must be installed the right way round.[1390] A subtlety appears once battery storage is added: a monitoring system that infers production from AC current cannot distinguish energy coming from the panels via the inverter from energy coming from the battery, so the battery appears as solar production at night and excess export flatlines during the day.[7WbO1lnsLC8][gAu8CvMjDrU] Per-panel monitoring is available with microinverters, down to DC voltage from an individual panel.[M4IiR4vW0aY]

Reliability in the field is dominated by the electronics rather than the panels. Fourteen microinverters of one brand ran for years without trouble, while a single 800 VA unit of another failed outright with two 440 watt panels connected.[puGooNKVE74] Hybrid inverters differ from older grid-tied units in being able to draw their own internal operating power from the solar side when the mains drops out, rather than shutting down entirely.[T2yNMQM_TEA]

## Recurring claims and their limits

Several categories of claim about panels recur and fail on the same arithmetic. Proposals to harvest energy the panel supposedly wastes through reflection founder on the fact that reflection is only about 5% of the loss budget and thermalization is the rest.[1575] Concentrator and multiple-reflection geometries fail because light does not usefully bounce off a solar cell — it is absorbed as heat.[1633] Claims of multiples over conventional panels run into the Shockley limit.[1633] Orbital mirrors reflecting sunlight onto ground arrays deliver on the order of milliwatts per square meter at the receiver, less than the residual consumption of the inverters needed to collect it.[wTzQg1N-SLI] And any product powered by a small panel is bounded by area: a device claiming to condense a liter of water in 150 minutes would need 0.6 square meters of 100% efficient tracking panel to do it, when a real 250 watt panel is 1.5 square meters and the fitted panel was around 30 centimeters on a side.[881][1121] Devices intended to replace panels on embodied-energy grounds face the opposite problem — a panel is the product of a highly optimised, cheap and energy-efficient manufacturing process, and alternatives with machined metal structures put far more energy in for far less out.[r4XQOMBmuKw][1389]
