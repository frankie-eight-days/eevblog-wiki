# measurement accuracy

Measurement accuracy is the degree to which an instrument's reading corresponds to the true value of the quantity being measured, and it is the property that determines whether a bench result means anything at all.[b-v3tz6beN4] It is distinct from resolution — the number of digits or counts a display can show — and the two are confused constantly, including by people who know better.[26][211] An instrument can display far more digits than its accuracy justifies, in which case the trailing figures carry no information and exist only for show.[1476]

## Accuracy versus resolution

Resolution is the fineness of the display: counts, digits, decimal places. It has nothing to do with accuracy.[26] Describing a meter as having an accuracy of four and a half digits, or an accuracy of 10,000 counts, is simply wrong; those figures are resolution.[26] A current measurement front end may resolve down to 100 nanoamps while being specified nowhere near that well in absolute terms.[269] The same split appears in decade resistance boxes, where a box specified to 0.1% at 1 kΩ can still be set with 0.1 ohm resolution — the resolution is finer than the accuracy of the value it produces.[211] On a benchtop power supply, an option that unlocks an extra digit of readback resolution does not change accuracy at all.[509]

## How accuracy is specified

A meter's accuracy is expressed as a percentage of the actual reading, not of full scale, plus a fixed number of counts.[26] A good handheld might be 0.1% plus two counts, which on a 1 volt measurement corresponds to an uncertainty of about one millivolt from the percentage term before the counts are added.[26] The counts term is where cheap instruments lose. A Fluke 87 V is quoted at 0.05% plus one count on basic DC volts, and the plus-one is the significant half of that figure — lesser meters advertise a similar 0.05% but pair it with plus four or plus five counts, which must be folded into any real uncertainty calculation.[10] The same pattern holds for LCR meters, where a 0.5% plus one count headline can beat more expensive instruments quoted at 0.5% with a higher count adder.[137] The counts term dominates when the reading has no decimal places to spare: a tweezer LCR meter specified at 5% plus three digits can read visibly wrong on a low-value part and still be inside its specification once the digits are counted.[1335] A meter that looks 0.04% out of a 0.025% class spec may likewise fall back inside once the plus-five-counts allowance is included.[712]

Accuracy specifications are also rarely a single number. LCR meters carry whole tables of figures broken out by range and test frequency, and the headline basic accuracy is met only in the middle ranges.[757][137][115] A 0.7% plus three digit basic spec can degrade to 1% plus five counts at the low end and 5% plus counts on a 10 millifarad range, and this range dependence is near-universal across the category.[115] Bridges show the same spread from the opposite direction: 0.01% in the comfortable middle, rising to about 1.5% out in the teraohms, where sufficient measuring current only exists if a high bridge voltage is applied.[1476] Temperature coefficient is part of the specification too; older instruments sometimes quote it per 10 kelvin rather than per degree Celsius.[482] Warm-up matters as well — an instrument switched on and used immediately is not yet in a state where its specification can be fairly judged.[613][FgkD8K0Ssdc]

## Verifying accuracy

Checking one instrument requires a better one. The working rule for calibration is a reference an order of magnitude — ten times — better than the device under test, so a 0.1% meter needs a 0.01% reference and comparing two identical meters against each other proves nothing.[286][1496] That ratio of ten is a test uncertainty ratio of 10; four is treated as the floor in the calibration industry rather than the target.[286] At the extreme metrology end the ratio can collapse to a factor of a few, at which point the exercise becomes considerably harder.[1496] Once the reference's own accuracy is folded in, agreement between two good instruments quickly becomes a question about the reference rather than the device.[461][377]

Spot checks against a reference source are the ordinary bench form of this. They are informative but weak evidence, since a single unit says nothing about a production population.[249][1378] Testing a whole batch of the same model against a reference generator addresses that directly, characterising how close a given design lands out of the box across ranges rather than how one favourable sample behaves.[852][Iwy8UVVQNkA]

## Improving accuracy in practice

Several techniques buy accuracy at the cost of speed or convenience. Integration time in a bench multimeter is set in number of power line cycles; more cycles slow the measurement but increase accuracy and reject 50/60 Hz noise, because the integration happens in the ADC rather than as smoothing applied afterwards.[1379] Pushing to 100 power line cycles is very slow and considerably more accurate, and can gain a digit of usable reading.[1379][489] On an oscilloscope, filling more of the vertical range with the signal reduces the error standard deviation of a measurement and changes how many decimal places the instrument is willing to report.[1226] High resolution or boxcar averaging mode differs from mathematical averaging in ways that range from negligible to large enough to change measurement accuracy and the apparent shape of the waveform.[878][1413] Analogue ohm scales are non-linear, so readings taken well into the range are more accurate than those near the crowded end.[1580] Clamp meters have a defined sweet spot for conductor position that is not always the jaw centre — on a 600 amp range it is central, while a lower current range may specify an offset position marked by arrows, and meeting the accuracy specification on that range requires using it.[3kdYGneg9xI]

## Fitness for purpose

Required accuracy follows from the measurement, not from what is available. Characterising a battery discharge curve is served perfectly well by 1% absolute accuracy in voltage and power, so a 0.05% class instrument adds nothing to the result.[774] Conversely, some measurements carry legal weight: electricity metering falls under legal metrology, where accuracy underpins a commercial transaction in the same way a post office scale does.[1446]

At the low end, a quoted figure can be honest and still misleading. A cheap meter quoted at 0.05% may meet that specification on one range out of the box and drift away from it, because the components chosen to hit the price cannot hold the figure over time.[6] This is the reason for keeping a second meter to cross-check anything important.[6] Marketing sometimes abandons the pretence altogether: one crowdfunded multimeter advertised "rockuracy" and its makers later conceded the measured performance was worse than initially claimed.[1238] Instruments do fail their published numbers under test — a wrist-worn meter specified at 0.5% measured around 3% on two DC volt ranges, an electronic load specified at 0.05% plus 0.05% of full scale read several tenths of a percent out, and a handheld's capacitance ranges came in 5 to 7% out against a 1% plus counts claim.[1706][1023][249] A microamp range reading 1.4 to 1.5% error where 0.1 to 0.2% was expected is roughly an order of magnitude wrong and indicates a fault rather than a tolerance.[133]

At the top end the figure becomes the selling point: a high-end real-time oscilloscope is marketed as "Engineered for unmatched real-time measurement accuracy." on the strength of its low jitter.[342] Elsewhere the numbers are simply the class of the tool — 0.008% basic accuracy on an impedance analyser[wjMIsM4sDw8], 0.1% across the ranges of a power analyser[589], roughly 0.005% one-year accuracy on a 6½ digit bench meter[613], 0.04% basic DC volts on an older microvolt DMM[658], 0.5% DC volts on an entry-level handheld[1692], 0.8% DC volts on a pocket meter[Iwy8UVVQNkA], about 2% typical on a thermocouple front end[1244], and 3% or so on an insulation resistance tester, which is more than adequate for what insulation testing asks.[468] Thermal stability is a separate axis: a handheld holding better than 0.01% across a 32 °C delta is doing well.[Wwz_fdU17aQ]
